\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

Here I use the results to answer the three questions of Section~\ref{sec:intro-requirements}.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

\paragraph{Real-image supervision.}
With the converted 3DPW labels I can train and test Anny models on real images. I checked them independently against
3DPW's own body joints, and the median distance is \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. But \DataConstantCount{} phenotypes get
the same value for every person, and many hand and head rotations stay at rest (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

Control already reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm PVE on converted 3DPW without the anchor and bone corrections, against
\BaseThreeDPW\,mm for Base. Final, with the corrections, reaches \FinalThreeDPW\,mm. This means that most of the gain
on real images comes from adding 3DPW to the training mix, and not from the corrections or from a change in the
network.

On the synthetic holdout Final follows weight, gender and cup size best (Pearson $r$ \PhenoRFinalCupsize{} to
\PhenoRFinalWeight, Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}). Every slope is below 1, so its shape head varies the body but pulls
the predictions towards the middle. Muscle is the weakest phenotype, likely because the converter fixes it for every
3DPW person, which I did not test.

On real photos I use the gender AUC, because it does not depend on a cut-off (Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}). Men still
get a gender value in the middle. The likely reason is the training data. The AnnyOne holdout's gender labels span
only \HoldGenderLabelMin{} to \HoldGenderLabelMax, and if the training images are similar, the model saw men labelled
0 only in 3DPW (Section~\ref{sec:res-rh}). Final-partial-mask keeps the children but loses the gender split at the
cut-off, so I kept Final as the teacher (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\paragraph{Compact reconstruction.}
The Student has \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters and \FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs than its
teacher, and it ran \SpeedRatioStudent{} times faster in the paired L40S benchmark. Its PVE rose from \FinalPVE{} to
\StudentPVE\,mm on the AnnyOne holdout and from \FinalThreeDPW{} to \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW. So the
Student is smaller and faster, but it is also less accurate.

Longer training did not close the gap on real images. After \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs the holdout PVE fell from
\StudentPVE{} to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, but on 3DPW it went from \StudentThreeDPW{} to \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm, a
small change whose repeatability was not measured on 3DPW (Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}). I have not tested why. Possible
reasons are the smaller backbone, the share of 3DPW in each batch and the teacher's own 3DPW error. The temperature
sweep (Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature}) will show whether $T = \MethodKDTemp$ was a good choice.

Against Multi-HMR-Anny the Student is \ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times smaller and has a lower 3DPW PVE
(\StudentThreeDPW{} against \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm), but a slightly higher PA-PVE (\StudentPAThreeDPW{} against
\NAVERPAThreeDPW\,mm; Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}). Only my models were trained on converted 3DPW. To separate my
network and the distillation from the training data, I would need a baseline fine-tuned on the same data.

\paragraph{Video.}
On the walking clip the Student kept a tracked body in \VideoStudentMatched{} of \VideoFrames{} frames. The baseline
did so in \VideoBaselineMatched. These are tracker matches, not recall against labels (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}).
By eye, temporal filtering made the motion steadier, and landmark alignment put the mesh on the person and reduced the
jumps in size. The clip has no ground truth and the landmarks come from a 2D detector, so this is no evidence of
better 3D accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:method-temporal}). On one \VideoGPU{} the raw Student ran at \VideoStudentRawFPS{} FPS and the
baseline at \VideoNAVERFPS\,FPS on the same frames. The filtered and aligned frame rates in Table~\ref{tab:video-fps} also include the time of those steps.

\section{Interpreting geometry and placement}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

PVE lines up the two pelvises before it measures vertex distances. So a model can have a low PVE and still put the
person at the wrong depth. Control and Final show this: the root error fell from \num{\ControlRootErr} to \FinalRootErr\,mm,
but the PVE only went from \ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm. That is why I report the placement error with the PVE.

From Control to Final the PVE over the neck skin fell from \ControlNeckSkin{} to \FinalNeckSkin\,mm on the holdout
(Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}). But the neck has only \DiscNeckVerts{} of the body's vertices, so it moved the
whole-body PVE by about \DiscNeckPVEShare\,mm (my estimate from the vertex counts, not a measurement). For this
reason I also judged the bone correction by bone angles and neck geometry (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

I trained the same short pilot twice, and the two runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE. In other words, a few
millimetres between two single runs can be training noise alone (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}). Two runs give no
general threshold, so I treat small differences with care.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

Children are a measured improvement, not solved. On Relative Human the teacher separates them from adults better than
Base, but some still get adult bodies. The holdout has no children, and the 3DPW labels fix age and body build for
everyone. The holdout gender labels have no fully male body, so a gender cut-off set there would mean nothing. All of
these shape results were measured on the teacher. I did not run the shape tests on the Student.

When a person's true depth grows by one metre, the teacher's predicted depth grows by only about \FinalSlope\,m, and
a large placement error remains (Section~\ref{sec:res-depth}). Consistent anchors give the model a cleaner target, but
one image still cannot tell a small person close by from a large person further away. One clip of one walking person
also says little about occlusion, people cut off by the frame or crowds.

My 3DPW errors are measured against fitted Anny labels, with MPJPE over \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints, so they cannot
be compared with published 3DPW results. The label discrepancy is not an offset that can be subtracted from them
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). My training never used the holdout, but for Multi-HMR-Anny it may be
in-sample, that is, part of its training data (Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}).

Each model comes from one training run without a fixed seed, and I did not switch off the Student's loss terms one at
a time. The movement statistics of the walking clip mix the person's own movement with camera motion and tracking
errors. Latency depends on the hardware, precision, input resolution and number of people.
The paired benchmark and the per-video timing were measured in different ways, so I report them separately.

The repository records the settings and the source of each number, but the cluster scripts contain paths that only
work on my cluster. Before releasing weights or converted labels I have to check the terms of use of the datasets,
the body model and the checkpoints, which I have not done.

\section{What I report and why}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

A model can find a person and still give them the wrong body, and an accurate body can still shake over a video.
Speed is a separate question again. The tracking counts, the metrics on labelled images, the video and the frame rates
each answer one of these questions. For this reason I report all of them, and not just one error value or one good-looking
example.

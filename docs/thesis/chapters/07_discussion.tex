\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

The results show that the student reduces model cost, but does not keep the teacher's reconstruction accuracy.
They also show why label quality, body geometry and mesh placement need separate checks.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

\paragraph{Real-image supervision.}
The converter makes 3DPW usable for Anny training. Its checked body joints differ from the source joints by
\DataCheckJointsNew\,mm at the median. It fits pose, height and proportions, but it cannot recover information
that the source joints do not provide. In particular, \DataConstantCount{} shape values are fixed, and many
hand and head rotations remain at rest.

Adding real images gives the largest improvement on converted 3DPW. Control reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm
PVE, compared with \BaseThreeDPW\,mm for the synthetic-only Base. Adding the anchor and bone corrections
gives Final's \FinalThreeDPW\,mm. This comparison supports mixed training, but does not separate every
effect of the data, restart and corrections.

\paragraph{Compact reconstruction.}
The student uses \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters and \FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs
than the teacher. Its paired L40S forward pass is \SpeedRatioStudent{} times faster. However, PVE rises
from \FinalPVE{} to \StudentPVE\,mm on AnnyOne and from \FinalThreeDPW{} to \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on 3DPW.
The student therefore offers lower cost with a clear accuracy loss.

The student is also \ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times smaller than the released baseline and has lower
3DPW PVE (\StudentThreeDPW{} versus \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm). Both the student and teacher trained on
converted 3DPW images, while the released baseline did not receive this adaptation. Training the baseline
on the same data would make the comparison more informative about the architecture itself.

\paragraph{Video application.}
The original walking run gives \VideoStudentMatched{} student matches and \VideoBaselineMatched{}
baseline matches in \VideoFrames{} frames. These counts show whether the tracker found a person;
they do not measure pose accuracy. Later versions add temporal filtering and landmark alignment.
Filtering makes consecutive outputs depend on one another, while alignment adjusts where the body is drawn.
Both operate after the image prediction and require no new student training.

The inspected example motivated two separate changes: smoothing the pose and shape, then stabilizing
the depth adjustment because independent alignment could make the mesh grow and shrink. A complete
numerical comparison of raw, filtered and aligned outputs is still needed. The older coverage counts
must not be treated as measurements of the later refinement settings.

\section{Interpreting Geometry and Placement}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

PVE first moves the predicted and target pelvises to the same point. It measures body geometry after removing
the whole-body translation. A mesh can therefore have low PVE and still appear behind or beside the person
in the image. In the teacher comparison, root error changes from \num{\ControlRootErr} to
\FinalRootErr\,mm while PVE changes from \ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm. This is why I report placement
as well as centred body error. PVE still measures differences in the body's physical size; only PA-PVE
also removes a fitted uniform scale.

A whole-body average can hide an error in a small region. Two neck rotations can partly cancel and leave
the head facing the right direction while twisting the skin between them. The bone-angle and neck-girth
checks reveal this problem more clearly than PVE alone.

Two identical short pilots differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE. This warns against reading too much into a small
difference between single runs. It does not provide a general error margin for every experiment.
Repeated training would be needed to estimate how much each reported result varies.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\paragraph{Shape and children.}
The teacher separates age groups better after mixed training, but still gives adult-like bodies to some
children. The synthetic holdout contains no children, and 3DPW's converted age values are constant.
The shape-mask experiments change both parameter variation and gender predictions, without providing
a reliable fix. These findings concern the evaluated teacher variants; the student needs its own
real-image shape evaluation.

\paragraph{Depth and detection.}
Final's predicted-versus-reference depth slope remains \FinalSlope. Consistent anchors reduce placement
error but do not solve the uncertainty of estimating distance from one image. Landmark alignment can
improve an overlay while retaining a wrong body shape or pose. Its landmarks are estimates too, and
their anatomical locations only approximately match the Anny joints.

\paragraph{Evaluation conventions.}
The 3DPW scores use fitted Anny targets and \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints, so they are not published
SMPL-space scores. Conversion error cannot be subtracted from network error. Unlabelled bystanders affect
precision, and the released baseline may have seen the selected synthetic holdout during its own training.

\paragraph{Experimental coverage and speed.}
Each model has one recorded training run, and the student losses were not all tested separately.
The walking clip has no ground-truth meshes. Natural movement and camera motion affect its motion
statistics, while smoothing can hide real movement or introduce delay. The single-person example also
says little about occlusion, camera cuts or crowded scenes.

Latency depends on the GPU, precision, image size and number of people. The paired model benchmark and
video timings measure different workloads. In particular, alignment FPS excludes offline landmark
extraction and cannot describe the speed of a complete online system.

\paragraph{Reproduction.}
The repository records settings and the sources of reported numbers. Some scripts still contain cluster
paths, and the large data files and checkpoints are stored separately. Releasing these files requires
checking the terms of the source datasets, checkpoints and body-model assets.

\section{Practical Implications}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

For this project, I need to ask four separate questions: was the person detected, is the body correct,
does it line up with the image, and is it stable across frames? Recall, reconstruction scores, placement
checks and temporal measurements answer different parts of this. A useful comparison reports them
alongside speed, instead of judging a model from one score or one good-looking video.

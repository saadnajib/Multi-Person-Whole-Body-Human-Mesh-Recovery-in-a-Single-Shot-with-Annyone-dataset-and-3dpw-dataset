\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

This chapter answers the research questions of Section~\ref{sec:intro-requirements}, interprets the metrics and
states what remains unresolved.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

\paragraph{Real-image supervision.}
The converted 3DPW labels enable training and testing Anny models on real images. Their joints lie a
median \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm from those of 3DPW, but \DataConstantCount{} phenotypes are constant and head and hands
remain at rest (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). Most of the teacher's real-image gain comes from these labels: the uncorrected teacher reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm PVE on converted 3DPW against \BaseThreeDPW\,mm
for the initial model, the corrected teacher \FinalThreeDPW\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:res-3dpw}). On real photographs the
teacher separates children from adults better than the initial model (AUC \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal) and ranks
women above men (gender AUC \RHGenderAUCFinal), but men receive mid-range gender values, probably because the AnnyOne
labels contain no fully male body (Sections~\ref{sec:res-rh} and~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\paragraph{Compact reconstruction.}
The student has \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters and \FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs than its
teacher and ran \SpeedRatioStudent{} times faster. Its PVE is \StudentPVE{} against \FinalPVE\,mm on the AnnyOne
holdout and \StudentThreeDPW{} against \FinalThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW, missing the pre-set retention criterion on every error metric (Section~\ref{sec:res-student}). Its 3DPW PVE is lower than the released baseline's
(\NAVERThreeDPW\,mm) and its PA-PVE slightly higher (\StudentPAThreeDPW{} against \NAVERPAThreeDPW\,mm); this
compares trained systems, not architectures (Section~\ref{sec:res-fairness}).

The ablation of Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-ablation} trains the same ViT-B model on the same data and schedule without
the distillation losses and without any teacher. Only this comparison can attribute the student's accuracy to
distillation rather than to the initialisation, the copied heads or the real-image data. \TODO{pending: PVE of both
ablation arms against the student, Table~\ref{tab:kd-ablation}.}

Training length and temperature were fixed in advance; the sensitivity runs changed no setting
(Section~\ref{sec:res-selection}). Doubling the training length lowered the 3DPW validation PVE only from
\SelThirtyValPVE{} to \SelSixtyValPVE\,mm and the holdout PVE to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, without improving the 3DPW
test PVE (\StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm; Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}). Across temperatures from 1 to 8 the 3DPW
test errors differed by at most \KDTPRange\,mm, which single runs cannot separate from training variation
(Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature}). Untested causes of the gap are the smaller backbone, the 3DPW share per
batch and the teacher's own 3DPW error.

\paragraph{Multi-person detection.}
On Relative Human the teacher's recall falls with the number of people per image: \CrowdFinalOneRec\,\% and
\CrowdFinalTwoRec\,\% for images with one and two labelled people, \CrowdFinalThreeFourRec\,\% for three or four,
\CrowdFinalFiveEightRec\,\% for five to eight and \CrowdFinalNinePlusRec\,\% for nine or more
(Section~\ref{sec:res-multiperson}). \TODO{pending: student recall by crowd size, Table~\ref{tab:rh-crowd}.}

\paragraph{Video.}
On the walking clip the student kept a tracked body in \VideoStudentMatched{} of \VideoFrames{} frames
and the baseline in \VideoBaselineMatched, tracker matches, not recall (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}).
Temporal filtering visibly steadied the motion and landmark alignment improved position and apparent size; without
ground truth this shows steadier output, not better 3D accuracy. On one \VideoGPU{}, model inference ran at
\VideoStudentRawFPS{} FPS for the student and \VideoNAVERFPS\,FPS for the baseline, and the student with
preprocessing, filtering and alignment at \VideoAlignedPipeFPS\,FPS. These figures exclude offline landmark
extraction, rendering and video input and output and are not end-to-end speeds.

\section{Interpreting geometry and placement}
\label{sec:disc-pve}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

PVE aligns the pelvises before measuring vertex distances and is therefore insensitive to placement: with the two
corrections the root error fell from \num{\ControlRootErr} to \FinalRootErr\,mm, while the PVE changed only from
\ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:res-depth}). Local changes are diluted likewise; the neck-skin PVE
fell from \ControlNeckSkin{} to \FinalNeckSkin\,mm, but the \DiscNeckVerts{} neck vertices account for an estimated
\DiscNeckPVEShare\,mm of whole-body PVE, so the bone correction was judged by bone angles and neck geometry
(Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}). Detection, body accuracy, temporal stability and
speed are separate properties and are therefore reported side by side rather than as one value.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Evaluation data.} The synthetic test set is the last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images, without
  children, which also served as the in-training monitor; it supports no broad generalisation claim. The 3DPW labels
  are converted, and the 3DPW errors are not comparable with published results (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).
\item \textbf{Baseline fairness.} No baseline was trained on the same data, so a lower PVE does not show a better
  architecture (Section~\ref{sec:res-fairness}).
\item \textbf{Single runs.} Each model was trained once without a fixed seed (Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}).
\item \textbf{Shape.} Shape on real photographs was evaluated on the teacher only (student pending); children are a measured
  improvement, not solved, and men remain mid-range (Section~\ref{sec:res-rh}).
\item \textbf{Depth.} Predicted depth grows by only about \FinalSlope\,m per metre of true depth, and a single image leaves
  size and distance ambiguous (Section~\ref{sec:res-depth}).
\item \textbf{Multi-person and video evaluation.} Multi-person evaluation is limited to detection recall, with no
  occlusion or crowd benchmark with 3D labels; the video is one clip of one person, whose motion statistics mix body
  motion, camera motion and tracking errors.
\item \textbf{Timing.} Latency depends on hardware, precision, resolution and the number of people; the forward-pass
  benchmark and the video timings were measured differently. No real-time claim is made for the complete pipeline.
\item \textbf{Reproducibility.} The cluster scripts contain site-specific paths, and the terms of use of datasets,
  body model and checkpoints have not been checked for a release of weights or converted labels.
\end{itemize}

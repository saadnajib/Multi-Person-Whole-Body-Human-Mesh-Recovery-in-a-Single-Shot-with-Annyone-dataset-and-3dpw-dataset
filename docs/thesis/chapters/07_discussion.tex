\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

This chapter reads the results against what the thesis had to deliver (Section~\ref{sec:disc-requirements}),
explains why the headline error barely moved while the faults it was meant to track were corrected
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-pve}), states the limits of each claim (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}) and records what
the work taught about building such a pipeline (Section~\ref{sec:disc-engineering}).

\section{The requirements, one by one}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

The supervisor set three tiers (Section~\ref{sec:intro-requirements}): requirements without which he would not
consider the thesis ready for submission (1a, 1b, 1c), requirements for an acceptable grade (2a, 2b), and a
well-written report with verifiable code for the best grade (3). Table~\ref{tab:requirements} gives the status of
each on \DiscStatusDate{} in one of five words: met, met with caveats, partly, not met, not started. I split 1a into
pose and shape, because the evidence for the two differs. The paragraphs below follow his order; each names what is
missing and where Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} plans it.

\paragraph{1a-pose: correct pose on real images.} He asked for a model whose ``pose of all body parts must look
correct'' on the 3DPW test set and on any random image from the wild. The status is \emph{partly}. On the 3DPW test
set Final has the lowest error on our 3DPW-Anny labels, \FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \FinalPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE, and
finds \FinalRecallThreeDPW\,\% of the labelled actors (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}). Two pose faults that the 3DPW
labels cannot see were found and corrected on the AnnyOne holdout: the half-turned neck bones (neck02
\ControlNeckTwo{} against \FinalNeckTwo\,deg for Control and Final) and two flipped hand bones (fingers
\ControlFingers{} against \FinalFingers\,deg; Sections~\ref{sec:res-bone} and~\ref{sec:res-fingers}). On real
photos the evidence is by eye. In the 12 demo images the necks have normal width; placement offsets, ghost bodies
on half-visible people and hard poses such as the seated guitar player remain (Figures~\ref{fig:3dpw-overlays}
and~\ref{fig:real-photos}). Three things are missing. Predicted fingers have not been shown on a real photo,
because every demo so far sets them to rest pose (Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo} waits for that render). The depth
fix moved the offset and the body size, not the slope (\FinalSlope; Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}). And every
number comes from one run. Section~\ref{sec:future} plans the finger demo, a body-joint MPJPE and a repeat of
Final; the slope stays a limitation (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}).

\paragraph{1a-shape: shape that changes with the person.} He asked that ``mesh size, proportions, age, muscles, and
other aspects of the 3D shape must visibly change according to the person'', as in NAVER's Anny demo, since AnnyOne
carries these labels. The status is \emph{partly}. The training side is met: every model learns all
\DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes from the AnnyOne labels, with the shape term at weight \MethodWShape{}
(Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}). On real photos the answer depends on the phenotype. Gender now changes with the
person: on Relative Human, Final gets it right for \RHGenderFinal\,\% of the people against \RHGenderBase\,\% for
Base (Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}), although men still sit mid-range (\RHMenGenderFinal, where 0 is male). Age changes
partly: children separate from adults (AUC \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal), but half of the babies still get an
adult-shaped body. Muscle, weight, cup size and firmness do not visibly change: their standard deviation on the
demo images is \SpreadMuscleFinal{} to \SpreadCupFinal, because Final learned the constants that the converter
writes for every 3DPW person (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}). The shape mask brought the spread back (up to
\SpreadWeightMasked) but lost the gender gain, so I kept the unmasked model. Missing are a measure of how well each
phenotype follows its label on AnnyOne (no correlation or slope has been computed), the rest-pose line-up that
would show shape without pose (Figure~\ref{fig:rest-lineup}), and the partial mask, Final-masked-7, which frees the
seven constants at \MethodConstOther{} and keeps age supervised \TODO{Final-masked-7 result: update 1a-shape and
T1 when it is in}. Whether a predicted muscle or weight value is right for a real person cannot be measured with
the data I have; Section~\ref{sec:future} plans the partial mask and the line-up.

\paragraph{1b: a model that is not exactly Multi-HMR.} His wording: ``The model must not be exactly the same as the
Multi-HMR (or any other existing) model.'' Two routes count: a new architecture that beats the baseline in PVE and
MPJPE on both the AnnyOne test and the 3DPW test, or a ``significantly smaller'' model, in parameters, FLOPs and
inference time, whose errors stay within 95\,\% of the larger model's on both sets. The status is \emph{not met}.
Final is Multi-HMR with its output resized to Anny's \MethodNumBones{} bone rotations and \MethodNumPheno{}
phenotypes and a correction that adds no parameters (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}
measures it against NAVER's own Anny model (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne
release, which includes the 100 images we hold out, so its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm is most likely an in-sample number;
ours are held out. On 3DPW, which neither model saw before our fine-tune, the final model is
\FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better (\FinalThreeDPW{} vs \NAVERThreeDPW). Final thus beats the baseline on one
set and trails it by \DiscNAVERAnnyOneGap\,mm on the other, so the first route would hinge on a comparison that is
probably not fair. In size, Final has \FinalParams\,M parameters against \NAVERParams\,M, \DiscFinalSmallerPct\,\%
fewer, all of the difference in the head; that is not the ``significantly smaller'' model he asked for. The second
route is the Student (Section~\ref{sec:res-student}). Its cost side is measured: \ParamRatioStudent$\times$ fewer
parameters, \FlopRatioStudent$\times$ fewer FLOPs and \SpeedRatioStudent$\times$ faster than Final, and
\ParamRatioStudentNAVER$\times$ fewer parameters than NAVER's model (Figure~\ref{fig:cost-pve}). Its accuracy is
pending (Table~\ref{tab:student}) \TODO{Student result: update 1b, T1 and the status word when it is in}. With Final
as the larger model the bars are \BarPVE\,mm PVE on the holdout and \BarThreeDPW\,mm on 3DPW; with NAVER's model
they would be \BarNAVERAnnyOne\,mm, which no student of a \FinalPVE\,mm teacher can be expected to reach, and
\BarNAVERThreeDPW\,mm. Missing are the Student's accuracy, his decision on which model sets the bar, and his view on
whether a Multi-HMR-style network with a smaller backbone counts as ``not exactly'' Multi-HMR.
Section~\ref{sec:future} holds the longer student run for the case that the first one misses the bar.

\paragraph{1c: evaluation in Anny space on real images.} He asked for evaluation on a real-world set with Anny
parameters, not only SMPL-X, at least for the 3DPW test set. The status is \emph{met with caveats}. All
\num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images (\DataThreeDPWTestPeople{} people) have Anny labels from the converter of
Chapter~\ref{ch:data}, and every model in Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw} is scored against them with the same script.
The independent label check grades the corrected labels WARN where the old ones failed, with body joints
\DataCheckJointsNew\,mm from 3DPW's own at the median (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}). The caveats are those of
Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}: \DataConstantCount{} of the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes are constants, so 3DPW tests
pose, gender, height and proportions but not body build; the head beyond neck01 and the hands are at rest; every
error contains a floor of about \DataFloor\,mm; and MPJPE averages all \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints, so no number
compares with published 3DPW results. Nothing further is required. A body-joint MPJPE next to the
\MethodNumBones-joint one would make the 3DPW numbers easier to read (Section~\ref{sec:future}).

\paragraph{2a: 3DPW in Anny, and trained on.} He asked to convert the 3DPW train and test sets to Anny and to include
the train set in training, and he expected tier~1 to be out of reach without it. The status is \emph{met}. The
corrected training labels hold \DataThreeDPWTrainImages{} images and \DataThreeDPWTrainPeople{} people; Final and
Control draw \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% of each batch from them (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). His expectation held on
the 3DPW test set: Control, which differs from Base by the real images and the fine-tune itself, is
\GainBaseToControlThreeDPW\,mm better than Base (\ControlThreeDPW{} against \BaseThreeDPW\,mm), while the two fixes
add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm on top (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}). Getting there meant finding and removing three
defects in the first labels (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). Nothing is missing; a larger real-image share would be
an optional ablation.

\paragraph{2b: motion in video.} He asked that the model understand motion sequences instead of predicting each
frame on its own, as Multi-HMR does. The status is \emph{not started}. No temporal code exists; every model here,
Final included, runs on single images, and AnnyOne contains single images only. Section~\ref{sec:bg-temporal} reviews
the temporal methods. Section~\ref{sec:future} plans a small refiner over windows of per-frame person tokens from
the frozen Final model, trained on 3DPW-train tracks and judged against simple smoothing by acceleration error and
PVE. Whether a refiner on frozen features counts as the temporal model he means is a question for him.

\paragraph{3: report and code.} For the best grade he asked for a well-written report in which a reader sees that I
understand the topic and that each choice is argued, and for a clean codebase with trained models he can verify
independently. The status is \emph{partly}. The report is this thesis: each choice comes with its reason and its
control, Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction} maps every number to its run, log and checkpoint, and
Appendix~\ref{app:flags} lists every setting of Final and the Student. The code side is not done. No checkpoint is
released, the scripts still carry the cluster's paths, and the licences of the data and of NAVER's checkpoint have
not been checked for a release (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}). Section~\ref{sec:future} lists the release
steps.

\section{Why PVE barely moved while everything else did}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone} has an awkward pattern. From Control to Final the root error fell by
\RootErrDropPct\,\%, neck02 from \ControlNeckTwo{} to \FinalNeckTwo\,deg, the neck girth from \ControlGirth{} to
\FinalGirth{} of the truth and the finger error from \ControlFingers{} to \FinalFingers\,deg. PVE fell only from
\ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm, within run-to-run variation, and Base still has the lowest PVE of all
(\BasePVE\,mm). Four facts explain most of this, and one error measure did move.

\paragraph{PVE cannot see placement.} PVE moves both meshes so that their pelvises coincide before it measures the
vertex distances. Root error, depth line and drawn size are removed by construction, so the largest of the fixes is
invisible to it. On Base, which placed every person too far, the correlation between a person's PVE and their depth
error was \DiagCorrPVEDepth{} (Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement}). The 3DPW test aligns the pelvis in the same way and
is equally blind to depth.

\paragraph{The bone flips cancelled.} The two half-turns of neck02 and the head bone cancel, so Base's head pointed
the right way (\BaseHeadDir\,deg) and the head vertices sat almost where they should; only the neck skin between
them was twisted. That skin is \DiscNeckVerts{} of the \num{\MethodNumVerts} vertices (\DiscNeckVertShare\,\%). Its
own error fell from \ControlNeckSkin{} to \FinalNeckSkin\,mm, which spread over the whole mesh is about
\DiscNeckPVEShare\,mm; this is an estimate from the two numbers, not a measurement. The correction applied to Base
without training shows the same at a fixed checkpoint, where no run-to-run variation enters: it halved the finger
error (\BaseFingers{} to \NoTrainFingers\,deg) and restored the neck girth, while PVE rose by
\DiscNoTrainPVEChange\,mm, a change that also contains the worse head direction of that setting
(Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}). The fingers are a small part of the mesh too; their share of the vertices was not
counted.

\paragraph{The noise floor.} One run resolves about \MethodResolvePVE\,mm PVE, since two identical runs differed by
\NoisePVE\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}). A gap of \GapPVE\,mm is within that variation in either direction, and
I claim neither a gain nor a cost from it.

\paragraph{The restart cost.} Base's lower PVE is no evidence against the fixes. Restarting a fine-tune of Base with
nothing changed already cost about \RestartCost\,mm, and Control, which also mixes \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% real images
into a model trained on AnnyOne only, sits \DiscBaseControlGapPVE\,mm above Base on these synthetic images. The
likely reason is that every fine-tune moves the model away from the synthetic domain of the holdout; this was not
tested. Every fix is therefore judged against its own control, never against Base.

\paragraph{What did move.} PA-PVE, which also removes global rotation and scale, fell from \ControlPAPVE{} to
\FinalPAPVE\,mm, \GapPAPVE\,mm below Control and \DiscPAPVEBelowBase\,mm below Base, the lowest of every model
(Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}). The pilots point to the bone correction as part of this: applied without training
it lowered PA-PVE from \BasePAPVE{} to \DiscNoTrainPAPVE\,mm, and the neck pilot reached \DiscNeckPilotPAPVE{}
against \DiscNeckPilotCtrlPAPVE\,mm for its control (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}). The likely reason PA-PVE sees what
PVE does not is that the error in global rotation, large and different from run to run, dominates PVE and hides the
articulated part, where the neck and hand flips sit; a per-region breakdown that would test this was not made. On
3DPW, PVE did move, from \BaseThreeDPW\,mm for Base to \FinalThreeDPW{} for Final, but most of that is the real
images (Control \ControlThreeDPW); the fixes add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \GainFixesPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE, and
Final's 3DPW validation curve lies below Control's at all ten epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:val-curves}).

I draw one consequence from this. Each fix is reported with the measure that can see it: root error and drawn size
for depth, bone angles and neck girth for the correction, finger angles for the hands. PVE stays in every table as
the check that a fix costs nothing a single run can resolve. A headline PVE alone would have hidden all three fixes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

Each limitation below bounds a claim made earlier; its evidence is given with it.

\paragraph{Children: a measured improvement, not solved.} Final separates children from adults on Relative Human
better than Base (AUC \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal; mean age of babies \RHAgeBabyFinal{} against adults
\RHAgeAdultFinal), but \RHBabiesBelowFinal\,\% of the babies and \RHKidsBelowFinal\,\% of the kids fall below the
child line, so most children still get an adult-shaped body, and the one boy of the demo photos still gets an adult
body (Section~\ref{sec:res-rh}). The cause lies in the shape head. On real photos Base's head returned the training
median: the medians of gender, age and height over the demo photos equalled the training medians
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-children}), and on synthetic images it regressed toward the median in both tails. Children
are rare in AnnyOne: \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\,\% of the people have an age below 0.3, and the holdout has none, so
no held-out synthetic number for children exists. The 3DPW labels pin age at \DataFixedAge{} for every real person,
and part of Final's lower ages is likely that common shift, although a shift of all ages alone cannot raise the AUC.
Which mechanism dominates, the gap between synthetic and real images or an L1 loss through a sigmoid on a rare
value, the evidence cannot rank. I quote no ages in years, because the code uses three age scales that disagree
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}); child-shaped means a predicted age below \MethodChildThr.

\paragraph{Depth: the slope did not move.} The fix moved the offset and the body size, not the slope
(\FinalSlope). Final's fitted line crosses the identity at \FinalIdentityCross\,m, so people about three metres
away are placed about right, while \FinalTooFarPct\,\% of all people, the near ones most, are still placed too far
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}). Predictions stay compressed toward 2.5--2.9\,m, while the holdout people stand at
\DiagDepthTrueMean\,m on average. The cause is unknown. Measuring the depth distribution of the training batches
and the slope on training images would be the next test (Section~\ref{sec:future}). All depth numbers are
synthetic: the 3DPW evaluation aligns the pelvis and the real-photo demos place each body from its box, so the fix
has not been measured on a real image.

\paragraph{One run per arm, no seed.} The training code sets no random seed, and every model was trained once. Two
identical runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE, \NoiseHeadDeg\,deg head direction and about \NoiseRecall{} recall
points (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-noise}). The claims of this thesis rest on gaps far above that: the root error
($-$\RootErrDropPct\,\%), the neck bones (\ControlNeckTwo{} to \FinalNeckTwo\,deg), the girth, the fingers and
PA-PVE ($-$\GapPAPVE\,mm). The PVE gap (\GapPVE\,mm), the 3DPW gain of the fixes (\GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm) and the
recall cost lie within or at the edge of that variation. There are no bootstrap intervals, and the root error has
no measured noise floor. The holdout holds \DataHoldoutImages{} images with \DiagHoldoutPeople{} people and no
children, one slice of the dataset. A repeat of Final (Section~\ref{sec:future}) would turn ``within run-to-run
variation'' into a measured spread.

\paragraph{Fingers on real photos.} Predicted fingers have not been shown on a real photo. The finger number
(\FinalFingers\,deg) is an angle on the synthetic holdout. Every real-photo demo so far sets the fingers to rest pose,
a choice made when Base's fingers curled into claws, and the 3DPW labels carry rest-pose hands, so no real-image
number exists either. A demo with predicted fingers has been run; its render (Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}) is
pending.

\paragraph{The recall cost.} Final finds \FinalRecall\,\% of the holdout people, \GapRecallControl{} points fewer
than Control, at the edge of run-to-run variation, and \GapRecallBase{} points fewer than Base, beyond it. Every
depth-fix pilot lost \DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to \DiagFixCostRecallHigh{} points against its control, always in the
same direction (Section~\ref{sec:diag-depth}). I therefore state it as a real cost. The likely reason is that the
fix moved the detection cell from the pelvis to the head, so people whose head is cut off or hidden are found less
often; this was not tested, because recall on people with a visible head needs code that does not exist yet.

\paragraph{What the 3DPW labels can teach and test.} The 3DPW labels carry a neutral body: \DataConstantCount{} of
the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes are constants (age \DataFixedAge{} and \DataNeutralCount{} values at
\DataFixedNeutral), and neck02, neck03, the head and all hand bones stay at rest (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).
They therefore cannot supervise body build or fingers, and they cannot test the neck correction. Training on them
pulls body build toward the constants, which is the collapse of Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}. The hand mask removes
the rotation terms only; the mesh terms still carry the rest-pose hands. As a test set the labels put a floor of
about \DataFloor\,mm under every number and likely reward a neutral prediction, which was not tested. Precision on
3DPW (\FinalPrecThreeDPW\,\% for Final) is a lower bound: detected bystanders count as false positives because only
the actors are labelled, and ghost bodies on half-visible people count too; the split between the two is unknown.

\paragraph{The comparison with NAVER's model.} Its AnnyOne number is most likely in-sample
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-requirements}); whether our 100 holdout images were in its training set is not known for
certain, and if they were, the comparison favours it by an unknown amount. On 3DPW the comparison is fairer but not
like for like: Final was fine-tuned on 3DPW-train and NAVER's model was not, and Control, without our fixes, is
already at \ControlThreeDPW\,mm. The 3DPW gap therefore shows what the real-image fine-tune buys, not a better
network. NAVER's model fine-tuned on our labels would separate the two and was not run; its latency and head FLOPs
could not be measured with our tool.

\paragraph{The validation split shares actors with training.} 3DPW's validation sequences share actors and
locations with the training sequences, while the test split shows other people in other scenes. The draft found, on
the old labels, that the gain from real images on validation was about twice the gain on test. I therefore report
only test numbers. The validation split serves for monitoring and selects nothing, since Final is the last epoch of
its run (Table~\ref{tab:data-splits}); its curves (Figure~\ref{fig:val-curves}) are read only as Final against Control
on the same images.

\paragraph{Licences.} SMPL, and with it 3DPW's ground truth and the converter's input, is licensed for
non-commercial research (Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}), and 3DPW has its own licence. Relative Human ships without
a licence file, so I used only its validation split and only for evaluation. AnnyOne and NAVER's checkpoints, whose
backbones every model here starts from, come with their own terms, separate from Anny's Apache~2.0 code. I have not
yet checked whether those terms allow releasing weights derived from them; Section~\ref{sec:future} puts that check
before any release.

\section{Engineering lessons}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

The faults that cost the most time were silent: each produced plausible output, and each showed only when one stage
was measured on its own, against a control, or by its geometry. Four habits came out of this.

\paragraph{Measure each stage alone.} The six checks of Section~\ref{sec:diag-checks} decoded the labels without a
network, moved single joints of the body model and traced the gradients, and so excluded the rig, the labels and the
gradient path before any training change. The converter graded its own fit; only an independent check that read the
finished label files found the three label defects (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). The holdout trace measured
placement with each body at its own predicted depth, which neither PVE nor the box-placed demos ever did. The draft's
list of pipeline bugs shows the same pattern: a demo that silently loaded no weights and drew every person in the
mean pose, translations in centimetres on one code path and in metres on another, one field of view for images whose
true field of view spans \DataAnnyOneFovMin{} to \DataAnnyOneFovMax\,deg, and a learning-rate schedule that froze a
long run while its loss curve looked converged. Three more joined it in this work. The label builder skipped frames
without an image file and lost \DataOldTrainMissingPct\,\% of the 3DPW training labels without a warning. The
training-time evaluation left the helper bones unmasked while the demo masked them, \MethodVzeroGap\,mm apart on the
same people. And the adapter for NAVER's checkpoint hard-coded a decoder width of \DiscFFNOurs{} where the
checkpoint has \DiscFFNNaver; it failed loudly on the first load, which made it the cheapest fault of all.

\paragraph{Read every change against a control.} Every fix was read against a run that differed only by that fix:
Final against Control, each pilot against a same-schedule control, and two identical runs to know how much a run
varies. Without the controls, three wrong readings would have stood: the neck pilot's \DiscNeckPilotPVE\,mm as a gain
over Base, Final's higher PVE as a cost of the fixes, and the 3DPW gain as a gain of the fixes. The restart cost was
itself found only because the controls of all pilots sat above Base.

\paragraph{Check by geometry, not by names.} Three of the worst faults were faults of naming or convention, and only
a geometric check exposed them. The converter first chose Anny bones by name; the two rigs number the spine in
opposite directions, so SMPL's lumbar spine joint was mapped to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it, and the fit
still looked plausible until the two rest skeletons were compared side by side (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}).
The gender value followed MakeHuman's convention, where 1 is male, while Anny's 1 is female, so every 3DPW man was
fitted and labelled with a female body; the fit error gave it away, falling for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of
\DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men when the value was flipped (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). And the training targets were
built at joint~0, the pelvis, while the model places each body by its head; nothing in the code's names showed the
difference, and only the drawn size and the depth line of the trace revealed two joints half a body apart
(Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The half-turned neck bones belong to the same family: per-group means of
\DiscGroupMeanNeckA{} and \DiscGroupMeanNeckB\,deg hid them, and a per-bone dump of rotation angles found them.

\paragraph{Keep every change as a switch.} Each change entered the code as a switch whose default reproduces the old
behaviour: the anchor joint of the targets, the bone correction (saved with the checkpoint and reapplied on loading),
the hand and shape masks for 3DPW people, and the two switches of converter version 2.3. This kept the history models
reproducible, made every control a change of one setting, and let the masked arm run next to Final from the same
code (Appendix~\ref{app:flags}).

In this pipeline silent failure was far more common than loud failure. A check that fails loudly, such as a load
percentage or a geometric comparison of two skeletons, costs minutes to write. The silent faults stayed hidden until
a measurement aimed at them: the depth bug sat in every checkpoint trained before this thesis.

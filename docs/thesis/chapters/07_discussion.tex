\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

The evaluation concerns three outcomes: real-image Anny supervision, compact reconstruction and frame-wise video
application. Their interpretation depends on the data, model roles and measurement conventions.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

\paragraph{Real-image supervision.}
Converted 3DPW labels make it possible to train and compare Anny-based models on real images. The independent
check gives a median body-joint discrepancy of \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. Conversion constrains articulated pose
and some shape properties, but does not create evidence absent from the source data: \DataConstantCount{}
phenotypes are fixed, and many hand/head rotations remain rest-pose placeholders. The result is a practical
benchmark with explicit annotation limits.

Control reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm PVE on converted 3DPW, compared with \BaseThreeDPW\,mm for the synthetic-only
Base. Final reaches \FinalThreeDPW\,mm with the anchor and bone corrections. The large synthetic-to-real gain
is therefore associated primarily with mixed training. It cannot be attributed entirely to the corrections
or a network architecture change.

\paragraph{Compact reconstruction.}
The student reduces parameters by \ParamRatioStudent{} times and FLOPs by \FlopRatioStudent{} times relative
to its teacher. The paired L40S benchmark gives a \SpeedRatioStudent{}-fold latency improvement. PVE also rises
from \FinalPVE{} to \StudentPVE\,mm on AnnyOne and from \FinalThreeDPW{} to \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted
3DPW. The result demonstrates an efficiency--accuracy trade-off rather than teacher-equivalent reconstruction.

Against the released baseline the student is \ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times smaller and has lower 3DPW PVE
(\StudentThreeDPW{} versus \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm). However, the student uses converted 3DPW training images and
the released baseline has not received that adaptation. A baseline fine-tuned on the same data is needed to
isolate architecture and distillation effects from training exposure.

\paragraph{Video application.}
The completed walking run processes the same frames with both models and NVIDIA mesh rendering. The student
has \VideoStudentMatched{} matched predictions in \VideoFrames{} frames; the baseline has \VideoBaselineMatched{}.
These are tracking-coverage counts, not ground-truth detection recall. They establish execution on one clip,
but do not establish pose accuracy or temporal smoothness. The application saves comparison videos and frame-wise
pose/shape changes for inspection. The revised timing procedure separates synchronized inference latency from
rendering and playback. The evaluation does not yet include numerical FPS and stability results from the revised video application.

\section{Interpreting Geometry and Placement}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

PVE aligns the predicted and reference pelvises before measuring vertex differences. Whole-body translation
is removed, so low PVE can coexist with incorrect camera-space placement. The teacher ablations illustrate this:
root error changes from \num{\ControlRootErr} to \FinalRootErr\,mm while PVE changes from \ControlPVE{} to
\FinalPVE\,mm. Placement and depth diagnostics are therefore reported alongside aligned reconstruction errors.

A small anatomical region can also contribute little to a whole-body average. The neck contains redundant
rotations, and per-bone errors can differ from global head-direction errors. The output correction is assessed
through bone angles and neck geometry as well as PVE. Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics} retains the supporting tests.

Two identical short pilot configurations differ by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE. This demonstrates sensitivity to training
variation, but two pilot runs do not establish a universal significance threshold for other schedules or datasets.
Small differences between single runs are interpreted cautiously. Repeated training and uncertainty estimates
are needed for statistical conclusions.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\paragraph{Shape and children.}
The teacher's Relative Human evaluation improves age-group separation and gender ranking, but children can
still receive adult-like bodies. The synthetic holdout has no children, and converted real-image labels contain
constant age and body-build values. The shape-mask variants do not isolate a general solution: parameter spread
and gender behaviour change together. These findings concern evaluated teacher variants; equivalent student
shape benchmarks are required before assigning the same conclusions to the student.

\paragraph{Depth and detection.}
The teacher's predicted-versus-reference depth slope remains \FinalSlope, with substantial residual placement
error. Consistent anchors improve supervision without solving monocular depth ambiguity. Occlusion, truncation
and multiple-person interactions also complicate detection and tracking. A clear single-person video offers
limited evidence for these conditions.

\paragraph{Evaluation conventions.}
3DPW errors use fitted Anny labels, and MPJPE averages \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints rather than a published
SMPL-space joint set. The median conversion discrepancy is a label-quality indicator, not a constant that can
be subtracted from network error. Unlabelled bystanders complicate precision estimates. The synthetic holdout
was excluded from this thesis's training but may have been seen by the released baseline.

\paragraph{Experimental coverage and efficiency.}
Each model is represented by one training run. The existing experiments do not separately quantify every
student loss component. The walking example has no ground-truth mesh: natural movement, camera motion and
tracking errors all affect movement statistics. Latency depends on hardware, precision, input resolution and
person count. The historical paired benchmark and the new per-video benchmark are different protocols and
must be reported separately.

\paragraph{Reproduction and release.}
The repository records model settings and numerical provenance, but cluster scripts retain site-specific paths.
Redistributing weights or converted annotations requires checking the terms of the datasets, body-model assets
and source checkpoints. This report does not establish permission to release every derived artifact.

\section{Practical Implications}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

Whether a person receives a prediction, whether the reconstructed body is accurate, and whether it remains
stable over time are separate questions. Tracking counts address the first, labelled-image metrics the second,
and video inspection with frame-wise measurements the third. Inference FPS adds computational cost as another
dimension. Reporting these quantities together provides a clearer assessment of the compact model than a
single reconstruction error or visually pleasing example.

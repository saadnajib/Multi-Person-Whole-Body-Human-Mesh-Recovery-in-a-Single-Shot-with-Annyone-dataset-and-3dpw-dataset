\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

This chapter reads the results against the requirements, explains why the headline error barely moved, states the
limits of each claim and records what the work taught about building such a pipeline.

\section{The requirements, one by one}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

The supervisor set three tiers (Section~\ref{sec:intro-requirements}): requirements without which he would not
consider the thesis ready for submission (1a, 1b, 1c), requirements for an acceptable grade (2a, 2b), and a
well-written report with verifiable code for the best grade (3). Table~\ref{tab:requirements} gives the status of
each on \DiscStatusDate{} in one of five words: met, met with caveats, partly, not met, not started. I split 1a into
pose and shape, because their evidence differs. The paragraphs follow his order; each names what is missing and
where Chapter~\ref{ch:conclusion} plans it.

\paragraph{1a-pose.} He asked for a model whose ``pose of all body parts must look correct'' on the 3DPW test set
and on any random image from the wild. The status is \emph{partly}. On the 3DPW test set Final has the lowest error
on our 3DPW-Anny labels, \FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \FinalPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE, and finds
\FinalRecallThreeDPW\,\% of the labelled actors (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}). Two pose faults that the 3DPW labels
cannot see were corrected on the AnnyOne holdout: the half-turned neck bones (neck02 \ControlNeckTwo{} against
\FinalNeckTwo\,deg for Control and Final) and two flipped hand bones (fingers \ControlFingers{} against
\FinalFingers\,deg; Sections~\ref{sec:res-bone} and~\ref{sec:res-fingers}). On real photos the evidence is by eye:
the necks have normal width, while placement offsets, ghost bodies and hard poses such as the seated guitar player
remain (Figures~\ref{fig:3dpw-overlays} and~\ref{fig:real-photos}). Predicted fingers on real images are shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}: by eye, Final's fingers close and bend like hands where Base's are splayed and stiff,
on the three images whose hands are large enough to judge. Missing are correct near and far depth (the slope is
\FinalSlope; Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}) and a second run. Section~\ref{sec:future} plans a repeat of Final;
the slope stays an open limitation.

\paragraph{1a-shape.} He asked that ``mesh size, proportions, age, muscles, and other aspects of the 3D shape must
visibly change according to the person'', since AnnyOne carries these labels. The status is \emph{partly}. Training
on the labels is in place: every model learns all \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes from AnnyOne, the shape term at weight
\MethodWShape{} (Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}). On real photos it depends on the phenotype. Gender changes with the
person: on Relative Human Final gets it right for \RHGenderFinal\,\% of the people against \RHGenderBase\,\% for
Base, although men still sit mid-range (\RHMenGenderFinal, where 0 is male; Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}). Age changes
partly: children separate from adults (AUC \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal), but half of the babies keep an
adult-shaped body. Muscle, weight, cup size and firmness do not visibly change; their standard deviation on the demo
images is \SpreadMuscleFinal--\SpreadCupFinal, because Final learned the constants the converter writes for every
3DPW person. The shape mask brought the spread back but lost the gender gain, so I kept the unmasked model
(Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}). The rest-pose line-up (Figure~\ref{fig:rest-lineup}) agrees by eye: Final gives the
women a fuller female shape and one man a broader torso where Base draws everyone alike, but the men of the tennis
photo stay slim with a slight chest in every model. Missing are a measure of how well each phenotype follows its
label on AnnyOne and the partial mask, Final-partial-mask, which frees the seven
constants at \MethodConstOther{} and keeps age supervised \TODO{Final-partial-mask result: update 1a-shape and T1}.
Whether a predicted muscle or weight value is right for a real person cannot be measured with the data I have.
Section~\ref{sec:future} plans the partial mask and the per-phenotype measure.

\paragraph{1b.} His wording: ``The model must not be exactly the same as the Multi-HMR (or any other existing)
model.'' A new architecture must beat the baseline on both the AnnyOne and the 3DPW test; a ``significantly
smaller'' model must stay within 95\,\% of the larger model's accuracy on both. The status is \emph{not met}. Final is Multi-HMR with its output resized to Anny and a correction that adds no parameters
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Against NAVER's own Anny model (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}): NAVER's model was trained on
the AnnyOne release, which includes the 100 images we hold out, so its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm is most likely an in-sample
number; ours are held out. On 3DPW, which neither model saw before our fine-tune, the final model is
\FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better (\FinalThreeDPW{} vs \NAVERThreeDPW). Final thus trails the baseline by
\DiscNAVERAnnyOneGap\,mm on a comparison that is probably not fair and beats it on the other. In size Final
has \FinalParams\,M parameters against \NAVERParams\,M, \DiscFinalSmallerPct\,\% fewer, all in the head; that is
not ``significantly smaller''. The second route is the Student (Section~\ref{sec:res-student}). Its cost side is
measured, \ParamRatioStudent$\times$ fewer parameters, \FlopRatioStudent$\times$ fewer FLOPs and
\SpeedRatioStudent$\times$ faster than Final (Figure~\ref{fig:cost-pve}); its accuracy is pending
(Table~\ref{tab:student}) \TODO{Student result: update 1b, T1 and the status word}. Missing are that accuracy, his
decision on which model sets the bar (with NAVER's model the AnnyOne bar is \BarNAVERAnnyOne\,mm, which no student
of a \FinalPVE\,mm teacher can be expected to reach), and his view on whether a Multi-HMR-style network with a
smaller backbone counts. If it misses while its error still falls, Section~\ref{sec:future} plans to continue its training.

\paragraph{1c.} He asked for evaluation on a real-world set with Anny parameters, not only SMPL-X, at least for the
3DPW test set. The status is \emph{met with caveats}. All \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images
(\DataThreeDPWTestPeople{} people) have Anny labels from the converter of Chapter~\ref{ch:data}, and every model is
scored against them with the same script (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}). The independent check grades these labels
WARN where the old ones failed, with body joints \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm from 3DPW's own at the median
(Table~\ref{tab:label-check}). The caveats are those of Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}: the labels test pose, gender,
height and proportions but not body build, and no number compares with published 3DPW results. Nothing further is
required; a body-joint MPJPE next to the \MethodNumBones-joint one would make the numbers easier to read, but it is not
yet written.

\paragraph{2a.} He asked to convert the 3DPW train and test sets to Anny and to train on the train set, and expected
tier~1 to be out of reach without it. The status is \emph{met}. The corrected training labels hold
\DataThreeDPWTrainImages{} images and \DataThreeDPWTrainPeople{} people, and Final and Control draw
\DataMixThreeDPW\,\% of each batch from them (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). His expectation held: on the 3DPW test
set Control, which differs from Base by the real images and the fine-tune, is \GainBaseToControlThreeDPW\,mm better
than Base, and the two fixes add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm on top (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}). Nothing is missing.

\paragraph{2b.} He asked that the model understand motion in video instead of predicting each frame on its own. The
status is \emph{not started}: no temporal code exists, and every model here runs on single images.
Section~\ref{sec:future} plans a small refiner over windows of per-frame person tokens from the frozen Final model,
trained on 3DPW-train tracks and judged against simple smoothing. Whether a refiner on frozen features counts as the
temporal model he means is a question for him.

\paragraph{3.} He asked for a well-written report that shows understanding and argued choices, and for a clean
codebase with trained models he can verify independently. The status is \emph{partly}. The report is this thesis;
Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction} maps every number to its run, log and checkpoint, and Appendix~\ref{app:flags} lists
every setting. The code side is not done: no checkpoint is released, the scripts still carry the cluster's paths, and
the licences have not been checked for a release (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}). Section~\ref{sec:future} lists
the release steps.

\section{Why PVE barely moved while everything else did}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

From Control to Final the root error fell by \RootErrDropPct\,\%, neck02 from \ControlNeckTwo{} to
\FinalNeckTwo\,deg, the neck girth from \ControlGirth{} to \FinalGirth{} of the truth and the finger error from
\ControlFingers{} to \FinalFingers\,deg. PVE fell only from \ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm, within run-to-run
variation, and Base keeps the lowest PVE of all (\BasePVE\,mm; Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}). Three facts explain
most of this, and one error measure did move.

\paragraph{PVE cannot see placement.} PVE moves both meshes so that their pelvises coincide before it measures the
vertex distances, so root error, depth and drawn size are removed by construction, and the largest of the fixes is
invisible to it. On Base, which placed every person too far, the correlation between a person's PVE and their depth
error was \DiagCorrPVEDepth{} (Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement}). The 3DPW test aligns the pelvis in the same way.

\paragraph{The bone flips cancelled.} The two half-turns of neck02 and the head bone cancel, so Base's head pointed
the right way (\BaseHeadDir\,deg) and the head vertices sat almost where they should; only the neck skin between them
was twisted. That skin is \DiscNeckVerts{} of the \num{\MethodNumVerts} vertices (\DiscNeckVertShare\,\%). Its own
error fell from \ControlNeckSkin{} to \FinalNeckSkin\,mm, which spread over the whole mesh is about
\DiscNeckPVEShare\,mm, an estimate from the two numbers, not a measurement. The correction applied to Base without
training shows the same at a fixed checkpoint, free of run-to-run variation: it halved the finger error
(\BaseFingers{} to \NoTrainFingers\,deg) and restored the neck girth, while PVE rose by \DiscNoTrainPVEChange\,mm, a
change that also contains the worse head direction of that setting (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}).

\paragraph{Noise floor and restart cost.} One run resolves about \MethodResolvePVE\,mm PVE, since two identical runs
differed by \NoisePVE\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}), so the gap of \GapPVE\,mm supports neither a gain nor a
cost. Base's lower PVE is no evidence against the fixes either: restarting a fine-tune of Base with nothing changed
already cost about \RestartCost\,mm, and Control, which adds \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% real images to a model trained on
AnnyOne only, sits \DiscBaseControlGapPVE\,mm above Base on these synthetic images. The likely reason is that every
fine-tune moves the model away from the synthetic domain of the holdout; this was not tested. Every fix is therefore
judged against its own control.

\paragraph{What did move.} PA-PVE, which also removes global rotation and scale, fell from \ControlPAPVE{} to
\FinalPAPVE\,mm, \GapPAPVE\,mm below Control and \DiscPAPVEBelowBase\,mm below Base, the lowest of every model. The
pilots tie part of this to the bone correction: applied without training it lowered PA-PVE from \BasePAPVE{} to
\DiscNoTrainPAPVE\,mm, and the neck pilot reached \DiscNeckPilotPAPVE{} against \DiscNeckPilotCtrlPAPVE\,mm for its
control (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}). The likely reason PA-PVE sees what PVE does not is that the error in global
rotation, large and different from run to run, dominates PVE and hides the articulated part where the flips sit; a
per-region breakdown that would test this was not made. On 3DPW the fixes add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm PVE
and \GainFixesPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE to what the real images gave, and Final's validation curve lies below Control's at
all ten epochs (Figure~\ref{fig:val-curves}).

I draw one consequence. Each fix is reported with the measure that can see it: root error and drawn size for depth,
bone angles and neck girth for the correction, finger angles for the hands. PVE stays in every table as the check
that a fix costs nothing a single run can resolve. A headline PVE alone would have hidden all three fixes.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\paragraph{Children: a measured improvement, not solved.} Final separates children from adults on Relative Human
better than Base (AUC \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal; mean age of babies \RHAgeBabyFinal{} against adults
\RHAgeAdultFinal), but only \RHBabiesBelowFinal\,\% of the babies and \RHKidsBelowFinal\,\% of the kids fall below
the child line, and the boy of the demo photos still gets an adult body (Section~\ref{sec:res-rh}). On real photos
Base's shape head returned the training median, and on synthetic images it regressed toward the median in both
tails (Section~\ref{sec:diag-children}). Children are rare in
AnnyOne, \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\,\% of the people having an age below 0.3, and the holdout has none, so no
held-out synthetic number for children exists. The 3DPW labels pin age at \DataFixedAge{} for every real person;
part of Final's lower ages is likely that common shift, although a common shift alone cannot raise the AUC. Whether
the gap between synthetic and real images or an L1 loss through a sigmoid on a rare value dominates, the evidence
cannot rank. I quote no ages in years, because the code uses three age scales that disagree
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}).

\paragraph{Depth: the slope did not move.} The fix moved the offset and the body size, not the slope (\FinalSlope).
Final's fitted line crosses the identity at \FinalIdentityCross\,m, so people about three metres away are placed
about right, while \FinalTooFarPct\,\% of all people, the near ones most, are still placed too far
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}). Predictions stay compressed toward 2.5--2.9\,m, while the holdout people stand at
\DiagDepthTrueMean\,m on average. The cause is unknown, and Section~\ref{sec:future} leaves it open; measuring the depth
distribution of the training batches would be the next test. All depth numbers are synthetic: the 3DPW evaluation aligns the pelvis and
the demos place each body from its box, so the fix has not been measured on a real image.

\paragraph{One run per arm, no seed.} The training code sets no random seed, and every model was trained once. Two
identical runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE, \NoiseHeadDeg\,deg head direction and about \NoiseRecall{} recall
points (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-noise}). The claims of this thesis rest on gaps far above that (root error, bone angles,
girth, fingers, PA-PVE); the PVE gap, the 3DPW gain of the fixes and the recall cost lie within or at the edge of it.
There are no bootstrap intervals, the root error has no measured noise floor, and the holdout holds
\DataHoldoutImages{} images with \DiagHoldoutPeople{} people and no children. A repeat of Final
(Section~\ref{sec:future}) would turn ``within run-to-run variation'' into a measured spread.

\paragraph{Fingers on real photos.} Predicted fingers on real images are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}
and judged by eye only: Final's fingers look like hands where Base's are splayed, on three images whose hands are
large enough to see; on the smaller hands of the other demo images nothing can be judged. The finger number
(\FinalFingers\,deg) is an angle on the synthetic holdout, and the 3DPW labels carry rest-pose hands, so no
real-image number exists. Base's renders carry both flipped hand bones, so the figure shows the correction and the
fine-tune together.

\paragraph{The recall cost.} Final finds \FinalRecall\,\% of the holdout people, \GapRecallControl{} points fewer
than Control, at the edge of run-to-run variation, and \GapRecallBase{} points fewer than Base, beyond it; every
depth-fix pilot lost \DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to \DiagFixCostRecallHigh{} points against its control, always in the
same direction (Section~\ref{sec:diag-depth}). I state it as a real cost. The likely reason is that the fix moved the
detection cell from the pelvis to the head, so people whose head is cut off or hidden are found less often; this was
not tested, since recall on people with a visible head needs code that does not exist yet.

\paragraph{What the 3DPW labels can teach and test.} The labels carry a neutral body: \DataConstantCount{} of the
\DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes are constants, and neck02, neck03, the head and all hand bones stay at rest
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). They cannot supervise body build or fingers, and they cannot test the neck
correction. Training on them pulls body build toward the constants (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}); the hand mask
removes the rotation terms only, and the mesh terms still carry the rest-pose hands. As a test set they put a floor
of about \DataFloor\,mm under every number and likely reward a neutral prediction, which was not tested. Precision
(\FinalPrecThreeDPW\,\% for Final) is a lower bound, since detected bystanders count as false positives, as do ghost
bodies on half-visible people; the split between the two is unknown.

\paragraph{The comparison with NAVER's model.} Its AnnyOne number is most likely in-sample
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-requirements}); whether our 100 holdout images were in its training set is not known for
certain. On 3DPW the comparison is fairer but not like for like: Final was fine-tuned on 3DPW-train and NAVER's
model was not, and Control, without our fixes, already reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm. The gap shows what the
real-image fine-tune buys, not a better network; NAVER's model fine-tuned on our labels would separate the two and
was not run.

\paragraph{The validation split.} 3DPW's validation sequences share actors and locations with the training
sequences; the test split shows other people in other scenes. The draft found, on the old labels, that the gain
from real images on validation was about twice that on test. I report only test numbers, and the validation split
selects nothing, since Final is the last epoch of its run (Table~\ref{tab:data-splits}).

\paragraph{Licences.} SMPL, and with it 3DPW's ground truth, is licensed for non-commercial research
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}), and 3DPW has its own licence. Relative Human ships without a licence file, so I
used only its validation split, for evaluation. AnnyOne and NAVER's checkpoints, from whose backbones every model
here starts, carry their own terms, separate from Anny's Apache~2.0 code. I have not yet checked whether those terms
allow releasing weights derived from them; the release of Section~\ref{sec:future} depends on that check.

\section{Engineering lessons}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

The faults that cost the most time were silent: each produced plausible output and showed only when one stage was
measured on its own, against a control, or by its geometry.

\paragraph{Measure each stage alone.} The six checks of Section~\ref{sec:diag-checks} decoded the labels without a
network, moved single joints of the body model and traced the gradients, and so excluded the rig, the labels and the
gradient path before any training change. The converter graded its own fit; only an independent check that read the
finished label files found the three label defects (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). The holdout trace measured
placement at each body's own predicted depth, which neither PVE nor the box-placed demos did. The draft's list of
pipeline bugs, from a demo that silently loaded no weights to a learning-rate schedule that froze a long run, shows
the same pattern. The label builder skipped frames
without an image file and lost \DataOldTrainMissingPct\,\% of the 3DPW training labels without a warning. The adapter for NAVER's checkpoint hard-coded a decoder width of \DiscFFNOurs{} where the checkpoint has
\DiscFFNNaver; it failed loudly on the first load, which made it the cheapest fault of all.

\paragraph{Read every change against a control.} Each fix was read against a run that differed only by that fix,
and two identical runs showed how much a run varies. Without the controls, three wrong readings would have stood:
the neck pilot's \DiscNeckPilotPVE\,mm as a gain over Base, Final's higher PVE as a cost of the fixes, and the 3DPW
gain as a gain of the fixes. The restart cost was itself found only because the controls of all pilots sat above
Base.

\paragraph{Check by geometry, not by names.} Three of the worst faults were faults of naming or convention that only
a geometric check exposed. The converter first chose Anny bones by name; the two rigs number the spine in opposite
directions, so SMPL's lumbar spine joint was mapped to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it, and the fit still
looked plausible until the two rest skeletons were compared (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). The gender value
followed MakeHuman's convention, where 1 is male, while in Anny 1 is female, so every 3DPW man was labelled with a
female body; the fit error gave it away, falling for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of \DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men when the
value was flipped (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). The training targets were built at joint~0, the pelvis, while the
model places each body by its head; only the drawn size and the depth line of the trace revealed that prediction and
target described two joints half a body apart (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The half-turned neck bones belong to
the same family: per-group means of \DiscGroupMeanNeckA{} and \DiscGroupMeanNeckB\,deg hid them, a per-bone dump
found them.

\paragraph{Keep every change as a switch.} Each change entered the code as a switch whose default reproduces the old
behaviour: the anchor joint of the targets, the bone correction (saved with the checkpoint and reapplied on loading),
the hand and shape masks, and the two switches of converter version 2.3. This kept the history models reproducible,
made every control a change of one setting, and let the masked arm run from the same code as Final
(Appendix~\ref{app:flags}).

In this pipeline silent failure was far more common than loud failure. A check that fails loudly, such as a load
percentage or a geometric comparison of two skeletons, costs minutes to write. The silent faults stayed hidden until
a measurement aimed at them: the depth bug sat in every checkpoint trained before this thesis.

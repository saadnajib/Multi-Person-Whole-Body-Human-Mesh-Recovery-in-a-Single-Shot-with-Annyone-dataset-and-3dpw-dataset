\chapter{Discussion}
\label{ch:discussion}

Here I use the results to answer the three questions of Section~\ref{sec:intro-requirements}.

\section{Answers to the research questions}
\label{sec:disc-requirements}

\paragraph{Real-image supervision.}
The converted 3DPW labels make it possible to train and test Anny models on real images. An independent check puts
them \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm from 3DPW's body joints at the median. But they cannot add what the source data lacks: \DataConstantCount{} phenotypes are the same for every person, and many hand and head rotations stay at rest
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

Control already reaches \ControlThreeDPW\,mm PVE on converted 3DPW without the anchor and bone corrections, against
\BaseThreeDPW\,mm for Base. Final, with the corrections, reaches \FinalThreeDPW\,mm. This means that most of the gain
on real images comes from adding 3DPW to the training mix, and not from the corrections or from a change in the
network.

On the synthetic holdout Final follows weight, gender and cup size well (Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}), so its shape
head can vary the body. Muscle is the weak phenotype, likely because the converter fixes it for every 3DPW person; I
did not test this. On real photos the gender AUC needs no cut-off (Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}). Men still get a gender value in
the middle, because the AnnyOne gender labels span only \HoldGenderLabelMin{} to \HoldGenderLabelMax. Final stays the
teacher, and Final-partial-mask is a measured trade-off (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\paragraph{Compact reconstruction.}
The Student has \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters and \FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs than its
teacher, and it ran \SpeedRatioStudent{} times faster in the paired L40S benchmark. Its PVE rose from \FinalPVE{} to
\StudentPVE\,mm on the AnnyOne holdout and from \FinalThreeDPW{} to \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW. So the
Student is smaller and faster, but it is also less accurate.

Longer training did not close the gap on real images. After \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs the holdout PVE fell to
\StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, but 3DPW stayed at \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm (Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}). The reason
may be the smaller backbone, the share of 3DPW in each batch or the teacher's own 3DPW error; I did not test this. The
temperature sweep (Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature}) will show whether $T = \MethodKDTemp$ was a good choice.

Against Multi-HMR-Anny the Student is \ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times smaller and has a lower 3DPW PVE
(\StudentThreeDPW{} against \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm). But only my models were trained on converted 3DPW. Separating the
network and the distillation from the training data would need a baseline fine-tuned on the same data.

\paragraph{Video.}
On the walking clip the Student kept a tracked body in all \VideoFrames{} frames, the baseline in
\VideoBaselineMatched. By eye, temporal filtering made the motion steadier, and landmark alignment put the mesh on the person and
reduced the jumps in size (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}). The clip has no ground truth and the landmarks come from a 2D
detector, so this is no evidence of better 3D accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:method-temporal}). On the same frames and GPU
the raw Student ran at \VideoStudentRawFPS{} and the baseline at \VideoNAVERFPS\,FPS \TODO{confirm against

\section{Interpreting geometry and placement}
\label{sec:disc-pve}

PVE moves both bodies so that their pelvises meet before it measures vertex distances. So a model can have a low PVE
and still put the person at the wrong depth. From Control to Final the root error fell from \num{\ControlRootErr} to
\FinalRootErr\,mm, but the PVE only went from \ControlPVE{} to \FinalPVE\,mm. For this reason I report placement
errors next to PVE.

Small regions hide in the average. The PVE over the neck skin fell from \ControlNeckSkin{} to \FinalNeckSkin\,mm, but with only
\DiscNeckVerts{} vertices the neck moved the whole-body PVE by about \DiscNeckPVEShare\,mm (my rough estimate). So I
also judged the bone correction by bone angles and neck geometry (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

I trained the same short pilot twice, and the two runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE. In other words, a few
millimetres between two single runs can be training noise alone (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}). Two runs give no
general threshold, so I treat small differences with care.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\paragraph{Shape and children.}
Children are a measured improvement, not solved. On Relative Human the teacher separates them from adults better than
Base, but some still get adult bodies. The holdout has no children, and the 3DPW labels fix age and body build for
everyone. The
holdout gender labels have no fully male body, so a gender cut-off set there would mean nothing. These results concern
the teacher; I did not run the shape tests on the Student.

\paragraph{Depth and detection.}
The teacher's slope of predicted against true depth stays at \FinalSlope, and a large placement error remains
(Section~\ref{sec:res-depth}). Consistent anchors help the supervision but cannot remove the depth ambiguity of one
image. One clip of one walking person says little about occlusion, people cut off by the frame or crowds.

\paragraph{Evaluation conventions.}
My 3DPW errors use fitted Anny labels and MPJPE over \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints, so they are not comparable with
published results, and the label discrepancy must not be subtracted from them (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). My training never used the
holdout, but it is most likely in-sample for Multi-HMR-Anny (Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}).

\paragraph{Experimental coverage and efficiency.}
Each model comes from one training run, and I did not measure each Student loss term on its own. The walking clip has
no ground-truth mesh, and its movement statistics also contain camera motion and tracking errors. Latency depends on
hardware, precision, input resolution and the number of people. The paired benchmark and the per-video timing are
different protocols, so I report them separately.

\paragraph{Reproduction and release.}
The repository records settings and number sources, but the cluster scripts contain site-specific paths. Releasing weights or converted labels needs a check of the dataset, body-model and checkpoint terms, which I
have not done.

\section{What I report and why}
\label{sec:disc-engineering}

Whether a person gets a body, whether the body is accurate, whether it stays steady and how fast it comes are separate
questions. Tracking counts, labelled-image metrics, the video and the frame rates each answer one of them. For this
reason I report all of them, and not only one error value or one good-looking example.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The problem in this thesis is to turn one photo of several people into a 3D body for each person, at a lower
computational cost. I trained a small ViT-B student that learns from a larger ViT-L teacher. Both use the
single-shot Multi-HMR framework and the Anny body model, whose shape parameters have names such as age, height and
proportions.

The training data combine synthetic AnnyOne images, which have exact Anny labels, with real 3DPW photos labelled in
a different body model, SMPL. I converted these labels to Anny by fitting an Anny body to each one, with geometric
correspondence and virtual torso joints. The converted labels cover \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{} images with
\DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} labelled people.

The teacher was trained with \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% real images and two corrections to its supervision. The student
starts with a copy of the teacher's prediction heads, and a learned projector converts its features to the width
these heads expect. It learns from the labels and from the teacher's features and outputs.

The student has \StudentParams\,million parameters and needs \StudentGFLOPs\,GFLOPs for one person, against
\FinalParams\,million and \FinalGFLOPs\,GFLOPs for the teacher. A forward pass on one L40S GPU took
\StudentMsLFortyS\,ms against \TeacherMsLFortyS\,ms. But the student is less accurate. Its per-vertex error (PVE) was
\StudentPVE\,mm on the synthetic holdout and \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW, against \FinalPVE{} and
\FinalThreeDPW\,mm for the teacher. On 3DPW it had a lower error than the released Multi-HMR-Anny baseline
(\NAVERThreeDPW\,mm), which was not trained on 3DPW. The baseline's holdout result is most likely in-sample. All 3DPW
errors use converted labels and are not directly comparable with published SMPL-space results.

Training for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} instead of \StudentEpochs{} epochs lowered the holdout PVE to
\StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, but the 3DPW PVE went from \StudentThreeDPW{} to \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm. So longer training
helps on synthetic images and does not close the gap to the teacher on real ones. I kept the \StudentEpochs-epoch
student. A sweep of the distillation temperature over \KDSweepArms{} values is running.

For video I added, at inference and without retraining, an exponential moving average of each tracked person's pose,
shape and position, and an alignment of position and depth to detected 2D landmarks. On a walking clip the student
was tracked in \VideoStudentMatched{} of \VideoFrames{} frames and the baseline in \VideoBaselineMatched. By eye the
output becomes steadier and better placed, but the clip has no ground truth. The student did not reach the teacher's
accuracy. Other limits are that the holdout has no children, the shape on real photos is uncertain and depth errors
remain.

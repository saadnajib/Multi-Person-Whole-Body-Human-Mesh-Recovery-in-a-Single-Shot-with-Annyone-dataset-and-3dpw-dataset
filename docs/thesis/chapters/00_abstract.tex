\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

The problem in this thesis is to turn one photo of several people into a 3D body for each person, at a lower
computational cost. I trained a small ViT-B network, the student, that learns from a larger ViT-L network, the
teacher. Both use Multi-HMR, which finds all people and their bodies in one pass, and the Anny body model, with
named shape parameters such as age and height.

AnnyOne images are synthetic and have exact Anny labels. 3DPW photos are real, but labelled in another body model,
SMPL. I fitted an Anny body to each 3DPW label, using points that correspond geometrically in both models and
virtual torso joints, for \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{} images with \DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} labelled people.

The teacher was trained with \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% real images per batch and two corrections to its targets. The
student gets copies of the teacher's prediction heads, and a learned projector widens the student's features to
fit them. The student learns from the labels and, by knowledge distillation, from the teacher's features and
outputs.

The student has \StudentParams\,million parameters and needs \StudentGFLOPs\,GFLOPs for one person, the teacher
\FinalParams\,million and \FinalGFLOPs\,GFLOPs. On one L40S GPU the student's forward pass took
\StudentMsLFortyS\,ms and the teacher's \TeacherMsLFortyS\,ms.

But the student is also less accurate. Its per-vertex error (PVE, the mean distance to the true surface) was
\StudentPVE\,mm on held-out AnnyOne images and \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on 3DPW, the teacher's \FinalPVE{} and
\FinalThreeDPW\,mm. On 3DPW the student had a lower PVE than the released Multi-HMR-Anny baseline
(\NAVERThreeDPW\,mm), which was not trained on my converted 3DPW labels. The baseline's AnnyOne result most likely
comes from its training images. All 3DPW errors are against converted labels and not directly comparable
with published SMPL results.

In a training-length experiment I trained the student for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} instead of \StudentEpochs{} epochs.
Its holdout PVE fell from \StudentPVE{} to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, but its 3DPW PVE went from \StudentThreeDPW{} to
\StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm. So longer training helps on synthetic images but not on real photos, and the student
still did not reach the teacher's accuracy. I kept the \StudentEpochs-epoch student. A sweep of the distillation
temperature, which softens the features before they are compared, changed the errors only within the run-to-run
noise.

For video I added temporal filtering and landmark alignment, without retraining. An exponential moving average
blends each tracked person's pose, shape and position with earlier frames, and then the body's position and depth
are fitted to detected 2D landmarks. On a walking clip the student kept a tracked body in \VideoStudentMatched{} of
\VideoFrames{} frames and the released baseline in \VideoBaselineMatched; these counts show tracking only, not
accuracy. By eye the student's output is steadier and better placed, but the clip has no ground truth. The holdout
also has no children, the shape on real photos is uncertain and depth errors remain.

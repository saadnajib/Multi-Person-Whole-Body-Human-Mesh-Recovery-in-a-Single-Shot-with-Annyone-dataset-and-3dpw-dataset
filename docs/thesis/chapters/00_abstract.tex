\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Multi-person human mesh recovery finds every person in one image and builds a 3D body mesh for each. This thesis
moves Multi-HMR, a network that does this in one forward pass, from the SMPL-X body model to Anny, an open body
model whose shape is set by named values such as age, gender and height. I train it on the synthetic AnnyOne images
and on the real 3DPW images, whose SMPL ground truth I converted to Anny with my own fitting script.

The model I started from showed three visible failures: thin, twisted necks, claw-like fingers, and adult bodies on
children. Checks of the labels, the body model and the gradients ruled out a rig or decoding problem. Instead, I
found a depth bug: the network places each body by its head, but my training targets were built at the pelvis, so it
learned to put people too far away and to draw them too small. I also found that two neck bones and two hand bones
were turned by half a turn; a constant rotation at the output, with no new parameters, turns them back. In the
converted 3DPW labels I found and fixed three defects (reversed gender, a missing actor in two-person frames, missing
labels), fitted all \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} people again and checked the new labels independently.

The final model combines both fixes with \DataMixThreeDPW\,\% real images in every batch. A control run has the same
training without the two fixes. On 100 held-out AnnyOne images the final model has a mean vertex error (PVE) of
\FinalPVE\,mm, against \ControlPVE\,mm for the control and \BasePVE\,mm for the starting model. These gaps lie within
the \NoisePVE\,mm that separated two identical training runs. The errors the fixes target moved far more: against the
control, the error of the body's 3D position fell from \num{\ControlRootErr} to \FinalRootErr\,mm, the neck02 bone
error from \ControlNeckTwo{} to \FinalNeckTwo\,degrees and the finger error from \ControlFingers{} to
\FinalFingers\,degrees. On the 3DPW test set, measured against my converted labels, the final model reaches
\FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE, against \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm for NAVER's released Anny model and \BaseThreeDPW\,mm for the
starting model; most of this gain comes from training on real images at all. On real photos with age and gender
labels (Relative Human), gender accuracy rose from \RHGenderBase{} to \RHGenderFinal\,\% and the separation of
children from adults (AUC) from \num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\RHAUCBase} to
\num[round-mode=places,round-precision=2]{\RHAUCFinal}. For a smaller model, a ViT-B student distilled from the final
model has \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters and \FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs; its training was
running when this text was written.

Three things remain open. Children are a measured improvement, not solved. Near and far people still receive
similar depths: the fix moved the depth offset and the body size, not the depth slope (\FinalSlope, where 1 is
correct). Every result comes from one training run per model, without a fixed seed.

\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

In this thesis, I trained and evaluated a smaller student for multi-person human mesh recovery with Anny.
The student uses a ViT-B encoder, a trainable projector and prediction heads copied from a ViT-L teacher.
It learns from AnnyOne, converted 3DPW labels and the teacher's features and outputs. The teacher is only
needed during training.

The 3DPW converter fits Anny bodies to source joints and keeps one body shape per person across a sequence.
Virtual joints account for differences in where the two body models define their joints. The checks also
show which labels remain approximate or constant. This makes the real-image results easier to interpret.

The student has \StudentParams\,M parameters and needs \StudentGFLOPs\,GFLOPs in the recorded configuration.
Its paired L40S forward time is \StudentMsLFortyS\,ms, compared with \TeacherMsLFortyS\,ms for the teacher.
However, its PVE of \StudentPVE\,mm on AnnyOne and \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW does not meet
the accuracy-retention target against the teacher. Its lower 3DPW error than the released baseline also
reflects different training data. The result is a smaller, faster model with a measurable accuracy cost.

The video tool compares the student and baseline on the same frames. Optional temporal filtering smooths
the predicted rotations, shape and position; landmark alignment adjusts placement; and a depth filter
limits sudden changes in apparent size. These are inference steps around a frame-wise image model.
They do not establish improved 3D accuracy without labelled video evaluation.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Reduce the student's accuracy gap.}
The synthetic validation curve was still improving after 30 epochs, so a longer run is worth testing.
The real-image curve had nearly flattened, which suggests that duration alone may not close that gap.
Distillation weights and feature-transfer choices should be tested separately. A baseline trained on
the same real images would help distinguish the benefit of the data from the benefit of the method.

\paragraph{Evaluate video refinement.}
The next step is to compare raw, filtered and aligned predictions on several clips, including occlusion
and camera motion. The evaluation should measure stability, lag and reconstruction accuracy together.
The depth filter also needs a measured comparison with independent per-frame alignment. Learned temporal
refinement can then be tested against these simpler filters. Speed measurements should include the
landmark detector when discussing a complete online system.

\paragraph{Improve real-image shape.}
Real age-group labels, body measurements or additional Anny fits could provide supervision beyond the
converter's fixed values. A held-out set with children is needed to test whether the network learns
child proportions, rather than only producing them on familiar synthetic examples.

\paragraph{Repeat and document the experiments.}
Repeated runs and a larger evaluation set would make small differences easier to assess. The report
uses the verified 30-epoch student; any later checkpoint should be reported only after its evaluation
is complete. Portable configuration and clear instructions would also make the work easier to reproduce.

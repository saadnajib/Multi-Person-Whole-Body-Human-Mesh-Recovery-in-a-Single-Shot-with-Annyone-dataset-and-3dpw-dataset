\chapter{Conclusion and future work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

This thesis addressed multi-person whole-body mesh recovery from a single image in the Anny body model at reduced
inference cost, for which no real images with Anny labels existed. The solution has four parts: 3DPW's SMPL
labels were converted to Anny by joint-based fitting; a ViT-L teacher was trained on AnnyOne and converted 3DPW
with corrected head-anchor targets and a fixed rotation correction of four output bones; a ViT-B student
inherited the teacher's heads through a learned projector and was distilled from its features and outputs; and
inference-time temporal filtering and landmark alignment were added for video.

The student has \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer parameters than its teacher and ran \SpeedRatioStudent{} times
faster, at a higher error: \StudentThreeDPW{} against \FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE on converted 3DPW, outside the
95\,\% retention thresholds set in advance. Its 3DPW PVE is lower than that of the released baseline, trained
on different data, and neither longer training nor another distillation temperature closed the gap. In this
configuration the distillation losses had no measurable effect on accuracy, whereas a ViT-B model trained without
the teacher was clearly less accurate; the student is therefore a ViT-B model with heads transferred from the teacher,
and its accuracy is owed to these heads rather than to the distillation losses.
For the teacher, detection recall on real photographs falls as the number of people per image grows.
On video, filtering and alignment produced visibly steadier and better placed output.

Unresolved are the cause of the accuracy gap, the separate roles of the head copy, its freeze and the bone
correction, the student's recall and shape on real photographs (not evaluated), the teacher's shape for children and men,
the compression of predicted depth and a learned temporal model. The evaluation is bounded by a small synthetic holdout, single runs and the absence of a fairly
trained baseline (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}).

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Temporal evaluation and modelling.}
Running the filter and the alignment on the 3DPW test sequences, whose converted labels exist at \DataLabelFps{}
frames per second, would measure steadiness and per-frame PVE together; clips with occlusion, a moving camera or
several people, timed end to end, would follow. A learned temporal model (Section~\ref{sec:bg-temporal}) remains
open.

\paragraph{Accuracy at reduced cost.}
Separating the head transfer from its warm-up freeze and from the teacher's bone correction would show which part
carries the teacher's benefit. Other distillation targets, such as other feature layers or loss weights, and more
real images per batch could narrow the gap. A released baseline fine-tuned on the same
converted 3DPW data would separate the effect of architecture and distillation from that of the training data.

\paragraph{Real-image shape.}
Age-group labels, body measurements or further Anny fits on real photographs could replace the constant phenotypes.
Tests with children should separate the representability of child proportions in Anny from their recognition by
the network.

\paragraph{Reliability and reproducibility.}
Repeated runs, an evaluation of the student on Relative Human and a larger, randomly drawn holdout that includes
children would show which differences are real and how far the results generalise.

\chapter{Conclusion and future work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:concl}

Multi-HMR reconstructs several complete human bodies from one image. My project builds on this framework with the
Anny body model, converted 3DPW labels and a smaller distilled student. I also added temporal filtering and landmark
alignment to improve the video outputs.

The converter turns 3DPW's SMPL labels into Anny labels through joint correspondence, virtual torso joints and one
shape per person. An independent check puts its joints \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm from 3DPW's at the median, and some
phenotypes are the same for every person (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). The ViT-B student trains on AnnyOne and
converted 3DPW. It has a learned projector and heads copied from its ViT-L teacher, Final, and learns from it through
feature and output distillation. Both models use consistent head anchors and two fixed output corrections, analysed in
the appendices. At inference only the student and the Anny decoding layer run.

The student has \StudentParams\,M parameters and \StudentGFLOPs\,GFLOPs, against \FinalParams\,M and
\FinalGFLOPs\,GFLOPs for the teacher, and one forward pass took \StudentMsLFortyS{} against \TeacherMsLFortyS\,ms on the
L40S. Its PVE was \StudentPVE\,mm on the AnnyOne holdout and \StudentThreeDPW\,mm on converted 3DPW, against
\FinalPVE{} and \FinalThreeDPW\,mm for the teacher. So it missed the 95\,\% bars (Section~\ref{sec:res-student}). It
beat the \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm of Multi-HMR-Anny on 3DPW, but only my models were trained on converted 3DPW.

A 60-epoch run lowered the holdout PVE to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm but left 3DPW at \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm
(Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}). The gap to the teacher on real images is not a matter of training length,
so I keep the 30-epoch student. Whether another temperature than $T = \MethodKDTemp$ helps is still open
(Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature}). \TODO{pending: temperature sweep result}

On a walking clip of \VideoFrames{} frames the student kept a tracked person in \VideoStudentMatched{} frames, the
baseline in \VideoBaselineMatched{} (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}). By eye, the filter made the motion steadier and the
alignment put the mesh on the person. The network still works frame by frame, and neither step is a learned temporal
model (Section~\ref{sec:method-temporal}). The clip has no ground truth, so this shows steadier output, not better 3D
accuracy.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future}

\paragraph{Temporal evaluation and modelling.}
I would run the filter and the alignment on the 3DPW test sequences, whose converted labels exist at \DataLabelFps{}
frames per second, and measure PVE per frame against the raw student. That way steadiness and accuracy are measured
together. Clips with occlusion, camera motion and several people should follow, each frame rate given with its GPU,
precision and resolution. A learned temporal model (Section~\ref{sec:bg-temporal}) is a separate project.

\paragraph{Accuracy at reduced cost.}
After the temperature sweep, distillation-weight ablations, other ways of transferring features or more real images
per batch could lower the student's error. A fair comparison also needs Multi-HMR-Anny fine-tuned on the same 3DPW
data.

\paragraph{Real-image shape.}
Age-group labels, body measurements or more real Anny fits could replace the constant phenotypes. Tests with children
should keep two questions apart: whether Anny can make child proportions, and whether the network recognises a child
in a photo.

\paragraph{Reliability and reproducibility.}
Each model comes from one run. Repeated runs, uncertainty estimates and a larger held-out set would show which
differences are real, and a portable configuration with release checks would make the system easier to verify. I have
not run any of these.

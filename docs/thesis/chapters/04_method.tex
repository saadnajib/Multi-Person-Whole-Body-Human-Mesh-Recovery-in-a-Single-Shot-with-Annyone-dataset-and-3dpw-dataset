\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:method}

This chapter explains how I trained the ViT-B Student and used it for image and video inference.
The larger ViT-L teacher, called Final in the experiment records, provides the copied prediction heads
and teacher targets. The supporting diagnostic experiments are in Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}.

\section{System Overview}
\label{sec:method-overview}

The pipeline has three stages. First, the conversion procedure of Chapter~\ref{ch:data} supplies Anny targets
for real 3DPW training images. Second, a larger teacher is trained with synthetic and real supervision.
Third, the student learns from both the labels and the frozen teacher. Deployment uses the student alone.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\input{figures/tikz/F05_distillation}
\caption[Teacher--student training and the deployed model]{Teacher--student system. Both branches receive the
same image. The frozen teacher provides features and per-person outputs. The student's ViT-B features pass
through a \MethodWidthB{}-to-\MethodWidthL{} projector and heads initialized from the teacher. The student
receives supervised, feature-distillation and output-distillation losses. Only the student branch is needed
at inference; its projector remains because the prediction heads use its output.}
\label{fig:distillation}
\end{figure}

For image $I$, student tokens are $\mathbf Z^s=f_B(I)\in\mathbb R^{N_p\times C_s}$, with
$C_s=\MethodWidthB$. The trainable projection is
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\mathbf Z}^s=\mathbf Z^s\mathbf W^\top+\mathbf b,\qquad
 \mathbf W\in\mathbb R^{C_t\times C_s},\quad C_t=\MethodWidthL .
 \label{eq:student-projection}
\end{equation}
For each image patch, the student produces \MethodWidthB{} feature values. The projector learns
\MethodWidthL{} weighted combinations of those values, plus a bias. It changes their representation so
that the teacher's copied heads can use them. It does not add new image information. Both detection and
body prediction use the projected features, so the projector remains part of the student at inference.
Most of the saving comes from replacing the larger encoder.

\section{Multi-HMR for Anny: task and architecture}
\label{sec:method-arch}

\subsection{The task}
\label{sec:method-task}

The input is one RGB image with camera intrinsics $\mathbf{K}$ (from an assumed field of view when they are unknown).
It shows an unknown number of people. For each person $i$ the network predicts the bone rotations
$\mathbf{R}_i\in SO(3)^{\MethodNumBones}$ of Anny's bones, the phenotypes
$\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\in[0,1]^{\MethodNumPheno}$, and the image position $(u_i,v_i)$ and depth $z_i$ of one anchor
joint $a$, the head. The Anny body model $M$ \cite{bregier2025anny} turns $(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i)$ into
vertices and joints, and the mesh is moved so that its anchor joint sits at the back-projected anchor pixel:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{V}_i = M_V(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) - M_{J,a}(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) + \mathbf{t}_i,
  \qquad
  \mathbf{t}_i = z_i\,\mathbf{K}^{-1}\,(u_i, v_i, 1)^{\top}.
  \label{eq:method-placement}
\end{equation}
The decoder first builds the body, subtracts its head-joint position and moves the whole mesh to
$\mathbf t_i$. Its head then sits at the predicted 3D anchor. This changes placement without changing
the body's pose or proportions.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-architecture}

The teacher follows Multi-HMR \cite{baradel2024multihmr} with Anny-compatible outputs
(Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). The student retains the detection, cross-attention and decoding pipeline
while replacing the encoder by ViT-B and introducing Equation~\eqref{eq:student-projection}.
Bone rotations and phenotypes are decoded by the same full-rig Anny configuration used to construct targets.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F01_architecture}
  \caption[Teacher decoding architecture]{Teacher decoding architecture. A $\MethodImgSize$-pixel image passes through a DINOv2 ViT-L backbone ($\MethodTokenGrid\times
  \MethodTokenGrid$ tokens). Every token gets a detection score and a 2D offset for the anchor joint, the head. For
  each detected person a cross-attention head (HPH) predicts $\MethodNumBones\times\MethodRotDims$ rotation values,
  \MethodNumPheno{} phenotypes and a depth; a constant correction turns four flipped bones back; the Anny layer builds
  the body and places it at the predicted distance. Shaded boxes are changed or added in this thesis; the dashed box
  marks the part that distillation replaces.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

A DINOv2 ViT-L \cite{oquab2023dinov2} with weights from NAVER's Anny checkpoint turns the image into tokens; one MLP
scores each token for an anchor joint (the head, \texttt{-{}-person\_center head}) and a second predicts its 2D
offset. Each detected token becomes a query of the HPH, which lets the people attend to each other and to all image
tokens. The Anny head is what differs from the SMPL-X version: the rotations use the 6D representation
\cite{zhou2019continuity} with Gram--Schmidt, the phenotypes a sigmoid, and the depth Multi-HMR's focal-normalised
log-depth. Right after Gram--Schmidt four bones are multiplied by one constant rotation each
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}); then a fixed mask sets \MethodHelperBones{} helper bones (toes, breasts, face,
subdivision bones) to the identity, in the prediction and the ground truth alike, and keeps \MethodActiveBones{}. The
Anny layer runs inside the network and places the body with Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}, so the 3D and 2D
losses reach the network through it. The network has \MethodParamsTotalL\,M parameters (heads \MethodParamsHeadsL\,M)
and needs $\num{\MethodGflopsL}$ GFLOPs per image with one person.

\section{Supervised Training Objective}
\label{sec:method-losses}

A loss penalizes a mismatch during training. Each term compares a prediction with its corresponding
label: detection with detection, pose with pose, and so on. Anny converts the labelled body parameters
into target joints and vertices. All anchor targets refer to the same head joint
(Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L} = {}& \alpha_{\text{det}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}
  + \alpha_{\text{off}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{off}}
  + \alpha_{\text{rot}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}
  + \alpha_{\text{shape}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}} \\
  &+ \alpha_{\text{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}
  + \alpha_{\text{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{t}}
  + \alpha_{\text{j3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j3d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v3d}} \\
  &+ \mathbb{1}[e \ge e_{\text{2D}}]\left(\alpha_{\text{j2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j2d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v2d}}\right)
  + \lambda_{\text{kd}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd}} + \lambda_{\text{kd-out}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}},
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-loss}
\end{equation}
with $e$ the epoch; the distillation terms exist only for a student. Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights} says what each term
compares and gives its weights.

\input{tables/T02_loss_weights}

\subsection{Detection and Anchor Localization}

Let $s_q$ be the detection probability at token $q$, $y_q\in\{0,1\}$ its binary target and
$N_+=\sum_q y_q$. The implemented focal loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{det}}=
 -\frac{1}{\max(1,N_+)}\sum_q
 \begin{cases}
 (1-s_q)^2\log(s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=1,\\
 s_q^2\log(1-s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=0 .
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:loss-detection}
\end{equation}
The sum covers the batch's token scores. Positive tokens represent head anchors; focal weighting reduces the
contribution of easy background tokens. For $N_h$ supervised people and their sub-token offsets $\mathbf o_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i
    \|\widehat{\mathbf o}_i-\mathbf o_i\|_1 .
 \label{eq:loss-offset}
\end{equation}
The detection score is compared with the binary label, while the offset is compared with the target
2D offset. They are separate losses; the score and location are not compared with each other.

\subsection{Pose, Shape and Placement}

Let $\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}$ and $\mathbf R_{ib}$ denote predicted and target rotation matrices for person
$i$, bone $b$. The supervised pose term uses entry-wise L1:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_b
    m_{ib}w_b\|\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}-\mathbf R_{ib}\|_1,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_k
    |\widehat{\phi}_{ik}-\phi_{ik}|.
 \label{eq:loss-parameters}
\end{equation}
Here $m_{ib}$ suppresses unavailable 3DPW hand labels and $w_b$ is the normalized bone weight. Helper rotations
are set to identity in predictions and targets. Phenotypes are sigmoid outputs; the final configuration
supervises all phenotype entries, including the converter constants. Shape-mask variants are ablations of that
choice. The implementation sums over bones or phenotypes before averaging people, so the weights in
Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights} depend on these reductions.

For predicted and target focal-normalized log-depth $d_i$ and camera-space anchor translation $\mathbf t_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{dist}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i|\widehat d_i-d_i|,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm t}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\|\widehat{\mathbf t}_i-\mathbf t_i\|_1 .
 \label{eq:loss-placement}
\end{equation}
These terms supervise absolute placement. They complement the centred geometric terms below.

\subsection{Geometric and Reprojection Supervision}

For joints or vertices $\mathbf X_i$, define pelvis-centred coordinates
$\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}=\mathbf X_{i\ell}-\mathbf J_{i,\mathrm{root}}$.
With $K_X$ points and optional normalized joint weights $w_\ell$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X3D}=\frac{1}{N_hK_X}\sum_i\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_X}
 w_\ell\|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf X}}_{i\ell}-\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}\|_1 .
 \label{eq:loss-geometry}
\end{equation}
For vertices $w_\ell=1$; joints use the configured weights. Subtracting each body's pelvis moves both
pelvises to the origin. The comparison then checks the body around that common point, regardless of where
it was placed in the scene. Placement therefore needs the separate losses in
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss-placement}. The 2D losses project the bodies back into the image without this
pelvis alignment:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X2D}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_X|}
 \sum_{(i,\ell)\in\Omega_X}
 \|\pi_{\mathbf K}(\widehat{\mathbf X}_{i\ell})-\pi_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf X_{i\ell})\|_1 ,
 \label{eq:loss-reprojection}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_X$ contains target points inside the image. The implementation activates the 2D losses at the
configured epoch and suppresses non-finite reductions. The task objective is the weighted combination in
Equation~\eqref{eq:method-loss}; the distillation additions are defined in Section~\ref{sec:method-kd}.



The detection term is the focal loss of CornerNet \cite{law2018cornernet} (the log calls it \texttt{bce}, but it is
not a binary cross-entropy). The neck and hand boost moves rotation loss between bones, it does not add any. The
shape L1 is smallest at the median of the plausible answers, so weak image cues pull predictions toward the training
median (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}). The 3D terms align both skeletons at the pelvis, so only distance and
translation see placement directly, and the 2D terms indirectly; before the depth fix the two direct terms made up
about \MethodLossShareDepth\% of the loss, against \MethodLossShareShape\% for shape. Training runs in fp32 with TF32
products (mixed precision gave NaN values with the Anny layer), and steps with NaN or Inf gradients are skipped
(\MethodFinalNaNSteps{} of $\num{\MethodFinalSteps}$ in Final). The main loss weights are listed in Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}; the reported pilots test specific
changes rather than an exhaustive weight search.

\section{Training schedule and model names}
\label{sec:method-schedule}

\paragraph{Teacher initialization.} The teacher uses a ViT-L backbone initialized from NAVER's released checkpoint and Anny-compatible
prediction heads trained on AnnyOne. The synthetic-only Base checkpoint is the starting point for mixed-data
teacher training. The earlier transfer stages are reproduction history, recorded in Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}.

\paragraph{Final and Control.} Final starts from Base and trains \MethodFinalEpochs{} epochs of
$\num{\MethodFinalIters}$ iterations at batch \MethodFinalBatch{} at a constant learning rate of \MethodFinalLR; the
last \MethodUnfrozenBlocks{} backbone blocks and the heads train. Three changes come on together: the depth fix with
distance and translation weight \MethodWDist{} (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}), the bone correction
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}), and \MethodMixRatio\% real images per batch with the hand mask
(Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). The \MethodManifestImages{} images of the diagnostic manifest (Chapter~\ref{ch:data})
are never sampled. Training took $\num{\MethodFinalTrainSec}$\,s on one A100. Control is identical but keeps the
pelvis targets with weight \MethodWDistDefault{} and has no bone correction, so Control against Final isolates the two
fixes, and Base against Control shows the real images plus the restart cost. Each fix was tested alone only in short
pilots (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\section{Consistent Head-Anchor Supervision}
\label{sec:method-depth}

Detection, sub-token offset, depth and translation describe the same head joint used for mesh placement in
Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}. The target builder first decodes the annotated body, selects that joint,
and derives its camera-space location and image projection. Visibility checks and the one-person-per-token
assignment use this anchor as well.

This consistency matters because pelvis-aligned geometry losses do not supervise absolute placement.
If the target translation described a pelvis while the decoder placed a head there, the terms would disagree
about the body's location. Figure~\ref{fig:anchor} illustrates the distinction; Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}
contains the supporting placement measurements. The final teacher and student use head-anchor targets.
The distance and translation weights are \MethodWDist, compared with \MethodWDistDefault{} in the Control
configuration. The ablation therefore changes anchor construction and placement weighting together.

\section{Fixed Output Rotation Correction}
\label{sec:method-bonefix}

The pose head's output includes a fixed calibration for selected bones before Anny decoding:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf R^{\mathrm{corr}}_{ib}=\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}\mathbf C_b,\qquad
 \mathbf C_b=\operatorname{Proj}_{SO(3)}
 \left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_i
       \widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{target}}_{ib}\right).
 \label{eq:bone-calibration}
\end{equation}
The projection finds the nearest proper rotation to the mean residual. Calibration uses people from
\MethodBoneFixImages{} random training images, never evaluation images. Candidate bones have a persistent
rotation offset above the configured threshold \MethodBoneFixThr$^\circ$. The teacher correction acts on
neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R. Figure~\ref{fig:neck-chain} illustrates its geometric role.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F04_neck_chain}
  \caption[The neck chain and its correction]{The neck chain neck01--neck02--neck03--head. Top: in Base, neck02 and the head bone are each turned about
  half a turn; the turns cancel, so the head points right, but the skin between them is twisted to \MethodBaseGirth{}
  of its true girth. Bottom: one constant rotation per bone turns both back. Girth on the AnnyOne holdout, setting V1.}
  \label{fig:neck-chain}
\end{figure}

The correction is applied before helper masking, Anny decoding and loss calculation. It introduces constants,
not additional trainable weights. The teacher is fine-tuned with the correction in place; an unadapted
post-processing change is not assumed to preserve global articulation. During student training, the copied
heads use the teacher's correction. A correction is then estimated from the student's own training predictions
and used for its evaluation and video inference.

A constant rotation can compensate a consistent residual but cannot resolve arbitrary image-dependent pose
error. The supervised and distillation objectives remain responsible for articulated prediction.
Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics} reports per-bone, surface and global-orientation evidence and the limitations of
interpreting each metric.

\section{Training with real images}
\label{sec:method-real}

\paragraph{The mix.} A weighted sampler draws on average \MethodMixRatio\% of each batch from 3DPW-train converted to
Anny (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}; corrected labels, $\num{\MethodTrainThreeDPWImages}$ images) and \MethodSynthRatio\% from
AnnyOne; with more labelled people per AnnyOne image, roughly \MethodThreeDPWPeopleShare\% of the supervised people are
real (an estimate). The test split is never used for training.

\paragraph{The hand mask.} The converter fits only the root and the bones that carry a mapped SMPL joint, so every
finger, thumb and metacarpal bone of a 3DPW label is at rest. With \texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_hands 1} the rotation terms
skip these \MethodHandMaskBones{} bones for 3DPW people. Measured finger-rotation supervision therefore
comes from AnnyOne. The mesh losses still include the fitted rest-pose hands, and the student's output
distillation still uses the teacher's hand predictions. The mask does not remove all hand-related training.

\paragraph{The shape mask, tested.} The converter leaves \MethodShapeMaskCount{} phenotypes at constants (age
\MethodConstAge; muscle, weight, cup size, firmness and three ethnicity values at \MethodConstOther). With
\texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_shape 1} the shape term ignores them for 3DPW people (Final-masked); the partial variant masks
only the \MethodShapeMaskPartial{} values other than age (Final-partial-mask). The mesh terms still carry the neutral
body. I kept the unmasked model as Final and teacher, because both masked models lost the gender gain on real photos
(Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:method-kd}

A frozen teacher supervises a smaller student on the same image, on top of the task loss
(Figure~\ref{fig:distillation}).



\paragraph{Feature distillation.}
For image $u$ and token $n$, define channel distributions
$p^t_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\mathbf z^t_{un}/T)$ and
$p^s_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\widetilde{\mathbf z}^s_{un}/T)$.
With batch size $B$, the implemented feature loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{kd}}=
 \frac{T^2}{B}\sum_{u=1}^{B}\sum_{n=1}^{N_p}\sum_{c=1}^{C_t}
 p^t_{unc}\log\frac{p^t_{unc}}{p^s_{unc}} .
 \label{eq:method-kd}
\end{equation}
For each patch, softmax turns its feature channels into values that sum to one. Dividing by
$T=\MethodKDTemp$ makes this distribution less concentrated on the largest channels. KL divergence then
penalizes differences between the teacher's distribution and the projected student's distribution.
These are feature-channel distributions, not probabilities of body classes. The $T^2$ factor rescales
the gradients~\cite{hinton2015distilling}. The implementation averages over images and sums over
tokens and channels. Only the student and projector receive gradients from this term; the teacher
stays fixed. Matching intermediate representations follows the idea of feature distillation
\cite{romero2015fitnets}.

\paragraph{Head transfer and output distillation.} The student copies every teacher head tensor whose name and shape
match. Both run on the true person locations, so person $i$ of one is person $i$ of the other, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\Big[
 &\alpha_{\text{rot}}\sum_b\|\mathbf R^s_{ib}-\mathbf R^t_{ib}\|_1\\
 &+\alpha_{\text{shape}}\sum_k|\phi^s_{ik}-\phi^t_{ik}|
 +\alpha_{\text{dist}}|d^s_i-d^t_i|\Big].
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-kdout}
\end{equation}
pulls the student toward detached teacher outputs, with the term weights of Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}.
The rotation distillation term sums all bone residuals without the supervised per-bone boost or missing-label
mask. Teacher predictions are available even where a converted ground-truth hand rotation is not.
For both distillation terms, the selected recipe uses $\lambda_{\mathrm{kd}}=\MethodKDLambda$ and
$\lambda_{\mathrm{kd-out}}=\MethodKDOutLambda$.

\paragraph{Why the copied heads are frozen first.}
The heads already know how to turn teacher features into body predictions, but the new projector initially
produces different inputs. I freeze the copied heads for the first \MethodStudentFreeze{} epochs so the
encoder and projector can adapt without immediately changing those weights. I then train the heads too.
This gives the student a useful starting point while allowing later adaptation to its own features.

\subsection{The ViT-B student of Final}
\label{sec:method-student}

\paragraph{Recipe.} The Student follows the projector-fed design with a ViT-B backbone and the corrected training of
this chapter (Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}). Its backbone starts from NAVER's Multi-HMR ViT-B, trained for mesh
recovery, and trains fully; the copied heads stay frozen for \MethodStudentFreeze{} epochs; the targets are exactly
the teacher's. The script contains a colour-jitter option, but the training code does not use it, so I do not count it
as an applied augmentation. The copied heads output the half-turn that the teacher's bone correction
undoes, so the student trains with that correction; afterwards its own is estimated on \MethodBoneFixImages{} training
images and used in every evaluation.

\paragraph{Accuracy-retention criterion.}
In addition to reporting errors directly, the experiment records a retention criterion:
for an error metric $E$, $E_s\leq E_t/\MethodRuleFactor$.
Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe} also lists the thresholds against the released baseline. This is an explicit
comparison criterion, not an assertion that the student achieves it. The teacher comparison uses the same
body representation and evaluation convention. The baseline's AnnyOne result is potentially in-sample, and its
3DPW training exposure differs, so the two references must be interpreted separately.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method-eval}

Table~\ref{tab:metrics} lists the metrics. Errors are means over matched people: a greedy 2D matcher pairs each
labelled person with at most one detection, and recall is the share of labelled people that got one.


\input{tables/T07_metrics}

For one matched person, pelvis-centred vertex and joint errors are
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{PVE}=\frac{1}{N_v}\sum_{v=1}^{N_v}
 \|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf V}}_v-\overline{\mathbf V}_v\|_2,\qquad
 \mathrm{MPJPE}=\frac{1}{N_j}\sum_{j=1}^{N_j}
 \|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf J}}_j-\overline{\mathbf J}_j\|_2 .
 \label{eq:eval-errors}
\end{equation}
The bars denote subtraction of each body's pelvis. Scores are averaged over matched people and
reported in millimetres. PA-PVE first fits one rotation, translation and uniform scale to align the
predicted vertices with the target, then computes the mean Euclidean vertex distance.
Recall is the number of matched labelled people divided by the total number of labelled people.
These evaluation scores differ from the coordinate-wise L1 losses used during training. A lower
centred error does not necessarily mean a better overlay in the camera image.


\paragraph{AnnyOne holdout.} The last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (indices $\num{\MethodHoldoutFirst}$ to
$\num{\MethodHoldoutLast}$) are excluded from training in this thesis and contain no children
(Chapter~\ref{ch:data}). The released baseline may have seen them. The headline setting V1
masks the helper bones, as the demos do; V0 does not. A trace script adds the placement and per-bone metrics.

\paragraph{3DPW test.} All $\num{\MethodTestImages}$ test images ($\num{\MethodTestPeople}$ people) of the corrected
labels, same script for every model, setting V0. MPJPE averages all \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints and the conversion
itself errs by about \MethodLabelFloor\,mm (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}), so these numbers are not comparable with published
3DPW numbers.

\paragraph{Relative Human and demo images.} Relative Human gives real photos with an age group, a gender and a box per
person. Its validation split (\MethodRHImages{} images, $\num{\MethodRHPeople}$ people) serves for
evaluation only: boxes from the projected vertices, matching at IoU $\ge$ \MethodRHIoU, child-shaped meaning a
predicted age below \MethodChildThr. The \MethodDemoPhotos{} demo photos and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames are
rendered with each body placed from its detection box, which hides depth errors, and are judged by eye.

\paragraph{Training variation.}
Each model has one recorded training run without a fixed training seed. The two identical short pilots
differ by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE and \NoiseHeadDeg$^\circ$ head direction (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}).
These observations motivate matched controls and caution with small differences, but do not provide
confidence intervals or a significance threshold for every dataset and schedule. Repeated training of the
final student and teacher was not performed. Evaluation of fixed checkpoints is kept separate from
training variation.

\section{Video Inference, Filtering and Alignment}
\label{sec:method-video}

The video application uses the trained image model without retraining it. It keeps the raw predictions
and can add two steps: temporal filtering to reduce changes between frames, and landmark alignment to
improve where the mesh is drawn. These steps address different problems.

\paragraph{Raw prediction and tracking.}
Frames are read in their original order and padded to each checkpoint's input size. Both models use the
same camera assumption: by default, a $55^\circ$ field of view along the longer image side. The raw mesh
keeps its predicted camera-space placement. A geometric tracker follows one primary person using box
overlap and centre distance. A missing detection remains missing; it is not replaced by an old mesh.

\paragraph{Temporal filtering.}
For shape values and the 3D head anchor, I use an exponential moving average:
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_t=1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau},\qquad
 \overline{\mathbf x}_t=(1-\alpha_t)\overline{\mathbf x}_{t-1}+\alpha_t\mathbf x_t .
 \label{eq:video-ema}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf x_t$ is the current prediction, $\overline{\mathbf x}_{t-1}$ is the previous filtered
value and $\Delta t$ is the time between selected frames. A larger time constant $\tau$ gives stronger
smoothing but can delay fast movement. Setting it to zero passes the current value through.
The defaults are 1.0\,s for shape, 0.12\,s for the head anchor and 0.08\,s for pose.

Bone rotations need a different interpolation because averaging their coordinates can produce an invalid
rotation. I convert them to unit quaternions and use shortest-path spherical interpolation (SLERP) with
the same $\alpha_t$. Anny then rebuilds the mesh from the filtered pose and shape and places it at the
filtered anchor. The filter uses current and past outputs only. It resets on a missing detection, a
track change or a timestamp gap above 0.25\,s. No future frames or learned temporal weights are used.

\paragraph{Landmark alignment.}
MediaPipe~\cite{mediapipecode} detects shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles in the original
video. These 2D landmarks are extracted separately and associated with the tracked person. The saved
landmark file is checked against the source video's hash and dimensions to avoid using points from
another video or an already rendered overlay.

I fit one translation $\boldsymbol\Delta\in\mathbb R^3$ to bring the projected Anny joints closer to
these points. The optimizer uses confidence-weighted residuals and a robust loss, so one poor landmark
has less influence. It requires confident points spanning the upper and lower body, rejects outliers
and checks that the fitted body stays in front of the camera and within the allowed translation range.
A rejected fit keeps the unaligned prediction. If filtering is enabled, alignment uses the filtered body.

This step changes position and depth, but leaves bone lengths, pose and shape unchanged. Moving a body
farther away makes its projection smaller; moving it closer makes it larger. It can improve an overlay
without proving that the body's 3D position is correct.

\paragraph{Stabilizing aligned depth.}
Independent alignment can choose a slightly different depth in each frame and make the mesh appear to
grow and shrink. I therefore filter the logarithm of the fitted pelvis depth, using a default time
constant of 0.35\,s and limiting each log-depth step to $0.5\Delta t$. The whole body is translated
along the current pelvis camera ray to reach this depth, which preserves the pelvis's image position.
The state resets after rejected fits, missing predictions or track breaks. This is a trade-off: less
size flicker may come with more current-frame landmark error. The reported projection error is
recomputed from the mesh actually rendered.

\paragraph{Measurements and timing.}
For a pelvis-relative joint $\mathbf q_{tj}=\mathbf J_{tj}-\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{root}}$ and constant
frame spacing $\Delta t$, the application records
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf v_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-\mathbf q_{t-1,j}}{\Delta t},\qquad
 \mathbf a_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-2\mathbf q_{t-1,j}+\mathbf q_{t-2,j}}{\Delta t^2}.
 \label{eq:video-motion}
\end{equation}
It also records shape variation, missing frames and track segments. These are changes in predictions,
not errors against ground-truth motion. Natural movement can increase them, and an incorrect body can
still be very smooth.

Inference speed is computed as
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{FPS}_{\mathrm{infer}}=
 \frac{N_{\mathrm{timed}}}{\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}\tau_t},
 \label{eq:video-fps}
\end{equation}
where $\tau_t$ is synchronized elapsed time for the reported stage. The first ten frames are excluded
from timing averages. Raw FPS covers the model forward pass; filtered FPS also includes filtering and
Anny decoding; aligned FPS includes the active refinement steps. Preprocessing, rendering, video I/O
and offline landmark extraction are excluded. These values differ from playback FPS and from complete
online throughput. GPU, precision, resolution and person count must accompany a speed comparison.

\chapter{Method}
\label{ch:method}

This chapter describes the network, its losses, the training runs and the evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:method-schedule}
defines the model names used in the rest of the thesis. The two changes that matter most are in
Sections~\ref{sec:method-depth} and~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}: the training targets now describe the joint the network
anchors, and a constant rotation at the output turns four flipped bones back. Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} shows how
the defects were found; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} measures what the changes did.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Multi-HMR for Anny: task, architecture, inputs and outputs}
\label{sec:method-arch}

\subsection{The task}
\label{sec:method-task}

The input is one RGB image with camera intrinsics $\mathbf{K}$ (taken from an assumed field of view when they are
unknown). It shows an unknown number of people. For each person $i$ the network predicts the parent-relative rotations
$\mathbf{R}_i\in SO(3)^{\MethodNumBones}$ of Anny's bones, the phenotypes
$\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\in[0,1]^{\MethodNumPheno}$, and the image position $(u_i,v_i)$ and depth $z_i$ of one anchor
joint $a$, the head. The Anny body model $M$ \cite{bregier2025anny} turns $(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i)$ into
vertices and joints, and the mesh is moved so that its anchor joint sits at the back-projected anchor pixel:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{V}_i = M_V(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) - M_{J,a}(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) + \mathbf{t}_i,
  \qquad
  \mathbf{t}_i = z_i\,\mathbf{K}^{-1}\,(u_i, v_i, 1)^{\top}.
  \label{eq:method-placement}
\end{equation}
The goal is a mesh with the right pose, shape and place in the camera frame, from one pass over the whole image, with
no person crops and no other video frames.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-architecture}

The network is Multi-HMR \cite{baradel2024multihmr} with its output resized for Anny (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}).
I did not change the backbone, the detection or the cross-attention head, so for requirement 1b (a model that is not
exactly Multi-HMR) the architecture is no answer; the route to 1b is the smaller student of Section~\ref{sec:method-kd}.

\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F01_architecture}
  \caption{The network. A $\MethodImgSize$-pixel image passes through a DINOv2 ViT-L backbone ($\MethodTokenGrid\times
  \MethodTokenGrid$ tokens). Every token gets a detection score and a 2D offset for the anchor joint, the head. For
  each detected person a cross-attention head (HPH) predicts $\MethodNumBones\times\MethodRotDims$ rotation values,
  \MethodNumPheno{} phenotypes and a depth; a constant correction turns four flipped bones back; the Anny layer builds
  the body and places it at the predicted distance. Shaded boxes are changed or added in this thesis; the dashed box
  marks the part that distillation replaces.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Backbone and detection.} A DINOv2 ViT-L with patch size \MethodPatchSize{} \cite{oquab2023dinov2} maps the
$\MethodImgSize\times\MethodImgSize$ input to $\MethodTokenGrid\times\MethodTokenGrid$ tokens of width \MethodWidthL;
AnnyOne renders ($\MethodRenderSize\times\MethodRenderSize$ pixels) are resized to this input. The backbone weights
come from NAVER's Anny checkpoint (Section~\ref{sec:method-schedule}). A two-layer MLP gives every token the
probability that a person's anchor joint lies in it; at inference a $3\times3$ non-maximum suppression and a threshold
keep the detected tokens. A second MLP predicts a 2D offset in token units, so the anchor pixel is
$(\text{token index}+0.5+\text{offset})\cdot\MethodPatchSize$. In every model of this thesis the anchor joint is the
head, Anny's bone at the base of the skull (\texttt{-{}-person\_center head}).

\paragraph{Human Perception Head (HPH).} Each detected token becomes one query, built from the token, a Fourier
embedding of its viewing ray through $\mathbf{K}$ (\MethodCamChannels{} channels), learned row and column embeddings,
and constant start values for pose, shape and camera. A transformer decoder with \MethodHPHDepth{} blocks,
\MethodHPHHeads{} heads of size \MethodHPHDimHead, width \MethodHPHDim{} and MLP width \MethodHPHMlp{} processes all
queries of an image together: in each block the people attend to each other and then to all image tokens. These are
the values in \texttt{model.py}; the draft of this thesis gave width 512 and 16 heads, which was wrong.

\paragraph{Anny head.} Three linear layers read the decoder's output token; they are what differs from the SMPL-X
version of Multi-HMR. \emph{Pose:} $\MethodNumBones\times\MethodRotDims$ values are added to the identity in the
continuous 6D representation \cite{zhou2019continuity} and orthonormalised by Gram--Schmidt into rotation matrices.
\emph{Shape:} \MethodNumPheno{} logits pass through a sigmoid, so every phenotype lies in $[0,1]$. \emph{Depth:} as in
Multi-HMR, a log-depth normalised by the focal length, $z=\exp(d\,f/f_n)$, with $f_n$ the focal length of a
\MethodFovN$^\circ$ field of view, clamped to \MethodMaxDist\,m. A fixed mask then forces \MethodHelperBones{} helper
bones (toes, breasts, face, subdivision bones) to the identity and keeps \MethodActiveBones{} predicted; the same mask
is applied to the ground truth in training. Right after Gram--Schmidt and before the mask, four bones are multiplied by
one constant rotation each (Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}).

\paragraph{Body-model layer and size.} The Anny model runs inside the network in PyTorch, with linear blend skinning,
and places the body with Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}; the 3D and 2D losses are back-propagated through it.
The network has \MethodParamsTotalL\,M parameters (heads \MethodParamsHeadsL\,M; NAVER's released Anny model has the
same backbone and a \MethodParamsHeadNaver\,M head) and needs $\num{\MethodGflopsL}$ GFLOPs per image with one person
(one multiply-add = two FLOPs). Table~\ref{tab:io} lists inputs and outputs.

\section{Losses in plain words}
\label{sec:method-losses}

Anny runs on each person's true parameters to give target vertices and joints; the anchor targets are built from one
joint (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L} = {}& \alpha_{\text{det}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}
  + \alpha_{\text{off}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{off}}
  + \alpha_{\text{rot}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}
  + \alpha_{\text{shape}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}} \\
  &+ \alpha_{\text{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}
  + \alpha_{\text{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{t}}
  + \alpha_{\text{j3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j3d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v3d}} \\
  &+ \mathbb{1}[e \ge e_{\text{2D}}]\left(\alpha_{\text{j2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j2d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v2d}}\right)
  + \lambda_{\text{kd}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd}} + \lambda_{\text{kd-out}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}},
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-loss}
\end{equation}
with $e$ the epoch; the distillation terms exist only for a student. Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights} says what each term
compares and gives its weights.

\input{tables/T02_loss_weights}

\paragraph{Detection, rotations, shape.} The detection term is the focal loss of CornerNet \cite{law2018cornernet}
in CenterNet's form; the log calls it \texttt{bce}, but it is not a binary cross-entropy. A second person whose anchor
falls into an occupied token is dropped from the targets. The Base recipe boosts neck, head and hand bones by
\MethodBoost{} and rescales all rotation weights to a mean of 1, so the boosted bones weigh $\times$\MethodBoostOn{}
and the rest $\times$\MethodBoostOff: the boost moves loss, it does not add any. The shape L1 is smallest at the median
of the plausible answers, so weak image cues pull predictions toward the training median, and the flat ends of the
sigmoid make extreme values slow to learn (Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\paragraph{Which terms see depth.} The 3D terms align both skeletons at the pelvis and carry no placement information.
Only distance and translation see placement directly, and the 2D terms indirectly, through the projection. Before the depth fix, distance and
translation made up about \MethodLossShareDepth\% of the total loss, against \MethodLossShareShape\% for shape.

\paragraph{Schedule of the 2D terms, masks, numerics.} The code default switches the 2D terms on at epoch
\MethodStartTwoDDefault; Final, Control and Student start from trained heads and use them from epoch
\MethodStartTwoD{} (the draft said otherwise). For 3DPW people some terms are masked (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). Training
runs in fp32 with TF32 products (mixed precision gave NaN values with the Anny layer); steps with NaN or Inf gradients
are skipped, \MethodFinalNaNSteps{} of $\num{\MethodFinalSteps}$ in Final. No weight was tuned in this thesis.

\section{Training schedule and model names}
\label{sec:method-schedule}

\paragraph{The Base lineage.} Training began from NAVER's public ViT-L checkpoint \texttt{multiHMR\_672\_L\_anny}.
Its head has a different structure, so only the backbone was transferred. Four stages followed: heads only (learning
rate \MethodStageOneLR{} per the training scripts, edited after the runs); the last \MethodUnfrozenBlocks{} of
\MethodBlocksL{} blocks unfrozen at \MethodFinalLR{} with the helper mask; the neck and hand boost; the shape weight
raised to \MethodWShape. Continuations used a learning rate ten times below the cold start, which on a partly trained
model had caused hundreds of NaN gradient events per epoch. Epoch \MethodBaseEpoch{} of the fourth stage is
\emph{Base}: synthetic images only, and the start of every fine-tune here.

\paragraph{Final and Control.} Final starts from Base and trains \MethodFinalEpochs{} epochs of
$\num{\MethodFinalIters}$ iterations at batch \MethodFinalBatch{} ($\num{\MethodFinalImages}$ images) at a constant
learning rate of \MethodFinalLR; the last \MethodUnfrozenBlocks{} backbone blocks and the heads train. Three changes
come on together: the depth fix with distance and translation weight \MethodWDist{} (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}),
the bone correction (Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}), and \MethodMixRatio\% real images per batch with the hand mask
(Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). The \MethodManifestImages{} images of the diagnostic manifest (Chapter~\ref{ch:data})
are never sampled. Training took $\num{\MethodFinalTrainSec}$\,s on one A100 (\MethodFinalImgPerSec{} images per
second). Control is identical but keeps the pelvis targets with weight \MethodWDistDefault{} and has no bone correction, so
Control against Final isolates the two fixes, and Base against Control shows the real images plus the restart cost.
Each fix was tested alone only in short pilots (Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\section{The depth anchor and its fix}
\label{sec:method-depth}

\paragraph{Anchor and targets.} The network places each person by the head: the detection token, the offset and the
predicted depth describe the head, and the mesh hangs from it (Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}). For AnnyOne
images, however, the training code built the translation, distance, token and offset targets at joint 0, the pelvis.
Every checkpoint before this thesis, Base included, was trained on these targets, so prediction and target described
two joints about half a body apart.

\paragraph{What the mismatch does.} The network must put its head joint where the pelvis target is. The offset head
tried to close the gap: in a one-batch debug print its offsets were $-\MethodOffsetLow$ to $-\MethodOffsetHigh$ tokens,
where a correct offset lies within $\pm\MethodOffsetLegal$. A mesh hanging from a head placed at the pelvis is drawn too
low. The 2D terms penalise this misfit, and the network can shrink it by moving the body away, where it is drawn
smaller; the pelvis-aligned 3D terms cannot object and the direct depth terms were weak. The mismatch is certain; the
mechanism is likely but was not isolated (Figure~\ref{fig:anchor}).
On the AnnyOne holdout Base predicts the pelvis depth as $\MethodBaseSlope\times\text{true}+\MethodBaseIntercept$\,m:
all \MethodHoldoutPeople{} matched people are too far (pelvis error \MethodBaseRootErr\,m), drawn at \MethodBaseSize{}
of their size although their 3D height is right (\MethodBaseHeightPred{} against \MethodBaseHeightTrue\,m). PVE aligns
the pelvises and the real-photo demos place bodies from their boxes, so neither showed it
(Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F03_anchor}
  \caption{The depth-anchor bug. (a) The model places each body by its head; (b) the training targets were built at
  the pelvis; (c) the 2D terms reconcile the two by moving the body away, where the same 3D body is drawn
  smaller. Bottom: the fitted line of predicted against true pelvis depth for the \MethodHoldoutPeople{}
  matched people of the AnnyOne holdout (setting V1), Base and Final. Schematic; the measured scatter is in
  Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}.}
  \label{fig:anchor}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The fix.} A single training switch builds all four anchor targets at the joint the model anchors (in
the code, the flag \texttt{-{}-gt\_center\_joint} set to \texttt{person\_center}); the visibility filter and the one-person-per-token rule move with them.
The network does not change. I also raised the distance and translation weights from \MethodWDistDefault{} to
\MethodWDist, because they were the only direct depth terms. In the 10-epoch pilots the switch alone reached a pelvis
error of \MethodPilotDistOneRoot\,m, and the switch with weight \MethodWDist{} reached \MethodPilotDistFiveRoot\,m; weight
\MethodWDistPilotMax{} was only tried for \MethodShortEpochs{} epochs. Final uses the switch with weight
\MethodWDist; Control keeps the pelvis targets with weight \MethodWDistDefault. The fix moved the offset and the drawn
size, not the slope: Final's line is $\MethodFinalSlope\times\text{true}+\MethodFinalIntercept$\,m
(Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).

\section{The bone correction}
\label{sec:method-bonefix}

\paragraph{What it corrects.} A per-bone dump on the holdout (Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}) showed two bones of the
neck chain turned half a turn in all \MethodFlippedCkpts{} checkpoints of the Base lineage: neck02 by
\MethodBaseNeckTwo$^\circ$ and the head bone by \MethodBaseHead$^\circ$ (median error of the parent-relative rotation).
The half-turns cancel, so the head points the right way (\MethodBaseGlobalHead$^\circ$ error) and PVE never showed the
flip, but the neck skin between them is twisted to \MethodBaseGirth{} of its true girth (Figure~\ref{fig:neck-chain}).


\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F04_neck_chain}
  \caption{The neck chain neck01--neck02--neck03--head. Top: in Base, neck02 and the head bone are each turned about
  half a turn; the turns cancel, so the head points right, but the skin between them is twisted to \MethodBaseGirth{}
  of its true girth. Bottom: one constant rotation per bone turns both back. Girth on the AnnyOne holdout, setting V1.}
  \label{fig:neck-chain}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{How it works.} The parent-relative rotation of each flipped bone $b$ is multiplied on the right by a
constant, $\mathbf{R}_b\leftarrow\mathbf{R}_b\mathbf{C}_b$. If the prediction is $\hat{\mathbf{R}}_b\mathbf{F}_b$ for
the true rotation $\hat{\mathbf{R}}_b$ and a constant offset $\mathbf{F}_b$, then $\mathbf{C}_b=\mathbf{F}_b^{\top}$
restores the truth. I estimate $\mathbf{C}_b$ as the rotation closest to the mean of
$\mathbf{R}_{b,k}^{\top}\hat{\mathbf{R}}_{b,k}$ over the people $k$ of \MethodBoneFixImages{} random training images,
never the holdout; the script selects every bone whose median error exceeds \MethodBoneFixThr$^\circ$ with a constant
offset. For Base it selected neck02 (\MethodCNeckTwo$^\circ$), head (\MethodCHead$^\circ$), metacarpal1.L, the base of
the left index finger (\MethodCMetacarpal$^\circ$), and finger4-1.R, a bone of the right ring finger
(\MethodCFinger$^\circ$). The product comes before the helper mask, the kinematics and the losses, so training, evaluation and demos all see
corrected rotations. The constants are saved with the checkpoint and reapplied on loading; the correction adds no
parameters. A constant suffices because the HPH runs once from constant start values and never sees its own output.

\paragraph{Why a loss cannot do it.} The L1 distance over matrix entries has a barrier along a twist: at 135$^\circ$
it is larger than at 180$^\circ$ (\MethodLOneHalfTurn{} against \MethodLOneFullTurn), so a partial turn back raises
the loss, and neck02 and head would have to turn back together. A pilot with a geodesic loss, which has no barrier,
moved neck02 only from \MethodBaseNeckTwo$^\circ$ to \MethodGeodesicAfter$^\circ$, and the neck boost cannot undo a
half-turn either.

\paragraph{Limits.} With the correction and no training the global head direction gets worse
(\MethodBaseGlobalHead$^\circ$ to \MethodZeroTrainGlobalHead$^\circ$): the head bone's offset varies between people, so
one constant per bone leaves the link between the two bones slightly wrong. Final therefore fine-tunes with the
correction in place; a two-sided correction was not implemented. The origin of the flip is unknown: the singular 6D start value would
give a tilt, which fits metacarpal1.L but not neck02's twist, and another ViT-L model with fresh heads flipped arm and
hand bones instead.

\section{Training with real images}
\label{sec:method-real}

\paragraph{The mix.} A weighted sampler draws on average \MethodMixRatio\% of each batch from 3DPW-train converted to
Anny (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}; corrected labels, $\num{\MethodTrainThreeDPWImages}$ images, $\num{\MethodTrainThreeDPWPeople}$
people) and \MethodSynthRatio\% from AnnyOne. With about \MethodPeoplePerImgThreeDPW{} labelled people per 3DPW image against
\MethodPeoplePerImgAnnyOne{} in AnnyOne, roughly \MethodThreeDPWPeopleShare\% of the supervised people are real (an
estimate, not a count). After each epoch \MethodValThreeDPWN{}
3DPW-validation images are evaluated; the test split is never used for training. The mix answers requirement 2a.

\paragraph{The hand mask.} Every person carries a flag, 1 for AnnyOne and 0 for 3DPW. The converter fits only the root
and the bones that carry a mapped SMPL joint, so every finger, thumb and metacarpal bone of a 3DPW label is at rest.
With \texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_hands 1} the rotation and global-rotation terms skip these \MethodHandMaskBones{} bones for
3DPW people, and the fingers are learned from AnnyOne only. The hand mask removes the rotation terms only; the mesh terms
still carry the rest-pose hands.

\paragraph{The shape mask, tested.} The converter fits height and proportions, takes gender from the 3DPW metadata,
and leaves \MethodShapeMaskCount{} phenotypes at constants: age \MethodConstAge, and muscle, weight, cup size, firmness
and the three ethnicity values at \MethodConstOther. With \texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_shape 1} the shape term ignores these
constants for 3DPW people; gender, height and proportions stay supervised. The mesh terms still carry the neutral body,
which is also what the 3DPW test PVE compares against. The partial variant (\texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_shape\_names}) masks only the \MethodShapeMaskPartial{} values at
\MethodConstOther{} and keeps age supervised. Final-masked uses the full mask; Final-masked-7, with the partial one, had
not finished when this chapter was written. I kept the unmasked model as Final and teacher: the masked one regained
body variety but lost the gender gain and part of the child gain on real photos (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}).

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:method-kd}

A frozen teacher supervises a smaller student on the same image, on top of the task loss
(Figure~\ref{fig:distillation}). Three mechanisms carry over from the first students to the final one.

\begin{figure}[!tbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F05_distillation}
  \caption{Distillation of the Student. The frozen ViT-L teacher (Final, backbone in bf16) and the ViT-B student see
  the same image. A projector maps the student's tokens from width \MethodWidthB{} to \MethodWidthL, and heads copied
  from the teacher read them. The feature loss compares the projected tokens with the teacher's ($T=\MethodKDTemp$);
  the output loss compares rotations, phenotypes and depth of the same people. Shaded boxes train.}
  \label{fig:distillation}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Feature distillation.} With teacher tokens $\mathbf{z}^{t}_n$ and the tokens $\mathbf{z}^{s}_n$ that the
student's heads see, both of width $C_t$,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd}} = T^2\cdot \frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}
  \mathrm{KL}\!\left(\sigma\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{z}^{t}_n}{T}\right)
  \,\Big\|\, \sigma\!\left(\tfrac{\mathbf{z}^{s}_n}{T}\right)\right),
\label{eq:method-kd}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma$ is the softmax over the channels of one token, $T$ the temperature \cite{hinton2015distilling}, and
$T^2$ keeps the gradient size comparable across temperatures. The loss sits between backbone and heads, so the student
copies the teacher's representation, not only its predictions, as in FitNets \cite{romero2015fitnets}.

\paragraph{Head transfer and output distillation.} Teacher and student are the same model class, so the student copies
every head tensor whose name and shape match. Both run on the true person locations during training, so person $i$ of
one is person $i$ of the other without matching, and
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}} = \alpha_{\text{rot}}\left\|\mathbf{R}^{s}-\mathbf{R}^{t}\right\|_1
 + \alpha_{\text{shape}}\left\|\boldsymbol{\phi}^{s}-\boldsymbol{\phi}^{t}\right\|_1
 + \alpha_{\text{dist}}\left|d^{s}-d^{t}\right|
\label{eq:method-kdout}
\end{equation}
pulls the student's outputs toward the teacher's detached outputs, with the task weights of
Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}.

\paragraph{Warm-up freeze and projector-fed heads.} Copied heads that read through random input layers give confident
wrong outputs, and their gradients damage the copied weights before the input layers adapt; the copied tensors
therefore stay frozen for the first epochs. The final design removes the random input layers: a linear projector maps
the student's tokens to the teacher's width once, every head is built at that width (\texttt{-{}-head\_dim}), all head
tensors transfer, and Equation~\eqref{eq:method-kd} trains the projector toward exactly the input the heads expect. The
deployed student is backbone, projector and heads. TF32 products and the teacher's backbone in bfloat16 (tokens cast back
to fp32) cut a ViT-S student's step from \MethodItTimeBefore{} to \MethodItTimeAfter\,s on an A100, with unchanged
evaluation numbers.

\subsection{History: the ViT-S students}
\label{sec:method-kd-history}

The first answer to the size requirement was a line of ViT-S students, Student-S runs S1 to S8 (v1 to v8 in the
code), trained before both fixes and the label correction; their teacher Base reached \MethodTeacherVzeroPVE\,mm
holdout PVE in setting V0. Feature distillation alone (S1, S2) left the student at \MethodStudentSOnePVE\,mm, because
nothing supervised its random heads. Copied heads (S3) kept their \MethodHeadTensorsWidth{} width-dependent input
tensors random and needed about \MethodStudentSWarmup{} epochs to recover. A backbone trained for mesh recovery (S4)
gave the largest gain (\MethodStudentSInitBefore{} to \MethodStudentSInitAfter\,mm), and projector-fed heads (S6) and
longer training at twice the batch (S6c) reached \MethodStudentSSixcPVE\,mm, still improving: the student was limited
by the images it had seen, not by its \MethodStudentSParams\,M parameters. Real images (S7, S8) did not lower the
holdout error. These numbers are V0 and their 3DPW numbers used the retired labels; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} keeps the
tables as history.

\subsection{The ViT-B student of Final}
\label{sec:method-student}

\paragraph{Recipe.} The Student follows the S6 design with a ViT-B backbone and the corrected training of this chapter
(Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}). Its backbone starts from NAVER's Multi-HMR ViT-B, trained for mesh recovery, and
trains fully; the copied heads stay frozen for \MethodStudentFreeze{} epochs; the targets are exactly the teacher's.
The job script's header mentions colour jitter, but no code in the repository reads such a setting, so I claim no
augmentation. The copied heads output the half-turn that the teacher's bone correction undoes, so the student trains
with that correction; afterwards its own is estimated on \MethodBoneFixImages{} training images
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}) and used in every evaluation. Its heads keep the teacher's width and are a larger
share of the student.

\paragraph{The 95\% rule.} Requirement 1b asks for a smaller model within 95\% of the large one. I read it as: student
error at most the teacher's error divided by \MethodRuleFactor, for PVE and MPJPE on both the AnnyOne holdout
(setting V1) and the 3DPW test set, with fewer parameters, fewer FLOPs and a faster forward pass. Against Final the
bars are \MethodBarHoldout\,mm holdout PVE and \MethodBarThreeDPW\,mm 3DPW test PVE; the job re-measures the teacher
and computes the bars from those numbers. Against Multi-HMR-Anny they would be \MethodBarNaverHoldout{} and
\MethodBarNaverThreeDPW\,mm. NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne release, which includes the \MethodHoldoutN{}
images we hold out, so its \MethodNaverHoldoutPVE\,mm is most likely an in-sample number; ours are held out. A student
of a \MethodTeacherHoldoutPVE\,mm teacher cannot be expected to reach \MethodBarNaverHoldout\,mm on AnnyOne, while the
3DPW bar of \MethodBarNaverThreeDPW\,mm lies above the teacher's own error. Which reference counts is the
supervisor's decision; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the student against both.

\paragraph{AnnyOne holdout.} The last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (indices $\num{\MethodHoldoutFirst}$ to
$\num{\MethodHoldoutLast}$), never trained on and without children (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}). The headline setting V1
masks the helper bones, as the demos do; V0 does not and reads \MethodVzeroGap\,mm higher (the draft's headline was
V0). A trace script adds placement metrics from the model's own depth and every bone's rotation error.

\paragraph{3DPW test.} All $\num{\MethodTestImages}$ test images ($\num{\MethodTestPeople}$ people) of the corrected
labels, with the same script for every model, in setting V0. MPJPE averages all \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints and the
conversion itself errs by about \MethodLabelFloor\,mm (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}), so these numbers are not comparable with
published 3DPW numbers. 3DPW labels only the actors; a correct detection of a bystander counts against precision.

\paragraph{Relative Human and demo images.} Relative Human gives real photos with an age group, a gender and a box per
person, but no 3D body. Its validation split (\MethodRHImages{} images, $\num{\MethodRHPeople}$ people) serves for evaluation
only: boxes from the projected vertices, one-to-one matching at IoU $\ge$ \MethodRHIoU, child-shaped meaning a
predicted age below \MethodChildThr, detected people only. The \MethodDemoPhotos{} demo photos and
\MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames are rendered with the fingers at rest (\texttt{-{}-relax\_hands 1}) and each body
placed from its detection box, which hides depth errors; they are judged by eye, with no metric.

\paragraph{One run, no seed.} The training code sets no random seed, and every model was trained once. Two identical
\MethodShortEpochs-epoch runs differed by \MethodNoisePVE\,mm PVE, \MethodNoiseHead$^\circ$ global head direction and
\MethodNoiseRecallPair{} recall points, so one run resolves about \MethodResolvePVE\,mm, \MethodResolveHead$^\circ$ and
\MethodNoiseRecall{} recall points; smaller gaps are within run-to-run variation. Restarting a fine-tune of Base with
nothing changed already costs about \MethodRestartCost\,mm PVE, so every fine-tune is compared with its own control,
never with Base alone. The pelvis error has no measured noise floor, and no bootstrap intervals were computed.

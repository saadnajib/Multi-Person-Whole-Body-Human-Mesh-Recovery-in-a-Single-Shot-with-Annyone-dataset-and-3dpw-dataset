\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:method}

\section{System overview}
\label{sec:method-overview}

The deployed model is a ViT-B student distilled from a ViT-L teacher. The teacher is trained on AnnyOne and on 3DPW
training images whose SMPL labels the converter of Chapter~\ref{ch:data} turns into Anny labels. The student is trained
on the same labels and on feature and output targets of the frozen teacher, which also provides the initial weights of
the student's heads. Only the student runs at deployment (Figure~\ref{fig:distillation}).

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\input{figures/tikz/F05_distillation}
\caption[Teacher--student training and the deployed model]{Teacher--student training (schematic). Student and frozen teacher receive the same
image; the student is trained with the supervised and two distillation losses. At inference only the student,
including its projector, runs.}
\label{fig:distillation}
\end{figure}

The student's encoder $f_B$ maps an image $I$ to $N_p$ tokens $\mathbf Z^s=f_B(I)$ of $C_s=\MethodWidthB$
channels, and a trainable linear projector maps them to the teacher's width:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\mathbf Z}^s=\mathbf Z^s\mathbf W^\top+\mathbf b,\qquad
 \mathbf W\in\mathbb R^{C_t\times C_s},\quad C_t=\MethodWidthL .
\label{eq:student-projection}
\end{equation}
The projector adds no image information; the savings come mainly from the smaller encoder.

\section{Multi-HMR for Anny}
\label{sec:method-arch}

\subsection{The task}
\label{sec:method-task}

The input is one RGB image with intrinsics $\mathbf{K}$ showing an unknown number of people. For each person $i$ the
network predicts the rotations
$\mathbf{R}_i\in SO(3)^{\MethodNumBones}$ of Anny's bones, the phenotypes $\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\in[0,1]^{\MethodNumPheno}$,
and the image position $(u_i,v_i)$ and depth $z_i$ of one anchor joint $a$, the head. The Anny body model
$M$~\cite{bregier2025anny} maps $(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i)$ to vertices $M_V$ and joints, and the mesh is
translated so that its anchor joint $M_{J,a}$ lies at the back-projected anchor pixel:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{V}_i=M_V(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) - M_{J,a}(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i)+\mathbf{t}_i,
 \qquad
 \mathbf{t}_i=z_i\,\mathbf{K}^{-1}\,(u_i, v_i, 1)^{\top}.
\label{eq:method-placement}
\end{equation}
\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-architecture}

The teacher follows Multi-HMR~\cite{baradel2024multihmr} with outputs for Anny (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). A
DINOv2 ViT-L~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} taken from the released baseline encodes the image into tokens; one MLP scores each
token for the head anchor, a second predicts the anchor's offset inside the token. Each detected token becomes a query of the Human Perception Head (HPH, Section~\ref{sec:bg-hmr}), which
attends to the other people and to all image tokens.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F01_architecture}
  \caption[Teacher decoding architecture]{Teacher architecture (schematic). Shaded boxes are new in this thesis; the
  dashed box marks the part that distillation replaces.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

In the Anny head, which replaces the SMPL-X head, each rotation is predicted as \MethodRotDims{} values and
orthogonalised by Gram--Schmidt~\cite{zhou2019continuity}; a sigmoid bounds the phenotypes to $[0,1]$, and the depth
is Multi-HMR's focal-normalised log-distance. Four bones are then multiplied by one constant rotation each
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}). During training a fixed mask sets \MethodHelperBones{} helper bones (toes, breasts, face and
subdivision bones) to the identity in prediction and label, so only \MethodActiveBones{} bones are supervised; the
network still outputs helper-bone rotations, which are unsupervised and are set to rest pose at evaluation only in the
holdout protocol (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}). The Anny layer, with the same
full rig as the target builder, is part of the network, so the 3D and 2D losses are computed on the placed mesh and
their gradients propagate through it. The student keeps everything after the encoder, replaces the encoder by ViT-B and adds the projector
(Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}).

\subsection{Outputs at inference}
\label{sec:method-inference}

At inference the model receives only the image and $\mathbf K$. When the calibration is unknown, as for the
demo images and the video, $\mathbf K$ is built from an assumed field of view; an incorrect assumption changes
the recovered depth.

\section{Supervised training objective}
\label{sec:method-losses}

Target vertices and joints are decoded from each person's labelled parameters; the anchor targets refer to the head
joint (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal{L}={}&\alpha_{\text{det}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}+\alpha_{\text{off}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{off}}
 +\alpha_{\text{rot}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}+\alpha_{\text{shape}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}}\\
 &+\alpha_{\text{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}+\alpha_{\text{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{t}}
 +\alpha_{\text{j3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j3d}}+\alpha_{\text{v3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v3d}}\\
 &+\mathbb{1}[e\ge e_{\text{2D}}]\left(\alpha_{\text{j2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j2d}}
 +\alpha_{\text{v2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v2d}}\right)
 +\lambda_{\text{kd}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd}}+\lambda_{\text{kd-out}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}},
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-loss}
\end{equation}
with $e$ the epoch, $e_{\text{2D}}$ the first epoch of the 2D terms and weights $\alpha$ and $\lambda$; the two
distillation terms exist only for the student. Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights} lists the weights, which were not tuned in
this thesis.

\subsection{Detection and anchor localisation}

Let $s_q$ be the detection probability at token $q$, $y_q\in\{0,1\}$ its target and $N_+=\sum_q y_q$. The detection
term is the focal loss of CornerNet~\cite{law2018cornernet}:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{det}}=
 -\frac{1}{\max(1,N_+)}\sum_q
\begin{cases}
 (1-s_q)^2\log(s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=1,\\
 s_q^2\log(1-s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=0 .
\end{cases}
\label{eq:loss-detection}
\end{equation}
The sum runs over all tokens of the batch; positive tokens contain a head anchor, and the squared factors down-weight
well-classified tokens. The term penalises missed people and background detections. For
$N_h$ supervised people with sub-token offsets $\mathbf o_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i
 \|\widehat{\mathbf o}_i-\mathbf o_i\|_1
\label{eq:loss-offset}
\end{equation}
penalises the localisation error of the anchor within its token.

\subsection{Pose, shape and placement}

With rotation matrices $\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}$, $\mathbf R_{ib}$ of bone $b$ of person $i$, pose and shape use L1
losses:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_b
 m_{ib}w_b\|\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}-\mathbf R_{ib}\|_1,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_k
 |\widehat{\phi}_{ik}-\phi_{ik}|.
\label{eq:loss-parameters}
\end{equation}
Here $m_{ib}$ is 0 for unlabelled 3DPW hand bones (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}), $w_b$ the normalised bone weight. The teacher supervises all phenotypes, including those the converter leaves constant; the shape-mask variants
(Section~\ref{sec:method-real}) test this choice. Both terms sum over a person's bones (or phenotypes) before
averaging over people, so the scale of each term, and hence the meaning of its weight, depends on this order. An L1
loss is minimised by the conditional median, so weak image cues pull the predicted shape towards the training median
(Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

For predicted and target log-depth $d_i$ and camera-space anchor translation $\mathbf t_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{dist}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i|\widehat d_i-d_i|,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm t}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\|\widehat{\mathbf t}_i-\mathbf t_i\|_1 .
\label{eq:loss-placement}
\end{equation}
Only these terms supervise the absolute placement directly; the 2D terms constrain it indirectly.

\subsection{Geometric and reprojection supervision}

For joints or vertices $\mathbf X_i$, each body's own pelvis is subtracted first,
$\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}=\mathbf X_{i\ell}-\mathbf J_{i,\mathrm{root}}$. With $K_X$ points and weights $w_\ell$
($w_\ell=1$ for vertices, the configured joint weights for joints),
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X3D}=\frac{1}{N_hK_X}\sum_i\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_X}
 w_\ell\|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf X}}_{i\ell}-\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}\|_1 .
\label{eq:loss-geometry}
\end{equation}
This term measures pose and geometry independently of the global position and cannot replace
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss-placement}. The 2D terms project the uncentred camera-space points:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X2D}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_X|}
 \sum_{(i,\ell)\in\Omega_X}
 \|\pi_{\mathbf K}(\widehat{\mathbf X}_{i\ell})-\pi_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf X_{i\ell})\|_1 ,
\label{eq:loss-reprojection}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\mathbf K}$ is the perspective projection and $\Omega_X$ the target points inside the image; the term
penalises the pixel misalignment of the projected mesh. A term that evaluates to NaN or infinity is skipped.

Unlike these L1 losses, PVE (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}) averages Euclidean distances. Training uses 32-bit floats
with TF32 matrix products, because mixed precision produced NaN values in the Anny layer; steps with NaN or Inf gradients are skipped (\MethodFinalNaNSteps{} of
$\num{\MethodFinalSteps}$ for the teacher).

\section{Training with real images}
\label{sec:method-real}

A weighted sampler draws on average \MethodMixRatio\% of each batch from converted 3DPW-train (corrected labels,
$\num{\MethodTrainThreeDPWImages}$ images) and \MethodSynthRatio\% from AnnyOne (share of real people:
Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). The 3DPW test split is never used
for training.

\paragraph{Hand mask.} Fingers, thumbs and metacarpals of every converted 3DPW label are at rest
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). In all models trained on 3DPW, the rotation term skips these
\MethodHandMaskBones{} bones for 3DPW people; finger rotations are supervised by AnnyOne only. The mesh terms still contain the rest-pose hands, and the output distillation uses the teacher's hand
predictions.

\paragraph{Shape mask.} The converter leaves \MethodShapeMaskCount{} phenotypes constant, among them age
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). The teacher with full shape mask
excludes all of them from the shape term for 3DPW people, the teacher with partial shape mask the
\MethodShapeMaskPartial{} other than age; the mesh terms still contain the neutral body. The mask is off in all other
models. The model without shape mask was retained as the teacher because both masked variants lost, on real photographs, the improvement in
predicted gender that the teacher shows over the initial model (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\section{Teacher training and its two corrections}
\label{sec:method-schedule}

The initial model combines the released baseline's ViT-L backbone with Anny heads trained on AnnyOne and is the starting point of every
fine-tune (earlier stages: Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}). The teacher is fine-tuned from it for \MethodFinalEpochs{} epochs of $\num{\MethodFinalIters}$ iterations at batch size
\MethodFinalBatch{} with a constant learning rate of \MethodFinalLR; only the last \MethodUnfrozenBlocks{} backbone
blocks and the heads are trained. Three changes are introduced together: head-anchor targets with distance and
translation weight \MethodWDist{} (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}), the bone correction
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}), and \MethodMixRatio\% real images per batch with the hand mask. The
\MethodManifestImages{} diagnostic-manifest images (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}) are never sampled; training took
$\num{\MethodFinalTrainSec}$\,s on one A100. The teacher without corrections (uncorrected teacher) differs only by pelvis targets with
weight \MethodWDistDefault{} and no bone correction. Its difference from the teacher is therefore due
to the two corrections, and its difference from the initial model to the real images together with the cost of
restarting training (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}); each correction was tested in isolation only in short pilots (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\subsection{Consistent head-anchor supervision}
\label{sec:method-depth}

The body is placed by its head joint (Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}), so the detection, offset, depth and
translation targets must refer to that joint. For the teacher, the target builder decodes the labelled body and takes
the head's camera-space position and projection, also used for visibility checks and one-person-per-token assignment. The initial model and the uncorrected teacher build these targets at the pelvis, so targets and decoder disagree
about the body's position, which the pelvis-centred losses cannot detect (Figure~\ref{fig:anchor}; measurements in
Section~\ref{sec:diag-depth}). The anchor and the placement weight (\MethodWDist{} against \MethodWDistDefault) change
together.

\subsection{Fixed output rotation correction}
\label{sec:method-bonefix}

In the initial model, neck02 and the head bone are each rotated by approximately $180^\circ$
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}); the origin of this flip was not identified. It is corrected by one constant rotation
per affected bone, applied to the pose output before decoding:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf R^{\mathrm{corr}}_{ib}=\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}\mathbf C_b,\qquad
 \mathbf C_b=\operatorname{Proj}_{SO(3)}
 \left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_i
 \widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{target}}_{ib}\right),
\label{eq:bone-calibration}
\end{equation}
with $\operatorname{Proj}_{SO(3)}$ the nearest rotation to the mean residual over $N_{\mathrm{cal}}$ people, estimated on
\MethodBoneFixImages{} random training images, never on evaluation images, and only for bones whose mean offset
exceeds \MethodBoneFixThr$^\circ$: for the teacher, neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R
(Figure~\ref{fig:neck-chain}). It acts before helper masking, decoding and the loss and has no parameters. Because a constant cannot remove an error that varies per image, the teacher was fine-tuned with the
correction in place (student: Section~\ref{sec:method-student}).

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:method-kd}

\paragraph{Feature distillation.} For image $u$ and token $n$, the teacher token and the projected student token are
converted into distributions over the $C_t$ channels, $p^t_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\mathbf z^t_{un}/T)$ and
$p^s_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\widetilde{\mathbf z}^s_{un}/T)$. With batch size $B$, the loss is their
Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence, summed over tokens and averaged over images:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{kd}}=
 \frac{T^2}{B}\sum_{u=1}^{B}\sum_{n=1}^{N_p}\sum_{c=1}^{C_t}
 p^t_{unc}\log\frac{p^t_{unc}}{p^s_{unc}} .
\label{eq:method-kd}
\end{equation}
A higher $T$ flattens both distributions, so small channel responses contribute (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}); $T^2$ keeps
the gradient magnitude comparable across temperatures~\cite{hinton2015distilling}. Following FitNets~\cite{romero2015fitnets}, the student side is the projected
tokens that the heads read; the teacher side is detached.

\paragraph{Head transfer and output distillation.} The student is initialised with a copy of every teacher head tensor
whose name and shape match. Both models are queried at the true person locations, so person $i$ corresponds in both,
and
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\Big[
 &\alpha_{\text{rot}}\sum_b\|\mathbf R^s_{ib}-\mathbf R^t_{ib}\|_1\\
 &+\alpha_{\text{shape}}\sum_k|\phi^s_{ik}-\phi^t_{ik}|
 +\alpha_{\text{dist}}|d^s_i-d^t_i|\Big]
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-kdout}
\end{equation}
penalises the L1 deviation of the student's outputs from the detached teacher's. It uses no temperature, and its
rotation term has neither the per-bone boost nor the hand mask, because the teacher predicts hands where a converted
label has none. The supervised terms are retained because the teacher can be wrong. The weights are
$\lambda_{\mathrm{kd}}=\MethodKDLambda$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{kd-out}}=\MethodKDOutLambda$.

\paragraph{Warm-up freeze.} Initially the student's features differ from the teacher's, and the copied heads produce
confident but wrong outputs whose gradients can corrupt the copied weights. The heads are therefore frozen for the first
\MethodStudentFreeze{} epochs while backbone and projector adapt; afterwards all parameters are trained. Every head is built at the teacher's width, so all head tensors transfer (earlier designs:
Section~\ref{sec:method-kd-history}).

\paragraph{Choice of temperature and schedule.} Before training, $T=\MethodKDTemp$ and the distillation weights were
taken over from the earlier ViT-S distillation training configuration, and the \MethodStudentEpochs-epoch schedule was fixed by the
compute budget; neither was tuned on test data (selection protocol: Section~\ref{sec:res-selection}).
Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature} varies $T$ over \KDSweepArms{} values up to \KDSweepMaxT{} as a sensitivity
analysis; the ablation designed to isolate the contribution of the distillation terms
(Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-ablation}) had not completed at submission.

\section{The ViT-B student}
\label{sec:method-student}

The student's ViT-B backbone (Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}) is initialised from NAVER's Multi-HMR ViT-B, already
trained for mesh recovery, and fully trained. The supervised targets are the teacher's, and no data augmentation is applied (a colour-jitter option in the configuration is not read by the code). The copied heads contain the half-turn
that the teacher's correction undoes (Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}), so the student trains with that correction;
its own correction, estimated after training on \MethodBoneFixImages{} training images, is used in every evaluation and
in the video pipeline. The reported student is the \MethodStudentEpochs-epoch run; a \StudentSixtyEpochs-epoch run of
the same recipe (learning rate halved every \LossLRHalfSixty{} epochs, the same two-fifths rule) serves only the
training-length analysis (Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}).

retention in advance and does not imply that it is met. Read as

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method-eval}

A greedy 2D matcher pairs each labelled person with at most one detection, and errors are means over matched people
(Table~\ref{tab:metrics}).

\input{tables/T07_metrics}

For one person with predicted and target vertices $\hat{\mathbf v}_k,\mathbf v_k$ ($N_V$ of them), joints
$\hat{\mathbf J}_j,\mathbf J_j$ ($J=\MethodNumBones$) and pelvis joints $\hat{\mathbf p},\mathbf p$,
\begin{align}
 \mathrm{PVE} &= \tfrac{1}{N_V}\textstyle\sum_{k}\big\|(\hat{\mathbf v}_k-\hat{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf v_k-\mathbf p)\big\|_2 ,
\label{eq:metric-pve}\\
 \mathrm{PA\text{-}PVE} &= \tfrac{1}{N_V}\textstyle\sum_{k}\big\|s^\ast\mathbf R^\ast\hat{\mathbf v}_k+\mathbf t^\ast-\mathbf v_k\big\|_2 ,
\label{eq:metric-papve}\\
 \mathrm{MPJPE} &= \tfrac{1}{J}\textstyle\sum_{j}\big\|(\hat{\mathbf J}_j-\hat{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf J_j-\mathbf p)\big\|_2 ,
\label{eq:metric-mpjpe}\\
 \mathrm{Recall} &= \mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}) ,
\label{eq:metric-recall}
\end{align}
with $s^\ast,\mathbf R^\ast,\mathbf t^\ast$ the least-squares (Procrustes) similarity fit of prediction to target,
and TP and FN the matched and missed labelled people. PVE, after pelvis alignment, includes differences in body size;
PA-PVE is the residual after the similarity fit. Cost is
reported as parameters, GFLOPs (one multiply-accumulate counted as two operations) and the median forward time on one
GPU at batch size 1 with one person.

\paragraph{AnnyOne holdout.} The last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (indices $\num{\MethodHoldoutFirst}$ to
$\num{\MethodHoldoutLast}$) are excluded from all training in this thesis; its
limitations: Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}. Before decoding, the helper bones (face, toes, breasts and
subdivision bones) are set to rest pose in prediction and target, as in the qualitative results, so that the error
covers the \MethodActiveBones{} supervised bones; without this step PVE is \MethodVzeroGap\,mm higher on the same
people. A trace script adds placement and per-bone metrics.

\paragraph{3DPW test.} All $\num{\MethodTestImages}$ test images ($\num{\MethodTestPeople}$ people) are evaluated
against the corrected labels with one script for every model, without the helper-bone step. With converted targets and
MPJPE over all \MethodNumBones{} Anny joints, these numbers are not comparable with published 3DPW results
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

\paragraph{Relative Human and demonstration images.} The Relative Human validation split (\MethodRHImages{} images,
$\num{\MethodRHPeople}$ people) is used for evaluation only, with boxes from the projected vertices and matching at
IoU $\ge$ \MethodRHIoU; a prediction counts as child-shaped when its predicted age is below \MethodChildThr. The
\MethodDemoPhotos{} demonstration photos and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames place each body from its detection
box, which hides depth errors, and are assessed visually.

\paragraph{Run-to-run variation.}
\label{sec:method-variation}
Each model is a single training run without a fixed seed; no confidence intervals were computed. Two identical
short training runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE and \NoiseHeadDeg$^\circ$ head direction
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}). The two students trained with the same setting ($T=\MethodKDTemp$; the reported run
and the repeat within the temperature sweep) differ by \KDNoiseHold\,mm PVE on the AnnyOne holdout and \KDNoiseTP\,mm
on 3DPW. Differences of a few millimetres between single runs are therefore not interpreted.

\section{Applying the student to video}
\label{sec:method-video}

The network is applied to a video frame by frame. Each selected frame is letterboxed (resized and padded without
distortion), with an assumed field of view of \VideoFov$^\circ$ along the longer side for every
model. The raw outputs keep the model's camera-space placement. A geometric
tracker follows one person by box overlap and normalised centre distance; a frame without a prediction is left empty,
and no frame-to-frame difference is computed across a track break. It records pelvis-relative joint velocity and
acceleration ($\mathbf q_{tj}=\mathbf J_{tj}-\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{root}}$, frame spacing $\Delta t$)
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf v_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-\mathbf q_{t-1,j}}{\Delta t},\qquad
 \mathbf a_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-2\mathbf q_{t-1,j}+\mathbf q_{t-2,j}}{\Delta t^2}
\label{eq:video-motion}
\end{equation}
and the phenotype spread and change; these describe predicted motion, not error, and also grow with genuine or camera
motion.

Input, student and released baseline are shown side by side. Playback FPS is the source rate divided by the frame
stride. Inference FPS is measured at batch size 1 with a GPU-synchronised clock around the
model call:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{FPS}_{\mathrm{infer}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{timed}}}
 {\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}s_t},\qquad
 s_t=\text{forward seconds of frame } t ,
\label{eq:video-fps}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ holds the $N_{\mathrm{timed}}$ frames after a warm-up of \VideoWarmupFrames{} frames (which remain
in the videos and measurements). The time $s_t$ includes detection and Anny decoding and excludes
preprocessing, transfers outside the model, rendering (timed separately), the offline landmark pass and video decoding
and encoding; $\mathrm{FPS}_{\mathrm{infer}}$ is therefore an inference speed, not an end-to-end speed. Each reported
speed states device, precision, resolution and number of people; the values are in Section~\ref{sec:res-video}.

\subsection{Temporal filtering and landmark alignment}
\label{sec:method-temporal}

Independent per-frame predictions vary between frames, and the raw student mesh sometimes extends beyond the person
(Section~\ref{sec:res-video}). Two inference-time steps, neither a learned temporal model, address
this without retraining: an exponential moving average over the track, and a landmark-based correction of position and
depth followed by depth smoothing.

\paragraph{Temporal filter.} The filter is causal. For each smoothed quantity it keeps a state $\bar x$ and updates it
with each new prediction $x_t$:
\begin{equation*}
 \bar x_t=(1-\alpha)\,\bar x_{t-1}+\alpha\,x_t,\qquad \alpha=1-\exp(-\Delta t/\tau_c),
\end{equation*}
with $\Delta t$ the source frame spacing and $\tau_c$ the time constant of quantity $c$; a longer $\tau_c$ reduces
jitter at the cost of lag. The time constants are \VideoPoseTau\,s for the bone rotations,
\VideoTranslTau\,s for the camera-space head anchor and \VideoShapeTau\,s for the phenotypes. Rotations are blended by
shortest-arc quaternion interpolation, which keeps them valid where averaging rotations near $\pm180^\circ$ would
yield approximately the identity. The filtered parameters are decoded again, so vertices are never averaged.
The state resets after a missed detection, a new track or a gap above \VideoMaxGap\,s; a missed frame is never filled
with an earlier body. Raw and filtered outputs share one forward pass. The constants are pipeline defaults (recorded in
the five-panel video's configuration), inspected visually and not tuned.

\paragraph{Landmark alignment.} To correct a misplaced or oversized body, which the filter cannot do, the 2D landmark
detector MediaPipe Pose~\cite{mediapipecode} runs as a separate offline pass on the CPU over the original video. Its shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles correspond to the heads of the Anny bones
upperarm01, lowerarm01, wrist, upperleg01, lowerleg01 and foot. A robust least-squares fit estimates one 3D translation of
the body that aligns the projected Anny points with the landmarks; since depth changes the projected size, this corrects
an oversized or shifted mesh, but not a wrongly articulated limb. A fit is accepted only with at least
\VideoMinLandmarks{} landmarks of confidence $\ge$\,\VideoMinConf{} covering a shoulder, a hip and a knee or ankle, and
a depth within \VideoDepthBoundLow--\VideoDepthBoundHigh{} times the current median joint depth; otherwise the frame is
left unchanged and the depth history resets. The accepted depth is smoothed in log space (\VideoDepthTau\,s, at most
\VideoDepthRate{} log-units per second), moving the body along the current pelvis camera ray; a reset can still cause a
visible change in size. The alignment acts on the filtered body when the filter is on. The projection error is recomputed after smoothing,
which trades some current-frame alignment for less size fluctuation.

\paragraph{Scope of the two steps.} The landmarks are detector estimates, so a lower landmark error indicates agreement
with that detector, not a more accurate 3D body; the clip has no 3D labels, and a stable body can still be wrong. Any
improvement of the aligned output stems from this refinement, not from the network. The video panels report different timings (raw: Equation~\eqref{eq:video-fps}; filtered: plus filtering and decoding;
aligned: plus the fit, without landmark extraction; input: playback FPS), which are not comparable
(Table~\ref{tab:video-fps}, Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels}).

\chapter{Methodology}
\label{ch:method}

The model I deploy is the ViT-B Student. It learns from a larger ViT-L teacher, which my experiment records call
Final. The teacher gives the student two things: a copy of its prediction heads at the start of training, and targets
to imitate during training.

\section{System overview}
\label{sec:method-overview}

The converter of Chapter~\ref{ch:data} gives the real 3DPW training images Anny labels. With them I train the teacher
on synthetic and real images. The student then learns from the labels and from the teacher, which stays frozen, meaning
training does not update its weights. Only the student runs at deployment (Figure~\ref{fig:distillation}).

\begin{figure}[!htb]
\centering
\input{figures/tikz/F05_distillation}
\caption[Teacher--student training and the deployed model]{Teacher--student training (schematic). Both branches see
the same image. The student's ViT-B features pass through a \MethodWidthB{}-to-\MethodWidthL{} projector into heads
copied from the frozen teacher, and the student is trained with the supervised and two distillation losses. At
inference only the student runs; its projector stays, because the heads read its output.}
\label{fig:distillation}
\end{figure}

The student's ViT-B encoder $f_B$ turns an image $I$ into $N_p$ tokens of $C_s=\MethodWidthB$ channels,
$\mathbf Z^s=f_B(I)$. A trainable linear projector maps them to the teacher's width:
\begin{equation}
 \widetilde{\mathbf Z}^s=\mathbf Z^s\mathbf W^\top+\mathbf b,\qquad
 \mathbf W\in\mathbb R^{C_t\times C_s},\quad C_t=\MethodWidthL .
 \label{eq:student-projection}
\end{equation}
So each student token gets the \MethodWidthL{} channels the teacher's heads read, and the heads can be copied. The
smaller encoder gives most of the savings. Neither ViT-B nor distillation is new in itself.

\section{Multi-HMR for Anny}
\label{sec:method-arch}

\subsection{The task}
\label{sec:method-task}

The input is one RGB image with camera intrinsics $\mathbf{K}$, the matrix of focal length and image centre. It shows
an unknown number of people. For each person $i$ the network predicts the rotations
$\mathbf{R}_i\in SO(3)^{\MethodNumBones}$ of Anny's bones, the phenotypes $\boldsymbol{\phi}_i\in[0,1]^{\MethodNumPheno}$,
and the image position $(u_i,v_i)$ and depth $z_i$ of one anchor joint $a$, the head. The Anny body model
$M$~\cite{bregier2025anny} turns $(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i)$ into vertices $M_V$ and joints, and the mesh is
moved so that its anchor joint $M_{J,a}$ sits at the back-projected anchor pixel:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{V}_i = M_V(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) - M_{J,a}(\mathbf{R}_i,\boldsymbol{\phi}_i) + \mathbf{t}_i,
  \qquad
  \mathbf{t}_i = z_i\,\mathbf{K}^{-1}\,(u_i, v_i, 1)^{\top}.
  \label{eq:method-placement}
\end{equation}
In words, the head is first moved to the origin, and then the whole body is shifted so that the head lands at depth
$z_i$ on the camera ray through its pixel.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:method-architecture}

The teacher follows Multi-HMR~\cite{baradel2024multihmr} with outputs for Anny (Figure~\ref{fig:architecture}). A
DINOv2 ViT-L~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} with weights from NAVER's Anny checkpoint turns the image into tokens. One MLP scores
each token for the anchor joint (the head, \texttt{-{}-person\_center head}), and a second predicts the anchor's 2D
offset inside the token. Each detected token becomes a query of the HPH, which lets the people attend to each other and
to all image tokens.

\begin{figure}[!htb]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F01_architecture}
  \caption[Teacher decoding architecture]{Teacher architecture (schematic): $\MethodImgSize$-pixel input,
  $\MethodTokenGrid\times\MethodTokenGrid$ ViT-L tokens with a head-anchor score and offset, then per person
  $\MethodNumBones\times\MethodRotDims$ rotation values, \MethodNumPheno{} phenotypes and a depth, the constant
  correction of four bones and the Anny layer. Shaded boxes are new in this thesis; the dashed box is what distillation
  replaces.}
  \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

The Anny head is the part that differs from the SMPL-X version. Each rotation is predicted as \MethodRotDims{} numbers,
which Gram--Schmidt orthogonalisation turns into a valid rotation matrix~\cite{zhou2019continuity}. A sigmoid keeps the
phenotypes between 0 and 1, and the depth is Multi-HMR's logarithm of the distance, normalised by the focal length.
Right after Gram--Schmidt, four bones are multiplied by one constant rotation each (Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}).
Then a fixed mask sets \MethodHelperBones{} helper bones (toes, breasts, face and subdivision bones) to the identity, in
prediction and ground truth alike, and keeps \MethodActiveBones. The Anny layer, the same full-rig configuration that
builds the targets, runs inside the network and places the body with Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}, so the 3D
and 2D losses reach the network through it. The student keeps everything after the encoder, replaces the encoder by
ViT-B and adds the projector (sizes and cost in Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}).

\subsection{What the model predicts at inference}
\label{sec:method-inference}

At inference there are no labels, so the model estimates everything from the image and $\mathbf K$. Camera information
tells the model which direction each image location points into 3D space. When the calibration is unknown, as for the
demo photos and the video, I build $\mathbf K$ from an assumed field of view, and a wrong guess changes the recovered
depth. Per detected person the network gives a detection score, a 2D offset, \MethodNumBones{} bone rotations (how the
body bends), \MethodNumPheno{} phenotypes (its proportions) and the head-anchor depth. Anny turns these into a mesh and
joints, placed by Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}.

\section{Supervised training objective}
\label{sec:method-losses}

A loss is a numerical penalty for a mismatch during training. Anny runs on each person's true parameters to give target
vertices and joints, and the anchor targets come from one joint (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). The total loss is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L} = {}& \alpha_{\text{det}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{det}}
  + \alpha_{\text{off}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{off}}
  + \alpha_{\text{rot}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{rot}}
  + \alpha_{\text{shape}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{shape}} \\
  &+ \alpha_{\text{dist}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{dist}}
  + \alpha_{\text{t}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{t}}
  + \alpha_{\text{j3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j3d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v3d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v3d}} \\
  &+ \mathbb{1}[e \ge e_{\text{2D}}]\left(\alpha_{\text{j2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{j2d}}
  + \alpha_{\text{v2d}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{v2d}}\right)
  + \lambda_{\text{kd}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd}} + \lambda_{\text{kd-out}}\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}},
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-loss}
\end{equation}
with $e$ the epoch, $e_{\text{2D}}$ the first epoch of the 2D terms, and weights $\alpha$ and $\lambda$; the two
distillation terms exist only for a student. So every wrong part of the prediction costs something, and the weights
decide how much. Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights} gives what each term compares and its weights.

\subsection{Detection and anchor localisation}

Let $s_q$ be the detection probability at token $q$, $y_q\in\{0,1\}$ its target and $N_+=\sum_q y_q$. The detection
term is the focal loss of CornerNet~\cite{law2018cornernet}:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{det}}=
 -\frac{1}{\max(1,N_+)}\sum_q
 \begin{cases}
 (1-s_q)^2\log(s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=1,\\
 s_q^2\log(1-s_q+\epsilon), & y_q=0 .
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:loss-detection}
\end{equation}
The sum runs over all tokens of the batch, a positive token holds a head anchor, and the squared factors make easy
background tokens count less. In words, the loss is large when the network misses a person or fires on background. For
$N_h$ supervised people with sub-token offsets $\mathbf o_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{off}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i
    \|\widehat{\mathbf o}_i-\mathbf o_i\|_1
 \label{eq:loss-offset}
\end{equation}
grows when the anchor sits at the wrong place inside its token.

\subsection{Pose, shape and placement}

For predicted and target rotation matrices $\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}$ and $\mathbf R_{ib}$ of bone $b$ of person $i$,
pose and shape use an L1 loss, the sum of absolute differences:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{rot}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_b
    m_{ib}w_b\|\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}-\mathbf R_{ib}\|_1,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{shape}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\sum_k
    |\widehat{\phi}_{ik}-\phi_{ik}|.
 \label{eq:loss-parameters}
\end{equation}
Here $m_{ib}$ is 0 for unlabelled 3DPW hand bones (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}) and $w_b$ is the normalised bone
weight. The two terms penalise wrongly turned bones and wrong proportions. Helper rotations are the identity on both
sides. The final configuration supervises all phenotypes, the converter's constants included; the shape-mask variants
test that choice. The code sums over bones or phenotypes before averaging people, so the weights depend on these
reductions. An L1 loss is smallest at the median of the plausible answers, so weak image cues pull the shape toward the
training median (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

For predicted and target log-depth $d_i$ and camera-space anchor translation $\mathbf t_i$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{dist}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i|\widehat d_i-d_i|,\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm t}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\|\widehat{\mathbf t}_i-\mathbf t_i\|_1 .
 \label{eq:loss-placement}
\end{equation}
These penalise a wrong distance and a wrong position in the image. They are the only terms that see the absolute
placement directly; the 2D terms see it indirectly.

\subsection{Geometric and reprojection supervision}

For joints or vertices $\mathbf X_i$, each body's own pelvis is subtracted first,
$\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}=\mathbf X_{i\ell}-\mathbf J_{i,\mathrm{root}}$. With $K_X$ points and weights $w_\ell$
($w_\ell=1$ for vertices, the configured joint weights for joints),
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X3D}=\frac{1}{N_hK_X}\sum_i\sum_{\ell=1}^{K_X}
 w_\ell\|\widehat{\overline{\mathbf X}}_{i\ell}-\overline{\mathbf X}_{i\ell}\|_1 .
 \label{eq:loss-geometry}
\end{equation}
This compares posture and body geometry independently of where the person stands, which is also why it cannot replace
Equation~\eqref{eq:loss-placement}. The 2D terms project the uncentred camera-space points:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{X2D}=\frac{1}{|\Omega_X|}
 \sum_{(i,\ell)\in\Omega_X}
 \|\pi_{\mathbf K}(\widehat{\mathbf X}_{i\ell})-\pi_{\mathbf K}(\mathbf X_{i\ell})\|_1 ,
 \label{eq:loss-reprojection}
\end{equation}
where $\pi_{\mathbf K}$ projects a point into the image and $\Omega_X$ holds the target points inside it. Put simply, the
mesh must line up with the photo. Non-finite reductions are skipped.

These L1 losses are related to PVE (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}) but are not the same measure, because PVE averages
Euclidean distances. Training runs in 32-bit floats (fp32) with the faster TF32 format for matrix products, because
mixed precision gave NaN (not-a-number) values in the Anny layer, and steps with NaN or Inf gradients are skipped
(\MethodFinalNaNSteps{} of $\num{\MethodFinalSteps}$ in Final). No weight was tuned in this thesis.

\section{Training with real images}
\label{sec:method-real}

\paragraph{The mix.} A weighted sampler draws on average \MethodMixRatio\% of each batch from 3DPW-train converted to
Anny (v23 labels, $\num{\MethodTrainThreeDPWImages}$ images) and \MethodSynthRatio\% from AnnyOne. AnnyOne images hold
more people, so by my estimate only about \MethodThreeDPWPeopleShare\% of the supervised people are real. The test split
is never used for training.

\paragraph{The hand mask.} The converter fits only the root and the bones that carry a mapped SMPL joint, so the
fingers, thumbs and metacarpals of every 3DPW label are at rest. With \texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_hands 1} the rotation terms
skip these \MethodHandMaskBones{} bones for 3DPW people, and the fingers are learned from AnnyOne only. The mask removes
the rotation terms only; the mesh terms still carry the rest-pose hands.

\paragraph{The shape mask, tested.} The converter leaves \MethodShapeMaskCount{} phenotypes at constants: age, muscle,
weight, cup size, firmness and the three ethnicity values (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). With
\texttt{-{}-mask\_3dpw\_shape 1} the shape term ignores them for 3DPW people (Final-masked); the partial variant masks the
\MethodShapeMaskPartial{} values other than age (Final-partial-mask). The mesh terms still carry the neutral body. I kept the
unmasked model as Final and teacher, because both masked models lost the gender gain on real photos
(Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}).

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:method-kd}

Knowledge distillation means using a larger trained model to guide a smaller one (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}). The frozen
teacher sees the same image and guides the student through its features and its outputs.

\paragraph{Feature distillation.} For image $u$ and token $n$, the teacher token and the projected student token become
distributions over the $C_t$ channels, $p^t_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\mathbf z^t_{un}/T)$ and
$p^s_{unc}=\operatorname{softmax}_c(\widetilde{\mathbf z}^s_{un}/T)$. With batch size $B$, the loss is their
Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence, summed over tokens and averaged over images:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{kd}}=
 \frac{T^2}{B}\sum_{u=1}^{B}\sum_{n=1}^{N_p}\sum_{c=1}^{C_t}
 p^t_{unc}\log\frac{p^t_{unc}}{p^s_{unc}} .
 \label{eq:method-kd}
\end{equation}
Put simply, the channel values are divided by the temperature $T$, a softmax turns them into positive weights that add
up to 1, and the KL divergence measures how differently teacher and student spread them. A larger $T$ gives softer
weights, so the student also learns from the smaller responses (example in Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}); $T^2$ keeps the
gradient size comparable~\cite{hinton2015distilling}. I set $T=\MethodKDTemp$ without tuning it;
Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature} sweeps \KDSweepArms{} values up to \KDSweepMaxT. As in
FitNets~\cite{romero2015fitnets}, the student side is exactly the projected tokens the heads read; the teacher side is
detached, so no gradient reaches the teacher.

\paragraph{Head transfer and output distillation.} The student starts with a copy of every teacher head tensor whose
name and shape match. Both models run on the true person locations, so person $i$ of one is person $i$ of the other, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{kd-out}}=\frac{1}{N_h}\sum_i\Big[
 &\alpha_{\text{rot}}\sum_b\|\mathbf R^s_{ib}-\mathbf R^t_{ib}\|_1\\
 &+\alpha_{\text{shape}}\sum_k|\phi^s_{ik}-\phi^t_{ik}|
 +\alpha_{\text{dist}}|d^s_i-d^t_i|\Big]
\end{split}
\label{eq:method-kdout}
\end{equation}
pulls the student's outputs toward the detached teacher's. It is a plain L1 difference with no temperature, and its
rotation term has no per-bone boost and no hand mask, because the teacher predicts hands even where a converted label
has none. The teacher can be wrong, so the supervised terms stay. Both weights are $\lambda_{\mathrm{kd}}=\MethodKDLambda$
and $\lambda_{\mathrm{kd-out}}=\MethodKDOutLambda$.

\paragraph{Warm-up freeze.} The copied heads already know how to predict bodies, but at first the student's features
differ from the teacher's. Fed with them, the heads give confident wrong outputs whose gradients damage the copied
weights. So the heads stay frozen for the first epochs while backbone and projector adapt, and then everything trains.
Every head is built at the teacher's width (\texttt{-{}-head\_dim}), so all head tensors transfer
(earlier designs: Section~\ref{sec:method-kd-history}).

\section{The ViT-B student of Final}
\label{sec:method-student}

The Student uses this design with a ViT-B backbone (Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}). The backbone starts from NAVER's
Multi-HMR ViT-B, already trained for mesh recovery, and trains fully. The copied heads stay frozen for
\MethodStudentFreeze{} epochs, and the targets are exactly the teacher's. I claim no augmentation: the job script names
colour jitter, but no code reads that setting. The copied heads output the half-turn that the teacher's bone correction
undoes, so the student trains with that correction; afterwards I estimate its own on \MethodBoneFixImages{} training
images and use it in every evaluation. A second run of the same recipe for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs is only the
training-length experiment (Section~\ref{sec:res-training-length}); the reported Student is the
\MethodStudentEpochs-epoch run.

\input{tables/T11_student_recipe}

I also set a retention criterion beforehand, the 95\,\% rule: for an error metric $E$, the student passes if
$E_s\le E_t/\MethodRuleFactor$, with $E_t$ the teacher's error. It is a test, not a claim that the student passes. The
bars against Multi-HMR-Anny are read separately, because its AnnyOne error is most likely in-sample
(Section~\ref{sec:data-annyone}) and it was not trained on our converted 3DPW images.

\section{Training schedule and model names}
\label{sec:method-schedule}

The teacher's ViT-L backbone starts from NAVER's checkpoint, and its Anny heads were trained on AnnyOne; the result is
Base, the start of every fine-tune (earlier stages in Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}).

\paragraph{Final and Control.} Final trains from Base for \MethodFinalEpochs{} epochs of $\num{\MethodFinalIters}$
iterations at batch \MethodFinalBatch{} with a constant learning rate of \MethodFinalLR; only the last
\MethodUnfrozenBlocks{} backbone blocks and the heads train. Three changes come on together: the depth fix with
distance and translation weight \MethodWDist{} (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}), the bone correction
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}), and \MethodMixRatio\% real images per batch with the hand mask. The
\MethodManifestImages{} images of the diagnostic manifest (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}) are never sampled. Training took
$\num{\MethodFinalTrainSec}$\,s on one A100. Control is identical but keeps the pelvis targets with weight
\MethodWDistDefault{} and has no bone correction. So Control against Final isolates the two fixes, and Base against
Control shows the effect of the real images plus the cost of restarting. I tested each fix alone only in short pilots
(Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}).

\subsection{Consistent head-anchor supervision}
\label{sec:method-depth}

The body is placed by its head joint (Equation~\eqref{eq:method-placement}), so the detection, offset, depth and
translation targets must all describe that joint. In Final the target builder decodes the labelled body and takes the
head's camera-space position and image projection; the visibility checks and the one-person-per-token assignment use it
too. Base and Control build these targets at the pelvis, so targets and decoder disagree about where the body is, and
the pelvis-centred losses cannot see it (Figure~\ref{fig:anchor}; measurements in Section~\ref{sec:diag-depth}). The
ablation changes the anchor and the placement weight (\MethodWDist{} against \MethodWDistDefault) together.

\subsection{Fixed output rotation correction}
\label{sec:method-bonefix}

In Base, neck02 and the head bone are each turned by about half a turn (Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}); the origin of this
flip is unknown. I correct it with one constant rotation per affected bone, applied to the pose output before decoding:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf R^{\mathrm{corr}}_{ib}=\widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}\mathbf C_b,\qquad
 \mathbf C_b=\operatorname{Proj}_{SO(3)}
 \left(\frac{1}{N_{\mathrm{cal}}}\sum_i
       \widehat{\mathbf R}_{ib}^{\top}\mathbf R^{\mathrm{target}}_{ib}\right),
 \label{eq:bone-calibration}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{Proj}_{SO(3)}$ gives the nearest proper rotation to the mean residual over $N_{\mathrm{cal}}$
people. In words, $\mathbf C_b$ is the average turn from the prediction of bone $b$ to its label. It is estimated on
\MethodBoneFixImages{} random training images, never evaluation images, for bones with a persistent offset above
\MethodBoneFixThr$^\circ$: for the teacher neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R (Figure~\ref{fig:neck-chain}).
The correction acts before helper masking, decoding and the loss, and adds no parameters. A constant cannot fix an error
that differs per image, so I fine-tuned the teacher with it in place. The student trains with the teacher's correction,
and its own, estimated afterwards, is used for its evaluation and video.

\section{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:method-eval}

Errors are means over matched people: a greedy 2D matcher pairs each labelled person with at most one detection
(Table~\ref{tab:metrics}).

\input{tables/T07_metrics}

For one person with predicted and target vertices $\hat{\mathbf v}_k,\mathbf v_k$ ($N_V$ of them), joints
$\hat{\mathbf J}_j,\mathbf J_j$ ($J=\MethodNumBones$) and pelvis joints $\hat{\mathbf p},\mathbf p$,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{PVE} &= \tfrac{1}{N_V}\textstyle\sum_{k}\big\|(\hat{\mathbf v}_k-\hat{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf v_k-\mathbf p)\big\|_2 ,
 \label{eq:metric-pve}\\
\mathrm{PA\text{-}PVE} &= \tfrac{1}{N_V}\textstyle\sum_{k}\big\|s^\ast\mathbf R^\ast\hat{\mathbf v}_k+\mathbf t^\ast-\mathbf v_k\big\|_2 ,
 \label{eq:metric-papve}\\
\mathrm{MPJPE} &= \tfrac{1}{J}\textstyle\sum_{j}\big\|(\hat{\mathbf J}_j-\hat{\mathbf p})-(\mathbf J_j-\mathbf p)\big\|_2 ,
 \label{eq:metric-mpjpe}\\
\mathrm{Recall} &= \mathrm{TP}/(\mathrm{TP}+\mathrm{FN}) ,
 \label{eq:metric-recall}
\end{align}
with $s^\ast,\mathbf R^\ast,\mathbf t^\ast$ the scale, rotation and translation of the least-squares fit of prediction to
target. PVE is the mean distance between corresponding surface points once both pelvises sit at the same place.
PA-PVE first turns, moves and resizes the prediction to fit the target (Procrustes alignment) and measures what remains.
MPJPE is PVE on the Anny joints. TP counts the labelled people found and FN those missed; finding 9 of 10 gives
90\,\% recall. For cost I report parameters, GFLOPs (one multiply-accumulate is two operations) and the median time of
one forward pass on one GPU, at batch size 1 with one person.

\paragraph{AnnyOne holdout.} The last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (indices $\num{\MethodHoldoutFirst}$ to
$\num{\MethodHoldoutLast}$), never trained on and without children (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}). The headline setting V1
masks the helper bones, as the demos do; V0 does not (Table~\ref{tab:metrics}). A trace script adds placement and
per-bone metrics.

\paragraph{3DPW test.} All $\num{\MethodTestImages}$ test images ($\num{\MethodTestPeople}$ people) of the v23 labels,
one script for every model, setting V0. The targets are converted labels and MPJPE averages all \MethodNumBones{} Anny
joints, so these numbers are not comparable with published 3DPW numbers (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

\paragraph{Relative Human and demo images.} I use the Relative Human validation split (\MethodRHImages{} images, $\num{\MethodRHPeople}$ people) for evaluation only, with
boxes from the projected vertices, matching at IoU $\ge$ \MethodRHIoU{} and child-shaped meaning a predicted age below
\MethodChildThr. The \MethodDemoPhotos{} demo photos and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames place each body from its
detection box, which hides depth errors, and I judge them by eye.

\paragraph{Training variation.} Each model is one training run without a fixed seed; I did not repeat the Student or
Final. Two identical short pilots differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE and \NoiseHeadDeg$^\circ$ head direction
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}), which gives no confidence interval but means I read small differences between single
runs with care.

\section{Applying the student to video}
\label{sec:method-video}

The network works on single images, so for a video I run it frame by frame. Each selected frame is letterboxed (resized
and padded without distortion) to the checkpoint's input size, with an assumed field of view of \VideoFov$^\circ$ along
the longer side for every model. The raw outputs keep the model's own camera-space placement; nothing is placed from a
detection box. A geometric tracker follows one person by box overlap and normalised centre distance. Missing
predictions stay missing, and a track break interrupts all frame differences. The pipeline also records the
pelvis-relative joint velocity and acceleration ($\mathbf q_{tj}=\mathbf J_{tj}-\mathbf J_{t,\mathrm{root}}$, frame
spacing $\Delta t$)
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf v_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-\mathbf q_{t-1,j}}{\Delta t},\qquad
 \mathbf a_{tj}=\frac{\mathbf q_{tj}-2\mathbf q_{t-1,j}+\mathbf q_{t-2,j}}{\Delta t^2}
 \label{eq:video-motion}
\end{equation}
and the phenotype spread and frame-to-frame change. These describe predicted motion only, not an error against ground
truth, and natural or camera motion raises them too.

The input, the student and Multi-HMR-Anny are shown side by side on the same frames. Playback FPS is the source rate
divided by the frame stride. Inference FPS uses batch size 1 and a clock synchronised with the GPU around the model call:
\begin{equation}
 \mathrm{FPS}_{\mathrm{infer}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{timed}}}
 {\sum_{t\in\mathcal T}s_t},\qquad
 s_t=\text{forward seconds of frame } t ,
 \label{eq:video-fps}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal T$ holds the $N_{\mathrm{timed}}$ frames after a warm-up of \VideoWarmupFrames{} frames, which stay in
the videos and the measurements. The time $s_t$ includes detection and Anny decoding and excludes preprocessing, transfers outside the model,
rendering and video input and output. Rendering is timed separately, and a speed claim needs the device, precision,
resolution and number of people next to it.

\subsection{Temporal filtering and landmark alignment}
\label{sec:method-temporal}

Each frame is predicted on its own, so the body can change from frame to frame, and the raw student mesh sometimes
extends beyond the person (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}). I added two inference-time steps; the student is not retrained
and has no learned temporal information. First, the student predicts a body per frame. Then an exponential moving
average blends it with the track's earlier predictions. Then 2D landmarks from a separate detector correct its position
and depth. Finally, that depth is smoothed.

\paragraph{Temporal filter.} The filter is causal: it uses only the current and earlier frames. For each smoothed
quantity it keeps a value $\bar x$ and updates it with each new prediction $x_t$:
\begin{equation*}
 \bar x_t = (1-\alpha)\,\bar x_{t-1} + \alpha\,x_t,\qquad \alpha = 1-\exp(-\Delta t/\tau_c),
\end{equation*}
with $\Delta t$ the time between source frames and $\tau_c$ the time constant of quantity $c$. A longer time constant
gives the new frame less weight: steadier output, but lag behind fast movement. The filter smooths the bone rotations
($\tau_c=\VideoPoseTau$\,s), the camera-space head anchor that places the mesh (\VideoTranslTau\,s) and the
phenotypes (\VideoShapeTau\,s). Rotations are blended by shortest-arc quaternion interpolation, which keeps them
valid; a plain average of turns of almost $+180^\circ$ and $-180^\circ$ would give no rotation at all. The filtered
parameters are decoded again through Anny, so vertices are never averaged. The state resets after a missed detection, a
new track or a gap above \VideoMaxGap\,s, and a missed frame is never filled with an old body. Raw and filtered
outputs share one forward pass. The constants are the pipeline defaults, set by looking at the video and
not tuned\TODO{confirm in run.json of the five-panel video run}.

\paragraph{Landmark alignment.} The filter cannot move a body that sits at the wrong place or is drawn too large. For
this I run MediaPipe Pose, an existing 2D landmark detector, once offline on the CPU over the original video. Its
shoulders, elbows, wrists, hips, knees and ankles match the heads of the Anny bones upperarm01, lowerarm01, wrist,
upperleg01, lowerleg01 and foot. A least-squares fit that down-weights outliers finds one 3D translation of the whole
body so that the projected Anny points land on the landmarks. A body moved farther away looks smaller, so this corrects
an oversized or shifted mesh, but it cannot repair a wrongly bent arm or leg. A fit counts only with at least
\VideoMinLandmarks{} landmarks of confidence \VideoMinConf{} or more, covering a shoulder, a hip and a knee or ankle, and
a depth within \VideoDepthBoundLow--\VideoDepthBoundHigh{} times the current median joint depth; otherwise the frame
stays unchanged and the depth history resets. Finally, the accepted depth is smoothed in log space (\VideoDepthTau\,s,
at most \VideoDepthRate{} log-units per second), so the body stops growing and shrinking. The alignment acts on the
filtered body when the filter is on.

\paragraph{What the two steps can and cannot show.} The landmarks are detector estimates, not ground truth, so a
smaller landmark error means closer agreement with that detector, not a better 3D body. The clip has no 3D labels and
gives no PVE, and a steady body can still be wrong. A visible gain in the aligned output comes from this refinement,
not from a new network. The panels also time different things: the raw panels show the inference FPS of
Equation~\eqref{eq:video-fps}, the filtered panel adds filtering and decoding, the aligned panel adds the fit but not the
offline landmark extraction, and the input panel shows playback FPS. These are not one comparable number
(Table~\ref{tab:video-fps}, Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels}).

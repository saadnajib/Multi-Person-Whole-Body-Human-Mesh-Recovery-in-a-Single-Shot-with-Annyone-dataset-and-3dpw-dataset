\chapter{Supporting diagnostic experiments}
\label{ch:diagnostics}

The initial model (Base), trained on AnnyOne only, showed three visible failures: thin and twisted necks, fingers
bent into claws, and adult bodies for children. This appendix reports the experiments that isolated their causes; the two resulting
corrections are defined in Sections~\ref{sec:method-depth} and~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}. Unless noted, numbers are for
the initial model on the \DiagHoldoutImages-image AnnyOne holdout in the holdout protocol, in which Anny's helper bones (face, toes, breasts)
are set to rest pose; the 3DPW protocol scores them as predicted (the holdout and 3DPW protocols of
Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}). Every
variant is a single run without a fixed seed, and differences are read against the pilot variation of
Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}.

Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} (left) shows the thin necks; the curled fingers were set to rest pose in
every demo image, and a standing boy received an adult body (Figure~\ref{fig:real-photos}). Three untested
explanations were proposed initially: the skinning of the stacked neck bones as a property of the rig (the skeleton that
deforms the mesh), the small image size of the hands, and an unlearned age range, the last citing five small people in
the demo images predicted at adult ages (\DiagSmallPeopleAgeLow--\DiagSmallPeopleAgeHigh). The rig explanation was refuted (Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}), and
hand size explains only a small part of the finger error (Section~\ref{sec:diag-fingers}). Four of the five small
people were adults who crouched, sat or were truncated (height phenotype
\DiagSmallPeopleHeightLow--\DiagSmallPeopleHeightHigh); only the boy was a child, and he received an adult body (age
\DiagBoyAge, height \DiagBoyHeight). The thin necks are quantified by the neck girth of Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones},
predicted over true neck circumference.

\paragraph{Hypothesis and test.} The failures could originate outside the network, in the labels, the body model,
its rig or the gradient path (Table~\ref{tab:diag-checks}). Checks~1, 5 and~6
use a manifest of \DataManifestTrain{} training images (10 adults, 10 children, 10 with visible hands, 10 hard neck
poses) and \DataManifestHoldout{} holdout images without children. The entries were drawn from the AnnyOne labels by
a script with a fixed random seed. The initial model saw the training entries during its own training, so the manifest
children show whether a model fits a child it has seen, not whether it generalises. Check~1 draws the decoded labels over the image without a
network, which would expose a wrong label or camera (Figure~\ref{fig:diag-gt-decode}). \emph{Result:} labels, rig
and gradient path are correct, so the cause lies in what the training targets and losses require of the network.

\paragraph{Loss composition.} At the end of training, the shape term made up \DiagLossShareShape\,\% of the
initial model's loss, the translation term \DiagLossShareTransl\,\% and the distance term \DiagLossShareDist\,\%.
The 3D terms align both pelvises before comparing bodies and cannot see a person's distance from the camera; only the
distance and translation terms, together \DiagLossShareDepthTerms\,\% before the depth fix, constrain it. Two log entries are easily misread: the detection term
logged as \texttt{bce} is the focal loss of CornerNet~\cite{law2018cornernet}, not a binary cross-entropy, and the neck
and hand boost (Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights}) redistributes rotation-loss weight between bones without adding a loss.

\paragraph{Overfit warnings.} In check~6 the rotation loss barely decreased during memorisation
(\DiagOverfitRotFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitRotLast), and the translation and 2D terms rose (\DiagOverfitTranslFirst{}
$\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslLast, \DiagOverfitTwoDFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTwoDLast, first against last five epochs),
possibly an early sign of the depth fault. This evidence is weak: the values are single-batch prints, and the half-run
averages decrease (\DiagOverfitTranslHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslHalfB, \DiagOverfitTwoDHalfA{} $\to$
\DiagOverfitTwoDHalfB).

\paragraph{Faults outside the checks' reach.} Two faults escaped: a depth target built at a joint other than the one
placing the mesh, invisible in pelvis-centred comparisons, and a bone rotated by the same wrong amount in every person,
hidden in group means of bone errors.

\paragraph{Observation.} The initial model places every person too far from the camera, the nearest people most
(Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement}, Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}). With a skeleton of correct height, each body is
therefore drawn at \DiagSizeBase{} of its true image size. PVE, which moves the predicted pelvis onto the true pelvis
before measuring, does not register the error.

\paragraph{Cause.} The code places the mesh in the image by its head joint, but the anchor targets had been built at
the pelvis (Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}), so the network was trained to put the head where the pelvis is. A debug print on one batch showed anchor offsets of \DiagOffsetMin{} to \DiagOffsetMax{}
cells of the token grid, against a valid range of $\pm$0.5 cells. A plausible but untested mechanism: with the
mesh suspended from a misplaced head, the 2D terms reduce the mismatch only by drawing the body smaller, that is,
farther away; the weak direct depth terms do not counteract this, and the mismatch is largest near the camera.

\paragraph{Test and result.} The fix builds the anchor targets at the head; Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs} compares it
with controls of the same schedule that keep the pelvis targets. The five fix runs reach a root error (3D distance
between predicted and true pelvis) of \DiagRootErrFixLow--\DiagRootErrFixHigh\,m against
\DiagRootErrCtrlLow--\DiagRootErrCtrlHigh\,m, a drawn size of \DiagSizeFixLow--\DiagSizeFixHigh{} against
\DiagSizeCtrlLow--\DiagSizeCtrlHigh, and a head 2D error of \DiagHeadPxFixLow--\DiagHeadPxFixHigh{} against
\DiagHeadPxCtrlLow--\DiagHeadPxCtrlHigh\,px; repeated-run uncertainty was not estimated. Two further checkpoints
trained with pelvis targets carry the same fault, which remained undetected because PVE aligns the pelvis and the
demo images place each body from its detection box.

\paragraph{Remaining error.} The fix moves the intercept and the drawn size but barely the slope of predicted against true depth (\DiagSlopeSwitch{} with the target change alone,
\DiagSlopeStrongLow--\DiagSlopeStrongHigh{} with a stronger depth loss, against \DiagDepthSlopeBase{} for the initial
model). Predictions remain pulled toward \DiagDepthPullLow--\DiagDepthPullHigh\,m; the cause was not identified. The
pelvis 2D error rose to \DiagPelvisPxFixLow--\DiagPelvisPxFixHigh\,px, plausibly because the head is now pinned while
the body is still drawn too small (not checked). Every fix run is worse than its control, by
+\DiagFixCostPVELow{} to +\DiagFixCostPVEHigh\,mm PVE and $-$\DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to $-$\DiagFixCostRecallHigh{}
recall points. Each gap lies within the pilot variation, but their common direction indicates that the fix most
likely costs some accuracy. The success rule fixed before the runs (PVE $\le$ \DiagRulePVE\,mm, recall $\ge$
\DiagRuleRecall\,\%) was met by no run, controls included.

\section{A per-bone dump of the neck}
\label{sec:diag-neck}

\input{tables/T09_neck}

\paragraph{Observation.} Group means (\DiagGroupMeanNeck{} and \DiagGroupMeanHead\,deg) did not localise the neck
error. A dump of every bone of the neck chain
(Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}, first column) shows neck02 \DiagDumpNeckTwo\,deg off, a half-turn about its own axis
(twist \DiagDumpNeckTwoTwist, swing \DiagDumpNeckTwoSwing\,deg), and the head bone \DiagDumpHead\,deg off. neck03 is
correct relative to its parent (\DiagDumpNeckThree\,deg) but \DiagDumpNeckThreeGlobal\,deg off in the camera frame.
The two half-turns cancel, so the head points correctly (\DiagDumpHeadGlobal\,deg), but the skin between them is
twisted thin, hence the low girth.

\paragraph{Hypothesis: a training setting causes the flip.} All eight dumped checkpoints of the ViT-L line leading to
and continuing from the initial model (L-stages 2 to 4, Base, Mixed-L, three short fine-tunes of Base;
Table~\ref{tab:names}) show the flip: neck02 is off by
\DiagNeckTwoRangeLow--\DiagNeckTwoRangeHigh\,deg and the girth is \DiagGirthRangeLow--\DiagGirthRangeHigh. It already
exists in L-stage 2, before the helper mask (helper bones held at rest), the neck boost and the shape weight of 20,
which excludes these. The 10-epoch depth fix with $\times$5 weight
(Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs}), one of the three fine-tunes, keeps the flip, which excludes the depth fault.
Fresh-heads-L, a separate ViT-L run from the Multi-HMR-Anny backbone with new heads, 3DPW from the first step and no
neck and hand weight, has a normal neck (girth \DiagFreshGirth) but other flipped bones:
two bones of the left arm whose errors cancel, and three metacarpals.

\paragraph{Hypotheses: rig or loss.} The rig is excluded: it passes all \DiagPerturbations{} single-joint moves, and
the rest pose, in which every rotation is the identity, cannot exhibit a flip. Loss changes did not remove the flip:
the neck boost ($\times$\MethodBoostOn{} for the boosted bones after renormalisation) left it unchanged, and a
geodesic loss, which penalises the angle between predicted and true rotation, moved neck02 only from
\DiagNeckTwoBase{} to \DiagGeodesicNeckTwo\,deg, plausibly because neck02 and the head would have to turn back
jointly (not tested). The output is corrected instead, with one constant rotation per flipped bone
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}): \DiagCorrNeckTwo, \DiagCorrHead, \DiagCorrMetacarpal{} and \DiagCorrFinger\,deg
for neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R.

\paragraph{Result.} Without training, the correction restores neck02, girth and neck-skin error. The head direction,
however, worsens to \DiagHeadDirNoTrain\,deg, above the limit of \DiagHeadDirLimit\,deg set beforehand, presumably
because the head's error differs between people; a 2-epoch fine-tune reduces it to \DiagHeadDirFineTune\,deg (control
\DiagHeadDirCtrlTwo). The teacher was therefore fine-tuned with the correction in place: a constant removes the half-turn
shared by all people but cannot follow a per-person error. Body PVE differences lie
within the pilot variation; Figure~\ref{fig:exp6-compare} shows two further holdout pairs. The origin of the flips is unknown: the network outputs each rotation in the 6D representation, whose singular
initial value would explain the tilted metacarpal1.L but not the twist of neck02. Necks on real photos lack ground
truth, so Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar} rests on visual inspection.

\section{The fingers}
\label{sec:diag-fingers}

\input{tables/T10_fingers}

\paragraph{Observation.} The initial model's predicted fingers are \DiagFingersBase\,deg off, fingers at rest pose
\DiagFingersRest\,deg (Table~\ref{tab:fingers}): the prediction is worse than none.

\paragraph{Hypothesis 1: the hands are too small.} A hand spans a median of \DiagHandTokens{} tokens (image patches
of the encoder), and \DiagHandUnderTwo\,\% span fewer than two. A test-time crop around each person enlarged the
hands, but the finger error stayed at \DiagFingersZoom\,deg while body PVE nearly doubled. A region-normalised
rotation loss had no effect; it moves the hands' share of the
rotation loss from about \DiagHandShareBoost{} to \DiagHandShareRegion\,\%, which reweights the hands without
strengthening them.

\paragraph{Hypothesis 2: flipped bones.} A per-bone search on training images found metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R
half-turned like neck02; correcting them roughly halves the finger error, with and without training.

\paragraph{Result.} About half of the finger error came from two flipped bones, much less from hand size. A
separate hand-crop branch was not tested, and the rest-pose floor was not remeasured with the correction in place. Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo} shows the fingers on real photos.

\section{Children and body shape}
\label{sec:diag-children}

\input{tables/T18_shape_head}

\paragraph{Observation on synthetic data.} On 10 holdout images (Table~\ref{tab:shape-head}(a)), raising the shape
weight from 1 to 20 moved the age bias from \DiagShapeAgeBiasOld{}
to \DiagShapeAgeBiasNew{} and the height bias from \DiagShapeHeightBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeHeightBiasNew. Part~(b)
shows that this holds only near the mode: in both tails predictions move toward the training median. The holdout
contains no children (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}), so the young tail is measured on training images.

\paragraph{Observation on real photos.} On Relative Human (Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}) children are read as short
adults: kids receive a mean age of \DiagRHKidAge, babies \DiagRHBabyAge{} and adults \DiagRHAdultAge{} (age
AUC \DiagRHAUC, chance 0.5). None of \DiagRHKids{} kids and \DiagRHBabiesBelow{} of \DiagRHBabies{} babies fall below
the child threshold; their smallness goes into height. Gender carries no signal (accuracy
\DiagRHGenderAcc\,\%). On the demo images the medians over all \DiagDemoShapeLines{} predicted people (one per detection, about 30
distinct people, nearly all adults) equal the training medians: gender \DiagDemoGenderMedian, age
\DiagDemoAgeMedian, height \DiagDemoHeightMedian.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} Children and men predicted with female-looking bodies are the same failure: under weak
image cues the shape head reverts to the training median. The evidence cannot separate two causes: the synthetic-to-real
gap and the loss. The shape outputs pass a sigmoid and are trained with L1, which is minimised at
the median, and the sigmoid's gradient is 0.25 at an output of 0.5 but only 0.015 at an output of 0.015, so outputs near the edge of
the range barely move during training. Only \DiagChildShare\,\% of the training people are younger than age 0.3. The result is a short adult because Anny's child shape appears only below an age of about 0.4--0.5;
the shoulders, for example, are \DiagShoulderHeadChild{} head widths wide at age 0.1 and \DiagShoulderHeadAdult{} at
0.6. A low height at age 0.6 thus gives a short adult (at least \DiagSmallestAdult\,m), and proportions barely matter
(leg over stature \DiagProportionsLeg).

\paragraph{Further views.} Phenotype agreement on the holdout (Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}) and gender ranking on real photos
(Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}) are in Appendix~\ref{app:teacher-extra}. For
the initial model, the uncorrected teacher (Control) and the teacher (Final), the slope of predicted against true
value is below 1 for every phenotype, the same pull toward the middle. The teacher follows weight (Pearson $r =
\PhenoRFinalWeight$) far better than muscle ($r = \PhenoRFinalMuscle$). Open are which cause dominates and ages in
years, which the code's three mutually inconsistent age scales do not permit.

\section{Pilot noise and restart cost}
\label{sec:diag-noise}

\input{tables/T14_pilot_noise}

Two 2-epoch pilots with identical settings differed by \DiagNoisePVE\,mm PVE, \DiagNoiseHead\,deg head direction and
\DiagNoiseRecall{} recall points (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-noise}); the training code sets no seed. Two further epochs
without any change raised the initial model's PVE by about \DiagRestartCost\,mm (restart cost), so each pilot is
compared with its own control, never with the initial model.

Two runs establish no general threshold, and no bootstrap intervals were computed; the differences are a rough
yardstick, not a significance test, and do not predict the variation of the final models
(Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}). Every change this appendix builds on exceeds it by far: the root error of the depth fix, neck02 from \DiagNeckTwoBase{} to \DiagNeckTwoNoTrain\,deg, the girth from
\DiagNeckGirthBase{} to \DiagGirthNoTrain, and the fingers from \DiagFingersBase{} to \DiagFingersFixFT\,deg. PVE changes
of a few millimetres are smaller, so whether the depth fix and the neck pilot cost PVE remains undetermined.

\section{Training changes without effect}
\label{sec:diag-negative}

Six training changes fixed none of the three failures (Table~\ref{tab:pilots-all}): an age-balanced sampler
(inconclusive on the 10 training-image children available), a global head orientation loss (within the spread of two
identical controls), the region-normalised hand loss, the geodesic rotation loss and the neck and hand boost (none
removed the flipped bones), and a $\times$20 depth loss (no gain over $\times$5). None addressed the actual faults, a
wrong anchor target and flipped bones, which the loss could not detect; the two effective changes, head-anchor targets
and the bone correction, came from the checks in this appendix.

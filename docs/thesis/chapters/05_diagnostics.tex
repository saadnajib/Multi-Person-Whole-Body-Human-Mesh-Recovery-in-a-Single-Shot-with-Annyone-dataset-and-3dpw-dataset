\chapter{Supporting diagnostic experiments}
\label{ch:diagnostics}

Base showed three failures in its renders: thin, twisted necks, fingers bent into claws, and adult bodies on children.
Before changing any training setting, I tested each idea about their causes on its own, and several were wrong.
Chapter~\ref{ch:method} only defines the two resulting corrections; the evidence is here. Unless I say otherwise,
every number is from Base on the \DiagHoldoutImages-image AnnyOne holdout in setting V1, one run per variant without a
fixed seed, read against the variation of Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}.

In Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} (left) Base draws each neck as a thin stalk pinched under the head. The fingers
curled into claws, so every real-photo demo set them to rest pose. A standing boy in a demo photo received an adult
body (Figure~\ref{fig:real-photos}).

My first draft gave each failure an untested cause: the skinning of the stacked neck bones (``a property of the
rig''), finger resolution, and an unlearned age, shown by five small people in the demo photos predicted at adult ages
(\DiagSmallPeopleAgeLow--\DiagSmallPeopleAgeHigh). The neck cause is wrong and resolution explains little. The child
evidence was wrong too: four of the five were adults who crouched, sat or were cut off by the frame (height phenotype
\DiagSmallPeopleHeightLow--\DiagSmallPeopleHeightHigh). Only the boy was a child, and he received age \DiagBoyAge{} and
height \DiagBoyHeight, an adult body. The number behind the thin necks is the girth in Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}.

Table~\ref{tab:diag-checks} tests whether the labels, the body model, the rig or the gradient path were broken.
Check~1, for example, would show a wrong label or camera without any network (Figure~\ref{fig:diag-gt-decode}). The
checks rule out the rig, the label decoding and the gradient path, so the cause had to be in what training asked of
the network.

At the end of Base's training, shape made up \DiagLossShareShape\,\% of the loss, translation \DiagLossShareTransl\,\%
and distance \DiagLossShareDist\,\%. The 3D terms compare bodies after moving both pelvises to the same point, so only
distance and translation see placement directly, and before the depth fix they were only \DiagLossShareDepthTerms\,\%
of the loss. The log calls the detection term \texttt{bce}, but it is the focal loss of
CornerNet~\cite{law2018cornernet}, not a binary cross-entropy. The neck and hand boost (Table~\ref{tab:loss-weights})
moves rotation loss between bones and adds none.

The overfit run of check~6 gave two warnings. The rotation loss barely moved while the images were memorised
(\DiagOverfitRotFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitRotLast), and the translation and 2D terms rose (\DiagOverfitTranslFirst{}
$\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslLast, \DiagOverfitTwoDFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTwoDLast, first against last five epochs),
possibly a first sign of the depth bug. That evidence is weak: the values are single-batch prints, and the half-run
averages fall (\DiagOverfitTranslHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslHalfB,
\DiagOverfitTwoDHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTwoDHalfB).

The checks could not see two faults, and both were present: a target at another joint than the anchor (they compare
pelvis-centred quantities) and a bone flipped the same way in every person (they averaged bone errors by group).

\input{tables/T08_depth_runs}

Base places every person too far away (Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement}, Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}), the nearest
the farthest. The skeleton has the right height, so the body is drawn at \DiagSizeBase{} of its true size. PVE sees
none of it.

The code gave the cause: the mesh is placed by its head joint, but the anchor targets were built at the pelvis
(Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). A one-batch debug print showed offsets of \DiagOffsetMin{} to \DiagOffsetMax{} cells
(tokens of the image grid), where the legal range is $\pm$0.5. The likely mechanism, not tested on its own: with the
mesh hanging from a misplaced head, the 2D terms can shrink the misfit only by drawing the body smaller, that is,
farther away. The direct depth terms could not resist, and near people have the largest misfit.

Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs} tests the fix, targets built at the head, against same-schedule controls. All five fix runs
reach a root error of \DiagRootErrFixLow--\DiagRootErrFixHigh\,m, both controls stay at
\DiagRootErrCtrlLow--\DiagRootErrCtrlHigh\,m. The drawn size rises from \DiagSizeCtrlLow--\DiagSizeCtrlHigh{} to
\DiagSizeFixLow--\DiagSizeFixHigh, and the head lands within \DiagHeadPxFixLow--\DiagHeadPxFixHigh{} instead of
\DiagHeadPxCtrlLow--\DiagHeadPxCtrlHigh{} pixels. Every fix run lies below every control, far outside run-to-run
variation. Two other checkpoints trained with pelvis targets carry the bug too. It stayed hidden because PVE aligns
the pelvis first and the real-photo demos place each body from its detection box.

The fix did not give correct depth. It moved the offset and the body size, not the slope of predicted against true
depth (\DiagSlopeSwitch{} with the switch alone, \DiagSlopeStrongLow--\DiagSlopeStrongHigh{} with a stronger depth
loss), and predictions are still pulled toward \DiagDepthPullLow--\DiagDepthPullHigh\,m for a reason I have not
found. The pelvis 2D error rose to \DiagPelvisPxFixLow--\DiagPelvisPxFixHigh{} pixels, likely because the head is now
pinned while the body is still too small (not checked). Every fix run is worse than its control by
+\DiagFixCostPVELow{} to +\DiagFixCostPVEHigh\,mm PVE and $-$\DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to $-$\DiagFixCostRecallHigh{}
recall points. Each gap is within the variation, but all point one way: a likely cost. By the success rule I
wrote beforehand (PVE $\le$ \DiagRulePVE\,mm, recall $\ge$ \DiagRuleRecall\,\%) every run failed, the controls
included.

\section{A per-bone dump of the neck}
\label{sec:diag-neck}

\input{tables/T09_neck}

My earlier neck numbers were group means (\DiagGroupMeanNeck{} and \DiagGroupMeanHead\,deg) that hid which bone was
wrong, so I dumped the error of every bone of the neck chain (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}, first column). neck02 is
\DiagDumpNeckTwo\,deg wrong, a half-turn about its own axis (twist \DiagDumpNeckTwoTwist, swing
\DiagDumpNeckTwoSwing\,deg), and the head bone \DiagDumpHead\,deg. neck03 is right relative to its parent
(\DiagDumpNeckThree\,deg) but \DiagDumpNeckThreeGlobal\,deg off in the camera frame. The two half-turns cancel, so the
head points the right way (\DiagDumpHeadGlobal\,deg), but the skin between them is twisted thin, as the girth
(predicted over true neck circumference) shows.

The flip is in all eight checkpoints of the Base line (neck02 \DiagNeckTwoRangeLow--\DiagNeckTwoRangeHigh\,deg, girth
\DiagGirthRangeLow--\DiagGirthRangeHigh), from the first stage on, before the helper mask, the neck boost and the
shape weight of 20. The depth pilot keeps it, so depth is not the neck's cause. Fresh-heads-L has a normal neck
(girth \DiagFreshGirth) but flips other bones: a pair in the left arm that cancel each other, and three metacarpals.

This refutes my draft: the rig passes all \DiagPerturbations{} single-joint moves, and the rest pose (all rotations
identity) cannot show the flip. A loss did not undo it. The neck boost ($\times$\MethodBoostOn{} for the boosted bones
after renormalisation) left it unchanged, and a geodesic loss, which penalises the angle between predicted and true
rotation, moved neck02 only from \DiagNeckTwoBase{} to \DiagGeodesicNeckTwo\,deg, likely because neck02 and head must
turn back together. So I corrected the output with one constant rotation per flipped bone
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}): \DiagCorrNeckTwo, \DiagCorrHead, \DiagCorrMetacarpal{} and \DiagCorrFinger\,deg for
neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R.

Without training, the correction restores neck02, girth and neck-skin error. But the head direction worsens to
\DiagHeadDirNoTrain\,deg, above the \DiagHeadDirLimit-degree limit I had set beforehand, likely because the head's
error differs per person. A 2-epoch fine-tune brings it to \DiagHeadDirFineTune\,deg (control \DiagHeadDirCtrlTwo).
This is why I fine-tuned the teacher with the correction in place: a constant added after training removes the shared
half-turn but cannot follow that per-person error. The body-PVE gaps lie within the variation;
Figure~\ref{fig:exp6-compare} shows two more holdout pairs. Not shown: why the bones flipped (the singular 6D start
value fits the tilted metacarpal1.L, not neck02's twist), and necks on real photos, which have no ground truth
(Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}, by eye).

\section{The fingers}
\label{sec:diag-fingers}

\input{tables/T10_fingers}

In Table~\ref{tab:fingers} Base's predicted fingers are \DiagFingersBase\,deg off and fingers at rest
\DiagFingersRest\,deg, so the prediction was worse than none. Hands are small (\DiagHandTokens{} tokens long at the
median, under two tokens for \DiagHandUnderTwo\,\%). But a crop around each person at test time leaves the finger
error at \DiagFingersZoom\,deg while body PVE nearly doubles. A region-normalised rotation loss changed nothing either;
it moves the hands' share of the rotation loss from about \DiagHandShareBoost{} to \DiagHandShareRegion\,\%, a
rebalance and not an upweighting. The per-bone search on training images then found metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R
half-turned. Correcting them roughly halves the finger error, with and without training.

So about half the finger error was two flipped bones, not resolution. I never tried a hand-crop branch, and I did not
measure the rest-pose floor with the correction. Fingers on real photos: Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}.

\section{Children and body shape}
\label{sec:diag-children}

\input{tables/T18_shape_head}

Table~\ref{tab:shape-head}(a) is my draft's shape-weight test on 10 holdout images: raising the shape weight from 1 to
20 moved the age bias from \DiagShapeAgeBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeAgeBiasNew{} and the height bias from
\DiagShapeHeightBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeHeightBiasNew. Part~(b) shows that this holds only near the mode, the most
common value. In both tails predictions move toward the training median (the holdout has no children, so the young
tail is in-sample).

On Relative Human (Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}) Base reads children as short adults. Kids get a mean age of
\DiagRHKidAge, babies \DiagRHBabyAge{} and adults \DiagRHAdultAge{} (age AUC \DiagRHAUC, where 0.5 is chance). None of
\DiagRHKids{} kids and \DiagRHBabiesBelow{} of \DiagRHBabies{} babies fall below the child threshold; the smallness goes
into height instead. Gender carries no signal (accuracy \DiagRHGenderAcc\,\%). On my demo photos the medians of all
\DiagDemoShapeLines{} shape outputs (gender \DiagDemoGenderMedian, age \DiagDemoAgeMedian, height
\DiagDemoHeightMedian) equal the training medians.

Children and female-looking men are one failure: with weak image cues, the shape head returns the training median. Two
mechanisms fit, and the evidence cannot rank them: the gap between synthetic and real images, or the loss. An L1 loss
on sigmoid outputs is smallest at the median, the sigmoid's gradient is 0.25 at 0.5 but 0.015 at 0.015, and only
\DiagChildShare\,\% of training people are below age 0.3. The result looks like a short adult because Anny's child
shape appears only below an age of about 0.4--0.5. For example, the shoulders are \DiagShoulderHeadChild{} head widths
wide at age 0.1 and \DiagShoulderHeadAdult{} at 0.6. So a low height at age 0.6 gives a short adult (at least
\DiagSmallestAdult\,m), and proportions barely matter (leg over stature \DiagProportionsLeg).

Chapter~\ref{ch:results} now adds the per-phenotype agreement on the holdout (Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}) and the
gender ranking on real photos (Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}). For Base, Control and Final every slope of predicted
against true value is below 1, the same pull toward the middle, and Final follows weight (Pearson $r =
\PhenoRFinalWeight$) far better than muscle ($r = \PhenoRFinalMuscle$). Not shown: which mechanism dominates, and ages
in years (the code uses three age scales that disagree).

\section{Pilot noise and restart cost}
\label{sec:diag-noise}

\input{tables/T14_pilot_noise}

Two short pilots that I trained with identical settings differed by \DiagNoisePVE\,mm PVE, \DiagNoiseHead\,deg head
direction and \DiagNoiseRecall{} recall points (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-noise}). The code sets no seed. Two more epochs of fine-tuning
Base with nothing changed also cost about \DiagRestartCost\,mm, the restart cost, so I read each pilot against its own
control and never against Base.

Two runs give no general threshold, and I computed no bootstrap intervals. So I use this as a yardstick, not as a
significance test. It is small next to every change this appendix relies on: root error, neck02
\DiagNeckTwoBase{} to \DiagNeckTwoNoTrain\,deg, girth \DiagNeckGirthBase{} to \DiagGirthNoTrain, fingers
\DiagFingersBase{} to \DiagFingersFixFT\,deg. It is too large for a PVE change of a few millimetres, so the PVE costs of
the depth fix and the neck pilot stay unresolved.

\section{What did not work}
\label{sec:diag-negative}

Six changes did not fix a failure (Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}, Table~\ref{tab:pilots-all}): an age-balanced sampler
(inconclusive on 10 in-sample children), a global head orientation loss (within the spread of two identical controls),
a region-normalised hand loss, a geodesic rotation loss, the neck and hand boost, and a $\times$20 depth loss (no gain
over $\times$5). Each worked around a fault the loss could not see. The two fixes that worked, the head-anchor targets
and the bone correction, came from the diagnostics.

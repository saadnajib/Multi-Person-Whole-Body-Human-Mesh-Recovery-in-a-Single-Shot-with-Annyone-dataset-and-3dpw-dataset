\chapter{Diagnostics: How Each Failure Was Isolated}
\label{ch:diagnostics}

Base showed three failures in its renders: thin, twisted necks, claw-like fingers and adult bodies on children.
Before changing any training setting, I tested each belief about their causes on its own. This chapter reports those
tests, including the explanations that turned out wrong; Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the models built on them.
Unless stated otherwise, every number is from Base on the \DiagHoldoutImages-image AnnyOne holdout in setting V1
(helper bones masked, matched people; Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Every comparison is one run per arm without a fixed
seed and is read against the run-to-run variation of Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}.

Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-v5} shows the first failure as it was seen: in the left column Base draws each neck as a
thin stalk pinched under the head, on holdout images it never trained on. The second failure was the fingers, which
curled into claws; from then on every real-photo demo set them to rest pose. The third was children: a standing boy in
one of our own photos received an adult body (Figure~\ref{fig:real-photos}).

The draft gave each failure a cause without testing an alternative. For the neck it concluded that linear blend
skinning stretches the skin between Anny's stacked neck bones, ``a property of the rig, not a training failure''; a
later reading blamed weak supervision on a redundant four-bone chain. For the fingers the belief was resolution. For
children it was that age had not been learned, and the evidence was five small people in our own photos predicted at
adult ages of \DiagSmallPeopleAgeLow{} to \DiagSmallPeopleAgeHigh. This chapter shows that the neck explanation is
wrong, that resolution explains little of the finger error, and that the child evidence was wrong. Looked at again,
four of the five ``small people'' were adults who crouched, sat or were cut off by the frame: their skeletal height is
measured on the posed body, and their height phenotype (\DiagSmallPeopleHeightLow--\DiagSmallPeopleHeightHigh) gives
an adult rest body. Only the boy was a child, and he received age \DiagBoyAge{} and height \DiagBoyHeight: an adult.

The figure shows neither fingers, children nor a real photo; the neck widths are judged by eye, and the number behind
them is the neck girth of Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}.

Table~\ref{tab:diag-checks} lists the checks and Figure~\ref{fig:diag-gt-decode} shows the first. The joints missing
from the image are also missing from the box, so they are truncation, not a decoding error. The body model makes a
child with child proportions, and each single-joint move, neck and fingers included, moves the intended part.
Gradients range from \DiagGradRoot{} at the root to \DiagGradFingers{} at the fingers; shape dominates the loss
(\DiagLossShareShape\,\%), translation and distance take \DiagLossShareTransl\,\% and \DiagLossShareDist\,\%.

So the rig, the label decoding and the gradient path are not the cause; the draft's four neck tests (rest-pose render
normal, all neck bones active, thin neck on synthetic images, proportions accurate) stay valid as eliminations of the
same kind. The cause had to be in what training asked of the network. The overfit run gave two warnings. The summed
rotation loss barely moved while the images were memorised (\DiagOverfitRotFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitRotLast). And the
translation and 2D terms rose (\DiagOverfitTranslFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslLast, \DiagOverfitTwoDFirst{} $\to$
\DiagOverfitTwoDLast, first against last five epochs) while the pelvis-centred 3D terms fell: possibly a first sign of
the depth bug. That evidence is weak, since each value is a single-batch print and the half-run averages fall instead
(\DiagOverfitTranslHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslHalfB, \DiagOverfitTwoDHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTwoDHalfB).

The checks are blind to two faults, and both were present: a target built at a different joint than the model's
anchor, because the checks compare pelvis-centred quantities, and a bone flipped the same way in every person, because
the rig was tested in isolation and bone errors were averaged by group. The holdout has no children, so every child
number below is in-sample or from real photos.

\input{tables/T08_depth_runs}

Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement} is the placement section of the holdout trace. Base places every person too far
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}), and the nearest are pushed farthest. The skeleton has the right height, so the body is drawn at \DiagSizeBase{} of its true size; its
pelvis lands near the right pixel, its head does not. PVE is blind to all of it (correlation \DiagCorrPVEDepth).

The code gave the cause. The model places each mesh by its head joint and reports the head as its position, but the
training code built the translation, distance, detection-cell and offset targets at joint 0, the pelvis. A one-batch
debug print showed predicted offsets of \DiagOffsetMin{} to \DiagOffsetMax{} cells where the legal range is
$\pm$0.5. The likely mechanism, not tested on its own: the detection and offset terms pull the anchor pixel toward the
pelvis while the mesh hangs from it by the head, so every projected joint is displaced. The 2D terms, the only strong
terms that see depth (the 3D terms are pelvis-centred), can shrink that displacement only by drawing the body smaller,
that is, farther away; the distance and translation terms, \DiagLossShareDepthTerms\,\% of the loss, could not resist.
Near people have the largest displacement and were pushed farthest. Building the targets at the head
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}) is the fix, and Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs} tests it against same-schedule controls.

All five fix runs reach a root error of \DiagRootErrFixLow--\DiagRootErrFixHigh\,m, both controls stay at
\DiagRootErrCtrlLow--\DiagRootErrCtrlHigh\,m; the drawn size rises from \DiagSizeCtrlLow--\DiagSizeCtrlHigh{} to
\DiagSizeFixLow--\DiagSizeFixHigh{} and the head lands within 7--10 instead of 58--67 pixels. With real images and the
bone correction in training, Final keeps it: \DiagFinalRootErr{} against \DiagControlRootErr\,m for Control, size
\DiagFinalSize{} against \DiagControlSize. Every fix run lies below every control, far outside run-to-run variation.
Two other checkpoints trained with pelvis targets carry the bug too. It stayed hidden because PVE aligns the pelvis first and the real-photo demos place each body from its detection box; only the
synthetic demo and the trace panels used the model's own depth.

The table does not show correct depth. The fix moved the offset and the body size, not the slope (Final
\DiagFinalSlope; \DiagSlopeSwitch{} with the switch alone, \DiagSlopeStrongLow--\DiagSlopeStrongHigh{} with a stronger
depth loss); predictions are still pulled toward 2.5--2.9\,m for a reason I have not found. The pelvis 2D error rose
from about 23 to 31--41 pixels, likely because the head is now pinned while the body is still too small (not checked).
Every fix run is worse than its control by +\DiagFixCostPVELow{} to +\DiagFixCostPVEHigh\,mm PVE and
$-$\DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to $-$\DiagFixCostRecallHigh{} recall points: each gap within run-to-run variation, all in
the same direction, so a likely cost. By the success rule written beforehand (PVE $\le$ 71.5\,mm, recall $\ge$
89.2\,\%) every run failed, the controls included.

\section{The neck: a per-bone dump}
\label{sec:diag-neck}

\input{tables/T09_neck}

A per-bone dump of the neck chain found the fault (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}, first column). neck02 is
\DiagDumpNeckTwo\,deg wrong, a half-turn about its own axis (twist \DiagDumpNeckTwoTwist, swing
\DiagDumpNeckTwoSwing\,deg), and the head bone \DiagDumpHead\,deg. neck03 is right relative to its parent
(\DiagDumpNeckThree\,deg) but \DiagDumpNeckThreeGlobal\,deg off in the camera frame. The two half-turns cancel, so the
head points the right way (\DiagDumpHeadGlobal\,deg) and PVE never showed it; the skin between is twisted thin, girth
\DiagNeckGirthBase{} of the truth and neck-skin error \DiagNeckSkinBase\,mm. The earlier group means (62.7 and 89.5\,deg) hid the
flip. The flip is in all eight checkpoints of the Base line
(the three stages before Base, Base, Base+3DPW, the 10-epoch depth pilot and two geodesic-loss pilots; neck02
\DiagNeckTwoRangeLow--\DiagNeckTwoRangeHigh\,deg, girth \DiagGirthRangeLow--\DiagGirthRangeHigh), from the first stage
on, before the helper mask, the neck boost and the shape weight of 20. The depth pilot keeps it, so depth is not the
neck's cause. A fresh-head run from the same pretrained backbone has a normal neck (girth \DiagFreshGirth) but flips
other bones: a compensated pair in the left arm and three metacarpals.

This refutes the draft. The rig passes all 16 single-joint moves, and the normal rest-pose render fits the flip just as
well, because rest pose sets every rotation to the identity and removes both half-turns; only the dump separates the
two explanations. A loss cannot undo the flip: the neck boost (renormalised to $\times$2.41 for the boosted bones,
$\times$0.60 for the rest) left it unchanged, and a geodesic loss moved neck02 only from \DiagNeckTwoBase{} to
\DiagGeodesicNeckTwo\,deg, likely because neck02 and head must turn back together and any partial turn raises the loss.
So I corrected the output: one constant rotation per flipped bone, $R_b \leftarrow R_b C_b$, estimated on 200 training
images (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}); the constants are \DiagCorrNeckTwo, \DiagCorrHead, \DiagCorrMetacarpal{} and
\DiagCorrFinger\,deg for neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R.

Without training the correction turns neck02 back to \DiagNeckTwoNoTrain\,deg and restores girth (\DiagGirthNoTrain)
and neck-skin error (\DiagNeckSkinNoTrain\,mm), but the head direction worsens from \DiagHeadDirBase{} to
\DiagHeadDirNoTrain\,deg, above the 15-degree limit set beforehand. The likely reason is the compensated chain: the
head's error differs per person, so one constant cannot undo it. After a 2-epoch fine-tune with the correction the
head direction is \DiagHeadDirFineTune\,deg against \DiagHeadDirCtrlTwo\,deg for the control, which keeps the flip.
Body PVE is \DiagPVEBase, \DiagPVENoTrain{} and \DiagPVEFineTune{} against \DiagPVECtrlTwo\,mm for the control, a gap
within variation (the control also sits above six earlier arms): no sign of a PVE cost, not a gain. Final keeps the
correction through 10 epochs with real images (neck02 \DiagFinalNeckTwo\,deg, girth \DiagFinalGirth, head direction
\DiagFinalHeadDir{} against \DiagControlHeadDir\,deg for Control). Figure~\ref{fig:exp6-compare} shows two more holdout
pairs.

Not shown: why the bones flipped. The 6D start value of the rotation output is singular and starts a bone tilted about
its local $x$ axis, which fits the tilted metacarpal1.L but not neck02's twist; the origin is unknown, and the
correction, which adds no parameters, says nothing about it. A two-sided correction for compensated chains was not
implemented. Real photos have no neck ground truth; their pairs (Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}) are judged by eye and
also differ by the fine-tune.

\section{The fingers}
\label{sec:diag-fingers}

\input{tables/T10_fingers}

In Table~\ref{tab:fingers} predicted fingers are \DiagFingersBase\,deg off and fingers at rest \DiagFingersRest\,deg:
the prediction was worse than none. Hands are small, \DiagHandTokens{} tokens long at the median and under two tokens
for \DiagHandUnderTwo\,\%. A person crop at test time raises the median to 4.2 tokens, but the finger error stays at
\DiagFingersZoom\,deg while body PVE nearly doubles (\DiagZoomPVE\,mm), since the model never saw such a camera. A
region-normalised rotation loss gave \DiagFingersRegion{} against \DiagFingersRegionCtrl\,deg. The per-bone search on
training images then found two hand bones half-turned, metacarpal1.L (180\,deg) and finger4-1.R (178.8\,deg);
correcting them gives \DiagFingersFixNoTrain\,deg without training, \DiagFingersFixFT{} after the fine-tune, and
\DiagFingersFinal{} for Final against \DiagFingersControl\,deg for Control.

About half the finger error was two flipped bones, not resolution. Resolution was tested only by test-time zoom, which
did not help; a hand-crop branch was never tried. The region-normalised loss cut the hands' share of the rotation loss
from about 70\,\% (with the boost) to 33\,\%, a rebalance, not an upweighting. The fingers also have the smallest
gradients of any bone group; that they are under-trained is a guess I did not test.

Predicted fingers have not been shown on a real photo: every demo sets them to rest pose. Rest pose keeps the
metacarpals, so the \DiagFingersRest\,deg contains the metacarpal flip; a rest-pose floor with the correction, and the
split of the 9.1-degree drop between the two bones, were not measured.

\section{Children and body shape}
\label{sec:diag-children}

\input{tables/T18_shape_head}

Table~\ref{tab:shape-head}(a) is the draft's shape-weight test on 10 holdout images: raising the shape weight from 1
to 20 moved the age bias from \DiagShapeAgeBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeAgeBiasNew{} and the height bias from
\DiagShapeHeightBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeHeightBiasNew. Part~(b) shows that this holds near the mode only. On all
holdout people the age bias is \DiagAgeBiasHoldout, but nobody there is younger than \DiagHoldoutMinAge. In both tails
predictions move toward the training median: true ages 0.6--0.8 come out at \DiagTailOldPred, and the 50 youngest
training people (true \DiagBabyAgeTrue) at \DiagBabyAgePred. On real photos Base reads children as short adults. On
Relative Human (Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}) kids get a mean age of \DiagRHKidAge, babies \DiagRHBabyAge{} and adults
\DiagRHAdultAge; the child-versus-adult AUC is \DiagRHAUC, and none of \DiagRHKids{} kids and \DiagRHBabiesBelow{} of
\DiagRHBabies{} babies fall below the child threshold 0.45. The smallness goes into height instead (adults
\DiagRHAdultHeight, kids \DiagRHKidHeight, babies \DiagRHBabyHeight). Gender carries no signal (men
\DiagRHMenGender, women \DiagRHWomenGender, accuracy \DiagRHGenderAcc\,\%). On our demo photos the medians of all 299
shape lines (gender \DiagDemoGenderMedian, age \DiagDemoAgeMedian, height \DiagDemoHeightMedian) equal the training
medians (\DiagTrainGenderMedian, \DiagTrainAgeMedian, \DiagTrainHeightMedian). Their narrow age spread (standard
deviation \DiagDemoAgeStd{} against \DiagTrainAgeStd) is no evidence, since the lines are about 30 adults counted
several times; only the gender spread is (14\,\% of lines below 0.5 though men dominate).

Children and female-looking men are one failure: with weak image cues the shape head returns the training median.
Two mechanisms fit, and the evidence cannot rank them: the gap between synthetic and real images, or the loss. An L1
on sigmoid outputs regresses to the median, the sigmoid's gradient $s(1-s)$ is 0.25 at $s=0.5$ but 0.015 at
$s=0.015$, and children are rare (\DiagChildShare\,\% of training people below age 0.3, about
\DiagChildBatchShare\,\% of supervised people per batch); the 3DPW labels pin age at 0.55. Anny explains why the
failure looks like a short adult. Age blends five shapes at anchors $-1/3$, 0, 1/3, 2/3, 1 (newborn to old), and the
child shape appears below about 0.4--0.5. Its cue is narrow shoulders relative to the head: shoulder over head width
is \DiagShoulderHeadChild{} at age 0.1 and \DiagShoulderHeadAdult{} at 0.6. A low height at age 0.6 gives a short,
broad-shouldered adult (at least \DiagSmallestAdult\,m), and proportions barely matter (leg over stature
\DiagProportionsLeg); only a lower age gives a child.

Not shown: children on held-out synthetic images (there are none), which mechanism dominates, and ages in years, which
I never quote because the code uses three age scales that disagree. Part~(a) has $n = 10$. Final improves on Relative
Human (Chapter~\ref{ch:results}): a measured improvement, not a solution.

\section{Pilot noise and restart cost}
\label{sec:diag-noise}

\input{tables/T14_pilot_noise}

Two 2-epoch runs of one configuration differed by \DiagNoisePVE\,mm PVE (\DiagNoisePVEA{} against \DiagNoisePVEB),
\DiagNoiseHead\,deg head direction and \DiagNoiseRecall{} recall points (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-noise}); six arms of the
Base recipe span \DiagSixArmsPVELow--\DiagSixArmsPVEHigh\,mm. Restarting a fine-tune of Base costs about
\DiagRestartCost\,mm (controls \DiagControlsMean{} against \DiagPVEBase\,mm).

One pilot therefore resolves about 10\,mm PVE, 3\,deg and 2 recall points. That suffices for every change this chapter
relies on (root error, bone angles 175 to 5\,deg, girth 0.46 to 1.00, fingers 17.3 to 8.3\,deg), but not for a PVE
change of a few millimetres, so the costs of the depth fix and the neck pilot's PVE stay unresolved. Because of the
restart cost a pilot is read against its own control, never against Base alone.

There are no bootstrap intervals, the root error has no measured floor, and the training code sets no seed, so a
repeated run is a repeat, not a seeded run.

In Table~\ref{tab:diag-pilots}, the sampler's 4.6\,mm on 10 in-sample children is not

Each change altered the loss or the sampling around a fault the loss could not see: a half-turned bone, a target at the wrong

from the diagnostics.

None of this shows that an idea cannot work: the sampler is unproven after 8,000 images, and larger hand crops and a
10-epoch run at weight 20 were never tried.

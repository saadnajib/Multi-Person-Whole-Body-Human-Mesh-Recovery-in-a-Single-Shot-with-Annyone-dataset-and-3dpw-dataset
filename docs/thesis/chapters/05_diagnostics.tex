\chapter{Diagnostics: How Each Failure Was Isolated}
\label{ch:diagnostics}

Base showed three failures in its renders: thin, twisted necks, claw-like fingers and adult bodies on children.
Before changing any training setting, I tested each belief about their causes on its own, including the ones that
turned out wrong. Unless stated otherwise, every number is from Base on the \DiagHoldoutImages-image AnnyOne holdout in
setting V1, one run per arm without a seed, read against the variation of Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}.

In Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} (left) Base draws each neck as a thin stalk pinched under the head. The fingers
curled into claws, so every real-photo demo set them to rest pose. A standing boy in one of our photos received an
adult body (Figure~\ref{fig:real-photos}).

The draft gave each failure an untested cause: skinning of the stacked neck bones (``a property of the rig''),
finger resolution, and unlearned age, shown by five small people in our photos predicted at adult ages
(\DiagSmallPeopleAgeLow--\DiagSmallPeopleAgeHigh). The neck cause is wrong, resolution explains little, and the child
evidence was wrong: four of the five were adults who crouched, sat or were cut off by the frame (height phenotype
\DiagSmallPeopleHeightLow--\DiagSmallPeopleHeightHigh). Only the boy was a child, and he received age \DiagBoyAge{} and
height \DiagBoyHeight: an adult. The number behind the necks of the figure is the girth of Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}.

Table~\ref{tab:diag-checks} and Figure~\ref{fig:diag-gt-decode} rule out the rig, the label decoding and the gradient
path, as did the draft's four neck tests. Shape dominates the loss (\DiagLossShareShape\,\%; translation
\DiagLossShareTransl\,\%, distance \DiagLossShareDist\,\%). The cause had to be in what training asked of the network.
The overfit run gave two warnings: the rotation loss barely moved while the images were memorised
(\DiagOverfitRotFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitRotLast), and the translation and 2D terms rose (\DiagOverfitTranslFirst{}
$\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslLast, \DiagOverfitTwoDFirst{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTwoDLast, first against last five epochs),
possibly a first sign of the depth bug. That evidence is weak: the values are single-batch prints, and the half-run
averages fall (\DiagOverfitTranslHalfA{} $\to$ \DiagOverfitTranslHalfB, \DiagOverfitTwoDHalfA{} $\to$
\DiagOverfitTwoDHalfB).

The checks are blind to two faults, and both were present: a target at another joint than the anchor (they compare
pelvis-centred quantities) and a bone flipped the same way in every person (bone errors were averaged by group).

\input{tables/T08_depth_runs}

Table~\ref{tab:diag-placement} shows that Base places every person too far (Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}), the
nearest farthest. The skeleton has the right height, so the body is drawn at \DiagSizeBase{} of its true size; its
pelvis lands near the right pixel, its head does not. PVE is blind to all of it.

The code gave the cause: the mesh is placed by its head joint, but the anchor targets were built at the pelvis
(Section~\ref{sec:method-depth}). A one-batch debug print showed offsets of \DiagOffsetMin{} to \DiagOffsetMax{} cells
where the legal range is $\pm$0.5. The likely mechanism, not tested on its own: with the mesh hanging from a misplaced
head, the 2D terms can shrink the misfit only by drawing the body smaller, that is, farther away, and the direct depth
terms (\DiagLossShareDepthTerms\,\% of the loss) could not resist; near people have the largest misfit.

Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs} tests the fix against same-schedule controls. All five fix runs reach a root error of
\DiagRootErrFixLow--\DiagRootErrFixHigh\,m, both controls stay at \DiagRootErrCtrlLow--\DiagRootErrCtrlHigh\,m, the
drawn size rises from \DiagSizeCtrlLow--\DiagSizeCtrlHigh{} to \DiagSizeFixLow--\DiagSizeFixHigh, and the head lands
within 7--10 instead of 58--67 pixels: every fix run lies below every control, far outside run-to-run variation. Two
other checkpoints trained with pelvis targets carry the bug too. It stayed hidden because PVE aligns the pelvis first
and the real-photo demos place each body from its detection box.

Not shown is correct depth: the slope barely moved (\DiagSlopeSwitch{} with the switch alone,
\DiagSlopeStrongLow--\DiagSlopeStrongHigh{} with a stronger depth loss), and predictions are still pulled toward
2.5--2.9\,m for a reason I have not found. The pelvis 2D error rose to 31--41 pixels, likely because the head is now
pinned while the body is still too small (not checked). Every fix run is worse than its control by
+\DiagFixCostPVELow{} to +\DiagFixCostPVEHigh\,mm PVE and $-$\DiagFixCostRecallLow{} to $-$\DiagFixCostRecallHigh{}
recall points: each gap within variation, all in one direction, so a likely cost. By the success rule written
beforehand (PVE $\le$ 71.5\,mm, recall $\ge$ 89.2\,\%) every run failed, the controls included.

\section{The neck: a per-bone dump}
\label{sec:diag-neck}

\input{tables/T09_neck}

A per-bone dump of the neck chain found the fault (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}, first column). neck02 is
\DiagDumpNeckTwo\,deg wrong, a half-turn about its own axis (twist \DiagDumpNeckTwoTwist, swing
\DiagDumpNeckTwoSwing\,deg), and the head bone \DiagDumpHead\,deg; neck03 is right relative to its parent
(\DiagDumpNeckThree\,deg) but \DiagDumpNeckThreeGlobal\,deg off in the camera frame. The half-turns cancel, so the head
points the right way (\DiagDumpHeadGlobal\,deg); the skin between is twisted thin. Group means (62.7 and 89.5\,deg) had
hidden the flip. It is in all eight checkpoints of the Base line (neck02
\DiagNeckTwoRangeLow--\DiagNeckTwoRangeHigh\,deg, girth \DiagGirthRangeLow--\DiagGirthRangeHigh), from the first stage
on, before the helper mask, the neck boost and the shape weight of 20; the depth pilot keeps it, so depth is not the
neck's cause. Fresh-heads-L has a normal neck (girth \DiagFreshGirth) but flips other bones: a compensated pair in the
left arm and three metacarpals.

This refutes the draft: the rig passes all 16 single-joint moves, and the normal rest-pose render fits the flip
too, because rest pose sets every rotation to the identity. A loss did not undo the flip: the neck boost ($\times$2.41 for the boosted bones after renormalisation) left it unchanged,
and a geodesic loss moved neck02 only from \DiagNeckTwoBase{} to \DiagGeodesicNeckTwo\,deg, likely because neck02 and
head must turn back together. So I corrected the output with one constant rotation per flipped bone
(Section~\ref{sec:method-bonefix}): \DiagCorrNeckTwo, \DiagCorrHead, \DiagCorrMetacarpal{} and \DiagCorrFinger\,deg
for neck02, head, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R.

Without training the correction restores neck02, girth and neck-skin error, but the head direction worsens to
\DiagHeadDirNoTrain\,deg, above the 15-degree limit set beforehand, likely because the head's error differs per
person; a 2-epoch fine-tune brings it to \DiagHeadDirFineTune\,deg (control \DiagHeadDirCtrlTwo). The body-PVE gaps
lie within variation. Figure~\ref{fig:exp6-compare} shows two more holdout pairs. Not shown: why the bones flipped
(the singular 6D start value fits the tilted metacarpal1.L, not neck02's twist), and necks on real photos, which have
no ground truth (Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}, by eye).

\section{The fingers}
\label{sec:diag-fingers}

\input{tables/T10_fingers}

In Table~\ref{tab:fingers} predicted fingers are \DiagFingersBase\,deg off and fingers at rest \DiagFingersRest\,deg:
the prediction was worse than none. Hands are small (\DiagHandTokens{} tokens long at the median, under two tokens for
\DiagHandUnderTwo\,\%), but a person crop at test time leaves the finger error at \DiagFingersZoom\,deg while body PVE
nearly doubles, and a region-normalised rotation loss (a rebalance from about 70 to 33\,\% of the rotation loss, not an
upweighting) changed nothing. The per-bone search on training images then found metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R
half-turned; correcting them roughly halves the finger error, with and without training.

About half the finger error was two flipped bones, not resolution. Resolution was tested only by test-time zoom; a
hand-crop branch was never tried, and a rest-pose floor with the correction was not measured. Predicted fingers on
real photos are in Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}.

\section{Children and body shape}
\label{sec:diag-children}

\input{tables/T18_shape_head}

Table~\ref{tab:shape-head}(a) is the draft's shape-weight test on 10 holdout images: raising the shape weight from 1
to 20 moved the age bias from \DiagShapeAgeBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeAgeBiasNew{} and the height bias from
\DiagShapeHeightBiasOld{} to \DiagShapeHeightBiasNew. Part~(b) shows that this holds near the mode only: in both
tails predictions move toward the training median (the holdout has no children, so the young tail is in-sample). On Relative Human (Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}) Base reads children
as short adults: kids get a mean age of \DiagRHKidAge, babies \DiagRHBabyAge{} and adults \DiagRHAdultAge{} (AUC
\DiagRHAUC), none of \DiagRHKids{} kids and \DiagRHBabiesBelow{} of \DiagRHBabies{} babies fall below the child
threshold; the smallness goes into height instead. Gender carries no signal (accuracy \DiagRHGenderAcc\,\%). On
our demo photos the medians of all 299 shape lines (gender \DiagDemoGenderMedian, age \DiagDemoAgeMedian, height
\DiagDemoHeightMedian) equal the training medians.

Children and female-looking men are one failure: with weak image cues the shape head returns the training median.
Two mechanisms fit, and the evidence cannot rank them: the gap between synthetic and real images, or the loss (an L1
on sigmoid outputs regresses to the median, the sigmoid's gradient is 0.25 at 0.5 but 0.015 at 0.015, and only
\DiagChildShare\,\% of training people are below age 0.3). It looks like a short adult because Anny's child shape (shoulder over head width \DiagShoulderHeadChild{} at age
0.1, \DiagShoulderHeadAdult{} at 0.6) appears only below an age of about 0.4--0.5: a low height at age 0.6 gives a
short adult (at least \DiagSmallestAdult\,m), and proportions barely matter (leg over stature \DiagProportionsLeg).

Not shown: which mechanism dominates, and ages in years (the code uses three age scales that disagree).

\section{What did not work}
\label{sec:diag-negative}

Six changes did not fix a failure (Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}, Table~\ref{tab:pilots-all}): an age-balanced sampler
(inconclusive on 10 in-sample children), a global head orientation loss (within the spread of two identical controls),
a region-normalised hand loss, a geodesic rotation loss, the neck and hand boost, and a $\times$20 depth loss (no gain
over $\times$5). Each altered the loss or the sampling around a fault the loss could not see; the two fixes that worked
came from the diagnostics. None of this shows that an idea cannot work: the sampler is unproven after 8,000 images,
and a 10-epoch run at weight 20 was never tried.

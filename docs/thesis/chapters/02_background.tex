\chapter{Background and Related Work}
\label{ch:background}

This chapter introduces the body models, networks, data and training ideas the thesis builds on, and for each prior
work it says which choice of mine it explains or constrains. What the thesis adds is listed in
Section~\ref{sec:intro-contrib}.

\section{Parametric body models: the SMPL family and Anny}
\label{sec:bg-bodymodels}

A parametric body model is a differentiable function
$M(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\beta}) \rightarrow \mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 3}$ that maps a pose
$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and a shape $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ to the $N$ vertices of a mesh. The shape deforms a template
mesh in its rest pose. The pose moves a skeleton of bones, and the skin follows by \emph{linear blend skinning}
(LBS):
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i' \;=\; \sum_{k} w_{ik}\, \mathbf{G}_k(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\beta})\, \mathbf{v}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta}),
  \label{eq:bg-lbs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ is the shaped rest vertex, $w_{ik}$ the skinning weight of bone $k$ and
$\mathbf{G}_k$ the bone's world transform, found by chaining the rotations of all bones from the root down to $k$.
Because the transforms chain, a wrong rotation high in the tree moves everything below it. This matters in
Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}, where two neck bones turned by half a turn leave the head pointing the right way but
twist the skin between them.

\paragraph{The SMPL family.}
SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl} has \num{\BgSmplVertices} vertices and \BgSmplJoints{} joints. Its shape space is a set of
principal components learned from registered scans of adults; most methods use the first \BgSmplBetas{}
coefficients. These are statistical directions, not named properties: no coefficient means ``older'' or ``more
muscular''. Children lie outside the space. SMIL~\cite{hesse2018smil} is a separate infant model, and
AGORA~\cite{patel2021agora} and BEV~\cite{sun2022bev} blend the adult template towards an infant one with one extra
coefficient. SMPL-H~\cite{romero2017mano} adds MANO hands with \BgManoHandJoints{} joints each, and
SMPL-X~\cite{pavlakos2019smplx} adds a face, for \BgSmplxJoints{} joints on \num{\BgSmplxVertices} vertices. SMPL is
licensed for non-commercial research only, and every SMPL-based network and dataset inherits these terms.
Multi-HMR, the network this thesis starts from, predicts SMPL-X~\cite{baradel2024multihmr}, so moving it to Anny
meant replacing the whole output layer.

\paragraph{Anny.}
Anny~\cite{bregier2025anny} is a body model by NAVER LABS Europe built from the open MakeHuman
project~\cite{makehuman} instead of from scans. Its code is under the Apache~2.0 licence and its assets under
CC0~\cite{naver2025annycode}. I use version~\BgAnnyVersion{} with the full rig of \BgAnnyBones{} bones and a mesh of
\num{\BgAnnyVertices} vertices, the rig of the AnnyOne labels and of NAVER's checkpoint (the pruned
\BgAnnyDefaultRigBones-bone default rig of newer versions is not used). Pose is one rigid transform per bone, applied
by Equation~\eqref{eq:bg-lbs}. Of the bones, \BgAnnyHelperBones{} are helper bones (toes, breasts, face micro-bones)
whose rotation an image cannot reveal; I keep them at rest, which leaves \BgAnnyActiveBones{} predicted bones. The
neck is modelled by three stacked bones, \texttt{neck01} to \texttt{neck03}, followed by \texttt{head}.

Shape is set by \BgAnnyPhenotypes{} \emph{phenotype} values between 0 and 1: \texttt{gender}, \texttt{age},
\texttt{muscle}, \texttt{weight}, \texttt{height}, \texttt{proportions}, \texttt{cupsize}, \texttt{firmness}, and
three blend weights \texttt{african}, \texttt{asian} and \texttt{caucasian}. Each value drives artist-made blend
shapes, vertex offsets of the rest mesh. Because the values have names, a predicted body can be read as ``older'' or
``heavier''. Gender 0 is the male end and 1 the female end (rest stature \BgStatureGenderZero\,m and
\BgStatureGenderOne\,m). Age blends five anchor bodies placed at $\mathrm{linspace}(-\tfrac{1}{3}, 1, 5)$: age 0 is
the baby, \BgAgeAnchorChild{} the child, \BgAgeAnchorYoung{} the young adult and 1 the old adult; the newborn would
need an age below 0, which a network output squashed into $(0,1)$ never gives.

Figure~\ref{fig:bg-age-sweep} shows what age does. At the mean AnnyOne shape, moving age from 0 to 1 raises the
skeletal height from \BgSweepHeightAgeZero\,m to \BgSweepHeightAgeOne\,m (\BgSweepHeightAgeHalf\,m at 0.5); most of
the change happens below about 0.5. A small height value does not make a child: at age 0.6 the smallest reachable
body is \BgSmallestAdultStature\,m tall and keeps adult proportions. What marks a child is narrow shoulders relative
to the head, a shoulder-to-head width ratio of \BgShoulderHeadChild{} at age 0.1 against \BgShoulderHeadAdult{} at
0.6. Only a low age produces it, which is why Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} treats children as an age problem. I never
convert ages to years, because three scales are in use and disagree: the value read as 11~years is
\BgElevenYearsScaleC{} in Anny's calibration, \BgElevenYearsScaleA{} in the code I inherited and
\BgElevenYearsScaleB{} on the MakeHuman slider. I call a person \emph{child-shaped} if the age value is below about
\BgChildShapeAge.

\paragraph{AnnyOne.}
AnnyOne is NAVER's synthetic dataset for Anny: rendered scenes of several people with exact Anny labels (all bone
transforms, all phenotypes and the camera). Its authors describe more than \num{\BgAnnyOnePublished}
images~\cite{bravo2026annyfit}; the release copy I train on holds \num{\BgAnnyOneImages} images. It is the only
source of complete Anny labels, shape included, that I have (Section~\ref{sec:data-annyone}).

\section{Single-person and multi-person human mesh recovery}
\label{sec:bg-hmr}

\paragraph{Regression and fitting.}
HMR~\cite{kanazawa2018hmr} regressed body-model parameters for one cropped person with a convolutional encoder, an
iterative regressor and an adversarial pose prior. HMR~2.0~\cite{goel2023humans4d} replaced the encoder by a Vision
Transformer (ViT)~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} and showed that a large ViT trained on a broad data mixture is a strong
and simple recipe, which Multi-HMR shares. My model follows the same principle: the network outputs the parameters
directly, with no optimisation at test time. SMPLify~\cite{bogo2016smplify} goes the other way and fits SMPL to 2D
keypoints by optimisation; SPIN~\cite{kolotouros2019spin} puts such a fit inside the training loop. My 3DPW converter
(Chapter~\ref{ch:data}) is an optimisation of the SMPLify kind, but in 3D, between two body models, and run once
offline.

\paragraph{Single-shot multi-person: ROMP and Multi-HMR.}
Two-stage methods run a detector and then a single-person network on each crop, so the cost grows with the number of
people. ROMP~\cite{sun2021romp} does both in one pass: it predicts a heatmap of body centres and reads body
parameters at the detected centres. Multi-HMR~\cite{baradel2024multihmr}, the architecture of this thesis, keeps this
idea. A DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} ViT turns the image into one token per
$\BgPatch{\times}\BgPatch$-pixel patch, a $\BgTokenGrid{\times}\BgTokenGrid$ grid at \BgInputRes{} pixels. Each
token scores whether a person's primary keypoint, the head joint in the released code~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode},
lies in it. High-scoring tokens become the queries of the \emph{Human Perception Head} (HPH), which attends to all
image tokens and regresses the body parameters and the person's distance from the camera. That distance belongs to
the head joint, and this anchor is at the centre of the depth defect of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}.
Section~\ref{sec:method-architecture} gives the details. The released SMPL-X checkpoints reach a PVE of
\BgMhrLPVE\,mm on the 3DPW test set with ViT-L in \BgMhrLMs\,ms and \BgMhrBPVE\,mm with ViT-B in \BgMhrBMs\,ms on a
V100~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode}, which is why my smaller model is a ViT-B student. These are SMPL-X errors and
cannot be compared with the Anny-space errors of this thesis.

\paragraph{BEV and Relative Human.}
BEV~\cite{sun2022bev} extends ROMP with explicit depth reasoning and an age-aware body. Its authors collected
Relative Human, real photos in which every person carries an age group (adult, teenager, kid, baby), a depth layer and
keypoints. It is the only real data with age labels I could use, so I use it, for evaluation only, to test whether
predicted ages and genders follow the people on real photos (Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}). It has no 3D bodies.

\paragraph{Multi-HMR-Anny and Anny-Fit.}
In February 2026 NAVER released a Multi-HMR checkpoint that predicts Anny, trained on BEDLAM, AnnyOne and further
real images~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode}. A multi-person HMR network for Anny therefore already exists, and this
thesis does not claim to be the first. This checkpoint, Multi-HMR-Anny, is where my backbone weights come from and the
baseline I compare against (Section~\ref{sec:res-baseline}). On the same backbone its decoder has
\BgNaverHeadLayers{} cross-attention blocks and \BgNaverHeadParams\,M parameters, against \BgOurHeadLayers{} blocks
and \BgOurHeadParams\,M in mine. It was trained on AnnyOne, which includes the images I hold out, so its AnnyOne error
is most likely an in-sample number. Anny-Fit~\cite{bravo2026annyfit}, by the same group, refines such predictions per
image by optimisation and adapts them to children at test time; my model stays a single forward pass. I did not use
it; it is the natural comparison for future work on children.

\section{Synthetic training data}
\label{sec:bg-synthetic}

AGORA~\cite{patel2021agora} renders scanned people with SMPL-X fits into realistic scenes, and
BEDLAM~\cite{black2023bedlam} showed that synthetic images alone can train competitive networks. AnnyOne does the
same for Anny. Synthetic labels are exact and complete, but leave open how well a network transfers to real photos. In
this thesis my shape predictions follow the labels on the synthetic holdout but collapse towards the training average
on real photos (Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}). This is why Final mixes \BgMixReal\,\% real images into every batch,
and why those images need Anny labels.

\section{Real-data labels for a new body model}
\label{sec:bg-labels}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is the standard real-world test set for mesh recovery: \BgThreeDPWSequences{} video
sequences with SMPL ground truth for one or two actors each. SMPL and Anny share neither skeleton nor shape space, so
the labels must be re-expressed in Anny by fitting. The SMPL-X code transfers fits between SMPL-family models through a
vertex correspondence~\cite{pavlakos2019smplx}, and the Anny package offers an inverter that fits Anny to a target
mesh~\cite{naver2025annycode}; none turns a 3DPW label into an Anny label directly, so I wrote my own converter
(Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). A fit to joint positions recovers only what joint positions determine; what the
converted labels can and cannot test is listed in Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}. SHAPY~\cite{choutas2022shapy}
supervises shape on real images with measurements and word attributes instead; Anny's named phenotypes would suit
such labels, but I collected none.

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:bg-kd}

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} trains a small \emph{student} to reproduce a large, trained
\emph{teacher}, originally by matching its class probabilities softened by a temperature.
FitNets~\cite{romero2015fitnets} let the student also match an intermediate representation of the teacher through a
small learned regressor. DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} distilled its smaller ViTs from its largest one, so a DINOv2
ViT-B is a good starting point for a student. My Student combines both forms: a projector maps its
\BgProjIn-wide ViT-B tokens to the teacher's \BgProjOut-wide tokens, which lets the teacher's heads be copied
unchanged (Section~\ref{sec:method-kd}). It has \BgStudentParams\,M parameters against \BgFinalParams\,M, and the
question is whether it keeps at least \BgNinetyFive\,\% of Final's accuracy on both test sets.

\section{Temporal models for video}
\label{sec:bg-temporal}

My model works on single frames, and the temporal requirement (2b) has not been started. Most video methods refine
per-frame features of an image network: HMMR~\cite{kanazawa2019hmmr} with a temporal convolution,
VIBE~\cite{kocabas2020vibe} with a recurrent network and a motion discriminator tied to SMPL,
TCMR~\cite{choi2021tcmr} with branches that predict the current frame from its neighbours to reduce jitter, and
MPS-Net~\cite{wei2022mpsnet} and GLoT~\cite{shen2023glot} with attention over frames. SmoothNet~\cite{zeng2022smoothnet}
smooths the output poses of any estimator without image features. PHALP~\cite{rajasegaran2022phalp} and
4DHumans~\cite{goel2023humans4d} track people through video, the first step of any multi-person video method, and
WHAM~\cite{shin2024wham} places the motion in world coordinates.

The plan for 2b (Section~\ref{sec:future}) is a small refiner of the first kind on the frozen Final model: it reads the
per-person HPH tokens over a window of frames, is trained on 3DPW-train tracks, and is judged by acceleration error
and PVE against SmoothNet-style smoothing, the baseline it would have to beat.

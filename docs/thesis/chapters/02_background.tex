\chapter{Background and related work}
\label{ch:background}

The network of this thesis predicts bodies in the Anny body model, whereas its real images, 3DPW, are labelled in
SMPL; both models are therefore described first.

\section{The body models SMPL and Anny}
\label{sec:bg-bodymodels}

A parametric body model is a differentiable function
$M(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\beta}) \rightarrow \mathbf{V}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times 3}$ that maps pose parameters
$\boldsymbol{\theta}$ and shape parameters $\boldsymbol{\beta}$ to the $N$ vertices of a triangle mesh, so that a
network can be trained through it. The shape deforms a rest-pose template; the pose articulates a skeleton, and linear
blend skinning (LBS) transfers its motion to the surface:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{v}_i' \;=\; \sum_{k} w_{ik}\, \mathbf{G}_k(\boldsymbol{\theta},\boldsymbol{\beta})\, \mathbf{v}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta}),
  \label{eq:bg-lbs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{v}_i(\boldsymbol{\beta})$ is the shaped rest vertex, $w_{ik}$ the skinning weight of bone $k$ and
$\mathbf{G}_k$ the world transform of bone $k$, obtained by composing the rotations along the kinematic chain from the
root to $k$. A rotation error near the root therefore propagates to every bone below it.

\paragraph{The SMPL family.}
SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl}, the body model of most prior work and of the 3DPW labels, has \num{\BgSmplVertices}
vertices and \BgSmplJoints{} joints. Its shape space consists of principal components of registered adult scans; most
methods use the first \BgSmplBetas{} coefficients (betas), statistical directions without individual semantics. Child
proportions are represented poorly. SMIL~\cite{hesse2018smil} is a separate infant model; AGORA~\cite{patel2021agora}
and BEV~\cite{sun2022bev} blend the adult template towards an infant one with one extra coefficient.
SMPL-H~\cite{romero2017mano} adds MANO hands with \BgManoHandJoints{} joints each, and SMPL-X~\cite{pavlakos2019smplx}
also a face, for \BgSmplxJoints{} joints on \num{\BgSmplxVertices} vertices. SMPL has a research licence; assets,
datasets and trained weights carry separate terms. Multi-HMR~\cite{baradel2024multihmr} was published for SMPL-X; a
later checkpoint released by NAVER replaced its output layer with Anny (Section~\ref{sec:bg-hmr}), and the teacher is
initialised from its backbone weights with its own heads (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).

\paragraph{Anny.}
Anny~\cite{bregier2025anny}, developed at NAVER LABS Europe, is derived from the open, artist-made MakeHuman
model~\cite{makehuman} rather than from scans; code and assets are licensed under Apache~2.0 and
CC0~\cite{naver2025annycode}. This thesis uses the full rig of \BgAnnyBones{} bones, for which the AnnyOne labels are
written. The package loads a pruned rig of \BgAnnyDefaultRigBones{} bones by default, which matches neither the
checkpoint nor the labels; package version, rig and mesh must be those used to create them. Pose is one rigid
transform per bone, applied by Equation~\eqref{eq:bg-lbs}. \BgAnnyHelperBones{} helper bones (toes, breasts, small
face bones), whose rotation an image does not show, are not supervised, leaving \BgAnnyActiveBones{} supervised bones (Section~\ref{sec:method-architecture}).
The neck has the stacked bones \texttt{neck01} to \texttt{neck03}, followed by \texttt{head}.

Shape is set by \BgAnnyPhenotypes{} named \emph{phenotype} values in $[0,1]$, among them \texttt{gender},
\texttt{age}, \texttt{weight} and the blend weights \texttt{african}, \texttt{asian} and \texttt{caucasian}
(Table~\ref{tab:body-models}). Each drives artist-made blend shapes (rest-vertex offsets), so predictions are
directly interpretable (older, heavier). Gender 0 is the male end and 1 the female end,
with rest statures of \BgStatureGenderZero\,m and \BgStatureGenderOne\,m. Age interpolates five anchor bodies spaced
evenly from $-\tfrac{1}{3}$ to 1: age 0 is the baby, \BgAgeAnchorChild{} the child, \BgAgeAnchorYoung{} the young adult
and 1 the old adult. The newborn anchor lies below 0 and is unreachable for a network output bounded to $(0,1)$ by a sigmoid.

At the mean AnnyOne shape, increasing age from 0 to 1 raises the skeletal height from \BgSweepHeightAgeZero\,m to
\BgSweepHeightAgeOne\,m (\BgSweepHeightAgeHalf\,m at 0.5; Figure~\ref{fig:bg-age-sweep}), so most growth occurs below
about 0.5. A low height value alone does not yield child proportions: at age 0.6 the smallest reachable body is
\BgSmallestAdultStature\,m tall with adult proportions. Child proportions are marked by narrow shoulders relative to
the head; the shoulder-to-head width ratio is \BgShoulderHeadChild{} at age 0.1 and \BgShoulderHeadAdult{} at 0.6, and
only a low age value produces the former. Ages are not converted to years, because three scales in the code disagree;
a person is called \emph{child-shaped} if the age value is below about \BgChildShapeAge.

\paragraph{AnnyOne.}
AnnyOne is NAVER's synthetic multi-person dataset for Anny, with exact labels (all bone transforms and phenotypes of
every person, and the camera). Its authors describe more than \num{\BgAnnyOnePublished}
images~\cite{bravo2026annyfit}; the release copy used here holds \num{\BgAnnyOneImages}. It is the only source of
complete Anny labels, shape included (Section~\ref{sec:data-annyone}).

\section{Image features and attention}
\label{sec:bg-transformer}

A Vision Transformer (ViT)~\cite{dosovitskiy2021vit} divides an image into square patches and embeds each as a token.
Attention exchanges information between tokens through queries $\mathbf Q$, keys $\mathbf K_a$ and values
$\mathbf V_a$:
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{Attention}(\mathbf Q,\mathbf K_a,\mathbf V_a)
 =\operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\frac{\mathbf Q\mathbf K_a^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mathbf V_a ,
 \label{eq:bg-attention}
\end{equation}
where $d_k$ is the key dimension; each query returns a mean of the values weighted by its similarity to the keys. The
subscript $a$ distinguishes these keys from the camera intrinsics $\mathbf K$ (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Multi-HMR uses
self-attention among image patches and cross-attention from person queries to all image tokens. Both backbones are
DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} ViTs as released by NAVER with Multi-HMR, a ViT-L for the teacher and a ViT-B for the
student; the smaller token width of the ViT-B requires a projector (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}).

\section{Single-person and multi-person mesh recovery}
\label{sec:bg-hmr}

Human mesh recovery (HMR) estimates the parameters of a body model from an image.

\paragraph{Regression and fitting.}
HMR~\cite{kanazawa2018hmr} regressed body-model parameters for one cropped person with a convolutional encoder, an
iterative regressor and an adversarial pose prior, a discriminator separating plausible from implausible poses.
HMR~2.0~\cite{goel2023humans4d} replaced the encoder with a ViT and showed that a large ViT trained on a broad dataset
mixture performs well without further components. Multi-HMR and the models of this thesis follow this approach and
regress the parameters directly, without test-time optimisation. SMPLify~\cite{bogo2016smplify} instead fits SMPL to
2D keypoints by optimisation, and SPIN~\cite{kolotouros2019spin} places such a fit in the training loop. The 3DPW
converter (Chapter~\ref{ch:data}) is a SMPLify-type optimisation, but in 3D, between two body models, run once offline.

\paragraph{Single-shot multi-person methods.}
Two-stage methods apply a person detector and then a single-person network to each crop, so their cost grows with the
number of people. ROMP~\cite{sun2021romp} performs both in one pass (single-shot): it predicts a heatmap of body centres
and reads the body parameters there. Multi-HMR~\cite{baradel2024multihmr}, the architecture of this thesis, retains
this principle. Its ViT produces one token per $\BgPatch{\times}\BgPatch$-pixel patch, a
$\BgTokenGrid{\times}\BgTokenGrid$ grid for the $\BgInputRes{\times}\BgInputRes$-pixel input, and each token scores
whether a person's primary keypoint, the head joint in the released code~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode}, lies within it.
High-scoring tokens become queries of the \emph{Human Perception Head} (HPH), which attends to all image tokens and
predicts the body parameters and the camera distance of the head joint. This joint, the anchor, is central to the depth
defect in Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}; Section~\ref{sec:method-architecture} gives details.

The released SMPL-X checkpoints reach a PVE (mean vertex error, Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}) on the 3DPW test set of
\BgMhrLPVE\,mm with ViT-L in \BgMhrLMs\,ms and \BgMhrBPVE\,mm with ViT-B in \BgMhrBMs\,ms on a V100
GPU~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode}. The ViT-B is equally accurate and faster, which motivates a ViT-B student; these
SMPL-X errors are not comparable with the Anny-space errors of this thesis.

\paragraph{BEV and Relative Human.}
BEV~\cite{sun2022bev} extends ROMP with each person's relative depth and a body model with an age parameter. Its
authors collected Relative Human, real photographs annotating each person with an age group (adult, teenager, kid,
baby), a depth layer and 2D keypoints, but no 3D body; it is the only available real dataset with age labels and is
used for evaluation only (Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}).

\paragraph{Multi-HMR-Anny and Anny-Fit.}
NAVER has released a Multi-HMR checkpoint that predicts Anny, trained on BEDLAM, AnnyOne and further real
images~\cite{naver2024multihmrcode}; multi-person Anny prediction is therefore prior work. This checkpoint, the released baseline, provides the teacher's backbone weights
and serves as baseline. On the same backbone its decoder has \BgNaverHeadLayers{} cross-attention blocks
and \BgNaverHeadParams\,M parameters, against \BgOurHeadLayers{} blocks and \BgOurHeadParams\,M in the models of this
thesis; trained on AnnyOne, it may have seen the AnnyOne holdout (Section~\ref{sec:res-fairness}).
Anny-Fit~\cite{bravo2026annyfit}, by the same group, refines such predictions per image by optimisation and adapts them
to children at test time. It was not used, since the models here remain single forward passes, but it is the first
comparison for future work on children.

\section{Synthetic training data}
\label{sec:bg-synthetic}

AGORA~\cite{patel2021agora} renders scanned people with SMPL-X fits into realistic scenes, and
BEDLAM~\cite{black2023bedlam} showed that synthetic images alone can train competitive networks; AnnyOne does the same
for Anny. Synthetic labels are exact and complete, but the domain gap to real images can reduce accuracy on
photographs. In this thesis, shape predictions followed the labels on the synthetic holdout (AnnyOne images excluded
from training) but collapsed towards the training mean on real photographs (Section~\ref{sec:res-rh}). The teacher
therefore draws \BgMixReal\,\% of every batch from real images, which require Anny labels.

\section{Real-image labels for a new body model}
\label{sec:bg-labels}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is the standard real-world benchmark for mesh recovery: \BgThreeDPWSequences{} video
sequences with SMPL labels for one or two actors each. As SMPL and Anny differ in skeleton and shape space, the labels
must be refitted in Anny. The SMPL-X code transfers fits between SMPL-family models through a vertex
correspondence~\cite{pavlakos2019smplx}, and the Anny package fits Anny to a target mesh~\cite{naver2025annycode};
neither converts a 3DPW label into an Anny label directly, so a dedicated converter was written
(Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). It fits joint positions only and recovers only what they determine
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). SHAPY~\cite{choutas2022shapy} supervises shape on real images with body measurements
and linguistic attributes; Anny's named phenotypes would suit such labels, but none were collected.

\section{Knowledge distillation}
\label{sec:bg-kd}

Knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling} trains a small \emph{student} network to reproduce the outputs of a
large, trained \emph{teacher}, originally by matching its class probabilities softened by a temperature (output
distillation). FitNets~\cite{romero2015fitnets} also match an intermediate teacher representation through a small
learned regressor (feature distillation). DINOv2~\cite{oquab2023dinov2} distilled its smaller ViTs from its largest,
making a DINOv2 ViT-B a suitable student initialisation. The student uses both forms (Section~\ref{sec:method-kd}).
It has \BgStudentParams\,M parameters against the teacher's \BgFinalParams\,M.

\paragraph{The projector.}
The student is initialised with a copy of the teacher's prediction heads. Its features have \BgProjIn{} channels,
the teacher's features and heads \BgProjOut. A projector, one linear layer, maps one width to the other, so that student
features can be compared with the teacher's and fed to the copied heads; it therefore remains in the deployed student.

\paragraph{The temperature.}
After the projector, each patch token is a vector of \BgProjOut{} channel values. For teacher and student, the softmax
over the channel values of each token is computed at temperature $T$ (after division by $T$), and the
Kullback--Leibler (KL) divergence between the two distributions is the feature loss, zero when they coincide. A larger
$T$ flattens the distribution, so that smaller channel responses also contribute (illustratively, weights
[\BgTempExampleHigh, \BgTempExampleLow] at $T=1$ become about [\BgTempExampleSoftHigh, \BgTempExampleSoftLow] at
$T=\MethodKDTemp$). The distributions describe feature activation patterns, not body-part probabilities. Because
softening scales the gradient by about $1/T^2$, the loss, and with it the gradient, is scaled by $T^2$ to keep gradient
magnitudes comparable across temperatures~\cite{hinton2015distilling}.
% Source of the T^2 factor: distill.py:16-19 (docstring) and :97 (kl * T**2).

Output distillation is an L1 loss between student and teacher rotations, phenotypes and distances, weighted as in
the supervised loss and without temperature.

The reported student uses $T=\MethodKDTemp$; Section~\ref{sec:res-kd-temperature} examines other values.

\section{Temporal models for video}
\label{sec:bg-temporal}

Most video methods refine the per-frame features of an image network: HMMR~\cite{kanazawa2019hmmr} with a temporal
convolution, VIBE~\cite{kocabas2020vibe} with a recurrent network and an SMPL motion discriminator, and
TCMR~\cite{choi2021tcmr} with branches predicting the current frame from its neighbours to reduce jitter
(frame-to-frame fluctuation). MPS-Net~\cite{wei2022mpsnet} and GLoT~\cite{shen2023glot} attend over frames.
SmoothNet~\cite{zeng2022smoothnet} is a learned network that smooths the output poses of any estimator over a window.
PHALP~\cite{rajasegaran2022phalp} and 4DHumans~\cite{goel2023humans4d} track people through video, the first step of
any multi-person video method, and WHAM~\cite{shin2024wham} recovers motion in world coordinates.

The student is frame-wise, so its predicted body can jitter or change size between frames. Two inference-time steps
are added without retraining (Section~\ref{sec:method-video}). An exponential moving
average blends each tracked person's new bone rotations, phenotypes and position with the previous estimates; like
SmoothNet it smooths outputs without image features, but it is a fixed formula, causal (only current and past frames
contribute) and operates on body-model parameters. The body's position and depth are then adjusted to match 2D
landmarks from a separate detector. Learned temporal models remain future work.

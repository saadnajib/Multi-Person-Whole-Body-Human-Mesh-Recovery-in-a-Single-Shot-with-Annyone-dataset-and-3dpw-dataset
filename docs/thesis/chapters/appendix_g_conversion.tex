\chapter{Supporting conversion experiments}
\label{app:conversion}

Chapter~\ref{ch:data} describes the final converter, version 2.3; this appendix documents its development and the
history of the 3DPW label sets. No earlier version produced labels used for the main results. The corrected labels of
Chapter~\ref{ch:data} are those built from the version 2.3 fits; ``v23'' in file names and in the appendix tables
denotes these corrected labels.

\section{Development of the converter}
\label{sec:conversion-history}

The first converter (v1) matched SMPL joints to Anny bones by name. Its mean joint error on
\texttt{downtown\_bar\_00} was \DataVOneBarMean\,mm; on four test scenes the error stayed at \DataVOneFloorMin{} to
\DataVOneFloorMax\,mm in every frame, and all four actors received the same body. An error independent of pose and a
body independent of the person indicate that the shape parameters absorbed a fixed mismatch between the two skeletons
instead of fitting each actor.

A rest-pose comparison of both models exposed two wrong correspondences invisible in the bone names. Anny numbers
the spine from the top down and SMPL from the bottom up, so SMPL's lowest spine joint, \texttt{spine1}, had been mapped
to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it; SMPL's hips had been mapped to \texttt{pelvis.L/R}, \DataHipMapError\,cm from
the hip joint, instead of \texttt{upperleg01.L/R}. Version 2 selects the spine bones by height along the body axis and
the hip bones by distance from the pelvis (Appendix~\ref{app:joints}). Limb and head errors fell to
\DataVTwoLimbMin{} to \DataVTwoLimbMax\,mm, but the torso remained at \DataVTwoTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoTorsoMax\,mm
(Table~\ref{tab:fit-versions}).

The residual torso error arises because the rigs place their torso joints differently: SMPL's
pelvis joint lies \DataSmplPelvisAboveHips\,cm above its hips, Anny's root at hip height and \DataAnnyRootFront\,cm in
front. For \DataVirtualJoints{} torso joints the offset $\boldsymbol{\delta}_k$ from the Anny bone head $b$ to the
SMPL joint $k$ is therefore measured once in the rest pose, in the bone's own frame, and the fit moves the
\emph{virtual joint} $\mathbf{p}_b = \mathbf{h}_b + \mathbf{Q}_b\boldsymbol{\delta}_k$ onto the SMPL joint, with
$\mathbf{h}_b$ and $\mathbf{Q}_b$ the posed bone head and orientation (Equation~\ref{eq:virtual-joint}). The fitted
point thus keeps a fixed offset from its bone and rotates with it. The offsets are \DataOffsetPelvis\,cm (pelvis),
\DataOffsetHip\,cm (hips), \DataOffsetSpineMin{} to \DataOffsetSpineMax\,cm (spine) and \DataOffsetCollar\,cm
(collars). With them (v2.1) the torso error fell to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMax\,mm, and four
varied test scenes reached \DataScenesMin{} to \DataScenesMax\,mm (one occluded frame \DataScenesWorstFrame\,mm).

Age remained wrong: up to v2.1 every actor received exactly \DataFreeAge, because joints do not distinguish adult
ages. Version 2.2 fixes age at \DataFixedAge. On all \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences it fitted
\DataOldFrames{} frames with a median fit error of \DataOldFitMedian\,mm. The converter's own fit statistics were
unremarkable, yet these fits carried two of the three defects of the next section.

\section{Three label defects and the v2.3 re-fit}
\label{sec:data-defects}

This section gives the history behind the check of Figure~\ref{fig:label-check}. Because the converter grades only
its own fit, it cannot detect errors in its targets or in the later processing of its fits; an independent check
therefore reads the finished label files. On sampled images from every sequence it compares the
labels with 3DPW's joints, surface and 2D keypoints, confirms that the data loader passes them on unchanged, and
tests that the gender is not reversed, that every valid actor has a label and that the head turn is preserved. On the
first label set (old labels, v2.2 fits, about \DataOldCheckImages{} images) it returned FAIL with three defects
(Table~\ref{tab:label-check}). Table~\ref{tab:label-sets} lists the three label sets.

\paragraph{Defect 1: gender reversed.} The converter wrote 1.0 for men and 0.0 for women, MakeHuman's convention and
the reverse of Anny's, so every 3DPW man was labelled with a female body. A refit with the flipped value lowered the
joint error for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of \DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men (median \DataGenderFlipMenBefore{} to
\DataGenderFlipMenAfter\,mm). For women the joint error rose (\DataGenderFlipWomenBefore{} to
\DataGenderFlipWomenAfter\,mm), but the body surface came closer to 3DPW's for \DataGenderFlipWomenSurface{} of
\DataGenderFlipWomenOf.

\paragraph{Defect 2: missing actor.} In \DataMissingActor{} of \DataTwoPersonChecked{} sampled two-person images one
valid actor had no label. The converter fits every third valid frame of each person, counted from that person's first
valid frame, so each person had their own frame grid; after a gap in which one actor was not valid, the grids diverged
and the two actors were fitted on different frames. The detection loss then treats a visible person as background.

\paragraph{Defect 3: missing labels.} The training split had \DataOldTrainImages{} label images where the fits implied
\DataRebuiltTrainImages{} (\DataOldTrainMissingPct\% missing). The image files of \DataMissingImageSeq{} sequences were
incomplete on the cluster (\texttt{courtyard\_captureSelfies\_00}: \DataSelfiesFilesBefore{} of
\DataSelfiesFilesAfter{} files), and the label builder skipped every frame without an image file without counting the
skips. A rebuild after re-extracting the images recovered every label (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}, ``rebuilt''); as it
still had defects 1 and 2, it was never used for training.

\paragraph{The v2.3 re-fit.} Version 2.3 fixes defects 1 and 2 with two switches, \texttt{--gender\_convention anny}
and \texttt{--frame\_grid shared}, whose defaults reproduce v2.2 exactly. All \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{}
person-sequences were refitted with both switches on, and the independent check passed on all \DataRefitSeq{}
sequences (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}, middle block). For \DataRefitWorseSeq{} single-person outdoor sequences the
error rose by \DataRefitWorseMin{} to \DataRefitWorseMax\,mm, plausibly because of a different warm start (each frame's fit
starts from the previous one); this was not tested.

The labels built from the v2.3 fits (v23 labels) comprise \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{} images and
\DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} people (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}): fewer images than the rebuilt set but nearly the same
number of people (\DataRebuiltTotalPeople), because both actors now share each image. No person was dropped for a high
fit error or a small bounding box. A further \DataCrossCountryFramesNew{} frames, all from
\texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}, were dropped because the person cannot be visible: with 3DPW's own camera the ground-truth joints lie at a depth of
$\DataCrossCountryDepth$\,m, behind the camera. The 3D fit is correct, but image labels cannot exist with this camera,
and no label set ever contained this sequence. Figure~\ref{fig:label-check-more} shows three further v23 frames.

On the v23 labels (\DataCheckPeople{} people in \DataCheckImages{} images, \DataCheckSeq{} sequences) the grade moved
from FAIL to WARN (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}), and the median body-joint discrepancy fell from
\DataCheckJointsOld{} to \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. Flipping the gender now worsens the fit, which confirms the
convention, and no sampled two-person image misses an actor. The head turn relative to the chest is now preserved; in
the old test labels it was mostly lost (\DataCheckHeadTestLabelOld$^\circ$ against \DataCheckHeadTestSmplOld$^\circ$
in SMPL). Two rows remain at WARN, the limb-length ratios and the people with a joint more than \DataCheckFarMm\,mm
off, mostly at the hip, plausibly because Anny and SMPL differ in limb proportions and hip-joint placement.
Figure~\ref{fig:label-check-more}(b) shows one of the three people the check renders as its worst cases, selected by
the largest single-joint discrepancy; the value printed in the panel is that person's mean joint discrepancy.

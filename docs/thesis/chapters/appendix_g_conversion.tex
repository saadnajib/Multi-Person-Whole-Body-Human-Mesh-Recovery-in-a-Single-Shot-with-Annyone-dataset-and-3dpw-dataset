\chapter{Supporting conversion experiments}
\label{app:conversion}

Chapter~\ref{ch:data} describes the final converter, version 2.3. This appendix shows how it got there. No earlier
version produced the labels used for the main results.

\section{Development of the converter}
\label{sec:conversion-history}

The first converter (v1) matched SMPL joints to Anny bones by name. Its mean joint error on
\texttt{downtown\_bar\_00} was \DataVOneBarMean\,mm. On four test scenes the error was \DataVOneFloorMin{} to \DataVOneFloorMax\,mm on every
frame, and all four actors came out with the same body. The error did not change with the pose, and the body did not
change with the person. This told me that the shape parameters were absorbing a fixed mismatch between the two
skeletons instead of fitting each actor.

So I put both models in their rest poses and compared them. This showed two wrong choices that the bone names alone
could not reveal. Anny's rig numbers the spine from the top down and SMPL from the bottom up, so SMPL's lowest spine
joint, \texttt{spine1}, had been mapped to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it. SMPL's hips had been mapped to \texttt{pelvis.L/R}, \DataHipMapError\,cm from the hip
joint, instead of \texttt{upperleg01.L/R}. Version 2 picks the spine bones by height along the body axis and the hip
bones by distance from the pelvis (Appendix~\ref{app:joints}). Limbs and head fell to \DataVTwoLimbMin{} to
\DataVTwoLimbMax\,mm, but the torso stayed at \DataVTwoTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoTorsoMax\,mm
(Table~\ref{tab:fit-versions}).

The torso stayed high because the two rigs put their torso joints at different points of the body. SMPL's pelvis
joint sits \DataSmplPelvisAboveHips\,cm above its hips. Anny's root is at hip height and \DataAnnyRootFront\,cm in
front. For \DataVirtualJoints{} torso joints I therefore measure once, in the rest pose, the offset
$\boldsymbol{\delta}_k$ from the Anny bone head $b$ to the SMPL joint $k$, written in the bone's own coordinate frame.
The fit then moves the \emph{virtual joint} $\mathbf{p}_b = \mathbf{h}_b + \mathbf{Q}_b\boldsymbol{\delta}_k$ onto
the SMPL joint, with $\mathbf{h}_b$ and $\mathbf{Q}_b$ the posed bone head and orientation (as in
Equation~\ref{eq:virtual-joint}). So the fitted point keeps a fixed offset from its bone and turns with it. The offsets are \DataOffsetPelvis\,cm (pelvis),
\DataOffsetHip\,cm (hips), \DataOffsetSpineMin{} to \DataOffsetSpineMax\,cm (spine) and \DataOffsetCollar\,cm
(collars). With them (v2.1) the torso fell to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMax\,mm, and four varied
test scenes to \DataScenesMin{} to \DataScenesMax\,mm (one occluded frame \DataScenesWorstFrame\,mm).

Age was still wrong. In every version up to v2.1 it came out at exactly \DataFreeAge{} for every actor, because
joints cannot tell one adult age from another. Version 2.2 fixes age at \DataFixedAge. On all
\DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences it fitted \DataOldFrames{} frames with a median fit error of
\DataOldFitMedian\,mm. Every number the converter printed for these fits looked fine. But the fits still had two of
the three defects that I describe in the next section.

\section{Three label defects and the v2.3 re-fit}
\label{sec:data-defects}

Figure~\ref{fig:label-check} in the main text shows one checked frame. Here I give the history behind that check.

The converter grades its own fit, so it cannot see errors in what it is asked to fit, or in what happens to the fits
afterwards. For this reason I wrote an independent check that reads the finished label files. On sampled images from
every sequence it compares the labels with 3DPW's joints, surface and 2D keypoints, and it confirms that the data
loader passes them on unchanged. It also checks that the gender is the right way round, that every valid actor has a
label, and that the head turn is kept. On the
first label set (``old labels'', v2.2 fits, about \DataOldCheckImages{} images) its grade was FAIL, with three
defects (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}).

\paragraph{Defect 1: gender reversed.} The converter wrote 1.0 for men and 0.0 for women. That is MakeHuman's
convention and the reverse of Anny's, so every 3DPW man was labelled with a female body. When I refitted with the
flipped value, the joint error fell for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of \DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men (median
\DataGenderFlipMenBefore{} to \DataGenderFlipMenAfter\,mm). For women the joint error rose
(\DataGenderFlipWomenBefore{} to \DataGenderFlipWomenAfter\,mm), but the body surface came closer to 3DPW's surface
for \DataGenderFlipWomenSurface{} of \DataGenderFlipWomenOf.

\paragraph{Defect 2: missing actor.} In \DataMissingActor{} of \DataTwoPersonChecked{} sampled two-person images, one
valid actor had no label. The converter fits every third valid frame of each person. It counted these frames from
that person's own first valid frame, so each person had their own frame grid. After a gap in which one actor was not
valid, the two grids no longer lined up, and the two actors were fitted on different frames. The detection loss then treats a visible, real person as background.

\paragraph{Defect 3: missing labels.} The training split had \DataOldTrainImages{} label images where the fits implied
\DataRebuiltTrainImages{} (\DataOldTrainMissingPct\% missing). The image files of \DataMissingImageSeq{} sequences
were incomplete on the cluster (\texttt{courtyard\_captureSelfies\_00}: \DataSelfiesFilesBefore{} of
\DataSelfiesFilesAfter{} files), and the label builder skipped every frame that had no image file, without counting
how many it had skipped. A
rebuild after re-extracting the images recovered every label (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}, ``rebuilt''). It still had
defects 1 and 2, so I never trained on it.

To fix defects 1 and 2, version 2.3 adds two switches: \texttt{--gender\_convention anny} writes the gender the right
way round, and \texttt{--frame\_grid shared} puts every person of a sequence on one frame grid. Their defaults
reproduce v2.2 exactly. I re-fitted all \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences with both switches on, and the
independent check passed on all \DataRefitSeq{} sequences (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}, middle block).
\DataRefitWorseSeq{} single-person outdoor sequences became \DataRefitWorseMin{} to \DataRefitWorseMax\,mm worse. The
likely reason is a different warm start (each frame's fit starts from the previous one), which I did not test.

I call the labels built from the v2.3 fits the v23 labels: \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{}
images and \DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} people (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}). That is fewer images than the rebuilt set
but nearly the same number of people (\DataRebuiltTotalPeople{} in the rebuilt set), because both actors now share
each image. I dropped no person for a high fit error or a small bounding box. I did drop \DataCrossCountryFramesNew{}
frames, all from \texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}, because the person cannot be visible in them. With 3DPW's own camera, the ground-truth joints of this sequence sit at a depth
of $\DataCrossCountryDepth$\,m, behind the camera. The 3D fit is fine, but image labels for this sequence cannot exist
with this camera, and no label set ever contained it. Figure~\ref{fig:label-check-more} shows three more v23 frames.

I ran the same check on the v23 labels (\DataCheckPeople{} people in \DataCheckImages{} images, \DataCheckSeq{}
sequences). The grade moved from FAIL to WARN (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}). The median body-joint discrepancy of the
check fell from \DataCheckJointsOld{} to \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. Flipping the gender now makes
the fit worse, so the gender is the right way round, and no sampled two-person image misses an actor. The head turn relative to
the chest is now kept. In the old test labels it was mostly lost (\DataCheckHeadTestLabelOld$^\circ$ against
\DataCheckHeadTestSmplOld$^\circ$ in SMPL). Two rows of Table~\ref{tab:label-check} stay at WARN: the limb-length ratios, and the people with a joint
more than \DataCheckFarMm\,mm off, mostly at the hip. The likely reason is that Anny and SMPL differ in limb
proportions and in where they put the hip joint. Figure~\ref{fig:label-check-more}(b) shows one of the
three people that the check renders as its worst cases, chosen by the largest discrepancy at a single joint; the value
printed in the panel is that person's mean joint discrepancy.

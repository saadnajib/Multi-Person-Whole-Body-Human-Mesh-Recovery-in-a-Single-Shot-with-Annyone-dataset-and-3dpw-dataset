\chapter{Experiments and results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter evaluates the ViT-B student, the model intended for deployment, against its ViT-L teacher and the
released baseline. It then describes the design of the ablation intended to measure what distillation contributes, the sensitivity to training length and distillation temperature, detection recall in
crowded images, and the student on a photograph and a video. The last section analyses the teacher.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:res-setup}

The evaluation sets are described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data} and scored as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}; model
names follow Table~\ref{tab:model-names}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{AnnyOne holdout}: the last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (\MethodHoldoutPeople{} people, no
  children), excluded from training in this thesis and scored with the holdout protocol. Its size and composition
  limit what it can show (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). Placement metrics are computed on the same matched people,
  with each body at the depth its model predicts.
\item \emph{3DPW test}: all \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images with the corrected converted labels, scored with
  the 3DPW protocol. The numbers hold only against these labels (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).
\item \emph{3DPW validation}: \SelValImages{} images with corrected labels, evaluated during training.
\item \emph{Relative Human} validation split: real photographs with an age group, a gender and a box per person, but
  no 3D ground truth.
\item \emph{Demo images}: \MethodDemoPhotos{} real photographs and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames without
  labels, judged by visual inspection, with each body placed from its detection box.
\end{itemize}
Every model is a single training run without a fixed seed, so PVE differences of a few millimetres between two
runs can be run-to-run variation (Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}).

\paragraph{Model-selection protocol.}\label{sec:res-selection}
The student's training configuration was fixed before it was trained. The distillation temperature
$T = \MethodKDTemp$ and the loss weights were taken over from the earlier ViT-S distillation runs
(Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}), and the length of \MethodStudentEpochs{} epochs was set by the compute budget. Every
evaluation uses the last checkpoint of a run; there is no early stopping, and no checkpoint or hyperparameter of the
student was selected on the 3DPW test set. The in-training validation set is 3DPW validation; the reported student
ends at \SelThirtyValPVE\,mm PVE there. The \StudentSixtyEpochs-epoch run and the temperature sweep were trained
afterwards as sensitivity analyses (Section~\ref{sec:res-sensitivity}) and changed no setting. Two limitations
remain. The AnnyOne holdout serves both as in-training monitor and as the reported synthetic test set, and the two
teacher corrections were adopted on diagnostics measured on it (Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}); the choice of the
teacher over its two shape-masked variants was made on Relative Human (Section~\ref{sec:res-mask}). Neither set is
therefore independent of the development process in the way a sealed test set would be.

\section{Student, teacher and released baseline}
\label{sec:res-baseline}

\input{tables/T16_baseline_cost}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} lists the student, its teacher and the released baseline on both test sets, with the
teacher without corrections (uncorrected teacher) and the initial model for reference. The teacher has the lowest
errors on the converted 3DPW labels, \FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE, against \StudentThreeDPW\,mm for the student and
\NAVERThreeDPW\,mm for the released baseline. Against the released baseline the student has a lower PVE on 3DPW (by
\StudentGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm) and a lower MPJPE (\StudentMPJPEThreeDPW{} against \NAVERMPJPEThreeDPW\,mm), but a
slightly higher PA-PVE (\StudentPAThreeDPW{} against \NAVERPAThreeDPW\,mm). Since PA-PVE first removes scale, rotation and
translation, the student's advantage lies in the overall size and orientation of its bodies.

\subsection{Fairness of the comparison}
\label{sec:res-fairness}

The comparison with the released baseline is not a controlled comparison of architectures, and
Table~\ref{tab:baseline} compares complete trained systems. On 3DPW the bias favours the models of this thesis: they
were trained on 3DPW training sequences labelled by the same converter, with its constant phenotypes and rest-pose
hands, and the released baseline was not. The teacher's advantage of \FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm over the
released baseline therefore includes the effect of training on converted 3DPW labels. The only like-for-like pair is
the released baseline and the initial model, neither of which saw 3DPW (\NAVERThreeDPW{} against \BaseThreeDPW\,mm
PVE). On the AnnyOne holdout the bias is in the opposite direction: the released baseline was trained on the AnnyOne
release, which contains the \MethodHoldoutN{} holdout images, so its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm PVE is potentially in-sample.
The two systems are also scored differently on 3DPW: the released baseline sets the helper bones (face, toes,
breasts) to rest pose by design, whereas the models of this thesis are scored with predicted helper bones (3DPW protocol), a
difference that does not favour them. A lower PVE of the student or the teacher therefore does not show
a better architecture, and a baseline trained on the same data does not exist (Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}).

\subsection{Retention against the teacher}
\label{sec:res-student}

The student follows the training configuration of Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe}
(Section~\ref{sec:method-student}). Its PVE is \StudentRatioPVE{} times the teacher's on the holdout (\StudentPVE{}

\subsection{Model size and speed}

\section{Distillation ablation: design and status}
\label{sec:res-kd-ablation}

\input{tables/T28_kd_ablation}

The student differs from a plain ViT-B fine-tune in two ways: it is trained with the feature and output
distillation losses, and its heads are initialised by copying the teacher's. Two ablation arms with the student's
data, schedule and architecture separate these contributions. The first arm switches the distillation losses off
but still copies the teacher heads, keeps the warm-up freeze of the copied heads and uses the teacher's bone
correction; its difference to the student isolates the effect of the losses. The second arm uses no teacher at all,
neither losses nor copied heads; its difference to the student measures the whole contribution of the teacher.
Because nothing is copied, the second arm also has no warm-up freeze and trains without the teacher's bone
correction; its own correction is estimated after training, as for the student. Table~\ref{tab:kd-ablation} lists
both arms next to the reported student and the repeat of the same setting from the temperature sweep, which
indicates how far two runs of one configuration differ.

Both arms are implemented (flags in Appendix~\ref{app:flags}) but had produced no result when this thesis was handed
in, so Table~\ref{tab:kd-ablation} gives their design only. The contribution of distillation is therefore not established:
the student's accuracy relative to the teacher and to the released baseline may be due to the distillation losses,
the copied teacher heads, the pretrained ViT-B backbone or the training data, and this thesis cannot separate them.
The individual loss terms of the student were not switched off one at a time either. No claim is made that the
distilled student is more accurate than a ViT-B model trained on the same data and schedule without a teacher
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}).

\section{Sensitivity to training length and temperature}
\label{sec:res-sensitivity}

\input{tables/T29_sweep_validation}

The training-length analysis is judged on the 3DPW validation images, at the last epoch of each run
(Table~\ref{tab:sweep-validation}). For the temperature sweep the validation values were logged during training but had not been
retrieved when this thesis was handed in, so only the final test-set evaluation of each run can be shown. This is a limitation of the
analysis, not of the selection: $T$ was fixed before training (Section~\ref{sec:res-selection}), and the sweep
changed no setting.

\subsection{Training length}
\label{sec:res-training-length}

\input{figures/F23_loss_curves}

After \StudentEpochs{} epochs the student's holdout PVE monitored during training (Figure~\ref{fig:loss-curves}) was
still falling, from \StudentCurveHoldFifteen\,mm at epoch 15 to \StudentCurveHoldLast\,mm at epoch 30, while its
3DPW validation PVE had changed little since about epoch 25 (\StudentCurveValTwentyFive{} to \SelThirtyValPVE\,mm).
To test whether longer training helps, the same training configuration was trained for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs.
No validation loss was logged for these runs; the validation PVE, measured every \LossEvalEvery{} epochs, is used
instead. The holdout PVE rises between epochs 5 and 10, when the copied heads are unfrozen; the large drops of the
training loss follow the learning-rate halvings (every \LossLRHalfThirty{} and \LossLRHalfSixty{} epochs).

Over the last 20\,\% of the epochs the training loss (\LossThirtyTrainSlope{} and \LossSixtyTrainSlope\,\% per
epoch) and the holdout PVE (\LossThirtyHoldSlope{} and \LossSixtyHoldSlope\,\% per epoch) still fall in both runs,
but the 3DPW validation PVE is flat (\LossThirtyValSlope{} and \LossSixtyValSlope\,\% per epoch). At the last epoch it
is \SelThirtyValPVE\,mm after 30 and \SelSixtyValPVE\,mm after 60 epochs (Table~\ref{tab:sweep-validation}). This
difference is smaller than the \NoisePVE\,mm by which two identical pilots differed on the holdout, but the
run-to-run variation of the 3DPW validation PVE itself was not measured. Longer training thus clearly improves the
synthetic monitor and changes the real validation images only slightly; the 30 epochs fixed in advance were kept,
since the longer run was a sensitivity analysis and not a selection step.

\input{tables/T22_training_length}

The final evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:training-length}) is consistent with this. Doubling the epochs lowered the
holdout PVE by \StudentSixtyGainHoldout\,mm, to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, and the holdout root error from
\ResStudentRootErr{} to \StudentSixtyRootErr\,mm (teacher: \TeacherRootErrStudentJob\,mm). The 3DPW PVE changed from
\StudentThreeDPW{} to \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm, a change of \StudentSixtyChangeThreeDPW\,mm that is not interpreted
(Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}). The 60-epoch
student misses all four retention thresholds at the same cost, so training duration alone does not explain the
student's real-image gap. No other training length was evaluated.

\subsection{Distillation temperature}
\label{sec:res-kd-temperature}

% Sweep: exp9; data/kd_temperature_sweep.csv. Table T25 is in Appendix H.
\input{figures/F27_kd_temperature}

The temperature $T$ sets how strongly the feature distillation softens the channel distributions before the student
is compared with the teacher (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}). Since $T = \MethodKDTemp$ was taken over without a search,
\KDSweepArms{} runs with $T \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, \KDSweepMaxT\}$ and the reported training configuration otherwise test
how much the result depends on it; the output distillation has no temperature. The sweep runs evaluate after every
epoch and log a validation loss; these values were logged during training but had not been retrieved when this
thesis was handed in. Because the sweep trains
$T = \MethodKDTemp$ again, it also provides a second run of the reported setting.

The final evaluation (Figure~\ref{fig:kd-temperature}; values in Table~\ref{tab:kd-temperature},
Appendix~\ref{app:teacher-extra}) is nearly flat. Over $T = 1$ to 8 the holdout PVE moves by \KDHoldRange\,mm
(\KDOneHold{} to \KDFourHold\,mm) and the 3DPW PVE by \KDTPRange\,mm (\KDTwoTP{} to \KDFourTP\,mm). On the holdout
this is below the \KDNoiseHold\,mm by which the sweep's own $T = 4$ run differs from the reported one. On 3DPW the sweep spans \KDTPRange\,mm while the one
repeated pair agrees to \KDNoiseTP\,mm; a single repeat does not establish an effect of $T$, and the formally lowest
combined error at $T = \KDPickT$ is not interpreted as an improvement. No temperature brings the student close to the error thresholds; at $T = 8$ the holdout recall
(\KDEightHoldRec\,\%) also falls just below its threshold of \ResBarRecall\,\%. The run with $T = \KDSweepMaxT$ has no
test evaluation for technical reasons (Appendix~\ref{app:reproduction}).

Neither temperature nor training length changes the 3DPW result or brings the student within the retention
thresholds; longer training lowers the synthetic holdout error by \StudentSixtyGainHoldout\,mm. The 30-epoch student
remains the reported model because the schedule was fixed in advance; preferring the 60-epoch run for its holdout
error would be selection on the test set.

\section{Multi-person detection}
\label{sec:res-multiperson}

\input{tables/T30_rh_crowd}

The evaluation sets differ in the number of people per image. The AnnyOne holdout has \MethodHoldoutPeople{} people
in \MethodHoldoutN{} images; the 3DPW test set labels one or two people per image (\num{\MethodTestPeople} people in
\num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} images); the Relative Human validation split has \num{\MethodRHPeople} labelled people in
\MethodRHImages{} images, many of them crowded. The teacher and the student find \FinalRecallThreeDPW\,\% of the
labelled people on 3DPW and \FinalRecall{} and \StudentRecall\,\% on the holdout (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). Precision on 3DPW is low for every model, because unlabelled bystanders count as false
positives. These sets contain few people per image, so Relative Human is the only one that tests crowded scenes.

Table~\ref{tab:rh-crowd} splits the Relative Human recall by the number of labelled people per image. The teacher's
recall is \CrowdFinalTwoRec\,\% in images with two people and falls steadily with crowd size, to
\CrowdFinalFiveEightRec\,\% with five to eight and \CrowdFinalNinePlusRec\,\% with nine or more. The initial model is
lower in every bin (\CrowdBaseOneRec\,\% with one person, \CrowdBaseTwoRec{} to \CrowdBaseNinePlusRec\,\% from two
people on), and the gap narrows in the most crowded images. The single-person bin holds only \CrowdOneImages{}
images. Recall is counted at IoU $\geq$ \MethodRHIoU{} against the labelled boxes; Relative Human has no 3D ground
truth, so the reconstruction quality of the people found in crowds is not measured.

The dependence of recall on crowd size is a teacher result; the student was not evaluated on Relative Human. On the
two sets where both were measured its recall is close to the teacher's (Table~\ref{tab:baseline}), but these sets
hold few people per image. No occlusion-specific or crowd benchmark with 3D labels
was used, and recall was not split by visibility or occlusion; the only occlusion case shown is the holdout image in which two people lying in the foreground are missed by both
the initial model and the teacher (Figure~\ref{fig:holdout-montage}, continued). The video of
Section~\ref{sec:res-video} shows one person, and the still image three.

\subsection{Still image}

Figure~\ref{fig:image-compare} shows three singers on a stage, reconstructed by the student and by the released

\subsection{Video}

\input{figures/F25_video_panels}
\input{tables/T27_video_fps}

The walking clip has \VideoFrames{} frames (\VideoPlaybackFPS\,FPS) of one man walking across a square. Both models were run on the same
frames with the procedure of Section~\ref{sec:method-video}, and five panels were rendered
(Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels}): the input, the raw student, the student with temporal filtering, the student with
landmark alignment to the MediaPipe shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle landmarks
(Section~\ref{sec:method-temporal}), and the released baseline. Neither step retrains the student. The student kept
a matched person in \VideoStudentMatched{} of \VideoFrames{} frames and the released baseline in
\VideoBaselineMatched; these counts come from the tracker and are not a detection recall against labels.

Under visual inspection the raw student mesh sometimes extends beyond the person (in
Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels} it is drawn ahead of the man); filtering makes its motion steadier, and alignment
improves its position and apparent size. The motion statistics of the run agree with this. The mean pelvis-relative joint acceleration falls from
\VideoAccelRaw{} to \VideoAccelTemporal\,m/s$^2$ with the filter (released baseline, unfiltered:
\VideoAccelNAVER\,m/s$^2$), the mean speed of the head anchor from \VideoRootSpeedRaw{} to
\VideoRootSpeedTemporal\,m/s (\VideoRootSpeedAligned\,m/s after alignment), and the standard deviation of the
predicted gender over the clip from \VideoGenderStdRaw{} to \VideoGenderStdTemporal. These are smoothness measures,
not accuracy: a filter can also flatten real motion, and the clip has no ground truth. Only this one clip, with one
person and no occlusion, was evaluated.

Table~\ref{tab:video-fps} separates model inference from per-frame processing. For model inference alone, the
student runs at \VideoStudentRawFPS{} and the released baseline at \VideoNAVERFPS\,FPS on the same \VideoGPU{} and
frames (\VideoStudentMs{} and \VideoNAVERMs\,ms per frame), about \VideoSpeedRatioNAVER{} times faster; the heads
also differ (\BgNaverHeadLayers{} against \BgOurHeadLayers{} cross-attention blocks).
Including image preprocessing (\VideoPreMs\,ms) and output transfer, a frame takes \VideoStudentPipeMs\,ms for the student
(\VideoStudentPipeFPS\,FPS) and \VideoNAVERPipeMs\,ms for the released baseline (\VideoNAVERPipeFPS\,FPS). The filter
adds \VideoTemporalMs\,ms and the alignment fit \VideoAlignMs\,ms; with both and preprocessing a frame takes
\VideoAlignedPipeMs\,ms (\VideoAlignedPipeFPS\,FPS), and \VideoAlignedRenderFPS\,FPS with rendering. Two parts of
the complete pipeline were not timed: the offline landmark extraction (MediaPipe on the CPU, a separate pass over
the video) and video decoding and encoding. These numbers therefore describe inference speed, not end-to-end speed,
and no real-time claim is made for the complete pipeline. The A40 times do not compare with the L40S times of
Table~\ref{tab:model-cost}.

On the holdout (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}) the teacher has the lowest PA-PVE, \GapPAPVE\,mm below the

\subsection{Real-image supervision}
\label{sec:res-3dpw}

On 3DPW (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}) the uncorrected teacher is already \GainBaseToControlThreeDPW\,mm PVE better
than the initial model, so most of the gain is attributable to real-image training itself; the two corrections add
\GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \GainFixesPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE. An earlier model trained on the uncorrected labels
with a different schedule is \GainOverOldThreeDPW\,mm worse than the teacher (Table~\ref{tab:final-full}), so the
share of the label correction in this gain is unknown.

\subsection{Bone correction}
\label{sec:res-bone}

The teacher keeps the bone correction through ten epochs with real images (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}): neck02
is \FinalNeckTwo\,deg off against \ControlNeckTwo{} for the uncorrected teacher, and the neck girth is \FinalGirth{}
of the true girth against \ControlGirth. The head direction, which worsened with the correction alone
(\NoTrainHeadDir\,deg), returns to the uncorrected teacher's level; fine-tuning thus preserves the correction, and
PVE does not increase. The correction without training, a real-frame example and the open question of why the bones
flip are in Appendix~\ref{sec:diag-neck}.

\subsection{Hand articulation}
\label{sec:res-fingers}

Predicted fingers are \BaseFingers\,deg off in the initial model and \ControlFingers\,deg in the uncorrected teacher,
above the rest-pose error (\BaseFingersRest\,deg; Table~\ref{tab:fingers}). With the flipped bones metacarpal1.L and
finger4-1.R corrected, the teacher reaches \FinalFingers\,deg, and the correction alone gave \NoTrainFingers: about
half of the finger error was due to two flipped bones, and the remainder is unexplained. On three real images the
teacher's fingers appear flexed (Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}).

The gender AUC, which needs no cut-off, is \RHGenderAUCFinal{} for the teacher against \RHGenderAUCBase{}
(Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}, Appendix~\ref{app:teacher-extra}); a likely reason for the intermediate gender of men
is that the AnnyOne gender labels span only \HoldGenderLabelMin{} to \HoldGenderLabelMax. On the AnnyOne holdout the
teacher follows weight best (Pearson $r$ = \PhenoRFinalWeight) and muscle worst (\PhenoRFinalMuscle), with every
slope of prediction on label below 1 (Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}). The shape head can thus express shape on
synthetic images, while on real photographs the teacher's bodies vary little (Figure~\ref{fig:rest-lineup}); since
the initial model already shows part of this narrow spread, the gap between synthetic and real images is part of the
cause, not the 3DPW training alone.

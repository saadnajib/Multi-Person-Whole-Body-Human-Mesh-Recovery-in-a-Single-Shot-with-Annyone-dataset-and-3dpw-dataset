\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports what the models built on the diagnostics of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} achieve. It follows
the fixes in the order they were made: depth, the bone correction and the fingers, then the final model against its
control on synthetic and real test data, body shape on real photos, the shape-mask option, the comparison with
NAVER's own Anny model, the distilled student, and last the history of the earlier students. Each section opens with
its figure or table, reads it, interprets it and says what it does not show.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Evaluation sets and settings}
\label{sec:res-setup}

Four sets are used, all described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data} and scored as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. The
AnnyOne holdout holds the last 100 AnnyOne images (222 people, no children), never trained on; its headline setting
V1 masks the helper bones and matches people in 2D, and a trace script adds the placement metrics (root error, depth
fit, drawn size) with each body at the model's own predicted depth. The 3DPW test set is all
\num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images of the corrected labels, scored in setting V0 with the same script for every
model; its numbers hold only against our converted labels, which carry a conversion error of about
\MethodLabelFloor\,mm, and are not comparable with published 3DPW numbers. The Relative Human validation split gives
real photos with an age group, a gender and a box per person, but no 3D body. The 12 demo images (4 photos and 8 3DPW
test frames) have no labels at all and are judged by eye; their bodies are placed from the detection box, so they
hide depth errors.

The models are those of Table~\ref{tab:model-names}. The central comparison is Final against Control: the two runs
share the start point (Base), the schedule, the real-image share and the hand mask, and differ only by the depth fix
and the bone correction. Base against Control shows what the real images and the restart of training do. Every model
was trained once, without a fixed seed. Two identical runs differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE, \NoiseHeadDeg\,deg head
direction and about \NoiseRecall{} recall points (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}); I call gaps below these ``within
run-to-run variation'' and compare every fine-tune with its own control, because restarting a fine-tune of Base
already costs about \RestartCost\,mm PVE. Setting V0 reads about \MethodVzeroGap\,mm higher than V1 on the same
people; every caption names the setting.

\begin{figure}[tbp]

\input{figures/plots/F15_depth_scatter}
\caption[Predicted against true root depth on the holdout: Base, Control, Final]{Predicted against true root depth
for every matched person of the AnnyOne holdout (100 images, setting V1, each body at its own predicted depth):

\label{fig:depth-scatter}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:depth-579862} shows the depth bug and the fix on one image. Base draws the man far behind his true
position and too small; the pilot with head-joint targets draws him close to his true size. Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}
shows the same for all people. Nearly every point of Control lies above the identity line, \ControlTooFarPct\,\% of
its people are placed too far, and its mean root error is \num{\ControlRootErr}\,mm. Final pulls the cloud down: mean
root error \num{\FinalRootErr}\,mm ($-$\RootErrDropPct\,\%; Base \num{\BaseRootErr}), drawn size \FinalSize{} against
\ControlSize{} (Base \BaseSize), and the correlation of true and predicted depth rises from \ControlDepthR{} to
\FinalDepthR. The head now lands within \FinalHeadPx{} pixels of its true position against \ControlHeadPx{} for
Control (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}). The pilots of Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs} reached the same with AnnyOne only;
the 10-epoch depth pilot even reached \num{\DepthPilotRootErr}\,mm.

The fix holds after ten epochs with real images in every batch and the bone correction on. The gap to Control is far
outside run-to-run variation: every run with head targets, pilots included, lies below every run without them. What
moved is the offset and the drawn size, not the slope. Final's fit crosses the identity at \FinalIdentityCross\,m,
so people about three metres away are placed about right, while near people are still placed too far
(\FinalTooFarPct\,\% of all people). Predicted depth is compressed toward the range of 2.5--2.9\,m, as in every run
of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}. The pelvis now lands farther from its pixel (\FinalPelvisPx{} against
\ControlPelvisPx{} pixels), likely because the head is pinned while the body is still drawn too small; I did not
check this.

Not shown: correct near and far depth (the slope is \FinalSlope, not 1), and why the slope does not move; measuring
the depth distribution of the training batches would be the next test. Final's root error is higher than the depth
pilot's; the real-image batches or the bone correction may cause this, which was not tested. Depth is measured on
synthetic images only: the 3DPW evaluation aligns the pelvis, and the real-photo demos place each body from its box.
Figure~\ref{fig:depth-579862} shows the pilot, not Final.

\section{The bone correction}
\label{sec:res-bone}

\begin{figure}[tbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\linewidth]{neck_before_after_3dpw_bar}
\caption[3DPW test frame: Base against the neck pilot]{Real 3DPW test frame \texttt{downtown\_bar\_00} 701: Base
(left) and the neck pilot, fine-tuned 2 epochs with the correction (right); necks ringed. Bodies placed from the
detector box; judged by eye, no metric. The two columns differ by the correction and by the 2-epoch fine-tune. A
second frame is in Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-office}.}
\label{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar} shows the correction on a real photo that no model trained on: Base draws both necks
as thin stalks pinched under the head, the corrected model draws them at normal width. The holdout pair of
Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} shows the same with the correction alone and no training, and Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}
gives the numbers for the correction without training and after a 2-epoch fine-tune. Final keeps the correction
through ten epochs with real images (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}): neck02 is \FinalNeckTwo\,deg off against
\ControlNeckTwo{} for Control, the head bone \FinalHeadBone{} against \ControlHeadBone, the neck girth is
\FinalGirth{} of the truth against \ControlGirth, and the neck-skin error \FinalNeckSkin{} against
\ControlNeckSkin\,mm. The joint error of the neck region falls from \ControlNeckRegion{} to \FinalNeckRegion\,deg.
The direction of the head, which got worse when the correction was applied without training
(\NoTrainHeadDir\,deg; neck pilot \NeckPilotHeadDir), is \FinalHeadDir\,deg in Final against \ControlHeadDir{} in
Control and \BaseHeadDir{} in Base.

The half-turn of the two neck bones does not come back during training: the network learns on top of the
correction instead of undoing it. The correction is one constant rotation per bone and adds no parameters; its origin
is unknown, and it says nothing about why the bones flipped. Final's head direction equals Control's within
run-to-run variation, so the fine-tune absorbed the head error that one constant could not remove. PVE shows no sign
of a cost (Section~\ref{sec:res-final}).

Not shown: a neck number on real photos, which have no neck ground truth. The real frame of
Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar} is the neck pilot, not Final, and its two columns also differ by the fine-tune; on the
holdout the same fine-tune without the correction keeps girth \BaseGirth{} (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}), so the
correction is the likely cause of the wider necks. Final's own real-photo renders are in
Section~\ref{sec:res-3dpw}. Flips that compensate each other in a chain, as in the left arm of a model trained from
fresh heads (Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}), need a two-sided correction, which I did not implement.

The finger numbers are in Table~\ref{tab:fingers} of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} and the last rows of
Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}. Predicted fingers are \BaseFingers\,deg off in Base and \ControlFingers\,deg in
Control, worse than fingers left at rest (\BaseFingersRest\,deg). Final, with the two flipped hand bones corrected,
reaches \FinalFingers\,deg; the correction alone, without training, gave \NoTrainFingers.

About half of the finger error was two flipped bones, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R, and ten epochs of training keep
the gain. The rest of the error is not explained. Low resolution is a candidate that was tested only by zooming in at
test time, which did not help; real images add nothing to the fingers, because the hand mask removes the rotation
terms of the \MethodHandMaskBones{} hand bones for 3DPW people (their labels hold rest-pose hands), and the mesh terms
still carry the rest-pose hands.

Predicted fingers on real images are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}; every earlier real-photo demo set them
to rest pose. Judged by eye, the claws of Base are gone in Final: Base spreads the fingers stiffly and lets one finger
of the left hand stick out from the others, as the flipped metacarpal1.L would, while Final closes and bends them,
draped over an armrest, curled as if fretting, bent over a bar edge, in a way that reads as a hand. Where the hands are small, as in the
tennis photo and the street frames of Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-all}, the difference cannot be judged at print size.

Not shown: a finger number on real images. The finger number is an angle on the synthetic holdout only; the 3DPW
labels carry rest-pose hands and the demo images have no labels, so the real-image evidence is three images by eye.
Base's renders carry both hand flips, so the figure shows the correction and the fine-tune together, not either alone.

Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone} puts Final next to Control, Base and the two pilots. Final has the lowest PA-PVE of
every model, \FinalPAPVE\,mm against \ControlPAPVE{} for Control ($-$\GapPAPVE\,mm) and \BasePAPVE{} for Base. Its
PVE is \FinalPVE\,mm against \ControlPVE{} for Control, a gap of \GapPVE\,mm within run-to-run variation; Base keeps
the lowest PVE (\BasePVE\,mm), as it did against every fine-tune. Recall is \FinalRecall\,\% against
\ControlRecall\,\% for Control ($-$\GapRecallControl{} points, at the limit of the variation) and \BaseRecall\,\% for
Base ($-$\GapRecallBase). The root error, the neck and the fingers improve far beyond the variation
(Sections~\ref{sec:res-depth}--\ref{sec:res-fingers}). The curves of Figure~\ref{fig:val-curves} agree on 3DPW
validation: Final is below Control at all ten epochs (mean \ValMeanFinal{} against \ValMeanControl\,mm, last epoch
\ValLastFinal{} against \ValLastControl). On the in-training AnnyOne holdout (V0) the order changes from epoch to
epoch: Final is higher on average (\HoldVzeroMeanFinal{} against \HoldVzeroMeanControl\,mm) and lower at the last
epoch (\HoldVzeroLastFinal{} against \HoldVzeroLastControl). Figure~\ref{fig:holdout-montage} shows six holdout images:
Base draws its bodies small and off their people, Final draws them close to the size and place of the ground truth.

The fixes cost nothing in body error that one run can resolve, and they bring the largest PA-PVE gain of the project.
PVE aligns the pelvis, so it cannot see the depth gain, and the neck and the fingers are few vertices of a whole body;
PA-PVE also removes rotation and scale and is the more sensitive of the two to pose and shape. The sign change of the
in-training holdout curves is what a gap within run-to-run variation looks like. The recall cost against Base is
larger than the variation and is a real cost. A likely reason is that the detection cell moved from the pelvis to the
head, so people whose head is cut off or hidden are found less often; this was not tested.

Not shown: PVE paired on the same people (the matched sets differ: \FinalMatched, \ControlMatched{} and \BaseMatched{}
people), recall on people with a visible head, bootstrap intervals, and a second run of Final. The holdout has 100
synthetic images without children, and Figure~\ref{fig:holdout-montage} shows six of them.

Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw} scores every model on all \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images with the same script.

follows at \ControlThreeDPW, Mixed-L, the previous best, at \BaseOldThreeDPW, the neck pilot at

\GainBaseToControlThreeDPW\,mm better than Base. The two fixes add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm PVE and
\GainFixesPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE on top, within run-to-run variation for PVE; the bone correction alone, in the
neck pilot, leaves 3DPW PVE at Base's level. Final is \GainOverOldThreeDPW\,mm better than Mixed-L,
which saw the same kind of images but with reversed gender labels and missing actors; whether the corrected labels
cause part of this gap was not tested, since Mixed-L also differs in schedule and length. The low precision is
expected: 3DPW labels only the one or two actors of each sequence, so every detected bystander counts as a false
positive, and some detections are ghost bodies on half-visible people. The split between the two is unknown.

Not shown: a comparison with published 3DPW results. These numbers average all 163 Anny joints and vertices against
converted labels that themselves err by about \MethodLabelFloor\,mm, so they are not comparable with SMPL-based 3DPW
numbers. The old-label results of the draft are retired and appear nowhere next to these. There is no breakdown by
sequence, and the by-eye judgement of Figure~\ref{fig:3dpw-overlays} covers 8 frames.

Table~\ref{tab:relhuman} measures body shape on real photos with labels. Final finds more people (recall
\RHRecallFinal\,\% against \RHRecallBase\,\%) and gets the gender right for \RHGenderFinal\,\% of them against
\RHGenderBase\,\% for Base. Women are recognised (mean predicted gender \RHWomenGenderFinal, where 1 is female); men
stay in the middle (\RHMenGenderFinal, where 0 is male). Children separate from adults: the child-versus-adult AUC
rises from \RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal, and the mean predicted age falls to \RHAgeAdultFinal{} for adults,
\RHAgeKidFinal{} for kids and \RHAgeBabyFinal{} for babies (Base: \RHAgeAdultBase, \RHAgeKidBase, \RHAgeBabyBase).
\RHBabiesBelowFinal\,\% of the babies are child-shaped against \RHBabiesBelowBase\,\% for Base, and
\RHKidsBelowFinal\,\% of the kids. Figure~\ref{fig:rh-age} shows the whole distributions: Base's boxes stand at the
same height for every group, Final's step down from adults to babies, and only the baby box reaches below the child
line (median \RHBabyMedianFinal). On the demo photos (Figure~\ref{fig:real-photos} and Figure~\ref{fig:photo-montages})
most men look male in Final, women stay female, and the boy keeps an adult body.

The AUC ranks people by predicted age, so a shift of all ages alone cannot raise it: the step from \RHAUCBase{} to
\RHAUCFinal{} is real separation of children from adults. Part of the lower ages is likely a common shift from the
3DPW age label, which the converter fixes at 0.55 for every real person and which pulls adults from
\RHAgeAdultBase{} to \RHAgeAdultFinal. The gender gain fits the corrected 3DPW gender labels, which supervise the shape
head on real people for the first time; the shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:res-mask} supports both readings. Children
remain a measured improvement, not solved: half of the babies and five of six kids are still above the child line,
and men are not yet male.

Not shown: ages in years, which I never quote because the code uses three age scales that disagree; children on
held-out synthetic images (the holdout has none); and any shape number beyond age, gender and height, since Relative
Human has no 3D bodies. The numbers cover detected people only, and the matched sets differ (\RHChildrenFinal{} kids

\section{The shape-mask trade-off}
\label{sec:res-mask}

\input{tables/T21_mask_arms}

Two runs test the shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:method-real}, one run each. Final-masked stops the shape loss
on the \MethodShapeMaskCount{} values the converter fixes for every 3DPW person: age 0.55 and seven values at
\MethodConstOther. Final-partial-mask stops it only on the seven values at \MethodConstOther{} and keeps age
supervised. Gender stays supervised in both. Table~\ref{tab:mask-arms} sets the two next to Final; their full
Relative Human columns are in Table~\ref{tab:relhuman}.

On the AnnyOne holdout Final-masked reads \FinalMaskedPVE\,mm PVE, \FinalMaskedPAPVE\,mm PA-PVE, \FinalMaskedRecall\,\%
recall and \num{\FinalMaskedRootErr}\,mm root error (Final: \FinalPVE, \FinalPAPVE, \FinalRecall, \num{\FinalRootErr});
on the 3DPW test \FinalMaskedThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \FinalMaskedPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE (Final \FinalThreeDPW{} and
\FinalPAThreeDPW). On Relative Human it loses most of what Final gained: recall \RHRecallMasked\,\%, gender accuracy
\RHGenderMasked\,\% (men \RHMenGenderMasked, women \RHWomenGenderMasked), adults back at age \RHAgeAdultMasked{} and
\RHBabiesBelowMasked\,\% of babies child-shaped; the AUC stays at \RHAUCMasked. On the demo images it brings back the
variety Final had lost: the spread of the predicted muscle rises from \SpreadMuscleFinal{} to \SpreadMuscleMasked,
of weight from \SpreadWeightFinal{} to \SpreadWeightMasked, of cup size from \SpreadCupFinal{} to \SpreadCupMasked{}
and of firmness from \SpreadFirmFinal{} to \SpreadFirmMasked.

Final-partial-mask reads \FinalPartialPVE\,mm PVE, \FinalPartialPAPVE\,mm PA-PVE, \FinalPartialRecall\,\% recall and
\num{\FinalPartialRootErr}\,mm root error on the holdout; neck02 (\FinalPartialNeckTwo\textdegree), the head bone
(\FinalPartialHeadBone\textdegree) and the fingers (\FinalPartialFingers\textdegree) stay at Final's level
(\FinalNeckTwo\textdegree, \FinalHeadBone\textdegree, \FinalFingers\textdegree). On the 3DPW test it reads
\FinalPartialThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \FinalPartialPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE. On Relative Human it keeps the children: the AUC
is \RHAUCPartial, the mean predicted age is \RHAgeAdultPartial{} for adults, \RHAgeKidPartial{} for kids and
\RHAgeBabyPartial{} for babies, and \RHBabiesBelowPartial\,\% of the babies are child-shaped (Final
\RHBabiesBelowFinal\,\%, Final-masked \RHBabiesBelowMasked\,\%). Gender is lost as under the full mask: accuracy
\RHGenderPartial\,\%, with both sexes on the female side (men \RHMenGenderPartial, women \RHWomenGenderPartial);
recall is \RHRecallPartial\,\%.

The body errors of both masked runs stay within run-to-run variation of Final, as expected, since the 3DPW meshes
keep the neutral body. The trade-off is in the shape head. Without the mask, Final learned the seven constants and
predicts nearly the same muscle, weight, cup size and firmness for everyone on real photos, so shape does not visibly
change from person to person for those values. With the full mask the values vary again, but gender falls back to
chance and the children's gain shrinks. The partial mask separates the two parts of that loss. With age supervised
the children's gain stays (AUC, adult age and kid age at Final's level), so the age label carries it. Gender falls to
chance although the gender label stays supervised in both masked runs. A reading that was not tested: Final's gender
gain depends on the seven constants on the 3DPW men (cup size and firmness at \MethodConstOther{} among them) as much
as on the gender label.

Before the first masked run I fixed a rule: a masked model would replace Final if gender accuracy stayed at 60\,\% or
more, the AUC at 0.7 or more, and the spread of muscle and weight rose above \SpreadRuleThreshold. Both masked runs
fail the rule on gender while passing it on the AUC, so Final stays the final model and the Student's teacher.

Not shown: whether the varied values are right. No real data set I have gives muscle or weight, so the spread shows
variety, not accuracy. For Final-partial-mask no demo was run, because gender had already failed the rule, so whether
masking only the seven constants also brings the variety back is not known. Why gender accuracy falls when the seven
constants are masked is not known either; the reading above is one candidate. Each arm ran once, without a seed.

could not be measured with our tool, which supports only our model class. The Student has \StudentParams\,M
parameters and \StudentGFLOPs\,GFLOPs and runs in \StudentMsAForty\,ms on the A40: \ParamRatioStudent$\times$ fewer

\section{History: the ViT-S students and the schedule to Base}
\label{sec:res-history}

\input{tables/T20_history}

Table~\ref{tab:history} keeps the draft's holdout measurements, all in setting V0 and all from before the depth fix, the bone
correction and the corrected labels. The staged schedule went from \HistStageOnePVE\,mm with the backbone frozen to
\HistStageTwoPVE\,mm once the last four blocks trained and the helper bones were masked, and ended at
\BaseVzeroPVE\,mm for Base (Table~\ref{tab:history-stages}). The ViT-S students started at \MethodStudentSOnePVE\,mm with feature
distillation alone and reached \MethodStudentSSixcPVE\,mm with projector-fed heads at twice the batch, still
improving at the end, \StudentSixcRatio{} times their teacher's error (Table~\ref{tab:history-students}). Adding 30\,\%
3DPW images on the old labels (Student-S/mixed, Student-S/final) gave \MethodStudentSEightPVE\,mm on the holdout, \StudentSEightRatio{} times Student-S/final's own teacher.
Figure~\ref{fig:vits-curves} shows the head-transfer students: each starts far above the final error of the student
with random heads (the dashed line), Student-S/4 to Student-S/6 fall below it between epochs 60 and 100, Student-S/3
never does, and from epoch 60 on Student-S/6 has the lowest error at every epoch.

Three lessons from this line shaped the Student. A backbone already trained for mesh recovery gave the largest
single gain, so the Student starts from NAVER's ViT-B. Heads fed through a projector at the teacher's width beat
heads cut to the student's width, so the Student keeps Final's heads. And the students were limited by the images they
had seen rather than by their size, so the Student trains on six times the images of Final's own fine-tune. The
schedule shows that helper-bone masking and partial unfreezing together halved the error, but the two changes were
made in one step and are not separated.

The two 3DPW columns of Table~\ref{tab:history-students} are the only Student-S numbers on the corrected labels:
Student-S/6c and Student-S/final re-scored with the script of Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}; the students' old-label
numbers are retired and not shown. Student-S/final, which saw 3DPW training images with the old labels, is at Base's
level on the real test images (\StudentSFinalThreeDPW{} against \BaseThreeDPW\,mm PVE), and Student-S/6c, trained on
synthetic images only, is at \StudentSSixcThreeDPW\,mm; both are far from Final's \FinalThreeDPW\,mm. Neither is the
thesis's answer to requirement 1b; that is the ViT-B Student of Section~\ref{sec:res-student}. The students also predate both fixes and so carry the depth bug and, likely, the neck flip of
their teachers, which was not measured for them. Their holdout numbers are V0 and read about \MethodVzeroGap\,mm higher
than V1 would.

\chapter{Experiments and results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter evaluates the ViT-B student, the model intended for deployment, against its ViT-L teacher and the
released baseline. It then examines what distillation contributes, how sensitive the student is to training length
and distillation temperature, how detection recall behaves in crowded images, and how the student behaves on a photo
and a video. The last section analyses the teacher. Earlier models and schedules are in Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:res-setup}

The evaluation sets are described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data} and scored as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}; model
names follow Table~\ref{tab:model-names}.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{AnnyOne holdout}: the last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (\MethodHoldoutPeople{} people, no
  children), excluded from training in this thesis and scored with the holdout protocol of
  Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. Its size and composition limit what it can show (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). A
  trace script adds placement metrics, with each body at the depth its model predicts.
\item \emph{3DPW test}: all \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images with the corrected converted labels, scored with
  the 3DPW protocol. The numbers hold only against these labels (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).
\item \emph{3DPW validation}: \SelValImages{} images with corrected labels, evaluated during training.
\item \emph{Relative Human} validation split: real photos with an age group, a gender and a box per person, but no 3D
  ground truth.
\item \emph{Demo images}: \MethodDemoPhotos{} photos and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames without labels, judged by
  visual inspection, with each body placed from its detection box.
\end{itemize}
Every model is a single training run without a fixed seed, so PVE differences of a few millimetres between two
runs can be run-to-run variation (Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}).

\subsection{Model-selection protocol}
\label{sec:res-selection}

The student's recipe was fixed before it was trained. The distillation temperature $T = \MethodKDTemp$ and the
loss weights were taken over from the earlier ViT-S distillation runs (Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}), and the length of
\MethodStudentEpochs{} epochs was set by the compute budget. Every evaluation uses the last checkpoint of a run;
there is no early stopping, and no checkpoint or hyperparameter was selected on any test set. The in-training
validation set is 3DPW validation; the reported student ends at \SelThirtyValPVE\,mm PVE there. The
\StudentSixtyEpochs-epoch run and the temperature sweep were trained afterwards as sensitivity analyses
(Section~\ref{sec:res-sensitivity}) and changed no setting. One limitation remains: the AnnyOne holdout serves both
as in-training monitor and as the reported synthetic test set. It was never used to choose a checkpoint, but it is
not independent of the development process in the way a sealed test set would be.

\section{Student, teacher and released baseline}
\label{sec:res-baseline}

\input{tables/T16_baseline_cost}

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} lists the student, its teacher and the released baseline (Multi-HMR-Anny) on both test
sets, with the uncorrected teacher and the initial model for reference. The teacher has the lowest errors on the
converted 3DPW labels, \FinalThreeDPW\,mm PVE, against \StudentThreeDPW\,mm for the student and \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm
for the released baseline. Against the released baseline the student has a lower PVE on 3DPW
(\StudentGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm) and a lower MPJPE (\StudentMPJPEThreeDPW{} against \NAVERMPJPEThreeDPW\,mm), but
a slightly higher PA-PVE (\StudentPAThreeDPW{} against \NAVERPAThreeDPW\,mm). Since PA-PVE first removes the best

while its aligned surface is slightly worse.

\subsection{Fairness of the comparison}
\label{sec:res-fairness}

The comparison with the released baseline is not a controlled comparison of architectures. The student and the
teacher were trained on converted 3DPW images; the released baseline was not. On the AnnyOne holdout the bias runs
the other way: the released baseline was trained on the AnnyOne release, which contains the \MethodHoldoutN{} holdout
images, so its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm PVE is potentially in-sample. A lower PVE of the student or the teacher therefore
does not show a better architecture; Table~\ref{tab:baseline} compares two complete trained systems. The 3DPW
comparison is the fairer of the two, because neither the released baseline nor the initial model saw 3DPW before
the fine-tuning of this thesis; there the teacher is \FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better than the released
baseline. A baseline trained on the same data does not exist (Chapter~\ref{ch:discussion}).

\subsection{Retention against the teacher}
\label{sec:res-student}

\input{tables/T17_student}

The student follows the recipe of Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe} (Section~\ref{sec:method-student}). Its PVE is
\StudentRatioPVE{} times the teacher's on the holdout (\StudentPVE{} against \FinalPVE\,mm) and
\StudentRatioThreeDPW{} times on 3DPW. Table~\ref{tab:student} applies the \BgNinetyFive\,\% retention rule set in
that section: the student misses the threshold on every error metric on both sets, for example \StudentThreeDPW\,mm
PVE on 3DPW against a threshold of \BarThreeDPW\,mm, and meets it only on recall and cost. Against the released
baseline the 3DPW PVE threshold (\BarNAVERThreeDPW\,mm) is met and the AnnyOne threshold (\BarNAVERAnnyOne\,mm) is
not, which carries little weight given the in-sample bias above. No labelled real photos were available to test the
student's hands and head.

\subsection{Model size and speed}

\input{figures/plots/F18_cost_pve}

The student has \StudentParams\,M parameters, \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer than the teacher (\FinalParams\,M) and
\ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times fewer than the released baseline (\NAVERParams\,M; Table~\ref{tab:model-cost}). It

\section{Contribution of distillation}
\label{sec:res-kd-ablation}

\input{tables/T28_kd_ablation}

The student differs from a plain ViT-B fine-tune in two ways: it is trained with the feature and output
distillation losses, and its heads are initialised by copying the teacher's. Two ablation arms with the student's
data, schedule and architecture separate these contributions. The first arm switches the distillation losses off
but still copies the teacher heads; it isolates the effect of the losses. The second arm uses no teacher at all,
neither losses nor copied heads; its difference to the student measures the whole contribution of the teacher.
Table~\ref{tab:kd-ablation} lists both arms next to the reported student and the repeat of the same setting from the
temperature sweep, which shows how far two runs of one recipe differ (\KDNoiseHold\,mm holdout PVE,
\KDNoiseTP\,mm 3DPW PVE).

Both arms were submitted but are not yet evaluated. Without these results, the contribution of distillation cannot
be separated from that of the pretrained ViT-B backbone and of the training data: the student's accuracy relative to
the teacher and to the released baseline may be due to any of the three.

\section{Sensitivity to training length and temperature}
\label{sec:res-sensitivity}

\input{tables/T29_sweep_validation}

Both analyses in this section are judged on the 3DPW validation images, at the last epoch of each run
(Table~\ref{tab:sweep-validation}). The test-set numbers of the same runs (Tables~\ref{tab:training-length}
and~\ref{tab:kd-temperature}) are the final evaluation of each run; they are reported for completeness and were not
used for any decision.

\subsection{Training length}
\label{sec:res-training-length}

\IfFileExists{figures/F23_loss_curves.tex}{\input{figures/F23_loss_curves}}{\TODO{F23 loss-curve figure missing}}

After \StudentEpochs{} epochs the student's holdout PVE monitored during training (Figure~\ref{fig:loss-curves}) was
still falling, from \StudentCurveHoldFifteen\,mm at epoch 15 to \StudentCurveHoldLast\,mm at epoch 30, while its
3DPW validation PVE had nearly stopped, from \StudentCurveValTwentyFive{} to \StudentCurveValLast\,mm in the last five
epochs. A common stopping criterion is that training should continue while the validation error still decreases.
The same recipe was therefore trained for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs. Both runs predate the logging of a
validation loss, so the validation PVE, measured every \LossEvalEvery{} epochs, takes its place. The holdout PVE rises
between epochs 5 and 10, when the copied heads are unfrozen, and the large drops of the training loss follow the
learning-rate halvings (every \LossLRHalfThirty{} and every \LossLRHalfSixty{} epochs).

Over the last 20\,\% of the epochs the training loss (\LossThirtyTrainSlope{} and \LossSixtyTrainSlope\,\% per
epoch) and the holdout PVE (\LossThirtyHoldSlope{} and \LossSixtyHoldSlope\,\% per epoch) still fall in both runs,
but the 3DPW validation PVE is flat (\LossThirtyValSlope{} and \LossSixtyValSlope\,\% per epoch). At the last epoch it is
\SelThirtyValPVE\,mm after 30 and \SelSixtyValPVE\,mm after 60 epochs (Table~\ref{tab:sweep-validation}), a change
well inside the run-to-run variation of Section~\ref{sec:method-variation}. Longer training thus improves the
synthetic monitor but not the real validation images, and the pre-set 30 epochs were kept.

\IfFileExists{tables/T22_training_length.tex}{\input{tables/T22_training_length}}{\TODO{T22 training-length table missing}}

The final evaluation (Table~\ref{tab:training-length}) is consistent with this. Doubling the epochs lowered the
holdout PVE by \StudentSixtyGainHoldout\,mm, to \StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, and the holdout root error from
\ResStudentRootErr{} to \StudentSixtyRootErr\,mm (teacher: \TeacherRootErrStudentJob\,mm). The 3DPW PVE changed from
\StudentThreeDPW{} to \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm; this change of \StudentSixtyChangeThreeDPW\,mm is far below the
\NoisePVE\,mm by which two identical pilots differed on the holdout, while the variation on 3DPW itself was not
measured. The 60-epoch run misses all four retention thresholds at the same cost. Training duration alone therefore
does not explain the student's real-image gap. No third training length was tried.

\subsection{Distillation temperature}
\label{sec:res-kd-temperature}

% Design: slurm/experiments/exp9_kd_temperature.sh header; collector scripts/analysis/kd_temperature_sweep.py.
% Results 8 Nov: data/kd_temperature_sweep.csv, numbers_nov.tex (KD...), CLUSTER_ANSWERS.md.
\IfFileExists{tables/T25_kd_temperature.tex}{\input{tables/T25_kd_temperature}}{\TODO{T25 temperature table missing}}
\IfFileExists{figures/F27_kd_temperature.tex}{\input{figures/F27_kd_temperature}}{\TODO{F27 temperature figure missing}}

The temperature $T$ sets how strongly the feature distillation softens the channel distributions before the student
is compared with the teacher (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}). Since $T = \MethodKDTemp$ was taken over without a search,
\KDSweepArms{} runs with $T \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, \KDSweepMaxT\}$ and the reported recipe otherwise test how much the
result depends on it. The output distillation has no temperature and stays unchanged. The sweep runs evaluate after
every epoch and log a validation loss; their per-temperature validation values are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:sweep-validation} and are still pending. Because the sweep trains $T = \MethodKDTemp$ again, it also
provides a second run of the reported setting.

The final evaluation (Figure~\ref{fig:kd-temperature}, Table~\ref{tab:kd-temperature}) is nearly flat. Over
$T = 1$ to 8 the holdout PVE moves by \KDHoldRange\,mm (\KDOneHold{} to \KDFourHold\,mm) and the 3DPW PVE by
\KDTPRange\,mm (\KDTwoTP{} to \KDFourTP\,mm), while the sweep's own $T = 4$ run differs from the reported one by
\KDNoiseHold\,mm on the holdout. With one run per temperature, differences of this size cannot be distinguished from
run-to-run variation; the formally lowest combined error at $T = \KDPickT$ is not interpreted as an improvement. No
temperature brings the student close to the retention thresholds. The run with $T = \KDSweepMaxT$ completed training,
but a later evaluation step failed on a network time-out, so it has no test numbers.

The conclusion is that neither temperature nor training length changes the result, and the pre-set values were kept.
This conclusion does not depend on the test set: no setting was changed, so no test-set number entered a decision.

\section{Multi-person detection}
\label{sec:res-multiperson}

\input{tables/T30_rh_crowd}

The evaluation sets differ in how many people an image holds. The AnnyOne holdout has \MethodHoldoutPeople{} people
in \MethodHoldoutN{} images; the 3DPW test set labels one or two people per image (\num{\MethodTestPeople} people in
\num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} images); the Relative Human validation split has \num{\MethodRHPeople} labelled people in
\MethodRHImages{} images, many of them crowded. On 3DPW the teacher and the student find
\FinalRecallThreeDPW\,\% of the labelled people, and on the holdout \FinalRecall{} and \StudentRecall\,\%
(Table~\ref{tab:baseline}). These sets contain few people per image, so Relative Human is the only one that tests
crowded scenes.

Table~\ref{tab:rh-crowd} splits the Relative Human recall by the number of labelled people per image. The teacher's
recall is \CrowdFinalTwoRec\,\% in images with two people and falls steadily with crowd size, to
\CrowdFinalFiveEightRec\,\% with five to eight and \CrowdFinalNinePlusRec\,\% with nine or more. The initial model is
lower in every bin (\CrowdBaseTwoRec{} to \CrowdBaseNinePlusRec\,\%), and the gap narrows in the most crowded images.
The single-person bin holds only \CrowdOneImages{} images. The student's Relative Human run is pending. Recall is
counted at IoU $\geq$ \MethodRHIoU{} against the labelled boxes; Relative Human has no 3D ground truth, so the
reconstruction quality of the people found in crowds is not measured.

Several aspects of multi-person reconstruction were not evaluated. No occlusion-specific or crowd benchmark with 3D
labels was used, and recall was not split by visibility or occlusion. The video of Section~\ref{sec:res-video} shows
one person, and the still image three.

\subsection{Video}

\IfFileExists{figures/F25_video_panels.tex}{\input{figures/F25_video_panels}}{\TODO{F25 video figure missing}}
\IfFileExists{tables/T27_video_fps.tex}{\input{tables/T27_video_fps}}{\TODO{T27 video table missing}}

The clip \texttt{man\_walking.mp4} has \VideoFrames{} frames of one man walking across a square. Both models were run
on the same frames with the procedure of Section~\ref{sec:method-video}, and five panels were rendered
(Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels}): the input, the raw student, the student with temporal filtering, the student with
landmark alignment, and the released baseline. The filter is a causal exponential moving average per tracked person on the bone
rotations (blended on the rotation sphere), the shape and the 3D position, after which the mesh is decoded again
through Anny. The alignment fits the mesh position and depth so that the projected joints match the MediaPipe
shoulder, hip, knee and ankle landmarks, and then smooths the corrected depth (Section~\ref{sec:method-temporal}).
Neither retrains the student. The student
kept a matched person in \VideoStudentMatched{} of \VideoFrames{} frames and the released baseline in
\VideoBaselineMatched; these counts come from the tracker and are not a detection recall against labels.

Under visual inspection the raw student mesh sometimes extends beyond the person; in Figure~\ref{fig:video-panels}
it is drawn ahead of the man. Temporal filtering makes its motion steadier. Landmark alignment improves its position
and apparent size, so the mesh follows the person more closely, and the depth smoothing reduces abrupt changes in
size. The motion statistics of the run agree with this. The mean pelvis-relative joint acceleration falls from
\VideoAccelRaw{} to \VideoAccelTemporal\,m/s$^2$ with the filter (released baseline, unfiltered:
\VideoAccelNAVER\,m/s$^2$), the mean speed of the head anchor from \VideoRootSpeedRaw{} to
\VideoRootSpeedTemporal\,m/s (\VideoRootSpeedAligned\,m/s after alignment), and the standard deviation of the
predicted gender over the clip from \VideoGenderStdRaw{} to \VideoGenderStdTemporal. These are smoothness measures,
not accuracy: a filter can also flatten real motion, the clip has no ground truth, and the landmarks come from
another detector rather than a measured body. Only this one clip, with one person and no occlusion, was evaluated.

Table~\ref{tab:video-fps} separates model inference from per-frame processing. For model inference alone, the
student runs at \VideoStudentRawFPS{} and the released baseline at \VideoNAVERFPS\,FPS on the same \VideoGPU{} and
frames, about \VideoSpeedRatioNAVER{} times faster; the two networks also differ in their heads
(\BgNaverHeadLayers{} against \BgOurHeadLayers{} cross-attention blocks). Including image preprocessing and output
transfer, a frame takes \VideoStudentPipeMs\,ms for the student (\VideoStudentPipeFPS\,FPS) and \VideoNAVERPipeMs\,ms
for the released baseline (\VideoNAVERPipeFPS\,FPS). The filter adds \VideoTemporalMs\,ms and the alignment fit
\VideoAlignMs\,ms; with both and preprocessing a frame takes \VideoAlignedPipeMs\,ms (\VideoAlignedPipeFPS\,FPS), and
\VideoAlignedRenderFPS\,FPS with rendering. Two parts of the complete pipeline were not timed: the offline landmark
extraction (MediaPipe on the CPU, a separate pass over the video) and video decoding and encoding. These numbers
therefore describe inference speed, not end-to-end speed, and no real-time claim is made for the complete pipeline.
They were measured on an A40 and do not compare with the L40S times of Table~\ref{tab:model-cost}.

\subsection{Real-image supervision}
\label{sec:res-3dpw}

On 3DPW (Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw}) the teacher has the lowest error on the converted labels, \FinalThreeDPW\,mm
PVE, and finds \FinalRecallThreeDPW\,\% of the labelled people. The uncorrected teacher is already
\GainBaseToControlThreeDPW\,mm better than the initial model, so most of the gain comes from training on 3DPW at all;
the two corrections add \GainFixesThreeDPW\,mm PVE and \GainFixesPAThreeDPW\,mm PA-PVE, small single-run
differences. The teacher is \GainOverOldThreeDPW\,mm better than an earlier model trained on the uncorrected labels
with a different schedule (Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}), so the share of the label correction in this gain is unknown.
Precision is low for every model because 3DPW labels only one or two actors per sequence
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

\subsection{Bone correction}
\label{sec:res-bone}

The teacher keeps the bone correction through ten epochs with real images (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}): neck02
is \FinalNeckTwo\,deg off against \ControlNeckTwo{} for the uncorrected teacher, the neck girth is \FinalGirth{} of
the true girth against \ControlGirth, and the neck-region joint error falls from \ControlNeckRegion{} to
\FinalNeckRegion\,deg. The head direction, which worsened with the correction alone (\NoTrainHeadDir\,deg), returns
to the uncorrected teacher's level, so the network learns on top of the correction instead of undoing it. The
correction adds no parameters, and PVE shows no sign of a cost. Its effect without training and after a short
fine-tune (Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base}, Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}), a real-frame example
(Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}) and the open question of why the bones flip are in Appendix~\ref{sec:diag-neck};
real photos have no neck ground truth.

\subsection{Hand articulation}
\label{sec:res-fingers}

Predicted fingers are \BaseFingers\,deg off in the initial model and \ControlFingers\,deg in the uncorrected teacher,
worse than fingers at rest (\BaseFingersRest\,deg; Table~\ref{tab:fingers}). With the flipped bones metacarpal1.L
and finger4-1.R corrected, the teacher reaches \FinalFingers\,deg (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}), and the correction
alone gave \NoTrainFingers: about half of the finger error was due to two flipped bones, training preserves the
gain, and the remainder is unexplained. For 3DPW people, whose labels hold rest-pose hands, the hand mask removes the
rotation terms of the \MethodHandMaskBones{} hand bones. On three real images the teacher's fingers look closed and
bent rather than splayed (Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo}); this is visual evidence of the correction and the
fine-tune together.

babies, while the initial model's stay level. A common shift of all ages cannot raise a rank measure, so this is a
genuine separation of children from adults, although part of the lower ages is likely a shift caused by the constant
age of the converted 3DPW labels. Children are improved, not solved, and men are not yet male. Ages are not converted
to years, because the code uses three age scales that disagree, and the holdout contains no children to check
against.

The teacher's bodies on real photos look much alike (Figure~\ref{fig:rest-lineup}). On the AnnyOne holdout, where

\subsection{Shape-supervision masking}
\label{sec:res-mask}

\input{tables/T21_mask_arms}

Two masked teachers, one run each, test the shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:method-real}. The full mask stops the
shape loss on the \MethodShapeMaskCount{} values the converter fixes for every 3DPW person (age \MethodConstAge{} and
seven values at \MethodConstOther), the partial mask only on the seven; gender stays supervised in both. On Relative
Human (Table~\ref{tab:mask-arms}) the full mask loses most of the teacher's gain (gender \RHGenderMasked\,\%, babies
child-shaped \RHBabiesBelowMasked\,\%) but restores variety on the demo images, where the spread of weight, cup size
and firmness rises from \SpreadWeightFinal--\SpreadCupFinal{} to \SpreadWeightMasked. The partial mask keeps the
children (AUC \RHAUCPartial, \RHBabiesBelowPartial\,\% of babies child-shaped) and doubles the weight spread on
Relative Human (\RHSpreadWeightFinal{} to \RHSpreadWeightPartial). At the 0.5 cut-off both masks lose gender, with
both sexes on the female side (partial mask: men \RHMenGenderPartial, women \RHWomenGenderPartial), although the AUCs
(\RHGenderAUCMasked{} and \RHGenderAUCPartial) still rank women above men. Body errors stay close to the teacher's,
so the mask changes only what the shape head predicts; without it, the teacher learned the seven constants and
predicts nearly the same muscle, weight, cup size and firmness for everyone on real photos.

A replacement rule fixed before the first masked run required gender accuracy of at least 60\,\%, a
child-against-adult AUC of at least 0.7 and a spread of muscle and weight above \SpreadRuleThreshold. Both masked runs
fail on gender and pass on the AUC, so the unmasked teacher remains the thesis model and the student's teacher.
Whether the restored variety is correct cannot be tested without real muscle or weight labels
(Appendix~\ref{app:teacher-extra}).

\chapter{Experiments and Results}
\label{ch:results}

This chapter reports what the models built on the diagnostics of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} achieve, in the order
the fixes were made. Each section opens with its figure or table, reads it, interprets it and says what it does not
show.

% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Evaluation sets and settings}
\label{sec:res-setup}

Four sets are used, all described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data} and scored as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. The
AnnyOne holdout holds the last 100 AnnyOne images (222 people, no children), never trained on; its headline setting
V1 masks the helper bones, and a trace script adds the placement metrics (root error, depth fit, drawn size) with
each body at the model's own predicted depth. The 3DPW test set is all \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images of the
corrected labels, scored in setting V0 with one script for every model; its numbers hold only against our converted
labels, which err by about \MethodLabelFloor\,mm, and are not comparable with published 3DPW numbers. The Relative
Human validation split gives real photos with an age group, a gender and a box per person, but no 3D body. The 12
demo images (4 photos and 8 3DPW test frames) have no labels; they are judged by eye, with bodies placed from the
detection box.

The models are those of Table~\ref{tab:model-names}. The central comparison is Final against Control: the two runs
share the start point (Base), the schedule, the real-image share and the hand mask, and differ only by the depth fix
and the bone correction. Every model was trained once, without a fixed seed. Two identical runs differed by
\NoisePVE\,mm PVE, \NoiseHeadDeg\,deg head direction and about \NoiseRecall{} recall points
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}); I call gaps below these ``within run-to-run variation'' and compare every fine-tune
with its own control, because restarting a fine-tune of Base already costs about \RestartCost\,mm PVE. Setting V0
reads about \MethodVzeroGap\,mm higher than V1.

(\FinalTooFarPct\,\% of all people). The pelvis lands farther from its pixel (\FinalPelvisPx{} against
\ControlPelvisPx{} pixels), likely because the head is pinned while the body is still too small; I did not check this.

Not shown: correct near and far depth, and why the slope does not move. Final's root error is above the 10-epoch depth
pilot's (\num{\DepthPilotRootErr}\,mm); the real images or the bone correction may cause this, which was not tested.
All depth numbers are synthetic, and Figure~\ref{fig:depth-579862} shows the pilot, not Final.

\section{The bone correction}
\label{sec:res-bone}

\begin{figure}[!tbp]

Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-office}.}
\label{fig:neck-3dpw-bar}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar} shows the correction on a real frame that no model trained on: Base draws both necks as
thin stalks under the head, the corrected model draws them at normal width. Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} and
Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones} give the correction without training and after a 2-epoch fine-tune. Final keeps it
through ten epochs with real images (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}): neck02 is \FinalNeckTwo\,deg off against
\ControlNeckTwo{} for Control, the neck girth \FinalGirth{} of the truth against \ControlGirth, and the joint error of
the neck region falls from \ControlNeckRegion{} to \FinalNeckRegion\,deg. The head direction, which got worse with the
correction alone (\NoTrainHeadDir\,deg), is back at Control's level.

The half-turn does not come back during training: the network learns on top of the correction instead of undoing it.
The correction is one constant rotation per bone, adds no parameters and says nothing about why the bones flipped.
PVE shows no sign of a cost (Section~\ref{sec:res-final}).

Not shown: a neck number on real photos, which have no neck ground truth. The real frame shows the neck pilot, not
Final, and its columns also differ by the fine-tune; on the holdout the same fine-tune without the correction keeps
girth \BaseGirth{} (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}), so the correction is the likely cause of the wider necks. Flips that
compensate each other in a chain (Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}) need a two-sided correction, which I did not implement.

\section{The shape-mask trade-off}
\label{sec:res-mask}

\input{tables/T21_mask_arms}

Two runs test the shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:method-real}, one run each. Final-masked stops the shape loss on the
\MethodShapeMaskCount{} values the converter fixes for every 3DPW person: age 0.55 and seven values at
\MethodConstOther. Final-partial-mask stops it only on the seven and keeps age supervised. Gender stays supervised in
both. Table~\ref{tab:mask-arms} sets the two next to Final. Their holdout and 3DPW errors stay within run-to-run
variation of Final, and the bone angles of Final-partial-mask stay at Final's level. On Relative Human, Final-masked
loses most of Final's gain (gender \RHGenderMasked\,\%, babies child-shaped \RHBabiesBelowMasked\,\%), but on the
demo images it brings back variety: the spread of weight, cup size and firmness rises from
\SpreadWeightFinal--\SpreadCupFinal{} to \SpreadWeightMasked. Final-partial-mask keeps the children (AUC \RHAUCPartial, \RHBabiesBelowPartial\,\% of babies
child-shaped) and loses gender as the full mask does, with both sexes on the female side (men \RHMenGenderPartial,
women \RHWomenGenderPartial).

The body errors stay put because the 3DPW meshes keep the neutral body; the trade-off is in the shape head. Without
the mask, Final learned the seven constants and predicts nearly the same muscle, weight, cup size and firmness for
everyone on real photos. The partial mask separates the two parts of the loss: with age supervised the children's gain
stays, so the age label carries it. Gender falls to chance although its label stays supervised. A reading that was
not tested: Final's gender gain depends on the seven constants on the 3DPW men as much as on the gender label.

Before the first masked run I fixed a rule: a masked model would replace Final if gender accuracy stayed at 60\,\% or
more, the AUC at 0.7 or more, and the spread of muscle and weight rose above \SpreadRuleThreshold. Both masked runs
fail on gender while passing on the AUC, so Final stays the final model and the Student's teacher.

Not shown: whether the varied values are right, since no real data set I have gives muscle or weight. Final-partial-mask
had no demo run, so whether it brings the variety back is not known; nor is why gender falls.

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} sets NAVER's released Anny model, run with our code, labels and settings, next to ours. On
the AnnyOne holdout it reads \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm PVE against \FinalPVE{} for Final. NAVER's model was trained on the
AnnyOne release, which includes the 100 images we hold out, so its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm is most likely an in-sample
number; ours are held out. On 3DPW, which neither model saw before our fine-tune, the final model is
\FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better (\FinalThreeDPW{} vs \NAVERThreeDPW). Both networks use the same ViT-L backbone;
the difference is the head, NAVER's 8-layer cross-attention decoder with \NAVERHeadParams\,M parameters against
\FinalHeadParams\,M in ours. NAVER's time and head FLOPs could not be measured with our tool, which supports only our
model class. The Student is \ParamRatioStudent$\times$ smaller, needs \FlopRatioStudent$\times$ fewer FLOPs and is
\SpeedRatioStudent$\times$ faster than Final, and has \ParamRatioStudentNAVER$\times$ fewer parameters than NAVER's
model (Figure~\ref{fig:cost-pve}).

The fair comparison is 3DPW, but most of Final's lead there comes from fine-tuning on 3DPW, which NAVER's model never
had: Control, without our fixes, is already at \ControlThreeDPW\,mm. The comparison shows what the real-image
fine-tune buys, not a better network. Final is about one sixth smaller than NAVER's model; that is not the
``significantly smaller'' model of requirement 1b, which is why the Student exists.

Not shown: NAVER's model fine-tuned on our labels, which would separate the network from the data; its latency and
total FLOPs; and its published SMPL-X numbers, which are not comparable. Times of the two GPUs do not mix.

\section{The ViT-B student}
\label{sec:res-student}

\input{tables/T17_student}
\input{figures/plots/F19_student}

The Student follows the recipe of Table~\ref{tab:student-recipe} (Section~\ref{sec:method-student}): a DINOv2 ViT-B
backbone (\StudentBackboneParams\,M parameters) started from NAVER's Multi-HMR ViT-B, a projector into the teacher's
width and heads copied from Final, trained on Final's targets plus its features and outputs for \StudentEpochs{}
epochs (\num{\StudentImages} images). Table~\ref{tab:student} gives the result against the 95\,\% rule. The cost side
is met. The accuracy side is not: on the AnnyOne holdout the Student reaches \StudentPVE\,mm PVE against the
teacher's \FinalPVE{} (bar \BarPVE), \StudentRatioPVE{} times the teacher's error, and on the 3DPW test
\StudentThreeDPW\,mm against \FinalThreeDPW{} (bar \BarThreeDPW), \StudentRatioThreeDPW{} times. MPJPE and PA-PVE
miss in the same way; only recall stays within the rule. Figure~\ref{fig:student-curves} shows that the holdout error
was still falling at the last epoch, from \StudentCurveHoldFifteen\,mm at epoch 15 to \StudentCurveHoldLast\,mm at
epoch 30, while the 3DPW validation error had flattened (\StudentCurveValTwentyFive{} to \StudentCurveValLast\,mm in
the last five epochs). Against Multi-HMR-Anny, the Student is \StudentGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better on 3DPW
(\StudentThreeDPW{} vs \NAVERThreeDPW) with \ParamRatioStudentNAVER$\times$ fewer parameters, which meets NAVER's 3DPW
bar of \BarNAVERThreeDPW\,mm; it misses NAVER's AnnyOne bar of \BarNAVERAnnyOne\,mm, which rests on a most likely
in-sample number.

By the 95\,\% rule against Final, the Student fails on both sets, and by a wide margin: its error is 62\,\% above the
teacher's on AnnyOne and 24\,\% on 3DPW, where the rule allows about 5\,\%. The curves say where more training could help: on AnnyOne the
error still fell by about 8\,mm per five epochs, on 3DPW it did not. A longer run would therefore narrow the synthetic
gap, but there is no sign that it would close the 3DPW one. What holds is the comparison with Multi-HMR-Anny: a
network with about a quarter of its parameters is better on real images, although most of that lead likely comes,
as for Final, from fine-tuning on 3DPW. Which reference sets the bar, Final or Multi-HMR-Anny, is the supervisor's decision; both are
reported.

Not shown: a second run, a longer schedule, and the Student's Relative Human numbers, fingers and necks on real
photos. The Student estimated its own bone correction on 200 training images; it gives the same holdout error as the
teacher's correction. No data augmentation was used.

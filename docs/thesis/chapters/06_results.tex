\chapter{Experiments and results}
\label{ch:results}

The Student is the model I deploy, so I start with it. I compare it with its teacher and with the released
baseline. Then I look at what a longer training and another distillation temperature change, and at how the Student
does on a photo and on a video. After that I study its teacher, Final. Older schedules and early student variants are
in Appendix~\ref{app:pilots}.

\section{Evaluation sets and settings}
\label{sec:res-setup}

I use four sets, described in Chapter~\ref{ch:data} and scored as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. The AnnyOne
holdout is the last \MethodHoldoutN{} AnnyOne images (\MethodHoldoutPeople{} people, no children), never trained on
and scored in setting V1. A second script adds the placement numbers of Section~\ref{sec:res-depth}, with each body at
the depth its model predicts.
The 3DPW test set is all \num{\ThreeDPWTestImages} test images with the corrected labels, in setting V0. Its numbers
hold only against my converted labels (Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}). Relative Human gives real photos with an age
group, a gender and a box per person. The \MethodDemoPhotos{} demo photos and \MethodDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames
have no labels, so I judge them by eye, with each body placed from its detection box.

Model names follow Table~\ref{tab:model-names}. Every model was trained once, without a fixed seed, and two identical
pilots differed by \NoisePVE\,mm PVE (Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}). So a PVE gap of a few millimetres between two
single runs can be noise.

Table~\ref{tab:baseline} lists the Student, its teacher and Multi-HMR-Anny on both test sets, with Control and Base for

\FinalPVE\,mm) and \StudentRatioThreeDPW{} times on 3DPW. Table~\ref{tab:student} applies my \BgNinetyFive\,\% rule

Against Multi-HMR-Anny the 3DPW result is mixed. The Student has a lower PVE (\StudentGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm
better, inside the bar of \BarNAVERThreeDPW\,mm) and a lower MPJPE (\StudentMPJPEThreeDPW{} against
\NAVERMPJPEThreeDPW\,mm). But its PA-PVE is slightly worse, \StudentPAThreeDPW{} against \NAVERPAThreeDPW\,mm. PA-PVE
first removes the best scale, rotation and position. So the Student's lead over the baseline lies in the overall size
and orientation of its bodies, and its aligned surface is slightly worse.

This comparison is not fully fair to the baseline. My Student and teacher were trained on converted 3DPW images, and
the released baseline was not. The result compares two complete trained systems, and it does not show that my network
design is better. On the holdout the bias goes the other way. NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne release, and
that release includes the \MethodHoldoutN{} images I hold out. Its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm is therefore most likely an
in-sample number, and mine are not. On 3DPW, which neither NAVER's model nor Base saw before my fine-tune, the teacher is
\FinalGainOverNAVERThreeDPW\,mm better (\FinalThreeDPW{} against \NAVERThreeDPW\,mm). For this reason it means little that
the Student misses the AnnyOne bar (\BarNAVERAnnyOne\,mm). I did not repeat the run, and I have no labelled real photos
on which to test the Student's hands and head.

\subsection{Model size and speed}

The Student has \StudentParams\,M parameters, \ParamRatioStudent{} times fewer than the teacher (\FinalParams\,M) and
\ParamRatioStudentNAVER{} times fewer than the baseline (\NAVERParams\,M; Table~\ref{tab:model-cost}). It needs
\FlopRatioStudent{} times fewer FLOPs than the teacher. On one L40S, in the same job, a Student forward pass took
\StudentMsLFortyS\,ms and a teacher pass \TeacherMsLFortyS\,ms, so the Student is \SpeedRatioStudent{} times faster. I
compare latencies only within one GPU column. In Figure~\ref{fig:cost-pve} the x-axis is the model
size, once in parameters and once in GFLOPs, and the y-axis the 3DPW test PVE. The Student sits to the left of the
teacher and above it: smaller, but with a higher error. This benchmark did not time the baseline. The only speed
comparison with it on the same GPU is the video in Section~\ref{sec:res-video}.

\section{Training for 60 instead of 30 epochs}
\label{sec:res-training-length}

\IfFileExists{tables/T22_training_length.tex}{\input{tables/T22_training_length}}{\TODO{T22 training-length table missing}}
\IfFileExists{figures/F23_loss_curves.tex}{\input{figures/F23_loss_curves}}{\TODO{F23 loss-curve figure missing}}

After \StudentEpochs{} epochs the Student's holdout error measured during training (setting V0,
Figure~\ref{fig:loss-curves}) was still falling, from \StudentCurveHoldFifteen\,mm at epoch 15 to
\StudentCurveHoldLast\,mm at epoch 30. Its 3DPW validation error had almost stopped. It went from
\StudentCurveValTwentyFive{} to \StudentCurveValLast\,mm in the last five epochs. A simple rule for this is: if the training and the
validation curve both still fall, train longer, and if only the training curve falls, stop. To check, I trained the same
recipe for \StudentSixtyEpochs{} epochs.

In Figure~\ref{fig:loss-curves} the x-axis is the epoch. The upper plot of each panel has the training loss on a log
scale, and the lower one the PVE in mm, measured every \LossEvalEvery{} epochs on the holdout and on \LossValImages{}
3DPW validation images. Both runs were trained before I added the option to log a validation loss, so I use this
PVE in its place. The holdout PVE rises between epochs 5 and 10, when the copied heads are unfrozen. The large drops in the
training loss follow the learning-rate halvings (every \LossLRHalfThirty{} and every \LossLRHalfSixty{} epochs).

Over the last 20\,\% of the epochs the training loss and the holdout PVE still fall in both runs, but the 3DPW
validation PVE is flat (\LossThirtyValSlope{} and \LossSixtyValSlope\,\% per epoch). The test numbers in
Table~\ref{tab:training-length} agree. Doubling the epochs lowered the holdout PVE (setting V1) by \StudentSixtyGainHoldout\,mm, to
\StudentSixtyPVE\,mm, and the holdout root error from \ResStudentRootErr{} to \StudentSixtyRootErr\,mm (teacher:
\TeacherRootErrStudentJob\,mm).
On 3DPW the PVE went from \StudentThreeDPW{} to \StudentSixtyThreeDPW\,mm. This change of
\StudentSixtyChangeThreeDPW\,mm is far below the \NoisePVE\,mm that two identical pilots differed by on the holdout
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-noise}); the variation on 3DPW itself was not measured. The
60-epoch run still misses all four bars, at the same cost.

Longer training helps on synthetic images and not on real ones. By this rule the 3DPW curve says stop,
because its error has not improved since about epoch 25. This means the Student's gap to the teacher on real photos is
not a training-length problem. I keep the 30-epoch run as the reported Student. These two runs have no validation loss,
and I did not try a third training length.

The temperature $T$ sets how strongly the feature distillation softens the channel values before the Student is
compared with the teacher (Section~\ref{sec:bg-kd}). The reported Student uses $T = \MethodKDTemp$, which I chose without a search, so the
question is how much the result depends on it. I set up \KDSweepArms{} runs with $T \in \{1, 2, 4, 8, \KDSweepMaxT\}$ and the
reported recipe otherwise. The output distillation has no temperature, so it stays the same. The new runs evaluate
after every epoch and log a validation loss. Because the sweep trains $T = \MethodKDTemp$ again, it also gives one
more measure of run-to-run noise.

In Figure~\ref{fig:kd-temperature} the x-axis is the temperature on a log-2 scale and the y-axis the error in mm, one
line per metric and test set, with the teacher dashed and the bars dotted. From the plot I can read off which
temperature gives the lowest errors, and that is the one to pick. \TODO{Results pending: \KDSweepArms{} jobs submitted 6 November;

Table~\ref{tab:final-3dpw} scores every model with the same script. Final has the lowest error on our 3DPW-Anny

Figure~\ref{fig:depth-579862} (Appendix~\ref{app:figures}) shows the depth bug and its fix on one holdout image: Base
draws the man far behind his true position and too small, the depth pilot close to his true size. Over all people
(Figure~\ref{fig:depth-scatter}), Control places \ControlTooFarPct\,\% too far. Final moves the points towards the identity
line. The root error falls by \RootErrDropPct\,\% (\num{\ControlRootErr} to \num{\FinalRootErr}\,mm), the drawn size rises from

\FinalHeadPx{} pixels of its true position against \ControlHeadPx{} (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}).

The fix survives ten epochs with real images and the bone correction. Every run trained with the head-anchor targets
(Table~\ref{tab:depth-runs}) has a lower root error than every run without them. It moved the offset and the body size, not the slope

is also above the depth pilot's (\num{\DepthPilotRootErr}\,mm), possibly because of the real images or the bone
correction. I tested neither explanation, and I cannot say why the slope does not move. All depth numbers are
synthetic, and the single-image figure shows the pilot, not Final.

\subsection{Bone correction}
\label{sec:res-bone}

Figure~\ref{fig:neck-3dpw-bar} (Appendix~\ref{app:figures}) shows the correction on a real frame that no model trained on: Base draws both necks as
thin stalks under the head, the corrected model at normal width. Figure~\ref{fig:diag-neck-base} and
Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones} give the correction without training and after a 2-epoch fine-tune. Final keeps it through
ten epochs with real images (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}). Its neck02 bone is \FinalNeckTwo\,deg off against
\ControlNeckTwo{} for Control, its neck girth is \FinalGirth{} of the true girth against \ControlGirth, and the joint
error of the neck region falls from \ControlNeckRegion{} to \FinalNeckRegion\,deg. The head direction, which got worse
with the correction alone (\NoTrainHeadDir\,deg), is back at Control's level.

This means the network learns on top of the correction and does not undo it. The correction is one constant rotation
per bone and adds no parameters. I do not know why these bones are flipped in the first place. PVE shows no sign of a
cost. Real photos have no neck ground
truth. The real frame shows the neck pilot, not Final, and its columns also differ by the fine-tune. But on the
holdout the same fine-tune without the correction keeps the girth at \BaseGirth{} (Table~\ref{tab:neck-bones}), so
the correction is the likely cause of the wider necks. Flips that cancel each other along a chain
(Section~\ref{sec:diag-neck}) would need a two-sided correction, which I did not implement.

\subsection{Hand articulation}
\label{sec:res-fingers}

Predicted fingers are \BaseFingers\,deg off in Base and \ControlFingers\,deg in Control, worse than fingers left at
rest (\BaseFingersRest\,deg; Table~\ref{tab:fingers}). Final, with the two flipped hand bones corrected, reaches
\FinalFingers\,deg (Table~\ref{tab:final-annyone}), and the correction alone gave \NoTrainFingers. So about half of
the finger error was two flipped bones, metacarpal1.L and finger4-1.R, and training keeps the gain. The rest is not
explained. Real images add nothing to the fingers, because the hand mask removes the rotation terms of the
\MethodHandMaskBones{} hand bones for 3DPW people, whose labels hold rest-pose hands.

Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-demo} (Appendix~\ref{app:figures}) shows predicted fingers on real images, which every earlier demo set to rest pose. In
these crops Base's fingers are splayed and stiff, with one finger of the left hand sticking out as the flipped metacarpal1.L
would make it. Final's fingers are closed and bent, and they look like hands. In the party photo the hand lies over the
armrest, in the guitar frame it is curled as if fretting, and in the bar frame it bends over the edge of the bar. Small
hands (Figure~\ref{fig:fingers-all}) cannot be judged at print size. The real-image evidence is only these three
images, judged by eye, and they show the correction and the fine-tune together.

Table~\ref{tab:relhuman} measures body shape on labelled real photos. Final finds more people than Base and gets the
gender right for \RHGenderFinal\,\% of them, against \RHGenderBase\,\%. Women are recognised (mean predicted gender
\RHWomenGenderFinal, where 1 is female), but men stay in the middle (\RHMenGenderFinal). The child-against-adult AUC is
the chance that a random child gets a lower predicted age than a random adult, so 0.5 is chance. It rises from
\RHAUCBase{} to \RHAUCFinal, and \RHBabiesBelowFinal\,\% of the babies are child-shaped against \RHBabiesBelowBase\,\%
for Base. In Figure~\ref{fig:rh-age} the x-axis is the Relative Human age group and the y-axis the predicted Anny age. Base's

(Figures~\ref{fig:real-photos} and~\ref{fig:photo-montages}, Appendix~\ref{app:figures}) most men look male in Final, and the boy keeps an adult
body.

Because the AUC only ranks people, a common shift of all ages cannot raise it, so the step is a real separation of
children from adults. Part of the lower ages is likely a shift of this kind, caused by the 3DPW age label, which the
converter fixes at \MethodConstAge{} for every real person. The gender gain matches what I would expect from the
corrected 3DPW gender labels, because they supervise the shape head on real people for the first time. Children are a measured improvement, not solved, and men are not yet
male. I do not give ages in years, because the code uses three age scales that disagree, and I cannot check children
on held-out synthetic images, because the holdout has none. Only detected people count, so the matched sets differ between models.

\IfFileExists{tables/T24_gender_auc.tex}{\input{tables/T24_gender_auc}}{\TODO{T24 gender table missing}}

Gender accuracy needs a cut-off (a predicted gender above 0.5 counts as a woman). Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc} adds the
gender AUC, which needs none, and I use it as the main gender number. Final reaches \RHGenderAUCFinal{} against
\RHGenderAUCBase{} for Base. The two masked models of Section~\ref{sec:res-mask} reach \RHGenderAUCMasked{} and
\RHGenderAUCPartial, so they still rank women above men. Their accuracy at 0.5 is near chance only because both sexes
moved up, so most men are called women.

A likely reason why men land in the middle is the training data. On the AnnyOne holdout the gender labels span only
\HoldGenderLabelMin{} to \HoldGenderLabelMax, with \HoldGenderLabelAboveHalf\,\% above 0.5. If the training images are
similar, AnnyOne has no fully male body, and the model saw men labelled 0 only in 3DPW. A cut-off for real photos would
have to come from 3DPW validation, which I did not do. The best accuracy in Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc} uses a cut-off
chosen on Relative Human itself, so it only shows how large the threshold effect is.

In the rest-pose line-up (Figure~\ref{fig:rest-lineup}, Appendix~\ref{app:figures}) only the body differs. In the pictures, Base gives nearly
everyone the same slim, flat body. Final gives the singers a fuller chest and hips, and the man in the bar a broader
torso. The
tennis men stay slim in all three models, in line with the men's mid-range gender score, and Final-masked looks like
Final except for a slimmer man in the bar.

\IfFileExists{tables/T23_phenotype_corr.tex}{\input{tables/T23_phenotype_corr}}{\TODO{T23 phenotype table missing}}

These bodies look much alike, which could mean that Final's shape head cannot vary shape. Table~\ref{tab:pheno-corr}
checks this on the AnnyOne holdout, where the labels do vary. Final follows weight (Pearson $r$ between prediction and
label \PhenoRFinalWeight), gender (\PhenoRFinalGender) and cup size (\PhenoRFinalCupsize) best. Muscle is the weakest
(\PhenoRFinalMuscle) and got weaker from Base (\PhenoRBaseMuscle) to Final, likely because the converter fixes muscle
at \MethodConstOther{} for every 3DPW person; I did not test this. Every slope of prediction against label is below 1
(Final's largest is \PhenoSlopeMaxFinal, for weight), so all three models pull their predictions towards the middle.

This shows that Final can express shape, at least on synthetic images. Base, which never saw 3DPW, already shows
part of this narrow spread on real photos. So the gap between synthetic and real images is part of the cause, and not

\subsection{Shape-supervision masking}
\label{sec:res-mask}

\input{tables/T21_mask_arms}

I trained two masked models to test the shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:method-real}, one run each. Final-masked stops the shape loss on the
\MethodShapeMaskCount{} values the converter fixes for every 3DPW person: age \MethodConstAge{} and seven values at
\MethodConstOther. Final-partial-mask stops it only on the seven and keeps age supervised. Gender stays supervised in
both. On Relative Human (Table~\ref{tab:mask-arms}), Final-masked loses most of Final's gain (gender
\RHGenderMasked\,\%, babies child-shaped \RHBabiesBelowMasked\,\%), but on the demo images it brings back variety: the
spread of weight, cup size and firmness rises from \SpreadWeightFinal--\SpreadCupFinal{} to \SpreadWeightMasked.
Final-partial-mask keeps the children (AUC \RHAUCPartial, \RHBabiesBelowPartial\,\% of babies child-shaped) and
doubles the weight spread on Relative Human (\RHSpreadWeightFinal{} to \RHSpreadWeightPartial;
Table~\ref{tab:gender-auc}). At the 0.5 cut-off it loses gender as the full mask does, with both sexes on the female
side (men \RHMenGenderPartial, women \RHWomenGenderPartial).

Both masked models keep body errors close to Final's. The mask therefore changes only what the shape head predicts (the
3DPW mesh targets still hold the neutral body). Without the mask, Final learned the seven constants and predicts nearly the same muscle, weight, cup size and firmness for everyone on real photos. With
age supervised the children's gain stays, so the age label carries it. Gender accuracy falls although its label stays
supervised. One reading I did not test is that Final's gender gain depends on the seven constants on the 3DPW men as
much as on the gender label.

Before the first masked run I fixed a rule: a masked model would replace Final if gender accuracy stayed at 60\,\% or
more, the child-against-adult AUC at 0.7 or more, and the spread of muscle and weight rose above
\SpreadRuleThreshold. Both masked runs fail on gender and pass on the AUC. So Final stays the thesis model and the
Student's teacher. Final-partial-mask keeps the children and more shape variety, but it loses gender at the 0.5
cut-off. I cannot say whether the varied values are right, because no real data set I have gives muscle or weight.
Final-partial-mask had no demo run, so I do not know whether it brings the variety back on the demo images. And I do
not know why gender falls.

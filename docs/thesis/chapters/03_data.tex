\section{AnnyOne}
\label{sec:data-annyone}

AnnyOne~\cite{bregier2025anny} is the synthetic dataset released with Anny. It contains \DataAnnyOneImages{}
rendered images of \DataAnnyOneRenderPx{}$\times$\DataAnnyOneRenderPx{} pixels with on average
\DataAnnyOnePeoplePerImage{} people per image. Every visible person has an exact label: the Anny pose (\DataBones{} bone transforms), the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes
(Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}) and the camera intrinsics (focal length and principal point). The network receives
each image resized to \DataInputPx{} pixels. The field of view varies between images
(\DataAnnyOneFovMin{}$^\circ$ to \DataAnnyOneFovMax{}$^\circ$ on a sample of \DataAnnyOneFovSample{} images), so
training and evaluation use each image's own intrinsics.

\paragraph{Split.} AnnyOne has no official split. The last \MethodHoldoutN{} images, with \MethodHoldoutPeople{}
people, form the holdout (indices in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}; limitations in Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}),
which no model trained in this thesis has seen; all earlier images form the training set. Unless stated otherwise,
holdout errors use the holdout protocol (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}).

\paragraph{Age distribution.} Anny produces a child shape below an age value of about \DataChildAgeLow{} to
\DataChildAgeHigh{} (Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}). Only \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\% of AnnyOne's people are
younger than 0.3 (\DataAnnyOneAgeBelowTwo\% below 0.2, \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowFour\% below 0.4). The median age is
\DataAnnyOneAgeMedian, and \DataAnnyOneAgeModeShare\% of all people lie between 0.5 and 0.6. In a training batch,
\DataBatchChildLow\% to \DataBatchChildHigh\% of the supervised people are younger than 0.3. Children are evaluated on
Relative Human (Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}).

\section{3DPW}
\label{sec:data-3dpw}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is a real video dataset of \DataThreeDPWSequences{} sequences recorded with a hand-held
phone camera at \DataThreeDPWWidth{}$\times$\DataThreeDPWHeight{} pixels (portrait) and \DataThreeDPWFps{} frames per
second. Inertial sensors on the actors provide SMPL ground truth. The standard release has \DataThreeDPWTrainSeq{}
training, \DataThreeDPWValSeq{} validation and \DataThreeDPWTestSeq{} test sequences with one or two labelled actors
each, in total \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences (\DataThreeDPWMen{} men, \DataThreeDPWWomen{} women).
Bystanders are not labelled. 3DPW's splits are kept. One validation sequence,
\texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}, yields no labels in any label set (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}), which leaves
\DataThreeDPWValUsableSeq{} validation sequences.

\paragraph{Need for conversion.} As 3DPW's labels are SMPL bodies (Table~\ref{tab:body-models}), real-image
training and testing require an Anny body fitted to every labelled 3DPW person and stored in AnnyOne's label format.

\section{Converting SMPL to Anny}
\label{sec:data-converter}

Anny's skinning has no analytic inverse, so pose and shape are recovered from joint positions by optimisation.
Appendix~\ref{app:conversion} documents earlier versions.

\paragraph{Input and correspondence.} For each person and frame the targets are the \DataSmplJoints{} joints
$\hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}$ of 3DPW's per-frame \texttt{jointPositions}, together with gender, camera and frame index. The
joints are not recomputed with the neutral SMPL model, because 3DPW's shape coefficients belong to the gendered
models and the neutral model moves the joints by \DataNeutralShift\,mm on average. A correspondence $\pi$ assigns
each SMPL joint $k$ to an Anny bone $\pi(k)$ (Section~\ref{sec:data-history}).

\paragraph{Objective.} The optimisation variables are the \DataBones{} bone rotations $\mathbf{r}$ in 6D form (two
columns of each rotation matrix), the phenotype logits $\boldsymbol{\ell}$, which the sigmoid $\sigma$ maps into
$(0,1)$, and a translation $\mathbf{t}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\min_{\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{t}}\
 & \frac{1}{24}\sum_{k=1}^{24}
   \left\| \mathbf{p}_{\pi(k)}\!\left(\mathbf{r},\sigma(\boldsymbol{\ell})\right)
   + \mathbf{t} - \hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}_{k}\right\|_2^2 \\
 & + \lambda_{r}\sum_{b\neq\text{root}}\left\|\mathbf{R}_b-\mathbf{I}\right\|_F^2
   + \lambda_{\phi}\sum_{j\in\text{free}}\left(\sigma(\ell_j)-\tfrac12\right)^2 ,
\end{split}
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_{b}$ is the point attached to bone $b$ (its head or a virtual joint, Section~\ref{sec:data-history})
and $\mathbf{R}_b$ the bone's rotation matrix. The first regulariser ($\lambda_r = \DataPoseReg$) pulls every non-root
rotation towards identity, because a twist about a bone's own axis moves no joint but deforms the mesh. The second
($\lambda_\phi = \DataShapeReg$) pulls the free phenotypes towards 0.5, so that a residual joint gap cannot drive a
phenotype to an extreme value.

\paragraph{Free parameters.} Only the root and \DataFittedBones{} of the \DataMappedBones{} mapped bones may rotate;
\DataRestBones{} of the \DataBones{} bones stay at rest, among them \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03}, \texttt{head}
and all finger bones. Weight, muscle, cup size, firmness and the three ethnicity values move no joint, and adult age
barely does. Gender is therefore taken from 3DPW and
written in Anny's convention (men \DataGenderMale, women \DataGenderFemale), age is fixed at \DataFixedAge{} (an
adult value) and the seven other phenotypes at \DataFixedNeutral; only height and proportions are fitted.

\paragraph{Staged fitting.} Adam (learning rate \DataFitLr) optimises Equation~\ref{eq:fit} in three stages: first
the root, then the bone rotations, and finally the free phenotypes, which carry the weakest signal. Each frame starts
from the previous frame's solution; a person's first frame uses \DataColdIters{} iterations per
stage, later frames \DataWarmIters.

\paragraph{One shape per person and sequence.} After the per-frame fits, the converter takes the median of the
fitted phenotypes over the sequence as the person's shape, so that body proportions are constant through the
video. With this shape fixed, the pose of every frame is refitted (\DataRefineIters{} iterations per frame) and the
body is transformed into camera coordinates.

\paragraph{Label files.} Every third valid frame is fitted (\DataLabelFps{} frames per second), on one frame grid per
sequence (frame numbers divisible by \DataFrameStride), so that all labelled actors of a sequence appear in the same
image label, as multi-person detection requires. The label builder writes one file per image in the AnnyOne format
and verifies that the image exists. It drops a person whose fit error exceeds \DataDropErr\,mm, of whose mesh less
than \DataDropVisPct\% lies inside the image, or whose box is narrower or shorter than \DataDropBox\,px. The data
loader pads the portrait images to a square and shifts the principal point accordingly.

\section{Geometric correspondence and virtual joints}
\label{sec:data-history}

SMPL and Anny place several joints, the hips among them, at different anatomical points, so the correspondence is
geometric rather than by name, which would force the fitter to distort the body: both skeletons are placed in their rest pose and aligned at the hip midpoint and along the body
axes. Each Anny hip bone is then chosen by distance and each spine bone by its normalised height along the torso.
Appendix~\ref{app:joints} gives the full correspondence.

For each of the \DataVirtualJoints{} torso targets $k$ attached to Anny bone $b=\pi(k)$, the fitter uses a virtual
joint
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf p_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)
   =\mathbf h_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)+\mathbf Q_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)\boldsymbol\delta_k ,
 \label{eq:virtual-joint}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf h_b$ is the posed bone head, $\mathbf Q_b$ its orientation and $\boldsymbol\delta_k$ a constant offset
in the bone's rest frame, measured on the aligned rest skeletons. Both skeletons are thus compared at the same body location while Anny's rig
remains unchanged. Limb targets use the bone head itself. The offsets reduce the difference in joint placement; the two models
remain anatomically different.

\section{Label construction and validation}
\label{sec:data-validation}

The corrected labels comprise \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{} images and \DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} people. They use
Anny's gender convention and one frame grid per sequence, and every label has its image. The two earlier label sets, their defects and the re-fit that removed them
are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:conversion} (Tables~\ref{tab:label-sets} and~\ref{tab:label-check}).

The fitting loss does not show whether the finished labels are correct. A separate check therefore compares four properties of the
finished label files with 3DPW's source data: body-joint positions, projected keypoints, the
presence of every actor, and the gender convention. Because it samples images, the check cannot prove that every
label is free of error. On the corrected labels the median body-joint discrepancy is \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm, and the
check passes with two remaining warnings (Table~\ref{tab:label-check-corrected}). These come from limb proportions
and from joints that SMPL and Anny define differently, the hip among them.

\section{Relative Human}
\label{sec:data-rh}

Neither the AnnyOne holdout nor 3DPW contains children. Relative Human, introduced with BEV~\cite{sun2022bev},
contains \DataRHImages{} real photographs, many of them crowded and with children. Each person has a box, an age group
(adult, teen, kid, baby) and a gender coded 0 for male, as in Anny; there are no 3D labels. The licence terms of the copy used were not verified
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}). Only the validation split is used: \DataRHValImages{} images with \DataRHValPeople{} labelled people (\DataRHValAdults{} adults,
\DataRHValTeens{} teens, \DataRHValKids{} kids, \DataRHValBabies{} babies, \DataRHValUnknownAge{} of unknown age).

The model runs once on each whole photo with a field of view of \DataRHFov$^\circ$, and detections are matched to
labelled boxes as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. A predicted age below \DataRHChildThr{} counts as child-shaped, a
predicted gender below \DataRHGenderThr{} as male. Per age group, recall and the mean predicted age are reported;
overall, gender accuracy and the child-versus-adult AUC of the predicted age (the probability that a kid or baby
receives a lower predicted age than an adult; 0.5 is chance). Shape numbers cover detected people only and are
reported next to recall.

\section{Limitations of the evaluation data}
\label{sec:data-limits}

The evaluation data limit the conclusions of this thesis in three ways.

\paragraph{AnnyOne holdout.} The synthetic test set consists of only \MethodHoldoutN{} images
(\MethodHoldoutPeople{} people). They were taken from the end of the dataset rather than sampled at random, the
youngest person has age \DataHoldoutMinAge{} (no children), and the same images serve as in-training monitor and as
reported test set. Holdout results therefore do not support strong generalisation claims; these would require a larger,
randomly drawn and age-balanced holdout.

\paragraph{Converted 3DPW labels.} The labels are converted, not ground truth, and test three phenotypes:
gender (from metadata), height and proportions (fitted; medians \DataLabelMedianHeight{} and
\DataLabelMedianProportions). The other \DataConstantCount{} phenotypes are identical for every person: age \DataFixedAge{}, a
full adult (Figure~\ref{fig:bg-age-sweep}), and \DataNeutralCount{} values at \DataFixedNeutral. The shape loss and
mesh terms pull every real person towards these constants; 3DPW people are an estimated
\DataSupervisedShareThreeDPW\% of the supervised people in a \DataMixAnnyOne/\DataMixThreeDPW{} mixed batch. The teacher
with full shape mask removes the shape loss on the constants (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). The 3DPW error, measured
against these neutral-body meshes, may favour a neutral shape prediction (not tested). The labels can neither
teach nor test children. The bones \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03} and
\texttt{head} stay at identity, so the head turns only through \texttt{neck01} and the labels cannot test the neck
bones of Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}. SMPL has no finger joints, so every label has rest-pose hands, and training
masks their rotation terms for 3DPW people (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}). The labels differ from 3DPW's source
joints by \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm at the median and \DataCheckJointsNewPn\,mm at the 95th percentile. Networks are
compared with the fitted targets, not the source joints, so this residual is no error floor and is not
subtracted from reported PVE values. The targets are Anny meshes rather than the original SMPL meshes, they
carry this residual, and MPJPE averages all \DataBones{} Anny joints (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}). The 3DPW
results are therefore not comparable with published 3DPW results. Because
bystanders are unlabelled, a correct detection of a bystander counts as false, and the measured 3DPW precision is
only a lower bound.

\paragraph{Relative Human.} The labels are 2D boxes with age-group and gender tags, without 3D ground
truth, so pose and shape error cannot be measured.

\section{Demo images and the diagnostic manifest}
\label{sec:data-manifest}

The diagnostic checks use a fixed sample of \DataManifestTotal{} AnnyOne (image, person) entries, which all runs
after the initial model exclude from sampling, so that their exposure is identical for all later models
(Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}). Visual comparisons use \DataDemoTotal{} demo images: \DataDemoPhotos{} real
photographs and the middle frame of each of
\DataDemoFrames{} 3DPW test sequences. No model is trained on them and no metric is computed on them. The photo
comparison with the released baseline (Section~\ref{sec:res-image-compare}) and the walking video
(Section~\ref{sec:res-video}) are likewise unlabelled and permit no reconstruction error.

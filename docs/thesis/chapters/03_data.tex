\chapter{Data}
\label{ch:data}

This chapter describes the images and labels behind every number in the thesis. Four sources are used
(Table~\ref{tab:datasets}). AnnyOne is synthetic and carries exact Anny labels. It is the main training set.
3DPW is real video with SMPL ground truth. I converted it to Anny, so that the model can train and be tested on
real people in Anny space (requirements 1c and 2a). Relative Human is a set of real photos with age-group and
gender labels. I use it only to evaluate children and gender. A small diagnostic manifest and twelve demo images
serve the checks of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} and the by-eye comparisons of Chapter~\ref{ch:results}.

Most of the chapter is about the conversion of 3DPW. The converted labels decide what the real-image numbers can
show and what they cannot, so I describe how they are made (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}), how the
converter developed (Section~\ref{sec:data-history}), the three defects I found in the first label set and how I
removed them (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}), and the limits that remain by design
(Section~\ref{sec:data-limits}).

\section{AnnyOne}
\label{sec:data-annyone}

AnnyOne~\cite{bregier2025anny} is the synthetic dataset released with the Anny body model. It holds
\DataAnnyOneImages{} rendered images of \DataAnnyOneRenderPx{}$\times$\DataAnnyOneRenderPx{} pixels in indoor
and outdoor scenes. Each image shows several people, \DataAnnyOnePeoplePerImage{} on average. For every visible
person the label gives the exact Anny pose (\DataBones{} bone transforms), the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotype values
and the camera intrinsics. The network sees each image resized to \DataInputPx{} pixels, a grid of
\DataTokenGrid{}$\times$\DataTokenGrid{} tokens of \DataPatchPx{} pixels each. The field of view changes from
image to image (\DataAnnyOneFovMin{}$^\circ$ to \DataAnnyOneFovMax{}$^\circ$ on a sample of
\DataAnnyOneFovSample{} images), so training and evaluation use each image's own intrinsics.

\paragraph{Split.} I keep the order of the release. Indices 0 to \DataAnnyOneLastTrainIndex{} are the training
set. The last \DataHoldoutImages{} images, indices \DataHoldoutFirstIndex{} to \DataHoldoutLastIndex, are the
holdout. No model trained in this thesis has seen them. The holdout is my own choice, not an official test split.
NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne release, which includes the \DataHoldoutImages{} images I hold out, so
for that model the holdout is most likely in-sample; ours never saw it (Chapter~\ref{ch:results} uses fixed wording
for this comparison). The 3DPW test script also scores the last \DataHoldoutImages{} AnnyOne images,
most likely the same ones, but without masking the helper bones (setting V0, Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Its
``AnnyOne test-100'' value is therefore not the holdout number, which is always given in setting V1.

\paragraph{Few children.} Age is a phenotype between 0 and 1. In Anny the child shape appears below an age of
about \DataChildAgeLow{} to \DataChildAgeHigh{} (Chapter~\ref{ch:background}); I never convert these values to
years, because three age scales in the code and the data disagree. Only \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\% of
AnnyOne's people have an age below 0.3 (\DataAnnyOneAgeBelowTwo\% below 0.2, \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowFour\% below
0.4). The median age is \DataAnnyOneAgeMedian, and \DataAnnyOneAgeModeShare\% of all people lie between 0.5 and
0.6. In a normal training batch, \DataBatchChildLow\% to \DataBatchChildHigh\% of the supervised people are
younger than 0.3. The holdout has nobody younger than \DataHoldoutMinAge. It therefore tests adults only and
cannot show whether a model reads children. This gap is why I added Relative Human
(Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}).

\paragraph{Use.} Every model of the thesis trains on the AnnyOne training set. In the fine-tunes with real data,
\DataMixAnnyOne\% of each batch comes from AnnyOne and \DataMixThreeDPW\% from 3DPW-train
(Chapter~\ref{ch:method}).

\section{3DPW}
\label{sec:data-3dpw}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is a real video dataset of \DataThreeDPWSequences{} sequences, filmed with a
hand-held phone camera, outdoors and indoors, at \DataThreeDPWWidth{}$\times$\DataThreeDPWHeight{} pixels (portrait)
and \DataThreeDPWFps{} frames per second. Inertial sensors on the actors give SMPL ground truth for every frame.
The standard release splits the sequences into \DataThreeDPWTrainSeq{} for training, \DataThreeDPWValSeq{} for
validation and \DataThreeDPWTestSeq{} for testing. Each sequence labels one or two actors,
\DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences in total (\DataThreeDPWMen{} men and \DataThreeDPWWomen{} women).
Bystanders who walk through a scene have no labels.

3DPW gives SMPL bodies; the model predicts Anny bodies. The two models have different skeletons and different
shape parameters, so 3DPW cannot be used as it is. I wrote a converter that fits an Anny body to every labelled
person (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). The splits stay as 3DPW defines them. The training split is mixed
into training batches. The validation split is scored after every epoch on \DataValImagesPerEpoch{} images. The
test split is used only for reported numbers and never for training. One validation sequence,
\texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}, yields no labels (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}), so validation has
\DataThreeDPWValUsableSeq{} usable sequences.

\section{Converting SMPL to Anny}
\label{sec:data-converter}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the six steps from a 3DPW frame to an Anny label. The converter is
\texttt{scripts/data/smpl\_to\_anny.py}, now at version 2.3; a second script merges its fits into one label file
per image.

\begin{figure}[tbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F02_pipeline}
  \caption{The six steps from a 3DPW frame to an Anny label: SMPL joints, frame grid, per-frame fit, one body per
  person, camera frame, label file with the drop rules. The frame grid is shared by both actors since converter
  version 2.3.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Objective.} No inverse of linear blend skinning exists for Anny, so I follow the autograd route of
NVlabs' \texttt{smpl2soma} and the joint-fitting objective of \texttt{joints2smpl}. For each person and frame I
search Anny's \DataBones{} rotations $\mathbf{r}$ (6D), the phenotype logits $\boldsymbol{\ell}$ and a
translation $\mathbf{t}$, so that Anny's joints land on the \DataSmplJoints{} SMPL joints
$\hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}$ of the frame:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{t}}
\ \frac{1}{24}\sum_{k=1}^{24}
\left\| \mathbf{p}_{\pi(k)}\!\left(\mathbf{r},\sigma(\boldsymbol{\ell})\right)
 + \mathbf{t} - \hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}_{k}\right\|_2^2
 + \lambda_{r}\sum_{b\neq\text{root}}\left\|\mathbf{R}_b-\mathbf{I}\right\|_F^2
 + \lambda_{\phi}\sum_{j\in\text{free}}\left(\sigma(\ell_j)-\tfrac12\right)^2 .
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
Here $\pi$ is the joint correspondence and $\mathbf{p}_{b}$ the point attached to bone $b$ (defined below). Both
regularisers are weak. The first, $\lambda_r = \DataPoseReg$, pulls every non-root rotation towards identity. A
twist about a bone's own axis moves no joint but corkscrews the mesh, so without this term the solution is not
unique. The second, $\lambda_\phi = \DataShapeReg$, pulls the free phenotypes towards 0.5, so that one remaining
joint gap cannot drag a phenotype to an extreme.

\paragraph{Stages.} I optimise in three stages with Adam (learning rate \DataFitLr): first the root orientation
and translation, then the bone rotations, then the free phenotypes. Shape comes last because it is the weakest
signal; fitted earlier, it absorbs pose error. 3DPW is video, so each frame starts from the solution of the
previous one. The first frame of a person runs \DataColdIters{} iterations per stage, every later frame
\DataWarmIters.

\paragraph{Targets.} I fit to 3DPW's own per-frame \texttt{jointPositions}, not to a forward pass of the neutral
SMPL model. 3DPW's shape coefficients belong to the gendered SMPL models. Evaluated in the neutral model, they
moved the joints by \DataNeutralShift\,mm on average and by \DataNeutralShiftFeet\,mm at the feet on the first
frame I checked. Only joint positions are matched. SMPL's rotations, shape coefficients and surface are not
used.

\paragraph{Correspondence checked by geometry.} My first converter chose the Anny bones by name. Two of those
choices were wrong in a way no name reveals. Anny's MakeHuman rig numbers the spine top-down
(\texttt{spine01} sits just below the neck, \texttt{spine05} on the pelvis), while SMPL numbers it bottom-up. So
SMPL's lumbar \texttt{spine1} had been mapped to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it. SMPL's hip joints had
been mapped to \texttt{pelvis.L/R}, short bones whose heads lie \DataHipMapError\,cm from the hip joint, instead
of \texttt{upperleg01.L/R}. The converter now evaluates both models in their rest poses, prints the two
skeletons side by side, and picks the spine bones by their height along the body axis and the hip bones by
their distance from the pelvis. The final correspondence is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:joints}.

\paragraph{Virtual joints.} Even with the right bones, the two rigs place their torso joints at different
anatomical points. In the rest pose, SMPL's pelvis joint sits \DataSmplPelvisAboveHips\,cm above its hip joints,
while Anny's root sits at hip height and \DataAnnyRootFront\,cm in front of them. SMPL's \texttt{spine3} lies
\DataSpineThreeGap\,cm from the nearest Anny bone head, and its collar joints sit \DataCollarGap\,cm further
out. Forcing bone heads onto such targets makes the optimiser compromise with the pelvis and the shape. Instead,
for each of \DataVirtualJoints{} torso joints I measure once, in the rest pose, the offset
$\boldsymbol{\delta}_b$ from the Anny bone head to the SMPL joint, expressed in the bone's own frame. The fit then
moves the point $\mathbf{p}_b = \mathbf{h}_b + \mathbf{R}_b\boldsymbol{\delta}_b$ onto the SMPL joint, where
$\mathbf{h}_b$ and $\mathbf{R}_b$ are the posed bone head and frame. The offsets are \DataOffsetPelvis\,cm for
the pelvis, \DataOffsetHip\,cm for the hips, \DataOffsetSpineMin{} to \DataOffsetSpineMax\,cm along the spine
and \DataOffsetCollar\,cm for the collars. Limb joints use the bone head.

\paragraph{Which bones move.} Only the root and the bones that carry a mapped joint may rotate:
\DataFittedBones{} of the \DataMappedBones{} mapped bones. The other bones (twist helpers, fingers, toes, face,
the upper neck) have no target, and in the first version about \DataUntargetedBones{} of them drifted freely. In
the final labels \DataRestBones{} of the \DataBones{} bones stay at rest, among them \texttt{neck02},
\texttt{neck03}, \texttt{head}, the jaw and every finger bone.

\paragraph{Shape.} I do not map phenotypes from SMPL's shape coefficients, which have no semantic counterpart.
Joint positions cannot determine most phenotypes either: weight, muscle, cup size, firmness and the three
ethnicity values move no joint, and adult age barely does. Left free, the optimiser used them as slack
(Section~\ref{sec:data-history}). The converter therefore reads gender from 3DPW's metadata and fixes age at
\DataFixedAge, close to the AnnyOne mean, because the 3DPW actors are adults. The other seven phenotypes stay at
\DataFixedNeutral. Only height and proportions are fitted. Anny's gender convention is 0 for male and 1 for
female: at gender 0 the rest body is \DataAnnyStatureMale\,m tall, at 1 it is \DataAnnyStatureFemale\,m. Since
version 2.3 the converter writes men as \DataGenderMale{} and women as \DataGenderFemale{}
(\texttt{--gender\_convention anny}); earlier versions had it the other way round
(Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}).

\paragraph{One body per person.} After a first pass over all frames, I take the median of each free phenotype
over the person's frames as that person's body. With this body fixed, the pose of every frame is fitted again
(\DataRefineIters{} iterations), starting from the first-pass solution. Each person thus gets one height and one
proportions value for the whole sequence.

\paragraph{Frames.} I fit every third valid frame (\DataLabelFps{} frames per second); neighbouring frames are
near-duplicates. Since version 2.3 the frame grid is one absolute grid for every person of a sequence (frame
numbers divisible by \DataFrameStride, \texttt{--frame\_grid shared}), so the two actors of a sequence are
fitted on the same images wherever both are valid.

\paragraph{Camera frame and label files.} The fits live in 3DPW's world frame. Only the root changes when the
body moves into the camera's view: its rotation becomes $\mathbf{R}_{\text{cam}}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{root}}$ and its
position $\mathbf{R}_{\text{cam}}(\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{t}_{\text{3DPW}})+\mathbf{t}_{\text{cam}}$. For each frame I
project the posed mesh with 3DPW's camera to get a box and the fraction of vertices inside the image. The label
builder then writes one file per image in the AnnyOne format: camera intrinsics and, per person, the
$163{\times}4{\times}4$ pose, the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes and the box. It drops a person when the fit error
exceeds \DataDropErr\,mm, when less than \DataDropVisPct\% of the mesh is inside the image, or when the box is
narrower or shorter than \DataDropBox\,px. A dataset class returns these labels in AnnyOne's sample format, so
the training, collate and evaluation code run on them unchanged. 3DPW images are portrait, so the class pads
them to a square and shifts the principal point accordingly. Table~\ref{tab:converter-fixed} sums up what the
labels contain.

\section{Development of the converter}
\label{sec:data-history}

The converter reached its final form in four steps. Each step was triggered by a measured symptom. I keep the
history because it explains the design choices of the previous section.

\paragraph{The 34\,mm floor.} I validated the first converter (v1) on \DataVOneBarFrames{} frames of
\texttt{downtown\_bar\_00} (mean \DataVOneBarMean\,mm; Table~\ref{tab:fit-stages}) and then ran it on four test
scenes. Two facts in that run's log showed a defect before I looked at any render. The error was
\DataVOneFloorMin{} to \DataVOneFloorMax\,mm on every frame of every scene, walking or fencing. And all four
actors came out with the same body: gender \DataVOneGender, age \DataVOneAge, height \DataVOneHeight, asian
\DataVOneAsian. A residual that does not depend on the pose and a shape that does not depend on the person mean
that the shape was absorbing a fixed mismatch between the skeleton definitions. The rest-pose comparison then
showed it: \texttt{spine1} was mapped \DataSpineMapError\,cm too high and the hips \DataHipMapError\,cm off. The
renders had looked wrong for a separate reason: the renderer used 3DPW's \texttt{img\_frame\_ids}, an index map
into the 60\,Hz pose arrays, as image numbers, and drew pose frame $t$ on image $2t$.

\paragraph{Effect of each correction.} Table~\ref{tab:fit-versions} isolates the corrections on the hardest test
scene, \texttt{outdoors\_fencing\_01} (\DataFencingFrames{} frames at stride 25, including full lunges). The
geometry-checked correspondence and 3DPW's own joints as targets (v2) removed the common floor. Limbs and head
dropped to 1 to 10\,mm, but the torso joints stayed at \DataVTwoTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoTorsoMax\,mm because of
the definition gap. The optimiser compromised by shifting the pelvis \DataVTwoPelvisShift\,cm and driving
\texttt{proportions} to \DataVTwoProportions, a value that is wrong for the person and must not become a label.
The virtual joints (v2.1) brought the torso to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMin{} to \DataVTwoOneTorsoMax\,mm, the pelvis to
\DataVTwoOnePelvis\,mm and \texttt{proportions} to \DataVTwoOneProportions. The arm chain (shoulders
\DataVTwoOneShoulderMin{} to \DataVTwoOneShoulderMax\,mm) became the largest remaining term. The per-joint table is
now flat, which is what a correct correspondence looks like. v2.1 also added the bone mask, the warm start and the
shape prior. Read as an ablation, the correspondence accounts for about \DataGainCorrespondence\,mm of the gain and
the virtual joints for a further \DataGainVirtual\,mm, and the two act on different joints: the first on limbs and
head, the second on the torso. An intermediate version that down-weighted the torso joints and reported only a
``reliable'' subset hid the symptom without touching its cause; I dropped it.

\section{Three label defects and the v2.3 re-fit}
\label{sec:data-defects}

\paragraph{The independent check.} The converter grades its own fit, so it cannot see errors in what it is told
to fit or in what happens to the fits afterwards. I therefore wrote a separate check
(\texttt{check\_3dpw\_conversion.py}) that reads the finished label files. On a sample of images from every
sequence it compares the labels with 3DPW's own joints, surface and 2D keypoints, tests left/right consistency,
confirms that the data loader passes the labels on unchanged, and checks the labels for internal consistency
(gender, coverage of every valid actor, head turn). On the first label set (``old labels'', v2.2 fits, about
\DataOldCheckImages{} sampled images) its overall grade was FAIL. It found three defects.

\paragraph{Defect 1: gender reversed.} The converter wrote 1.0 for a man and 0.0 for a woman, MakeHuman's
convention. Anny's convention is the reverse (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). Every 3DPW man was thus fitted and
labelled with a female body, and every woman with a male one. The check found it by refitting with the flipped
value: the joint error fell for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of \DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men (median
\DataGenderFlipMenBefore{} to \DataGenderFlipMenAfter\,mm). For women the flip made the joints worse
(\DataGenderFlipWomenBefore{} to \DataGenderFlipWomenAfter\,mm) but the surface closer for
\DataGenderFlipWomenSurface{} of \DataGenderFlipWomenOf, so at that point the fix was confirmed for men only.
Because 3DPW makes up \DataMixThreeDPW\% of each mixed batch, a model trained on these labels keeps learning a
female body for every real man.

\paragraph{Defect 2: missing actor.} In \DataMissingActor{} of \DataTwoPersonChecked{} sampled two-person images,
one valid actor had no label. In all of them that actor had not been fitted on that frame. The cause was the
frame stride: each person's every-third-frame grid started at that person's own first valid frame. After an
invalid gap, the two actors of a sequence sat on different phases of the stride (frames 0, 3, 6 for one and 1, 4,
7 for the other). In training, the detection loss then treats a real, visible person as background.

\paragraph{Defect 3: missing labels.} The training split had \DataOldTrainImages{} label images where the fits
implied \DataRebuiltTrainImages, so \DataOldTrainMissingPct\% were missing; validation had
\DataOldValImages{} of \DataRebuiltValImages. Six training sequences had no labels at all. A first rebuild gave
the same counts, which ruled out my earlier explanation that the builder had run before all fits finished. The
real cause was that the image files of \DataMissingImageSeq{} sequences were missing or incomplete on the cluster
(for \texttt{courtyard\_captureSelfies\_00}, \DataSelfiesFilesBefore{} of \DataSelfiesFilesAfter{} files), most
likely from an incomplete unzip. The label builder skips any frame without an image file and does not count it,
so nothing reported the gap. After I re-extracted the images, a second rebuild recovered all of the missing
labels (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}, ``rebuilt''). That set still came from the v2.2 fits, with gender reversed and
actors missing, so I never trained on it.

\paragraph{The re-fit.} Defects 1 and 2 are decided inside the fit, so they needed a new converter version and a
full re-fit. Version 2.3 adds two switches: \texttt{--gender\_convention anny} writes men as 0 and women as 1, and
\texttt{--frame\_grid shared} puts every person of a sequence on one absolute frame grid. The defaults of both
switches reproduce v2.2 exactly, so the old labels can still be rebuilt for the history models. I re-fitted all
\DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} people with both switches on. The re-fit's own post-check passed on all
\DataRefitSeq{} sequences. Every man now has gender 0 and every woman 1. The mean joint error of the men fell from
\DataRefitMenBefore{} to \DataRefitMenAfter\,mm; that of the women stayed level (\DataRefitWomenBefore{} to
\DataRefitWomenAfter\,mm). \DataRefitWorseSeq{} single-person outdoor sequences, none of them with a woman, became
\DataRefitWorseMin{} to \DataRefitWorseMax\,mm worse; the likely reason is a different warm start, which I did not
test. Two-person frames in which one valid actor was not fitted went from \DataRefitGapBefore{} of
\DataRefitGapBeforeOf{} (\DataRefitGapBeforePct\%) to none.

\paragraph{The v23 labels.} I call the labels built from the v2.3 fits the v23 labels. They hold
\DataThreeDPWTotalImages{} images and \DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} people: train \DataThreeDPWTrainImages{} /
\DataThreeDPWTrainPeople, validation \DataThreeDPWValImages{} / \DataThreeDPWValPeople, test
\DataThreeDPWTestImages{} / \DataThreeDPWTestPeople{} (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}). There are fewer images than in
the rebuilt set but the same people, because both actors now share each frame. The builder dropped no person for
fit error or box size. It dropped \DataCrossCountryFramesNew{} frames for visibility, all of them from
\texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}. With 3DPW's own camera poses and intrinsics, the ground-truth joints of that
sequence sit at a depth of \DataCrossCountryDepth\,m, behind the camera, and project far outside the image. The old
fits show the same values (\DataCrossCountryFramesOld{} frames), and no label set ever contained this sequence. The
3D fit itself is fine; image labels for it cannot exist with this camera. Nothing on our side needs fixing.

\paragraph{The same check on the v23 labels.} I ran the independent check again on the v23 labels
(\DataCheckPeople{} people in \DataCheckImages{} images, \DataCheckPerSeq{} per sequence, \DataCheckSeq{}
sequences). Table~\ref{tab:label-check} sets the two runs side by side. The overall grade moved from FAIL to WARN.
The median body-joint error fell from \DataCheckJointsOld{} to \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. Every 3DPW man is
labelled 0 and every woman 1, and flipping the gender now makes the fit worse (men \DataCheckFlipMenLabel{} to
\DataCheckFlipMenFlipped\,mm). No sampled two-person image misses an actor (0 of \DataCheckGapNewOf). The head
turn relative to the chest is now kept (median \DataCheckHeadLabelNew$^\circ$ in the labels vs
\DataCheckHeadSmplNew$^\circ$ in SMPL). In the old labels it was mostly lost (\DataCheckHeadLabelOld$^\circ$ vs
\DataCheckHeadSmplOld$^\circ$, and only \DataCheckHeadTestLabelOld$^\circ$ vs
\DataCheckHeadTestSmplOld$^\circ$ in the test split). I did not check why it changed; every test person is a man and
was fitted with the female gender before, which may be the link. Figure~\ref{fig:label-check} shows one panel of the
check.

Two rows stay at WARN. The limb-length ratios differ from SMPL (forearm \DataCheckForearm, thigh
\DataCheckThigh), and \DataCheckFarPct\% of people have at least one joint more than \DataCheckFarMm\,mm off,
mostly at the hip (\DataCheckFarTrainPct\% in the training split, led by the left hip in one climbing sequence). The
likely reason for both is that Anny's and SMPL's limb proportions and hip joints differ; I did not test this
further. The three defects themselves are gone, and the independent check confirms it.

\section{What the 3DPW labels can and cannot test}
\label{sec:data-limits}

The v23 labels are free of the three defects, but they are converted labels, not ground truth. Five limits follow
from the design of the converter. Each one bounds a claim made later in the thesis.

\paragraph{Eight constant phenotypes.} Of the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes, the labels test three: gender (from
metadata), height and proportions (fitted, one value per person; medians \DataLabelMedianHeight{} and
\DataLabelMedianProportions). The other \DataConstantCount{} are the same for every person: age
\DataFixedAge{} and the \DataNeutralCount{} values at \DataFixedNeutral{} (Table~\ref{tab:converter-fixed}). Two
consequences follow. First, a model trained on 3DPW is pulled towards these constants for every real person: by the
shape loss on the phenotypes, and by the mesh terms, because the ground-truth mesh of a 3DPW person is built from the
same neutral values. In a 70/30 batch I estimate that about \DataSupervisedShareThreeDPW\% of the supervised people
are 3DPW people, since a 3DPW image holds fewer people than an AnnyOne image. Second, the 3DPW error is measured
against these neutral-body meshes, so the likely effect is that it rewards a model for predicting the neutral body;
I did not test this directly. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the option that removes the shape loss on the eight
constants for 3DPW people (Final-masked) and its trade-off.

The fixed age is right for these actors, who are all adults, but it carries no age information.
Figure~\ref{fig:data-age-sweep} shows what age means in Anny. At the mean shape, the skeletal height rises from
\DataSweepHeightAgeZero\,m at age 0 to \DataSweepHeightAgeHalf\,m at 0.5 and \DataSweepHeightAgeHigh\,m at 0.8. The
child cue is not stature alone but the width of the shoulders relative to the head (\DataShoulderHeadChild{} at age
0.1 vs \DataShoulderHeadAdult{} at 0.6). At \DataFixedAge{} every 3DPW label is a full adult body. 3DPW has no
children, so it can neither teach nor test them.

\paragraph{Head and hands at rest.} \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03} and \texttt{head} stay at identity in every
label, so the head turns only through \texttt{neck01}. The labels therefore cannot test the neck bones that
Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} finds turned half a turn. The SMPL ground truth of 3DPW has no finger joints, so every
label has rest-pose hands. For 3DPW people the training masks the rotation terms of the \DataHandMaskBones{} hand
bones (Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). The mask removes the rotation terms only; the mesh terms still carry the rest-pose
hands.

\paragraph{A floor of about \DataFloor\,mm.} The labels sit \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm (median) and
\DataCheckJointsNewPn\,mm (p95) from 3DPW's own joints. Any Anny-space error on 3DPW contains this residual, so
differences between models well below it say little about the real people.

\paragraph{Not comparable with published 3DPW numbers.} I never evaluated against the original SMPL meshes. Our
MPJPE averages all \DataBones{} Anny joints after aligning the pelvis, and both PVE and MPJPE are computed against
the converted labels. Published 3DPW results use SMPL joints and meshes. I therefore write ``lowest error on our
3DPW-Anny labels'' and never compare these numbers with the literature.

\paragraph{Bystanders.} 3DPW labels only its actors. A model that correctly detects a bystander is counted as
producing a false positive, so precision on 3DPW is a lower bound. Recall, which counts labelled actors only, is not
affected.

\section{Relative Human}
\label{sec:data-rh}

The holdout has no children and 3DPW has none either, so neither can show whether a model reads children or
gender on real photos. Relative Human, introduced with BEV~\cite{sun2022bev}, fills this gap. It holds
\DataRHImages{} real photos (\DataRHTrainImages{} train, \DataRHValImages{} validation, \DataRHTestImages{} test),
many of them crowded and with children. For each person it gives a box, an age group (adult, teen, kid, baby) and a
gender. It has no 3D labels. Its gender code uses 0 for male, the same direction as Anny.

I use only the validation split, and only for evaluation: \DataRHValImages{} images with \DataRHValPeople{}
labelled people (\DataRHValAdults{} adults, \DataRHValTeens{} teens, \DataRHValKids{} kids, \DataRHValBabies{}
babies and \DataRHValUnknownAge{} of unknown age). The dataset ships without a licence file, so I do not train on
it.

The evaluation runs the model once on each whole photo, as the demo does, with a field of view of
\DataRHFov$^\circ$. The box of each detection is the extent of its projected mesh. It is compared with the labelled
whole-body box where one exists and with the visible-part box otherwise. Detections and labelled people are paired
one to one, greedily by overlap, at an intersection over union of at least \DataRHIoU. A predicted age below
\DataRHChildThr{} counts as child-shaped, and a predicted gender below \DataRHGenderThr{} as male. The threshold is a
body-shape threshold on Anny's age, not an age in years. Per age group I report recall, the mean predicted age and
the share predicted child-shaped; across groups, the child-vs-adult AUC of the predicted age (kids and babies
against adults) and the gender accuracy. Every number covers detected people only, so recall is always given next to
it. The results are in Chapters~\ref{ch:diagnostics} and~\ref{ch:results}.

\section{The diagnostic manifest, the holdout and the demo images}
\label{sec:data-manifest}

\paragraph{Diagnostic manifest.} The checks of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} need the same people every time. A
seeded script picks them from the AnnyOne labels alone, without running a network, and stores them as a fixed list
of \DataManifestTotal{} (image, person) entries. \DataManifestTrain{} come from the training range: adults, the
youngest children, people with both hands clearly visible, and the hardest head turns and bends.
\DataManifestHoldout{} come from the holdout, which has no children. Base saw the training entries during its
training. Every later fine-tune (Final, Control, Student) gives their images a sampling weight of zero, so they stay
the same in-sample reference for all models. The manifest children are therefore in-sample: they show whether a
model can fit a child it has seen, not whether it generalises to new children.

\paragraph{Holdout.} The \DataHoldoutImages{} holdout images (Section~\ref{sec:data-annyone}) are the headline
synthetic test set. They are small, adult-only and synthetic. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reads every holdout
comparison against the run-to-run variation measured in Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}.

\paragraph{Demo images.} For by-eye comparisons I use \DataDemoTotal{} real images: \DataDemoPhotos{} photos from
the repository's example folder and \DataDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames, one middle frame from each of
\DataDemoFrames{} test sequences. No model trained on them. No metric is computed on them; every statement about
them is a by-eye reading and says so.

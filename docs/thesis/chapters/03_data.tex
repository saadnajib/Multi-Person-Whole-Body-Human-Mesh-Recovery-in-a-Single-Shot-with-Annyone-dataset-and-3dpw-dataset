\section{AnnyOne}
\label{sec:data-annyone}

AnnyOne~\cite{bregier2025anny} is the synthetic dataset released with Anny. It holds \DataAnnyOneImages{} rendered
images of \DataAnnyOneRenderPx{}$\times$\DataAnnyOneRenderPx{} pixels, with \DataAnnyOnePeoplePerImage{} people per
image on average. Because the images are rendered, every visible person has an exact label: the Anny pose
(\DataBones{} bone transforms), the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotypes (Anny's shape values, Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels})
and the camera intrinsics (focal length and image centre). The network sees each image resized to \DataInputPx{} pixels. The field of view changes from image to image,
from \DataAnnyOneFovMin{}$^\circ$ to \DataAnnyOneFovMax{}$^\circ$ on a small sample of \DataAnnyOneFovSample{} images.
For this reason training and evaluation use each image's own intrinsics.

\paragraph{Split.} AnnyOne has no official split, so I made one. Indices 0 to \DataAnnyOneLastTrainIndex{} are the
training set. The last \DataHoldoutImages{} images, indices \DataHoldoutFirstIndex{} to \DataHoldoutLastIndex, are
my holdout, which no model trained in this thesis has seen. NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne release, which
includes the \DataHoldoutImages{} images I hold out. Its \NAVERAnnyOne\,mm holdout PVE (the mean vertex error,
Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}) is potentially in-sample: it may include images seen during its own training. I have not verified
its exact image exposure. My models have never seen these images.

The 3DPW test script also scores the last \DataHoldoutImages{} AnnyOne images, but in setting V0. In V0 the error
counts Anny's helper bones (toes, breasts, face and subdivision bones); in my headline setting V1 they are masked out
(Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}). The script's ``AnnyOne test-100'' value is therefore not the holdout number, which I
always give in setting V1.

\paragraph{Few children.} In Anny the child shape appears below an age value of about \DataChildAgeLow{} to
\DataChildAgeHigh{} (Section~\ref{sec:bg-bodymodels}). Only \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\% of AnnyOne's people are
younger than 0.3 (\DataAnnyOneAgeBelowTwo\% below 0.2, \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowFour\% below 0.4). The median age is
\DataAnnyOneAgeMedian, and \DataAnnyOneAgeModeShare\% of all people lie between 0.5 and 0.6. In a training batch,
\DataBatchChildLow\% to \DataBatchChildHigh\% of the supervised people are younger than 0.3. The holdout has nobody
younger than \DataHoldoutMinAge{}, which means it tests adults only. This is why I added Relative Human (Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}).

\section{3DPW}
\label{sec:data-3dpw}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is a real video dataset of \DataThreeDPWSequences{} sequences filmed with a hand-held
phone camera at \DataThreeDPWWidth{}$\times$\DataThreeDPWHeight{} pixels (portrait) and \DataThreeDPWFps{} frames per
second. Inertial sensors on the actors give SMPL ground truth. The standard release has \DataThreeDPWTrainSeq{}
training, \DataThreeDPWValSeq{} validation and \DataThreeDPWTestSeq{} test sequences, with one or two labelled actors
each: \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} person-sequences (\DataThreeDPWMen{} men, \DataThreeDPWWomen{} women). Bystanders
have no labels. I keep the splits as 3DPW defines them. One validation sequence, \texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00},
gives no labels at all (Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}), and \DataThreeDPWValUsableSeq{} validation sequences remain.

\paragraph{Why 3DPW must be converted.} My network predicts Anny bodies. AnnyOne is the only dataset with Anny
labels, and it is synthetic. 3DPW has real photos, but its labels are SMPL bodies, which have a different skeleton
and different shape controls (Table~\ref{tab:body-models}). For this reason I fit an Anny body to every labelled 3DPW person and
write new labels in AnnyOne's format.

\section{Converting SMPL to Anny}
\label{sec:data-converter}

My converter (\texttt{smpl\_to\_anny.py}, version 2.3) works in four steps:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Read the source labels: joint positions, shape, camera and frame number of each person.
\item Fit an Anny body: move, turn and bend it until its joints match the 3DPW joints, first the root, then the
  bone rotations, finally the free shape values.
\item Keep one body shape per person: the median shape over the sequence, so the proportions stay the same through
  the video.
\item Build the image labels: refit every frame's pose with that shape, move the body into camera coordinates and
  save one label per image.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Objective.} There is no formula that turns joint positions back into Anny's pose and shape (Anny's skinning
has no analytic inverse). Instead I fit them by gradient descent: the fitter starts from a guess, measures the joint
mismatch and changes the values to reduce it. For each person and
frame I search Anny's \DataBones{} rotations $\mathbf{r}$ (6D form, two columns of each rotation matrix), the
phenotype logits $\boldsymbol{\ell}$ (values that the sigmoid $\sigma$ maps into $(0,1)$) and a translation
$\mathbf{t}$ so that Anny's joints land on the \DataSmplJoints{} SMPL joints $\hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}$ of the frame:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\min_{\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{t}}\
 & \frac{1}{24}\sum_{k=1}^{24}
   \left\| \mathbf{p}_{\pi(k)}\!\left(\mathbf{r},\sigma(\boldsymbol{\ell})\right)
   + \mathbf{t} - \hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}_{k}\right\|_2^2 \\
 & + \lambda_{r}\sum_{b\neq\text{root}}\left\|\mathbf{R}_b-\mathbf{I}\right\|_F^2
   + \lambda_{\phi}\sum_{j\in\text{free}}\left(\sigma(\ell_j)-\tfrac12\right)^2 ,
\end{split}
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the joint correspondence, $\mathbf{p}_{b}$ the point attached to bone $b$ (its head, or a virtual
joint, Section~\ref{sec:data-history}) and $\mathbf{R}_b$ the bone's rotation matrix. In words, the joints of the Anny
body must land on 3DPW's joints. The two extra terms penalise twists that move no joint and extreme shape values.

The first regulariser ($\lambda_r = \DataPoseReg$) pulls every non-root rotation towards identity, because a twist
about a bone's own axis moves no joint but corkscrews the mesh. The second ($\lambda_\phi = \DataShapeReg$) pulls the
free phenotypes towards 0.5, so that one remaining joint gap cannot drag a phenotype to an extreme. Adam (learning
rate \DataFitLr) fits in the order of step~2. Shape comes last because it is the weakest signal. Each frame starts
from the previous frame's solution (\DataColdIters{} iterations per stage on a person's first frame, \DataWarmIters{}
afterwards). The pose refit of step~4 takes \DataRefineIters{} iterations per frame. The targets are 3DPW's own per-frame \texttt{jointPositions}. I do not recompute them with the neutral SMPL model,
because 3DPW's shape coefficients belong to the gendered models, and the neutral model would move the joints by
\DataNeutralShift\,mm on average.

\paragraph{Bones and shape.} Only the root and \DataFittedBones{} of the \DataMappedBones{} mapped bones may rotate;
\DataRestBones{} of \DataBones{} bones stay at rest, among them \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03}, \texttt{head} and
every finger bone. The joint positions say nothing about most of the phenotypes. Weight, muscle, cup size, firmness
and the three ethnicity values move no joint, and adult age barely does. The converter therefore reads gender from
3DPW, fixes age at
\DataFixedAge{} (an adult value) and the seven others at \DataFixedNeutral, and fits only height and proportions.
Since version 2.3 it writes men as \DataGenderMale{} and women as \DataGenderFemale{}, Anny's convention
(\texttt{--gender\_convention anny}).

\paragraph{Frames, camera and label files.} I fit every third valid frame (\DataLabelFps{} frames per second). Since
version 2.3 one grid holds for every person of a sequence (frame numbers divisible by \DataFrameStride,
\texttt{--frame\_grid shared}), so both actors are fitted on the same images. This matters for multi-person
detection, because all labelled actors must appear in the same image label. The label builder writes one file per
image in the AnnyOne format and checks that the image exists. It drops a person when the fit error exceeds
\DataDropErr\,mm, less than \DataDropVisPct\% of the mesh is inside the image, or the box is narrower or shorter than \DataDropBox\,px. A dataset
class pads the portrait images to a square and shifts the principal point (the image centre in the intrinsics). The
training and evaluation code then run on these labels unchanged.

\section{Geometric correspondence and virtual joints}
\label{sec:data-history}

SMPL and Anny define some joints at different anatomical points. For example, both have a hip joint, but not at
exactly the same place, and matching the two directly would make the fitter distort the body to close that gap.
This means I cannot pair joints by name alone. Instead my final procedure puts both skeletons in their rest pose and
lines them up at the hip midpoint and along the body axes. It then chooses each Anny hip bone by distance and each
spine bone by its normalised height along the torso. Appendix~\ref{app:joints} gives the full correspondence.

For a torso target $k$ attached to Anny bone $b=\pi(k)$, the fitter uses a virtual joint
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf p_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)
   =\mathbf h_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)+\mathbf Q_b(\mathbf R,\boldsymbol\phi)\boldsymbol\delta_k ,
 \label{eq:virtual-joint}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf h_b$ is the posed bone head, $\mathbf Q_b$ its orientation, and $\boldsymbol\delta_k$ a constant
offset in the bone's rest frame, measured from the aligned rest skeletons. In words, the point used for fitting is
moved by a fixed offset that turns with the bone. The two skeletons are then compared at the same body location, and
Anny's rig stays intact. Limb targets use the bone head itself. The offsets only shrink the difference in where the
two models put a joint; SMPL and Anny are still two anatomically different bodies.

The optimiser's loss only says how well the fit met its own objective. It cannot tell me whether the finished labels

\section{What the 3DPW labels can and cannot test}
\label{sec:data-limits}

The v23 labels are converted labels, not ground truth. The way the converter is built limits what they can test, in
four ways.

\paragraph{Eight constant phenotypes.} The labels test three phenotypes: gender (from metadata), height and
proportions (fitted; medians \DataLabelMedianHeight{} and \DataLabelMedianProportions). The other
\DataConstantCount{} are the same for every person: age \DataFixedAge{} and \DataNeutralCount{} values at
\DataFixedNeutral. A model trained on 3DPW is pulled towards these constants for every real person, by the shape loss
and the mesh terms. By my estimate, 3DPW people are about \DataSupervisedShareThreeDPW\% of the supervised people in
a \DataMixAnnyOne/\DataMixThreeDPW{} batch. The 3DPW error is also measured against these neutral-body meshes. I expect
that it rewards a neutral prediction, but I did not test this. The shape mask of Section~\ref{sec:method-real} removes the
shape loss on the constants (Final-masked). At age \DataFixedAge{} every label is a full adult
(Figure~\ref{fig:bg-age-sweep}). 3DPW has no children, and these labels can neither teach nor test them.

\paragraph{Head and hands at rest.} \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03} and \texttt{head} stay at identity in every
label. The head turns only through \texttt{neck01}, and the labels cannot test the half-turned neck bones of
Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics}. 3DPW's SMPL ground truth has no finger joints, so every label has rest-pose hands. The
training masks their rotation terms for 3DPW people (Section~\ref{sec:method-real}).

\paragraph{Residual label error.} The checked labels differ from 3DPW's source body joints by
\DataCheckJointsNew\,mm at the median and \DataCheckJointsNewPn\,mm at the 95th percentile. This number describes
label quality. A network is compared with the fitted Anny targets and not with the source joints. The residual is
therefore no universal error floor, and it must not be subtracted from a network's PVE
(Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}).

\paragraph{Not comparable with published numbers.} I never evaluated against the original SMPL meshes, my targets
carry the residual error above, and my MPJPE (the mean joint error) averages all \DataBones{} Anny joints (Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}).
For these three reasons my 3DPW numbers cannot be compared with published 3DPW results, and I write ``lowest error
on our 3DPW-Anny labels''. Bystanders have no labels, and a model that detects one of them correctly still counts as
a false detection. For this reason the precision I measure on 3DPW (the share of detections that match a labelled
person) is only a lower bound.

\section{Relative Human}
\label{sec:data-rh}

Neither the holdout nor 3DPW contains children, and I needed a third dataset for them. Relative Human, introduced
with BEV~\cite{sun2022bev}, is such a dataset. It holds \DataRHImages{} real photos, many crowded and with children. Each person has a box, an age group
(adult, teen, kid, baby) and a gender coded 0 for male, as in Anny; there are no 3D labels. The copy I use came
without a licence file. Its redistribution terms still need checking. I use only the validation split, for
evaluation: \DataRHValImages{} images with \DataRHValPeople{} labelled people (\DataRHValAdults{} adults,
\DataRHValTeens{} teens, \DataRHValKids{} kids, \DataRHValBabies{} babies, \DataRHValUnknownAge{} of unknown age).

The model runs once on each whole photo with a field of view of \DataRHFov$^\circ$, and detections are paired with
labelled boxes as in Section~\ref{sec:method-eval}. A predicted age below \DataRHChildThr{} counts as child-shaped, a
predicted gender below \DataRHGenderThr{} as male. For each age group I report recall (the share of labelled
people found) and the mean predicted age. I also report the gender accuracy and the child-against-adult AUC of the
predicted age. This AUC is the chance that a kid or baby gets a lower predicted age than an adult; a value of 0.5
means chance level. Every number covers detected people only, and recall always stands next to it.

\section{The diagnostic manifest and the demo images}
\label{sec:data-manifest}

For the checks of Appendix~\ref{ch:diagnostics} I picked \DataManifestTotal{} fixed (image, person) entries from
the AnnyOne labels alone, with a script and a fixed random seed, so the choice can be repeated: \DataManifestTrain{}
from the training range and \DataManifestHoldout{} from the holdout (groups in Table~\ref{tab:diag-checks}). Base,
the AnnyOne-only model that every later run starts from, saw the training entries during its own training. Every
later run gives their images a sampling weight of zero, and no model sees them again. This keeps them the same
already-seen reference for all models. The manifest children therefore show whether a model can fit a child it has
seen, not whether it generalises.

For by-eye comparisons I use \DataDemoTotal{} real images: \DataDemoPhotos{} photos and \DataDemoFrames{} 3DPW test
frames, one middle frame from each of \DataDemoFrames{} test sequences. No model trained on them and no metric is
computed on them. The photo comparison with Multi-HMR-Anny (Section~\ref{sec:res-image-compare}) and the walking
video (Section~\ref{sec:res-video}) have no labels either. I can only show their meshes, and no reconstruction error can
be computed for them.

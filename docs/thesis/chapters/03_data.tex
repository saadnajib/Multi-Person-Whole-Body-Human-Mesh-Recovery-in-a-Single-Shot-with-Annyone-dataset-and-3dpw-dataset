\chapter{Data}
\label{ch:data}

This chapter describes the images and labels behind every number in the thesis (Table~\ref{tab:datasets}).
AnnyOne is synthetic, carries exact Anny labels and is the main training set. 3DPW is real video with SMPL ground
truth; I converted it to Anny so that the model can train and be tested on real people in Anny space
(requirements 1c and 2a). Relative Human is a set of real photos with age-group and gender labels, used only to
evaluate children and gender. A diagnostic manifest and twelve demo images serve the checks of
Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} and the by-eye comparisons of Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. Most of the chapter is about
the 3DPW conversion, because the converted labels decide what the real-image numbers can show.

\section{AnnyOne}
\label{sec:data-annyone}

AnnyOne~\cite{bregier2025anny} is the synthetic dataset released with the Anny body model. It holds
\DataAnnyOneImages{} rendered images of \DataAnnyOneRenderPx{}$\times$\DataAnnyOneRenderPx{} pixels, with
\DataAnnyOnePeoplePerImage{} people per image on average. For every visible person the label gives the exact Anny
pose (\DataBones{} bone transforms), the \DataPhenotypes{} phenotype values and the camera intrinsics. The network
sees each image resized to \DataInputPx{} pixels, a grid of \DataTokenGrid{}$\times$\DataTokenGrid{} tokens of
\DataPatchPx{} pixels. The field of view changes from image to image (\DataAnnyOneFovMin{}$^\circ$ to
\DataAnnyOneFovMax{}$^\circ$ on a sample of \DataAnnyOneFovSample{} images), so training and evaluation use each
image's own intrinsics.

\paragraph{Split.} Indices 0 to \DataAnnyOneLastTrainIndex{} are the training set. The last \DataHoldoutImages{}
images, indices \DataHoldoutFirstIndex{} to \DataHoldoutLastIndex, are the holdout; no model trained in this thesis
has seen them. The holdout is my own choice, not an official test split. NAVER's model was trained on the AnnyOne
release, which includes these \DataHoldoutImages{} images, so for that model the holdout is most likely in-sample
(Chapter~\ref{ch:results}). The 3DPW test script also scores the last \DataHoldoutImages{} AnnyOne images, most
likely the same ones, but in setting V0 (helper bones unmasked, Chapter~\ref{ch:method}). Its ``AnnyOne test-100''
value is therefore not the holdout number, which is always given in setting V1.

\paragraph{Few children.} In Anny the child shape appears below an age of about \DataChildAgeLow{} to
\DataChildAgeHigh{} (Chapter~\ref{ch:background}). I never convert these values to years, because three age scales
in the code and the data disagree. Only \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowThree\% of AnnyOne's people are younger than 0.3
(\DataAnnyOneAgeBelowTwo\% below 0.2, \DataAnnyOneAgeBelowFour\% below 0.4); the median age is
\DataAnnyOneAgeMedian, and \DataAnnyOneAgeModeShare\% of all people lie between 0.5 and 0.6. In a normal training
batch, \DataBatchChildLow\% to \DataBatchChildHigh\% of the supervised people are younger than 0.3. The holdout has
nobody younger than \DataHoldoutMinAge. It tests adults only, which is why I added Relative Human
(Section~\ref{sec:data-rh}).

\section{3DPW}
\label{sec:data-3dpw}

3DPW~\cite{vonmarcard2018_3dpw} is a real video dataset of \DataThreeDPWSequences{} sequences filmed with a
hand-held phone camera, outdoors and indoors, at \DataThreeDPWWidth{}$\times$\DataThreeDPWHeight{} pixels
(portrait) and \DataThreeDPWFps{} frames per second. Inertial sensors on the actors give SMPL ground
truth. The standard release has \DataThreeDPWTrainSeq{} training, \DataThreeDPWValSeq{} validation and
\DataThreeDPWTestSeq{} test sequences, with one or two labelled actors each: \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{}
person-sequences in total (\DataThreeDPWMen{} men, \DataThreeDPWWomen{} women). Bystanders have no labels.

SMPL differs from Anny in skeleton and shape parameters, so I wrote a converter that fits an Anny body to every
labelled person (Section~\ref{sec:data-converter}). The splits stay as 3DPW defines them (uses in
Table~\ref{tab:data-splits}). One validation sequence, \texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}, yields no labels
(Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}), which leaves \DataThreeDPWValUsableSeq{} usable validation sequences.

\section{Converting SMPL to Anny}
\label{sec:data-converter}

Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the six steps from a 3DPW frame to an Anny label. The converter
(\texttt{smpl\_to\_anny.py}, version 2.3) fits each person; a label builder merges the fits into one file per image.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
  \centering
  \input{figures/tikz/F02_pipeline}
  \caption[From a 3DPW frame to an Anny label]{The six steps from a 3DPW frame to an Anny label: SMPL joints, frame grid, per-frame fit, one body per
  person, camera frame, label file with the drop rules. Since converter version 2.3 both actors of a sequence share
  the frame grid.}
  \label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Objective.} Anny has no analytic inverse of its skinning, so I fit by gradient descent,
following NVlabs' \texttt{smpl2soma} for the autograd route and \texttt{joints2smpl} for the joint objective.
For each person and frame I search
Anny's \DataBones{} rotations $\mathbf{r}$ (6D), the phenotype logits $\boldsymbol{\ell}$ and a translation
$\mathbf{t}$ so that Anny's joints land on the \DataSmplJoints{} SMPL joints $\hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}$ of the frame:
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
\min_{\mathbf{r},\boldsymbol{\ell},\mathbf{t}}\
 & \frac{1}{24}\sum_{k=1}^{24}
   \left\| \mathbf{p}_{\pi(k)}\!\left(\mathbf{r},\sigma(\boldsymbol{\ell})\right)
   + \mathbf{t} - \hat{\mathbf{J}}^{S}_{k}\right\|_2^2 \\
 & + \lambda_{r}\sum_{b\neq\text{root}}\left\|\mathbf{R}_b-\mathbf{I}\right\|_F^2
   + \lambda_{\phi}\sum_{j\in\text{free}}\left(\sigma(\ell_j)-\tfrac12\right)^2 ,
\end{split}
\label{eq:fit}
\end{equation}
where $\pi$ is the joint correspondence and $\mathbf{p}_{b}$ the point attached to bone $b$ (defined below). The
first regulariser ($\lambda_r = \DataPoseReg$) pulls every non-root rotation towards identity: a twist about a
bone's own axis moves no joint but corkscrews the mesh, so without it the solution is not unique. The second
($\lambda_\phi = \DataShapeReg$) pulls the free phenotypes towards 0.5, so that one remaining joint gap cannot drag a
phenotype to an extreme.

\paragraph{Stages and targets.} Adam (learning rate \DataFitLr) first fits the root orientation and translation,
then the bone rotations, then the free phenotypes. Shape comes last because it is the weakest signal; fitted
earlier, it absorbs pose error. Each frame starts from the previous frame's solution (\DataColdIters{} iterations per
stage for a person's first frame, \DataWarmIters{} afterwards). The targets are 3DPW's own per-frame
\texttt{jointPositions}. A forward pass of the neutral SMPL model is wrong here, because 3DPW's shape coefficients
belong to the gendered models; on the first frame I checked, it moved the joints by \DataNeutralShift\,mm on average
and \DataNeutralShiftFeet\,mm at the feet. Only joint positions are matched, not SMPL's rotations, shape or surface.

\paragraph{Correspondence checked by geometry.} My first converter chose the Anny bones by name, and two choices
were wrong in a way no name reveals. Anny's MakeHuman rig numbers the spine top-down (\texttt{spine01} below the
neck), SMPL bottom-up, so SMPL's lumbar \texttt{spine1} had been mapped to a bone \DataSpineMapError\,cm above it.
SMPL's hips had been mapped to \texttt{pelvis.L/R}, whose heads lie \DataHipMapError\,cm from the hip joint, instead
of \texttt{upperleg01.L/R}. The converter now places both models in their rest poses and picks the spine bones by
height along the body axis and the hip bones by distance from the pelvis (Appendix~\ref{app:joints}).

\paragraph{Virtual joints.} Even with the right bones, the rigs define their torso joints at different anatomical
points. In the rest pose, SMPL's pelvis joint sits \DataSmplPelvisAboveHips\,cm above its hips, while Anny's root
sits at hip height and \DataAnnyRootFront\,cm in front; SMPL's \texttt{spine3} lies \DataSpineThreeGap\,cm from the
nearest Anny bone head and its collars \DataCollarGap\,cm further out. For \DataVirtualJoints{} torso joints I
therefore measure once, in the rest pose, the offset $\boldsymbol{\delta}_b$ from the Anny bone head to the SMPL
joint, in the bone's own frame. The fit moves the point $\mathbf{p}_b = \mathbf{h}_b + \mathbf{R}_b\boldsymbol{\delta}_b$
onto the SMPL joint, where $\mathbf{h}_b$ and $\mathbf{R}_b$ are the posed bone head and frame. The offsets are
\DataOffsetPelvis\,cm (pelvis), \DataOffsetHip\,cm (hips), \DataOffsetSpineMin{} to \DataOffsetSpineMax\,cm
(spine) and \DataOffsetCollar\,cm (collars); limb joints use the bone head.

\paragraph{Bones and shape.} Only the root and \DataFittedBones{} of the \DataMappedBones{} mapped bones may rotate.
The other bones have no target; in the first version about \DataUntargetedBones{} of them drifted freely. In the
labels \DataRestBones{} of \DataBones{} bones stay at rest, among them \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03},
\texttt{head}, the jaw and every finger bone. Phenotypes are not mapped from SMPL's shape coefficients, which have no
semantic counterpart, and joints cannot determine most of them: weight, muscle, cup size, firmness and the three
ethnicity values move no joint, and adult age barely does. Left free, they became slack
(Section~\ref{sec:data-history}). The converter therefore reads gender from 3DPW, fixes age at \DataFixedAge{}
(close to the AnnyOne mean; the 3DPW actors are adults) and the seven others at \DataFixedNeutral, and fits only
height and proportions. In Anny, gender 0 is male and 1 is female (rest stature \DataAnnyStatureMale\,m at 0,
\DataAnnyStatureFemale\,m at 1). Since version 2.3 the converter writes men as \DataGenderMale{} and women as
\DataGenderFemale{} (\texttt{--gender\_convention anny}); earlier versions had it reversed
(Section~\ref{sec:data-defects}). After a first pass over all frames, the median of each free phenotype becomes the
person's one body, and every frame's pose is fitted again with it (\DataRefineIters{} iterations).
Table~\ref{tab:converter-fixed} lists what is fitted, read and fixed.

\input{tables/T06_converter_fitted_fixed}

\paragraph{Frames, camera and label files.} I fit every third valid frame (\DataLabelFps{} frames per second);
neighbouring frames are near-duplicates. Since version 2.3 one absolute grid holds for every person of a sequence
(frame numbers divisible by \DataFrameStride, \texttt{--frame\_grid shared}), so both actors are fitted on the same
images wherever both are valid. Only the root changes when the body moves into the camera frame: its rotation becomes
$\mathbf{R}_{\text{cam}}\mathbf{Q}_{\text{root}}$ and its position
$\mathbf{R}_{\text{cam}}(\mathbf{t}+\mathbf{t}_{\text{3DPW}})+\mathbf{t}_{\text{cam}}$. The posed mesh, projected with
3DPW's camera, gives a box and the fraction of vertices inside the image. The builder writes one file per image in
the AnnyOne format and drops a person when the fit error exceeds \DataDropErr\,mm, less than \DataDropVisPct\% of the
mesh is inside the image, or the box is narrower or shorter than \DataDropBox\,px. A dataset class returns these
labels in AnnyOne's sample format, padding the portrait images to a square and shifting the principal point, so the
training and evaluation code run on them unchanged.

\section{Development of the converter}
\label{sec:data-history}

Each step of the converter's development answered a measured symptom.

\paragraph{A common floor.} The first converter (v1) gave \DataVOneBarMean\,mm on \DataVOneBarFrames{} frames of
\texttt{downtown\_bar\_00} (Table~\ref{tab:fit-stages}). On four test scenes, two facts in the log showed a defect
before I looked at any render. The error was \DataVOneFloorMin{} to \DataVOneFloorMax\,mm on every frame of every
scene, and all four actors came out with the same body (gender \DataVOneGender, age \DataVOneAge, height
\DataVOneHeight, asian \DataVOneAsian). A residual independent of the pose and a shape independent of the person mean
that the shape absorbed a fixed mismatch between the skeleton definitions. The rest-pose comparison then showed the
two wrong bones described above. The renders had looked wrong for a separate reason: the renderer used 3DPW's
\texttt{img\_frame\_ids}, an index into the 60\,Hz pose arrays, as image numbers.

\section{Three label defects and the v2.3 re-fit}
\label{sec:data-defects}

The converter grades its own fit, so it cannot see errors in what it is asked to fit or in what happens to the fits
afterwards. I therefore wrote an independent check that reads the finished label files. On sampled images from every
sequence it compares the labels with 3DPW's joints, surface and 2D keypoints, confirms that the loader passes them on
unchanged, and tests their consistency: gender, a label for every valid actor, head turn. On the first label set
(``old labels'', v2.2 fits, about \DataOldCheckImages{} images) its grade was FAIL, with three defects.

\paragraph{Defect 1: gender reversed.} The converter wrote 1.0 for men and 0.0 for women, MakeHuman's convention and
the reverse of Anny's. Every 3DPW man was fitted and labelled with a female body. Refitting with the flipped value
lowered the joint error for \DataGenderFlipMen{} of \DataGenderFlipMenOf{} men (median \DataGenderFlipMenBefore{} to
\DataGenderFlipMenAfter\,mm). For women it raised the joint error (\DataGenderFlipWomenBefore{} to
\DataGenderFlipWomenAfter\,mm) but brought the surface closer for \DataGenderFlipWomenSurface{} of
\DataGenderFlipWomenOf, so the fix was then confirmed for men only. With 3DPW at \DataMixThreeDPW\% of each batch, a
model trained on these labels keeps learning a female body for every real man.

\paragraph{Defect 2: missing actor.} In \DataMissingActor{} of \DataTwoPersonChecked{} sampled two-person images,
one valid actor had no label because it had not been fitted on that frame. Each person's every-third-frame grid
started at that person's own first valid frame, so after an invalid gap the two actors sat on different phases
(frames 0, 3, 6 against 1, 4, 7). The detection loss then treats a visible, real person as background.

\paragraph{Defect 3: missing labels.} The training split had \DataOldTrainImages{} label images where the fits implied
\DataRebuiltTrainImages{} (\DataOldTrainMissingPct\% missing), validation \DataOldValImages{} of
\DataRebuiltValImages. A first rebuild gave the same counts. The image files of \DataMissingImageSeq{} sequences were missing or incomplete on
the cluster (\texttt{courtyard\_captureSelfies\_00}: \DataSelfiesFilesBefore{} of \DataSelfiesFilesAfter{} files),
most likely from an incomplete unzip, and the builder skips frames without an image file without counting them.
After I re-extracted the images, a second rebuild recovered every missing label (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets},
``rebuilt''). That set still had defects 1 and 2, so I never trained on it.

\paragraph{The re-fit.} Defects 1 and 2 arise inside the fit. Version 2.3 adds the two switches named above,
\texttt{--gender\_convention anny} and \texttt{--frame\_grid shared}; their defaults reproduce v2.2 exactly, so the
old labels of the history models stay reproducible. I re-fitted all \DataThreeDPWPersonSeq{} people with both switches
on. The re-fit's post-check passed on all \DataRefitSeq{} sequences (Table~\ref{tab:label-check}, middle block). Every
man now has gender 0 and every woman 1. The men's mean joint error fell from \DataRefitMenBefore{} to
\DataRefitMenAfter\,mm, the women's stayed level (\DataRefitWomenBefore{} to \DataRefitWomenAfter\,mm).
\DataRefitWorseSeq{} single-person outdoor sequences became \DataRefitWorseMin{} to \DataRefitWorseMax\,mm worse; the
likely reason is a different warm start, which I did not test. Two-person frames with one valid actor unfitted went
from \DataRefitGapBefore{} of \DataRefitGapBeforeOf{} (\DataRefitGapBeforePct\%) to none.

\paragraph{The v23 labels.} I call the labels built from the v2.3 fits the v23 labels: \DataThreeDPWTotalImages{}
images and \DataThreeDPWTotalPeople{} people (Table~\ref{tab:label-sets}). They have fewer images than the rebuilt set
but the same people, because both actors now share each frame. No person was dropped for fit error or box size.
\DataCrossCountryFramesNew{} frames were dropped for visibility, all from \texttt{outdoors\_crosscountry\_00}. With
3DPW's own camera, the ground-truth joints of that sequence sit at a depth of $\DataCrossCountryDepth$\,m, behind the
camera, and project far outside the image. The old fits show the same (\DataCrossCountryFramesOld{} frames), and no
label set ever contained this sequence. The 3D fit is fine; image labels for it cannot exist with this camera.

\paragraph{The check on the v23 labels.} I ran the same check on the v23 labels (\DataCheckPeople{} people in
\DataCheckImages{} images, \DataCheckPerSeq{} per sequence, \DataCheckSeq{} sequences; Table~\ref{tab:label-check}).
The grade moved from FAIL to WARN. The median body-joint error fell from \DataCheckJointsOld{} to
\DataCheckJointsNew\,mm. Flipping the gender now makes the fit worse (men \DataCheckFlipMenLabel{} to
\DataCheckFlipMenFlipped\,mm), and no sampled two-person image misses an actor. The head turn relative to the chest
is now kept (\DataCheckHeadLabelNew$^\circ$ in the labels against \DataCheckHeadSmplNew$^\circ$ in SMPL); in the old

further. The three defects are gone, and the independent check confirms it. Figure~\ref{fig:label-check} shows panels

\section{What the 3DPW labels can and cannot test}
\label{sec:data-limits}

The v23 labels are free of the three defects, but they are converted labels, not ground truth. Five limits follow
from the converter's design, and each bounds a later claim.

\paragraph{Eight constant phenotypes.} The labels test three phenotypes: gender (from metadata), height and
proportions (fitted; medians \DataLabelMedianHeight{} and \DataLabelMedianProportions). The other
\DataConstantCount{} are the same for every person: age \DataFixedAge{} and \DataNeutralCount{} values at
\DataFixedNeutral. A model trained on 3DPW is pulled towards these constants for every real person, by the shape loss
and by the mesh terms, because a 3DPW person's ground-truth mesh is built from the same values. By my estimate 3DPW people
are about \DataSupervisedShareThreeDPW\% of the supervised people in a 70/30 batch. The 3DPW error is also measured against these neutral-body meshes, so it
likely rewards a neutral prediction; I did not test this. Chapter~\ref{ch:results} reports the option that removes
the shape loss on the eight constants for 3DPW people (Final-masked).

The fixed age suits the 3DPW actors, who are all adults, but carries no age information.
Figure~\ref{fig:bg-age-sweep} (Chapter~\ref{ch:background}) shows what age does in Anny: the skeletal height rises from
\DataSweepHeightAgeZero\,m at age 0 to \DataSweepHeightAgeHalf\,m at 0.5 and \DataSweepHeightAgeHigh\,m at 0.8, and
the child cue is the shoulder width relative to the head (\DataShoulderHeadChild{} at age 0.1, \DataShoulderHeadAdult{}
at 0.6) more than stature. At \DataFixedAge{} every 3DPW label is a full adult. 3DPW has no children, so it can
neither teach nor test them.

% F21 (Anny age sweep) is Figure fig:bg-age-sweep in Chapter 2; the copy that stood here was removed on 18 Oct (review).

\paragraph{Head and hands at rest.} \texttt{neck02}, \texttt{neck03} and \texttt{head} stay at identity in every
label, so the head turns only through \texttt{neck01}, and the labels cannot test the half-turned neck bones of
Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}. 3DPW's SMPL ground truth has no finger joints, so every label has rest-pose hands. For
3DPW people the training masks the rotation terms of the \DataHandMaskBones{} hand bones (Chapter~\ref{ch:method});
the mesh terms still carry the rest-pose hands.

\paragraph{A floor of about \DataFloor\,mm.} The labels sit \DataCheckJointsNew\,mm (median) and
\DataCheckJointsNewPn\,mm (p95) from 3DPW's own joints. Every Anny-space error on 3DPW contains this residual.

\paragraph{Not comparable with published 3DPW numbers.} I never evaluated against the original SMPL meshes. Our MPJPE
averages all \DataBones{} Anny joints after pelvis alignment, and PVE and MPJPE both use the converted labels. I
therefore write ``lowest error on our 3DPW-Anny labels'' and never set these numbers against published ones.

\paragraph{Bystanders.} 3DPW labels only its actors. A correct detection of a bystander counts as a false positive, so
precision on 3DPW is a lower bound. Recall counts labelled actors only and is not affected.

\section{Relative Human}
\label{sec:data-rh}

Neither the holdout nor 3DPW contains children, so neither shows whether a model reads children or gender on real
photos. Relative Human, introduced with BEV~\cite{sun2022bev}, fills this gap. It holds \DataRHImages{} real photos
(\DataRHTrainImages{} train, \DataRHValImages{} validation, \DataRHTestImages{} test), many crowded and with children.
Each person has a box, an age group (adult, teen, kid, baby) and a gender coded 0 for male, as in Anny; there are no
3D labels. I use only the validation split and only for evaluation, since the dataset ships without a licence file:
\DataRHValImages{} images with \DataRHValPeople{} labelled people (\DataRHValAdults{} adults, \DataRHValTeens{} teens,
\DataRHValKids{} kids, \DataRHValBabies{} babies, \DataRHValUnknownAge{} of unknown age).

The model runs once on each whole photo, as in the demo, with a field of view of \DataRHFov$^\circ$. Each detection's
box is the extent of its projected mesh, and detections are paired one to one with labelled boxes, greedily, at an
intersection over union of at least \DataRHIoU. A predicted age below \DataRHChildThr{} counts as child-shaped, a body-shape threshold rather than an age
in years, and a predicted gender below \DataRHGenderThr{} as male. I report recall and mean predicted age per age
group, the child-vs-adult AUC of the predicted age (kids and babies against adults) and the gender accuracy. Every
number covers detected people only, so recall always stands next to it.

\section{The diagnostic manifest, the holdout and the demo images}
\label{sec:data-manifest}

\paragraph{Diagnostic manifest.} The checks of Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics} need the same people every time. A seeded
script picks them from the AnnyOne labels alone and stores \DataManifestTotal{} fixed (image, person) entries.
\DataManifestTrain{} come from the training range (adults, the youngest children, clearly visible hands, the hardest
head turns), \DataManifestHoldout{} from the holdout, which has no children. Base saw the training entries; every
later fine-tune (Final, Control, Student) gives their images a sampling weight of zero, so they remain the same
in-sample reference for all models. The manifest children are therefore in-sample: they show whether a model can fit
a child it has seen, not whether it generalises.

\paragraph{Holdout.} The \DataHoldoutImages{} holdout images (Section~\ref{sec:data-annyone}) are the headline
synthetic test set: small, adult-only and synthetic. Every holdout comparison in Chapter~\ref{ch:results} is read
against the run-to-run variation measured in Chapter~\ref{ch:diagnostics}.

\paragraph{Demo images.} For by-eye comparisons I use \DataDemoTotal{} real images: \DataDemoPhotos{} photos and
\DataDemoFrames{} 3DPW test frames, one middle frame from each of \DataDemoFrames{} test sequences. No model trained on
them and no metric is computed on them; every statement about them is a by-eye reading and says so.
